About a decade ago (2), Wilhelm Brandenstein dealt rather thoroughly with
the IE clusters ms and ns. Starting from Franz Specht's claim that a phonetic
change of ms to ns cannot be evidenced in IE (3), Brandenstein tries to
establish that the IE cluster nasal + s is preserved only when the consonants
are separated by a syllable boundary, whereas tautosyllabic *ms becomes
ns and original *ns is never maintained in the same position. This argument
is chiefly based on Indo-Iranian and Greek evidence and entails a couple of
interesting corollaries for the history of Indo-European :
a. the accusative plural ending -ns reflects an original -ms ( : [ace. sg.] -m) ;
b. IE *mens 'moon, month' arose from leveling in the paradigm * mêmes- :

Cercle belge de linguistique in 1953. It was the outcome of further inquiry regarding the
etymology of Gmc. *ansuz 'ruling god', which I connected with Hitt. haHSuS 'king' in
Études germaniques, 8.36-44 (1953). There, I derived these words from IE *ans- ( : Lat.
änsa 'handle', Olcel. ses [< *ansjô] 'thread-hole in a shoe', etc.), which I connected with
IE *aT}gh- ( : Lat. angö 'I squeeze [esp. the throat]', Gk. αγχω 'I hug, strangle', etc.),
positing a common IE root *an- (< PIE *H2én-) 'tie'. In private correspondence,

Wörterbuch, 35 [Bern, 1947]), with assimilation in place of articulation of the labial nasal
