exhibited only three vowel qualities (?, u, a) was shown to be incor

phonemes (*/i/ and */e/) versus one back phoneme (#/u/). But while

some have presented the Proto-Germanic short/lax vowels in a rec
tangle which gives a false impression of symmetry,2 others have at
tempted to restore a genuine symmetry by assuming that after certain
early vowel modifications (or 'umlauts') produced by both vocalic and

consonantal influences, Proto-Germanic came to exhibit the parallel
phonemes */i ~ e/ and */ u ~ o/.3
In a recent contribution on Proto-Germanic morphophonemics,
Frans van Coetsem notes that the ensuing controversy has not yet been
settled one way or the other and?having lamented "the difficulty of

w-umlaut [and] (2) what the changes were in nonstressed position"?
concludes that "even if there was w-umlaut in PGmc, there may or
may not have been total complementarity of i and e."4
