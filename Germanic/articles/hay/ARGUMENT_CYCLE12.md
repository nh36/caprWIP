# Historical argument: what did the direct singular of ‘hay’ inherit?

Cycle 12, based on `45e8e95`. This model supersedes the synthesis, not
the observations, of [`ARGUMENT_CYCLE11.md`](ARGUMENT_CYCLE11.md).
Trace provenance and the source audit are in `RESEARCH_MEMO_CYCLE12.md`.
The falsifiable claim is that the earliest Anglian direct forms need
not have acquired their nucleus or their glide from another stem. An
independently established incompatible nucleus or prior regular loss of
final /i/ in this environment would weigh against that claim.

## 1. Evidence and elementary propositions

“Conditional” describes an inference from stated premises, not a
manuscript reading. Each entry gives the evidence, what constrains it,
and what would change if it failed.

### Adversarial review and selected thesis

- **A written lookalike is not survival.** Remove E07 and the spelling
  observation (E06) remains, but C05 loses its lexical force. Grant E07
  and the result is still an early written correspondence, not quantity,
  hiatus, or case. E08 supplies a genuine competing spelling route.
- **Gliding cannot be a sound law invented for this noun.** E11 supplies
  time for hiatus to persist, E12 supplies a mechanism, E13 an ordinary
  way to spell the product. Their conjunction makes H1 viable, but none
  independently fixes final front-vowel conditioning. A matched early
  /e.i/ > /ē/ example preceding Vespasian would put H1 under real pressure.
- **Contraction has the more specific vowel-quality parallel; it also
  destroys the segment that H2 has to restore.** Counting operations is
  not enough. H3 has stronger precedent as a morphological type but no
  identified English donor; *glīg* cannot by itself fill that gap for H2.
- **Do not export a scribe's vowel.** E15 weighs against treating short
  /ej/ as sufficient for Farman if the accent and digraph indicate length.
  The earlier Mercian ⟨heg⟩ leaves the contrast unmarked; length in
  Farman does not establish it everywhere or rule out earlier survival.
- **Differing stress nuclei, not late spelling conventions, drive the
  dialectal verdict.** The high West Saxon forms are positive contrary
  evidence for the tested unchanged mid-vowel continuations, regardless
  of Eadwine. The absence of that contrast in Anglian cannot prove H1,
  but makes importing the whole stem unnecessary there as an inference.

**Thesis.** Under early apocope, the inherited direct stem gives a
historically motivated explanation for the earliest Anglian mid vowel.
Regular /hej/ is defensible where neither length nor prior contraction
is established, and is preferable to a special reading of ⟨g⟩ as hiatus;
the record does not choose it over contraction plus glide restoration.
Where length is favoured, contraction plus glide restoration is a
sufficient, not a uniquely established, repair. The Kentish spelling is
positive corroboration of the correspondence, not proof of inherited
direct morphology. West Saxon high vocalism gives a separate, positive
reason for transfer if apocope was early. The meaningful distinction
from ‘bed’ is the possible survival of the inherited vocalism, not a
categorical decision about the chronology.
