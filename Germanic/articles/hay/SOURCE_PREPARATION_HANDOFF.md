## Established observations

- The noun ‘hay’ is securely identified in numerous Old English glosses.
  The Paris Psalter provides three manuscript-verified dative singulars
  *hege* at PPs 101.4, 101.9, and 102.14 (BnF lat. 8824, fols. 116r,
  116v, 118v; s. xi med.). These establish an overtly inflected cell, but
  do not date an oblique donor stem before i-umlaut.
- Rushworth 1 (Farman) has printed *hóeg* at Mt 6:30 and 14:19 in three
  editions; the manuscript pages are not included in the available
  Digital Bodleian selection. The corrected census covers the complete
  fol. 1r–Mk 2:15 stint: 196 non-target types/299 tokens plus target
  *hóeg* (one type/two tokens), 197 types/301 Old English tokens total.
  The bounded matched calibration finds short V+glide *weg/dæg* accented
  in 1/53 tokens (1/9 surface types; 1/2 lexical types), long V+glide
  controls *stīg* (1/2 tokens) and *stīgan* (0/12), and short-HL controls
  in 5/133 tokens (5/23 surface types; 4/5 lexical types). The expanded
  long-V+glide sample totals 1/14 accented tokens, 1/10 surface types,
  and 1/2 related lexical types. *Stígas* at Mt 3:3, fol. 4v, is
  supported as accusative plural by Latin *semitas eius*; *stige* at
  Mark 1:3, fol. 52v, is an uncertain alternative beside *gongas*.
  Both are following-vowel forms. One *stīgan* token, *astig* at Mt
  27:40, fol. 48v, has a closed stressed root syllable but is a prefixed
  disyllabic imperative; the other eleven are multisyllabic. No
  long-V+glide monosyllabic word-form control was found. The two
  lexemes share a root and are not independent evidence. Short-V+glide
  closed monosyllables are 1/26 accented and forms with following vowel
  graphs 0/27; *weig* at Mark 1:3 (fol. 52v) is included. Eight
  uncertain *gang*-family tokens and one *dægne* are reported separately
  and excluded. Counting short-HL controls as short gives 6/186
  accented (6/32 surface types; 5/7 lexical types); treating them as
  lengthened gives 6/147 (6/33; 5/7). This selected sample is
  descriptive, not a population rate; repeated tokens are not
  independent. The acutes suggest Farman's own late tenth-century
  practice, not ninth-century quantity.
- The spelling *cyniges heiweg* is secure in Sawyer S 1196, BL Cotton
  Augustus ii.16, a surviving sheet dated s. ix med. The transaction is
  conventionally dated ?859 by Lowe; the date may be erroneous, and the
  witness-list may be a separate writing stage. ‘Hay-way’ is plausible,
  not settled: ‘high-way’ and an enclosure-related analysis remain live.
  The compound does not attest an independent nominative singular.
- Cooper (2003: 61 nn. 151, 155) knew of S 1196 and deliberately excluded
  it from his chronology of highway terminology because he suspected
  corruption. The anomalous dating, Latin/Old English transition, apparent
  *cyniges heiweg* / *cyninges strete* correspondence, and unusual
  *communis via* are documentary concerns independent of lexical
  identification. The chronological anomaly is not independent evidence
  that *heiweg* means ‘highway’. Cooper's fourteen-charter corpus and the
  limits of the Sawyer electronic transcripts are in
  `COOPER_CHARTER_CORPUS.tsv`.
- Ritt (2000) is now held and consulted. His alternative analysis of
  Aldred's accentuation is recorded separately from Farman's evidence:
  the Matthew census is 426 accents (318 graphically closed, 108 open);
  his p. 508 table accounts for 328/376 non-ambiguous occurrences
  (87.3%), with 48 exceptions. His p. 509 CVC arithmetic is inconsistent,
  so the reported 98% predictive accuracy is not reproduced.
- The two direct ⟨hieg⟩ readings are verified in Eadwine Psalter PPs
  71.16 and 146.8 (Harsley 1889: 124, 241), Trinity College Cambridge
  MS R.17.1, fols. 125r and 258r, Romanum column. Neither has a visible
  accent or correction. The fol. 258r stint is attributed to OE3; fol.
  125r's hand is not securely identified. Faulkner (2017), using an
  author preprint, argues that the gloss largely derives from an older
  exemplar but also records selective modernization and correction by
  its twelfth-century scribes. These particular forms cannot be assigned
  to the exemplar or to contemporary Canterbury English. Their dialect
  and exemplar language remain unresolved; normalized *hīeġ* is not a
  manuscript reading.
- The bounded Lowe-catalogued charter search found no securely identified
  Kentish lexical *hēah* token. Cooper's separate fourteen-charter corpus
  includes *higi wegge* in S 508 (transaction 946; surviving CCCC 111,
  s. xii 2), *heige weye* in S 727 (transaction 964; surviving Lansdowne
  442, s. xiv ex.), and further *hig weg(e)* spellings in later copies.
  Their presence is relevant, but their lexical identity and the
  historical source of ⟨i⟩ are not independently established. The
  different compounds S 1338 *heahstræt*, S 1361 *hæhstræte*, and S 911
  *hehstræte* show heah-, hæh-, and heh- spellings, not an independently
  demonstrated development to ⟨hei-⟩. The bounded evidence therefore
  supplies no secure, independent derivation of S 1196 ⟨hei-⟩ from
  *hēah*. S 332 (transaction 863; Canterbury D.C.
  M 14, s. ix med.; Lowe App. II.17) has Latin witness-name *Heahmund*,
  not the English adjective. S 1203 (transaction 875; BL Cotton Augustus
  ii.89, s. ix 2; Lowe App. III.60) has *Heahulf* as a Latin witness-name;
  the manuscript's relation to the 890–923 confirmation is disputed.
  S 327 (?860, date altered to 790; Cotton Charter viii.29; Lowe App.
  II.15) has *Meodowæge*, not an independent ‘high’. S 331 (862; Cotton
  Charter viii.32; Lowe App. II.16) and S 344 (873; Stowe Charters 19;
  Lowe App. II.21) yield no diagnostic token. S 350 (transaction 898;
  Canterbury D.C. F 150, s. ix/x; Lowe App. II.23) has short-vowel
  *bradan weg*, but its language date is disputed. False positive S 403
  *heagam* is Sussex, transaction 930, with s. xiii/xiv copies. The same-scribe
  corpus has short-vowel S 1195 *dei*; the expanded
  search adds S 350 *bradan weg* as a further short-vowel control. No
  diagnostic long-vowel-plus-palatal-glide charter control was found.
  The targeted negative result is bounded to the inspected witnesses and
  spelling families, not a general claim about Kentish.

## Claims remaining genuinely disputed

- The lexical identity and historical stem shape of S 1196 *heiweg* and
  the first element of S 328 *Hegforde*; spelling and boundary context do
  not decide hay, high, or enclosure analyses.
- Whether Farman's printed acutes reflect long quantity in his witness;
  the calibration is too small and selected to quantify that inference.
  It cannot be projected to ninth-century Kentish.
- The direct-cell vowel quantity and syllabification, and whether a
  protected oblique stem was available before i-umlaut. The Paris Psalter
  datives are late; they do not establish the chronology needed by the
  proposed analogy.
- The historical inference among the surviving derivations, including
  whether analogy is necessary. The source observations alone do not
  decide this.
- Whether the Eadwine Psalter ⟨hieg⟩ readings are inherited, contemporary,
  or revised spellings, and what dialect or exemplar language they
  represent. Hogg's three mechanisms for ⟨ie⟩ must be assessed for ‘hay’
  before either reading constrains its historical derivation.

  It may clarify relevant Kentish place-name segmentation (Electronic

  meaning or phonology of S 1196 *heiweg*. Wallenberg 1934, *The
  Place-Names of Kent*, is a different book and not a substitute.

  on the existing ILL request. Its reprint in *Untersuchungen zur
  germanischen und deutschen Sprachgeschichte* (1971), pp. 65–73 (some
  catalogues list [1972]), is registered but not held. Either could
  establish Dal's original early-loss argument and how it addresses
  Dahl's objections.

  unheld; it may provide authoritative texts and commentary for S 1196
  and the scribe-7 comparisons.
- Ross and Stanley 1956, Book 2, is Jones's cited source; verify whether
  it adds manuscript detail beyond the editions already checked. Its

## Remaining limitations

The Farman census relies on Tamoto's searchable extraction and edition
readings; the target manuscript leaves cannot be checked online. Harsley's
Eadwine ⟨hieg⟩ readings are directly verified in the manuscript, but the
language of the exemplar and the dialect of these glosses remain
unassigned. Faulkner's preprint/published pagination correspondence is
not established. The British Library's available S 1196 preview was too
faint for direct reading; Lindisfarne target images could not be
retrieved reliably at usable resolution, and S 328 was not available as
an adequate image. Cooper's charter negatives are limited to electronic
transcripts, and the charter search was bounded, not a manuscript-wide
collation. Several transaction dates, copy dates, and textual layers
remain disputed. The private local reference library is not portable
automatically between worktrees. No historical interpretation is
adjudicated here.
