# Research memorandum: survival of the inherited direct singular

## Identity and question

Research-only comparison, `research/hay-article`, base `131bbe91`. No
production rule or registry verdict is in scope. The falsifiable claim is
that early Anglian ⟨heg⟩ can continue the inherited direct singular without
analogy. An independent transition from stressed /e/ plus unstressed /i/ to
/ej/, or a secure scribal parallel for ⟨eg⟩ representing /e.i/, would support
it. A securely bounded early contraction in the same word-final environment,
coupled with evidence that ⟨g⟩ there requires inherited or restored /j/, would
rule out the retained-hiatus route.

## Existing state, diagnosis and witness roles

The frozen comparison selects the direct singular and contrasts A `hei`
with B2 `hīeġ`; neither spelling is a phonetic transcription. The isolated
research assay starts from intermediate inputs and tests loss, contraction,
consonant restoration and a protected-stem output separately. These are
conditional derivations, not a production firing census or evidence for a
new chronology edge. The controls *glī*, *ēde* and *gecēd* are historical
witnesses to coalescence, not witnesses to the same contraction date;
*pyle* is a w-loss control without hiatus. No resyllabification rule is
introduced simply to obtain a match. No production trace or baseline is
regenerated.

## Sources and historical analysis

Ringe & Taylor (2014: 53, 173, 246) identify OHG *hewi* as the inherited
direct singular and *houwi* as remodelled; Gothic *hawi* is outside the
West Germanic ordering problem. Their English derivation replaces
\*hæwi with the protected \*hēaj before umlaut; the single arrow covers
West Saxon *hīeġ* and Mercian and Northumbrian *hēġ*. The passage does
not supply separate Anglian evidence for the analogical step or for
vowel quantity. Their oblique stem and English outcome are not the
regular early-apocope direct cell.

The post-umlaut, pre-general-syncope loss of noninitial \*w before fully
unstressed \*i remains supported (Ringe & Taylor 2014: 257–261; Campbell
1959: §406). On this account, \*hawi > \*hæwi > \*hewi > \*he.i.
Ringe & Taylor (2014: 318) explicitly leave the interval before
contraction open and allow /i.i/ to have contracted earlier than unlike
vowels. Their *gecēd* in Vespasian is relevant to chronology in the same
tradition (p. 260), but the conditioning is nonfinal; it does not date
contraction of /e.i/ at the word edge. Épinal and Corpus *streide* may
preserve earlier syllabification or transmit spelling; the disyllabic
scansion at *Beowulf* 2436 is evidence about a verse, not a calendar date
for Mercian contraction (Campbell §237.1.b; Hogg §5.133). Neither shows
that ⟨eg⟩ represented hiatus. In the checked controls, no independently
motivated /e.i/ > /ej/ was identified. The *dei/deg* and *weig* spellings
control the spelling of a glide, not its origin.

The manuscript record and candidate register are distinguished in
`MORPHOLOGICAL_CONCORDANCE.tsv`. Unmarked comparisons do not acquire
syntactic case from Latin alone. The Rushworth *gesittan on hóeg* admits
a positional reading, but the uninflected gloss does not rule out a
directional construal. Count it separately from secure datives.
The three Paris *hege* prove inflection, not a wholesale levelled paradigm.

## Local source checks and census

The dated set comprises 44 edition-, apparatus- or image-supported
records: 24 singular accusative, two singular nominative, one endingless
singular dative, three overt singular datives, 12 uncertain and two
isolated glosses. The Bede record is a composite editorial reading;
parallel manuscript copies and related glosses count separately as
tokens but are grouped by textual tradition. No secure genitive or
plural is identified. The earliest assigned accusative is ninth-century
Vespasian, the earliest nominatives are in the related late-tenth-century
John glosses, and the secure endingless Colloquy dative cannot be ordered
relative to the three Paris *hege* forms. The positional reading of
Farman would supply one earlier endingless instance.

The local DOE handoff supplies additional readings in the Wildhagen,
Brenner, Roeder, Kimmens, Rosier, Campbell, Harsley, Lindelöf, Sisam and
Liles editions. The article gives their parallel loci separately from the
dated case census. In particular, two editorially marked Vitellius
readings are excluded, and the corpus *hyg* at Lambeth Ps 101:5 is
kept outside the counted set pending reconciliation with the earlier
transcription *hig*. The ninth-century comparisons do not identify a
unique English case. Cockayne's prognostic and Thorpe's Institutes
provide accusatives, not nominatives; the former witness is twelfth
century and the latter's base witness has not been dated here.

Sweet (1885: 450 and glossary) and Bryan (1915: 8, 23, 25) take
*hei weg* with ‘hay’; Dahl adopts the interpretation but misdates the
charter to 839. Bosworth–Toller assign it to *hēah-weg* by comparison
with *hēah-strǣt*; Clark Hall's scanned p. 152 gives a tentative
‘highway?’. Cooper (2003: 61 nn. 151, 155) knew and set aside S 1196.
Possible co-reference with *cyninges strete* is not a lexical gloss.
The Kentish spelling of ‘day’ supplies a positive glide-spelling
control; a matched early local ‘high’ development remains unshown.
The best-supported reading is provisionally ‘hay-road’, not a secure
attestation of a simplex stem. Worcester F.174 gives the only secure
simplex exact ⟨hei⟩ in the checked record, in the thirteenth century;
its older parallel reads *hig*.

## Adversarial assessment

| Claim | Strongest case | What blocks the stronger conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early Mercian direct survival | Secure Vespasian ‘hay’; no independent quantity measurement; the /e.i/ terminus is open | Ordinary ⟨eg⟩ suggests /j/; neither onset of that glide by resyllabification nor a hiatus spelling convention is independently shown |
| Consonant-only restoration | Contraction explains the nucleus; *glī* and *glīg* coexist in the closest nominal control | The analysis of *glīg* as restoration is itself historical inference; /i.i/ and /e.i/ need not contract together; *hē* ‘hay’ is not attested |
| Uniform protected stem | Independently motivated protected \*auj; compatible with West Saxon high vowels and Anglian smoothing | Identical Anglian output after contraction and /j/ restoration; evidence for the donor's availability is comparative, not a dated English levelling event |
| S 1196 supplies an early ⟨hei⟩ | Sweet and Bryan's lexical association; independent landscape analogue in S 445; no matched Kentish ‘high’ reflex | Cooper's co-reference is plausible and the document is irregular; the compound neither settles its lexical identity nor selects the simplex direct stem |
| Early long quantity | Contracted and protected routes yield length; Farman favours it later | An accent is local evidence; neither quantity nor syllabification is measured in the earliest Mercian target |
| Obliques were too rare to supply a model | Many endingless uses, overt *-e* in one textual tradition | Biblical-text distribution is not a model of prehistoric frequency; productive class patterns and donor availability matter independently |

## Verdict, propagation and residue

**REFORMULATE the article's inference; DEFER attribution of Vespasian
⟨heg⟩ to an unremodelled direct stem.** Retain the early hiatus, the
positive evidence for eventual contraction elsewhere, and the
consonant-restoration alternative. No independently established regular
route yet connects hiatus directly to attested ⟨heg⟩, but absence of such
a demonstration is not evidence of whole-stem replacement. The earliest
Anglian spellings do not independently establish length. Under early
apocope and the consonantal reconstruction, protected vocalism remains
the best-supported source of the West Saxon forms; late apocope makes
that vocalism regular. The chronological advantage of early apocope
for inherited continental relics and of late apocope for *bedd* stand
separately from the English analysis of ‘hay’.

The decision applies only to the article, its concordance and supporting
research records. No production changes, new chronological edge,
fingerprint change, or production regression are claimed. Untested
comparative claims, inaccessible readings and independently pending
acquisitions remain explicitly qualified.
