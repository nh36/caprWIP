# Hay research unresolved evidence

## Current status after Cycle 11

- Early apocope yields hiatus; the date of word-final /e.i/ contraction
  is open. A regular /e.i/ → /ej/ and ⟨eg⟩ as a hiatus spelling lack
  independent evidence. Neither regular survival nor whole-stem
  replacement is established for Vespasian.
- Back-vowel desyllabification after *w*-loss is an independent structural
  parallel, and Vespasian supplies nonfinal /e.i/ contraction and
  reverse-order /i.e/ retention. Neither dates final /e.i/ or establishes
  an orthographic convention for it.
- Vowel length is not independently established in earliest Mercian.
  Farman's repeated acute favours length locally, not generally.
- Dated census: one secure endingless dative in a witness whose date
  overlaps the Paris Psalter; Farman may supply an earlier one.
  The updated inventory has 123 distinct simplex occurrences, 66
  verified; the source groups are not independent phonological witnesses.
- S 1196 probably merits considering ‘hay-road’ first, but the lexical
  identification remains provisional and neither it nor S 328 decides
  the history of a simplex form. The pending acquisitions remain out of
  scope.

## Historical status after cycle 9 (qualified above)

Cycle 8's central result rested on a source-critical error and is
corrected in `RESEARCH_MEMO_CYCLE9.md`. Loss of \*w before unstressed
\*i is an established regular change (R&T 2014: 257–61 §6.7.1; Campbell
§§237.1.b, 406; Hogg §5.133); the regular early-apocope outcome of the
direct cell is \*hē (via hiatus \*he.i and contraction), not
\*\*hewe. Consequently:

- Anglian hēġ may continue the inherited direct cell with only the
  analogical restoration of -ġ (precedent: glī → glīg); WS hīeġ requires
  the oblique vocalism (stem replacement/import) under early apocope.
  The necessity and scope of analogy are dialect-differentiated.
- Under late apocope both dialects are regular without analogy; Dahl's
  variant restores -ġ analogically by his own statement.
- Quantity: long in the attested period on all surviving derivations
  (the early-apocope route now by contraction); the pre-contraction
  hiatus stage (⟨ei⟩, streide-type) could persist into the earliest
  written period, so the earliest ⟨hei-⟩ items remain hedged.
- The mene/hay minimal-pair symmetry and the bedd-equivalent weighting
  of 'hay' are withdrawn.

Still unresolved: the apocope chronology itself; the status of OHG hewi
(inherited vs remodelled); the dialect/exemplar of the Eadwine ⟨hieg⟩
readings; the lexical identity of S 1196 heiweg and S 328 Hegforde; the
inheritance of the mene-type relics.

## Current status after cycle 8 (superseded by cycle 9 above)

Cycle 8 resolved the central open question of cycle 7. The regular
development of the inherited direct cell under early nasal apocope is now
established from first principles (DERIVATIONS.md, cycle-8 section;
experiments/): PWGmc \*hawi (= attested OHG *hewi*) regularly yields
disyllabic \*\*hewe with retained w, secured by the attested minimal-pair
controls *eowu* 'ewe' (R&T 5.1.2, 6.6.2, 6.9.4) and *cleweþa* 'itch'
(R&T 5.1.2), and by the light-syllable -i > -e class (*mete*, *mene*).
Consequences now current:

- The frozen Model A step `OEWLossBeforeI` is refuted as history: no
  w-loss before \*i is documented anywhere, and *eowu*/*cleweþa*
  positively contradict it. The A0/A1 chains to \*hei/\*hē are
  historically indefensible; the hiatus-resolution and resyllabification
  routes are moot because their input never arises.
- Under early apocope, whole-stem levelling from the oblique cells is
  **required** in every dialect; under late apocope the attested paradigm
  is regular without analogy (B2), or with analogical -ġ only (Dahl's
  ungeminated late variant). 'Hay' therefore patterns with *nett*,
  *bedd*, *cynn* as an analogical liability of the early chronology.
- The cycle-6/7 instruction not to treat "every viable derivation
  predicts a long direct vowel" as current is itself superseded: the
  only regular short-vowel candidate, \*\*hewe, is excluded by every
  attested spelling (all monosyllabic, w-less), so every derivation
  compatible with the record (B2, Dahl, A2 levelling) yields a long
  umlauted nucleus. This is an argued conclusion, not the retired
  cycle-4 assertion.
- The cycle-7 statement that the applicability of the West Saxon ⟨ie⟩
  developments to 'hay' "remains unsettled" is narrowed: of Hogg's three
  sources of WS ⟨ie⟩, only i-umlaut of a diphthong is reachable from the
  'hay' etymon; palatalization of /eː/ requires a preceding palatal
  onset that 'hay' never had, and i+e hiatus requires a stem-final /iː/
  that no viable derivation supplies before the oblique stem is already
  in place. WS *hig/hyg* presuppose īe (or ī) by the regular LWS
  developments and cannot continue \*\*hewe or \*hei.

Still genuinely unresolved after cycle 8:

- The apocope chronology itself. 'Hay' now weighs on the late side with
  the other light neuter ja-stems, but early apocope plus levelling
  remains available, and the *mene*-relic argument for early apocope
  stands. The word constrains but does not decide the debate.
- The dialect, exemplar language and transmission layer of the two
  Eadwine ⟨hieg⟩ readings.
- The lexical identity of S 1196 *heiweg* (possible, not probable
  witness) and of S 328 *Hegforde* first element (unresolved).
- Direct quantity evidence: the Farman acute remains probabilistic and
  the calibration limits stand; the quantity conclusion above rests on
  derivational exhaustion, not on a direct measurement.

## Current status after cycle 7 (superseded by cycle 8 above)

The final bounded verification is complete. Eadwine Psalter ⟨hieg⟩ at
PPs 71.16 and 146.8 is directly confirmed on fols. 125r and 258r,
Romanum column; neither has a visible accent or correction. Harsley's
first citation is p. 124, not p. 72. Faulkner's account of an older
exemplar and selective twelfth-century modernization does not determine
whether either spelling was inherited, contemporary, or locally revised;
the dialect and exemplar language remain unresolved.

The Farman calibration now contains long-V+glide *stīg* (1/2 tokens) and
*stīgan* (0/12), for 1/14 accented tokens, 1/10 surface types, and 1/2
related lexical types. There is no long-V+glide monosyllabic word-form
control. The only closed stressed-root token is prefixed disyllabic
*astig* (0/1); see `FARMAN_ACCENT_CALIBRATION.tsv`.

The cycle-4 categorical exclusion of Model A on West Saxon vocalism is
superseded, not adjudicated: the direct ⟨hieg⟩ readings are verified but
not dialect-assigned, and the applicability of the several West Saxon
⟨ie⟩ developments to ‘hay’ remains unsettled. The regular development
and quantity of the inherited direct form under Model A remain open.
Do not treat the earlier claim that every viable derivation predicts a
long direct vowel as current.

Only Dal (1934), Wallenberg (1931: 203–205), and Brooks and Kelly (2013)
remain acquisition items being handled separately by the project owner.
Other acquisition notes in the historical cycle records below are not
new acquisition requests. The cycle records are retained as history;

## Outstanding-evidence register (cycle 3, 24 Nov 2026)

Questions not settled by cycle 3; current status is noted below:

1. **Farman's acutes in the manuscript.** Resolvable only by direct
   inspection (or new digitization) of Bodleian Auct. D. 2. 19, fols.
   containing Mt 6:30 and Mt 14:19; the online selection excludes them.
2. **Aldred's accent practice as a control.** Ritt 2000 is now acquired
   and consulted; see `LINDISFARNE_EVIDENCE.md`.
3. **Dal's original 1934 early-loss argument.** The original article
   remains on the existing ILL request; the 1971 reprint, pp. 65–73, is
   registered but unheld. The argument is presently known through Dahl's
   and Hogg–Fulk's reports.
4. **The pre-umlaut dating of the levelled oblique stem.** No attestation
   can reach it: the earliest overt oblique (PPs *hege*, s. xi) postdates
   i-umlaut by centuries. Only comparative evidence (the status of OHG
   *hewi* as inheritance or remodelling) bears on it.
5. **Direct-case quantity.** Farman's two printed *hóeg* acutes have
   bounded, selected calibration and suggest his own practice only; they
   do not establish ninth-century quantity. The metre is demonstrated
   unusable (Fulk 1992: 410–414) and ME reflexes are neutralized
   (Minkova 2014: 178).
6. **Ru2's exact exemplar.** Kotake 2016 leaves direct copying from Li
   unproven; a full collation of Owun's non-R–Y readings against the
   Aldredian manuscripts would be needed, and does not affect the hay
   evidence materially.

These are substantive research questions, not metadata or ingestion failures.
