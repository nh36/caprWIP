# What the Anglian vowel requires

This is the current extension of the [independent argument model](ARGUMENT_CYCLE12.md).
E01–E20, C01–C09 and H1–H5 retain their identities and source loci.
Only the dependency and strength changes below supersede the earlier
assessment. The [comparative source audit](RESEARCH_MEMO_CYCLE13.md)
records the original page references.

## Components and competing histories

- **C05:** E06–10 give the written correspondence, not an exclusive
  phonological prediction. *Dei* independently calibrates /Vj/ only.
- **C07:** E15–16 and E24 distinguish unmarked quantity from shortness
  and regional vowel quality from historical source. Farman shifts the
  local comparison toward long-vowel histories; West Saxon high vowels
  are counterevidence to applying H1 or H2 unchanged there.
- **C08:** E17–19 and E21–23 add a positive consideration for retention
  or generalization of the direct nucleus alongside class pressure for
  the protected stem. A reconstructed donor is not a directional
  argument; a mass noun does not guarantee outward levelling.
- **C10, principal result:** C01 + C06 + C07 permit an inherited
  Anglian mid vowel via **H1 or H2**, not both. H1 (A) needs E25 and
  predicts short /ej/ in the proposed direct continuation. H2 (B)
  needs final contraction (E14) **and** an accessible source and
  transfer of /j/ (C08, E19); it predicts long /eːj/. H3 (C),
  C03 + C08, replaces both components before umlaut on Ringe and
  Taylor's account. It explains the West Saxon contrast and the long
  Anglian forms, but the early Anglian record supplies no positive
  requirement for importing its vowel. H4 supplies the protected-type
  nucleus and glide regularly under a different order. H5 may combine
  regional histories without requiring different dates of apocope.
- **C11, stronger hypothesis:** actual analogy-free survival requires
  H1 and evidence discriminating it from H2–H4, such as demonstrably
  short quantity or retained hiatus in a secure direct form with an
  independently different protected alternant. It is not established.
- **C09, chronological consequence:** C10 and E20 suffice to distinguish
  the explanatory burden of ‘hay’ from that of ‘bed’. C05 corroborates
  the relevance of the early prediction; C08 bears on rival directions
  and the interpretation of donors. Neither is logically necessary
  to C10, nor is C11. No chronology is established by this comparison.

## Sensitivity and adversarial assessment before revision

| Change in a premise | What is lost | What survives |
|---|---|---|
| Reject final resyllabification | H1 via /ej/ and the corresponding case for C11. There is no independent ⟨g⟩ hiatus route. | H2 still keeps the nucleus if final contraction and restoration remain defensible. |
| Reject ‘hay-road’ | C05 as an early written match for this lexeme. | The original edition reading, C01, secure Anglian simplex forms, C07–08 and the H1/H2 comparison. |
| Establish length in Farman | Bare short /ej/ no longer accounts for Farman. | H2–H4; earlier Mercian quantity remains open without a demonstrated continuity of pronunciation. |
| Reject the closeness of Ball and Stiles's analogy | E22 cannot motivate a directional preference for ‘hay’. | E17–19, the competing donor possibilities, and C10 on phonological grounds. |
| Accept protected vocalism in West Saxon | A uniform unmodified inherited mid vowel is untenable. | H5 and C10; neither West Saxon high vocalism nor the late unassigned ⟨hieg⟩ implies transfer of the Anglian vowel. |
| Exclude both motivated inherited-nucleus paths | Retract the positive alternative underlying C10 and qualify C09. | Distinct regular predictions, the written correspondence, and the limits of quantity and morphology remain. |

An exact match of letters is not a matched phonological environment.
Lack of a macron or acute is not evidence of shortness. Class levelling
and a protected donor are genuine support for H3, and West Saxon
vocalism is a positive reason for it; the unobserved event is not the
ground for rejecting H3. Conversely, H2 needs both a regular process
whose target environment remains uncertain and a plausible source of
the glide; ‘glee’ does not independently prove its application.
The defensible conclusion is nonnecessity of importing Anglian
vocalism, not historical preference for a uniquely recovered form.
