# Hay literature matrix (artifact A)

Comparative reconstruction of the published accounts of OE *hīeġ* / *hēġ* /
*heg* / *heig* / *hig* 'hay' < PGmc \*hawją (neuter ja-stem). All page
references were checked against the held originals or their verified text
extracts; the consultation trail is in `PASSAGES.tsv`,
`docs/references/hay-source-critical-reading-2026-11-22.md`, and
`docs/references/sc047_early_apocope/nominal-levelling.md`. Citation keys
are from `docs/refs.bib`, supplemented by `article_refs.bib` for
research-scoped items (Dahl1938, Hogg1979Analogy, Adamczyk2018, Dal1934).

Chronology labels: **early apocope** = loss of both final short oral \*-a(z)
and nasal \*-ą before WGmc j-gemination; **split/late** = oral \*-az lost
before gemination, nasal \*-ą lost after it; **no-gemination (hay only)** =
\*-awj- never geminated in English regardless of apocope chronology.

## 1. Ringe & Taylor 2014 [@RingeTaylor2014, pp. 53, 173, 246]

- **Chronology**: early apocope. Both \*-az and \*-ą lost before gemination
  (pp. 44–47); gemination of \*wj was "reversible": PNWGmc \*awj >
  PWGmc \*[aw'w'] > pre-OE \*[auj], "in principle the same as Campbell
  1962:46" (p. 53).
- **Direct sg.**: inherited ungeminated \*hawi, explicitly "the inherited
  nom.-acc. sg.", parallel OHG *hewi* (p. 53). Derivation pp. 173, 246:
  PGmc \*hawją > PWGmc \*hawi (direct), \*[haww'-] (protected) > \*hawi,
  \*hauj- > \*hǣwi, \*hēaj- >— WS *hīeġ*, Angl *hēġ*.
- **Analogy**: the sign ">—" marks analogical replacement at the \*hǣwi
  stage. They never state what \*hǣwi would have become regularly. The
  analogy explains the whole attested stem shape (vocalism īe/ē **and**
  final -ġ), not just one feature.
- **Evidence**: OHG *hewi* (inherited direct sg.) beside *houwi*
  (gemination levelled in from the obliques); OE *mete* : *mettas*;
  parallels \*gliwi > *glīg*, *ēg*/*īeg* 'island', *strēgan*, *cīegan*.
- **Difficulty**: the regular fate of \*hǣwi is left unstated; the
  replacement has no demonstrated pre-i-umlaut donor stem. The late Paris
  Psalter supplies dative *hege* ×3, but cannot date that stem to the
  proposed analogical period (see CONCORDANCE.tsv and RESEARCH_MEMO_CYCLE3.md).

## 2. Campbell 1959 [@Campbell1959, §§120.2, 579(4)]

- **Chronology**: both endings lost before gemination (§576), i.e. early
  apocope, following Dal 1934 as reported by Hogg–Fulk. Dal's 1934 article
  has a 1971 reprint, pp. 65–73, but neither version is held or directly
  consulted here.
- **Direct sg.**: §579(4): "\*hewi, \*gliwi without gemination would be the
  undisturbed developments"; the attested nom.-acc. sg. of both types is
  "under influence of the other cases"; *glīg* is a "re-formation (§411)".
  §120.2: WGmc \*hauuj > OE *hēg*, *hīg*; "generally the u of auuj is lost,
  final result ēg or īeg".
- **Analogy**: required for the attested direct form; same scope as
  Ringe–Taylor (whole stem).
- **Difficulty**: §120.2 (geminate-route outcome) and §579(4) (ungeminated
  \*hewi as the undisturbed development) describe the protected and the
  inherited direct stems respectively; the long vowel of his "final result
  ēg or īeg" is a product of the geminate route, not an independent datum.

## 3. Wright 1914 [@Wright1914, §§270 + Note, 357]

- **Mechanism**: j lost intervocalically after long vowel/diphthong; later
  a consonantal glide (written g) developed in hiatus. §270 Note: "Forms
  like nom. hieg ... had the final g from the inflected forms, as gen.
  hieges, dat. hiege."
- **Analogy**: narrower than Ringe–Taylor/Campbell: only the final **-g**
  is analogical; the vocalism is taken as regular. His *hīeges*, *hīege*
  are **paradigm illustrations, not manuscript citations**. The late Paris
  Psalter dative *hege* ×3 is manuscript-attested, but does not demonstrate
  a pre-i-umlaut donor stem (see CONCORDANCE.tsv).
- **Difficulty**: presupposes the long-diphthong stem in the direct cell
  before j-loss, i.e. implicitly the geminate-route vocalism.

## 4. Hogg, Grammar vol. 1 [@HoggGrammar1992, §§2.76, 3.18, 5.129–134]

- **Direct derivation** §3.18: \*hawwj- > \*hauwj- > \*hēawj- > \*hīewj- >
  WS *hīġ*, nWS *hēġ*; j lost after high diphthong, w after low; the loss
  cannot precede i-umlaut; "the phonology of the change is obscure". Starts
  from the **geminated** stem (late-apocope assumption, cf. Hogg–Fulk
  §2.23).
- **Orthography** §2.76: /j/ spelled ⟨g⟩; ⟨i⟩ mainly initial/early/before
  u/loans; ⟨ig⟩ is not a general spelling of /j/.
- **Hiatus** §§5.129–134: a stressed vowel before a hiatus vowel "would be
  already long, since it occurred finally in a syllable"; long V + front V
  resolves by **loss of the unstressed vowel** (not diphthongization, which
  is confined to back second vowels); early Mercian glossaries still show
  unresolved hiatus (CorpGl *strēidæ* etc.).
- **Relevance**: supplies the only independently motivated lengthening that
  could touch a hiatus sequence /e.i/, but its resolution deletes the /i/
  rather than consonantizing it (see DERIVATIONS.md, A1).

## 5. Hogg & Fulk 2011 vol. 2 [@HoggFulk2011, §§2.20–2.24, p. 20 §2.23]

- **Chronology**: split/late. §2.23: "nom.sg. \*-az was lost before ...
  gemination, giving \*sagjaz > \*sagi but ... acc.sg. \*-ã remained until
  after gemination". "It is noteworthy ... that **\*sege**, the expected
  reflex of \*sagi, never occurs in OE ... We must therefore assume, with
  Dahl (1938: 81–6), that only \*-az was lost prior to gemination and that
  acc.sg. *secg*, neut.sg. cynn are regular forms", the masc. nom. sg.
  re-formed by nom./acc. syncretism; "Alternatively ... following Campbell
  (1977: §576), see also Dal (1934), that both inflexions were lost prior
  to gemination, in which event all geminated forms in the nom.acc.sg.
  would be re-formations from the oblique forms or the plural."
- **Direct sg. of 'hay'**: under their account the neuter direct sg. kept
  nasal \*-ą through gemination, so the geminate stem (and hence the īe/ē
  vocalism and final -ġ) is **regular** in the direct cell; no analogy
  needed for this word (= CAPR Model B2).
- **Evidence**: total absence of *sege*-type masculines; *cynn*, *nett*,
  *bedd* as regular neuters.
- **Difficulty**: OHG *hewi*, Goth. *hawi* (inherited ungeminated direct
  forms elsewhere in Germanic) must be regional or remodelled; comparative
  ungeminated relics (Stiles's *mene*) likewise.

## 6. Dahl 1938 [@Dahl1938, pp. 81–86 and the hay section]

- **Chronology**: late nasal apocope — gemination precedes \*-ą loss
  (*cynn* < \*kunją regular; Weser runic *kunni*). Dahl 1938 reports Dal
  as defending an apocope-before-gemination account resting mainly on OHG
  *hewi*/*gewi* [@Dahl1938, pp. 82–84]. The Dal original is unavailable and
  is not directly consulted here.
- **Hay specifically — a third analysis**: **no gemination at all** in
  English \*-awj-: "no recorded w-forms of OE nouns containing PrG -aui-";
  diphthong differentiation of \*aw was already under way when \*-j became
  final; final -i lost after long vowels/diphthongs. Derivation: nom.-acc.
  sg. \*hauja̯ > \*hǣoi(ə) > \*hǣoi > \*hīe, \*hē → *hī(e)g*, *heg*, with
  **-g reintroduced from the inflected forms** (gen. \*haujas > *hī(e)ges*).
  He "strongly doubts" \*hewi ever existed in English despite Goth. *hawi*,
  OHG *hewi*.
- **Corpus** (his early-OE material; extends the CAPR ledger): VP *heg*
  36/2, 71/16, 91/8, 101/5, 101/12, 102/15, 103/14 (acc.), 104/35, 105/20,
  128/6, 146/8; VH *heg* (acc.) 7/4; Kentish charters *Hegforde* (A.D. 858,
  Ct 28/14) and *heiweg* (tentatively ‘hay-road’; interpretation disputed;
  Dahl prints "A.D. 839", a slip for
  859 — he dates the same charter, S 1196 = Ct 43, to 859 when citing
  *Scufelingforde*; cycle 4) — the earliest
  ⟨hei⟩ spelling. "Inflected forms are thus not to be found in my
  material." Late OE prevailing spelling *hig*; *hieg* "very rare". Also
  Ep/Erf/Cp ivy glosses (*ibaei*, *ifeg*, *ifegn*) referred to \*hauja- as
  second element.
- **Analogy**: even his non-analogical vocalism needs the final -g from the
  inflected forms — i.e. **every** published account imports the -g of the
  direct singular from the paradigm except the late-apocope geminate route.

## 7. Hogg 1979 [@Hogg1979Analogy, §5, pp. 68–73]

- Endorses Dahl's late apocope ("Dahl's position seems to be correct").
- *hyll* vs. *hyse*/*mete*/*ile*: both continue a pre-analogical paradigm
  nom. \*hyle / acc. *hyll*; syncretism pressure levelled *hyll*-type nouns
  toward the geminate and *hyse*-type nouns toward the plain nominative:
  "the balance ... is a very slender one", direction near-equipollent.
- **Relevance**: direct OE evidence that mixed geminate/non-geminate
  paradigms existed and were levelled in **both** directions — so an
  early-apocope \*hīeġ-by-levelling account is typologically unremarkable,
  but so would be survival of an ungeminated direct form.

## 8. Jones 1989 [@Jones1989, pp. 21, 55, 149–152]

- p. 21 (§2.2.2): Lindisfarne ⟨gefraign-⟩, ⟨gefreign-⟩, ⟨heig⟩, ⟨ðeign⟩
  beside ⟨gefregn⟩, ⟨heg⟩, ⟨ðegn⟩ — [j] "remain[s] intact and produce[s]
  diphthongization", possibly early ME-type Breaking.
- p. 55, Table 2.29 (image-verified): classes ⟨heg⟩ (glossed 'meadow') in
  the **short** [ɛ]/[ɛi] set; ⟨weg⟩ → ⟨wég⟩/⟨we⟩ with the Lindisfarne acute
  read as "increased stressed vowel duration".
- pp. 149–150 (§3.4.2 opening + Table 3.17, image-verified): transcribes
  the LOE alternants with **short** e — [hej]/[heij] 'hay' — but prints
  Table 3.17 WS forms **long**: ⟨hēg⟩ [heej̆], ⟨hīg⟩ [hiij̆]; ⟨clæg⟩ [klæj̆]
  short (against conventional *clǣg*); ME *heigh/heiȝe/heyȝ* → *hai/hei/ai*.
- p. 152 (sheet 166, image-verified 2026-11-23): trigger effacement [hej] →
  [heij] → [hei]; ⟨heigh⟩/⟨hai⟩, ⟨cleiȝ⟩/⟨clei⟩ alternants. (The former
  PASSAGES.tsv locator "p. 162" was wrong: printed p. 162 = sheet 176 is
  pre-nasal ⟨frensh⟩/⟨henge⟩ material.)
- **Relevance**: Jones's own quantity notation for 'hay' is internally
  mixed (short [hej] in the Breaking discussion, long [heej̆] in Table
  3.17); his gloss 'meadow' in Table 2.29 may conflate the 'hedge/enclosure'
  word. His diphthongization reading of ⟨heig⟩ is spelling-driven and does
  not presuppose either apocope chronology.

## 9. Minkova 2014 [@Minkova2014, pp. 64, 178–179]

- p. 178 (image-verified at 400 dpi): "the etymological length in [ej] as
  in OE *hēġ* 'hay', also spelled ⟨heig⟩, and (presumably) [ej] as in OE
  *weġ* 'way' < Gmc \*weg-, should not have been preserved as /eːj/ in hay
  and /ej/ in way" — an argument **against** contrastive diphthongal
  length, from the ME merger of 'hay' and 'way'. She assumes the
  conventional long ē of *hēġ*; she does not re-derive it.
- p. 64: *hege* 'enclosure, fence' > ME "hay (now dial.)" — the HEDGE word;
  homograph control for the concordance.
- **Relevance**: shows that ME and ModE reflexes cannot recover the OE
  quantity contrast before /j/; 'hay' and 'way' merge either way.

## 10. Boutkan 1995 [@Boutkan1995, pp. 209–213, 435–436]

- Gemination before low apocope (late order), against Hogg–Fulk's
  oral-first split; northern vs. southern treatment of final j; apparent
  ungeminated relics attributed to later i-stem or ending remodelling;
  finds early-loss-plus-levelling unnecessary. Arguments, not dated
  inscriptions.

## 11. Stiles 1995/2013, Versloot 2019 [@Stiles1995; @Stiles2013; @Versloot2019]

- Stiles 2013, pp. 15–16: early order supported by comparative ungeminated
  relics (OE *mene*, OS *(hals-)meni* vs. OHG *menni*).
- Stiles 1995 (original chapter, pp. 177–220; OCR
  `docs/references/local/stiles/stiles_1995_pp177-220.txt`): no direct hay
  material; relevant background only — Anglo-Frisian i-umlaut operates even
  with "lost \*-i" (Ingvaeonic), unlike OS/OHG retained-i umlaut; OE
  breaking precedes palatalization. The 2018 revision is a distinct
  manifestation [@Stiles1995Revised2018].
- Versloot 2019, pp. 78–92: late Frisian reflex of low endings; written
  *-u* possibly a reduced vowel; qualifies any uniform cutoff.

## 12. Adamczyk 2018 [@Adamczyk2018, ch. 5, i-stem discussion]

- *mettas*, *hyssas*, *illas* geminates in light masc. i-stems arise from
  ja-stem interaction; alternation *metas* vs. *mettas*, *mete* vs. *mette*
  diagnostic of restructuring direction. Background support for
  bidirectional levelling (cf. Hogg 1979).

## 13. Goering 2025 [@Goering2025, p. 301 + n. 4]

- Rejects universal compensatory lengthening from h-loss; *īfeġ* 'ivy'
  possibly < \*iβ-hēg (Hogg 2011: 170–1) but better an original simplex
  with \*ei-/\*i- ablaut. Peripheral: constrains one proposed 'hay'
  compound etymology and one lengthening mechanism.

## Why each account invokes analogy — comparative summary

| Account | Chronology | Direct sg. regular outcome | What analogy must supply | Donor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ringe–Taylor | early | \*hǣwi (fate unstated) | entire stem (vocalism īe/ē + -ġ) | protected \*hēaj- |
| Campbell | early | \*hewi "undisturbed" | entire stem | other cases |
| Wright | (geminate vocalism assumed) | hīe- regular, -g not | final -g only | gen./dat. *hīeges*, *hīege* (paradigm forms, unattested) |
| Dahl | late + no gemination in -awj- | \*hīe, \*hē | final -g only | inflected \*hī(e)ges |
| Hogg–Fulk / Hogg 1979 | split (nasal late) | *hīeġ* regular | none (for this word) | — |
| Boutkan | late | geminate regular | none | — |

The disagreement is therefore not one problem but two: (i) the **vocalism**
of the direct cell (early apocope predicts e-vocalism via \*hæwi/\*hewi;
late/geminate and Dahl's no-gemination routes predict īe/ē); (ii) the
**final -ġ**, which every account except the geminate route borrows from
inflected forms. The dative *hege* is attested in the late Paris Psalter,
but no source establishes that form as a pre-i-umlaut donor.
