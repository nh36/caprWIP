commit 113101ac5f169b589e1a9647cdf24d32cdd4d919. Companion artifacts:

## Findings supporting the starting thesis (early apocope, no analogy)

1. The alleged analogical donor (gen./dat. *hīeġes/hīeġe*) is unattested
   in OE — the analogy rests on reconstructed forms (PARADIGM.md).
2. No independent evidence identified in the sources examined establishes the conventional long vowel of the
   direct singular; editorial acutes and ME/ModE reflexes are
   non-probative (QUANTITY_EVIDENCE.md; Minkova 2014: 178).
3. The Anglian/Kentish record (⟨heg⟩ VP ×11, VH, Bede; ⟨hei-⟩ 839) and
   the c. 1150 ⟨hei⟩ are spelling-compatible with the early-apocope
   output /hei ~ hej/.
4. OHG *hewi*, Goth. *hawi* prove the ungeminated direct cell existed in
   Germanic; Stiles's *mene*-type relics support early loss comparatively.

## Findings against the starting thesis

1. **The WS vocalism is fatal to a fully regular early-apocope account.**
   ⟨hieg/hig/hyg⟩ require īe < \*ēa + i-umlaut; the early-apocope direct
   stem (\*hæwi > \*hewi) contains no \*ēa at any stage, and no attested
   change manufactures one. (DERIVATIONS.md A0/A1.)
2. No independently motivated regular chain repairs this. The only
   genuine candidate, Hogg's hiatus resolution, yields *hē* by deleting
   the final vowel — it supplies length but destroys the final segment,
   cannot give WS īe, and still needs the paradigm for -ġ. The *glīg*
   parallel is itself called a re-formation by Campbell. **Negative
   result, firmly established.**
3. Even the most analogy-averse published account (Dahl) imports the
   final -g from inflected forms. No published or constructible account
   derives the attested ⟨-g⟩ forms with zero paradigm influence except
   the geminate (B2) route.
4. The parallel diagnostics all run the other way: *nett*, *bedd*,
   *cynn* direct cells are regular only under B2 (frozen captures;
   dossier disposition).
5. The Anglian/Kentish forms that are compatible with early apocope are
   equally compatible with smoothed geminate-route *hēġ* — they have no
   discriminating power.

## Answers to the required final questions

**1. What did OE manuscripts actually write, and which cells are
attested?** ⟨heg⟩ (Mercian VP ×11, VH, Bede, charter 858), ⟨hei-⟩
(Kentish 839, compound), ⟨hig/hyg⟩ (late WS psalters, gospels, Colloquy,
glossaries), ⟨heig⟩ (Dialogues C, Royal Mk 6.39), ⟨haig⟩ (Hatton 38),
⟨hei⟩ (c. 1150 glossary). Only direct (nom./acc.) cells and endingless
dative syntax (*mid hig*) are attested. **No overtly inflected token was
identified in the sources examined.**

**2. Strongest independent evidence for a long vowel? Decisive?** There
is none that is independent. The length of *hīeġ/hēġ* is theory-internal
(regular output of the geminate stem); editorial acutes are not
manuscript evidence; the ME merger of 'hay' and 'way' (Minkova) shows
later reflexes cannot recover the quantity. Not decisive — genuinely open.

**3. Can the early-apocope direct form develop regularly into an attested
OE form without analogy?** Into **WS *hīeġ***: no. Into the **Anglian/
Kentish ⟨heg⟩/⟨hei-⟩ spellings**: possibly — /hei ~ hej/ is
spelling-compatible with them — but those spellings are equally the
regular smoothed reflex of geminate-route *hēġ*, so compatibility is not
confirmation.

**4. If so, what changes are required and are they chronologically
possible?** For the compatibility reading: only reinterpretation of final
/i/ as /j/ (trivial, undatable). Hogg's hiatus resolution (post-umlaut,
8th-c. completion) is independently motivated and chronologically
possible but yields *hē*, not *heg*, without paradigm -g.

**5. If not, where does the regular derivation fail?** At the vocalism:
nothing turns \*he(w)i into WS īe. Secondarily at the final consonant:
the written -ġ of all normal OE spellings is supplied by the paradigm in
every non-geminate account including Dahl's.

**6. What supports the analogical extension, and how secure is the
donor?** Typology: Hogg 1979's hyll/hyse levelling in both directions;
comparatively, OHG *houwi* (geminate levelled into the direct cell)
beside *hewi* is a direct parallel for exactly this transfer. The donor's
phonological shape (\*hēaj- > hīeġ-) is secure as a reconstruction — both
models derive *hīeġes/hīeġe* identically — but it is attested in no OE
document. Analogy is **possible and typologically supported, but not
positively evidenced** for this word; under B2 it is unnecessary.

**7. Comparison with the later-apocope account?** B2 derives every
attested WS and Anglian form regularly, in all cells, with no analogy,
and its parallel predictions (*nett*, *bedd*, *cynn*) are the attested
forms. For this word B2 is strictly simpler. A2 (early apocope +
levelling) is empirically indistinguishable from B2 in OE; only the
continental ungeminated forms keep it alive.

**8. Does the evidence bear on the general chronology or only on 'hay'?**
Only weakly on the general question, and in B2's favor via the parallel
neuters. 'Hay' itself is a poor diagnostic: Dahl's no-gemination analysis
of \*-awj- (no w-forms recorded in OE) would detach it from the
gemination question entirely, and the A2/B2 convergence erases the OE
difference. The general chronology must be decided on *nett/bedd/cynn*,
the *secg*/*sege* gap, and the comparative relics — as the SC047 dossier
already concluded ("the evidence does not settle a general OE oral/nasal
sequence").

**9. What is genuinely original here?** (i) The source-critical
concordance and the demonstration that **no overtly inflected OE token was
identified in the sources examined** —
the donor of every analogical account is unattested; (ii) the
demonstration that the long vowel of the direct singular has no
independent empirical support (the quantity is a theorem of the geminate
derivation, not a datum); (iii) the explicit negative result that no
independently motivated regular chain carries the early-apocope output to
WS *hīeġ*; (iv) the observation that the early-apocope output is
spelling-compatible with precisely the early Anglian/Kentish record; (v)
assembling Dahl's third route, which removes 'hay' from the gemination
debate altogether. Jones's and Minkova's observations are theirs, not
ours, and are so credited.

**10. Retain, narrow, or change the thesis?** **Narrow it.** The original
thesis — that the early-apocope direct form developed regularly into an
attested OE form, making Ringe–Taylor's analogy unnecessary — fails for
West Saxon and is undecidable for Anglian/Kentish. The defensible article
is: (a) the attested record is direct-case-only; (b) the reconstructed
long vowel is evidentially unsupported though derivationally motivated;
(c) WS *hīeġ* requires the geminate-shaped stem, regularly under late
nasal apocope or by levelling under early apocope — the word cannot
discriminate the chronologies; (d) the analogical machinery of the
handbooks operates between two unattested forms. The article should not
claim that Ringe–Taylor's chronology is vindicated by 'hay', and should
report the negative A1 result explicitly.
