## Key paradigm findings

1. **No inflected OE token of 'hay' is attested anywhere in the collated
   material.** Wright's *hīeges*, *hīege* are paradigm illustrations. The
   proposed analogical donor of the direct singular's shape is therefore
   itself a reconstruction, never an observed OE form.
2. Every attested token is direct (nom./acc.) or syntactically dative
   without an ending, or compound-internal.
3. The WS/late-WS spellings ⟨hig⟩, ⟨hieg⟩, ⟨hyg⟩ show the i/ie vocalism
   that only the geminate-route stem (\*ēa + i-umlaut > īe) produces
   regularly; the Anglian ⟨heg⟩ and Kentish ⟨hei-⟩ are compatible both with
   smoothed/Anglian ēġ from the geminate stem and, in principle, with an
   ungeminated \*hewi > \*hei reflex.
4. Parallel copies (Dialogues C/O/H; the WS gospel MSS) are single textual
   traditions, not independent lexical witnesses.
5. Excluded homographs: plural pronoun *hig*; *hege* 'hedge, enclosure'
   (Minkova 2014: 64 lists its ME continuation "hay (now dial.)");
   *gærs*/*gers* translations; Rushworth *heggetgrese* is a compound, not a
   bare token.
