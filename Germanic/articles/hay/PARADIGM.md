## Key paradigm findings

1. **Cycle-3 revision: overtly inflected tokens of 'hay' exist.** The
   Paris Psalter supplies dative singular *hege* three times (101.4,
   101.9, 102.14), each secured lexically (facing Latin *faenum*, mowing
   and withering context), grammatically (complement of *gelīc*/*anlīce*
   with resumptive *þam þe* and agreeing *māwenum*), and at manuscript
   level (BnF lat. 8824, Gallica images). Dahl's "Inflected forms are
   thus not to be found in my material" remains true of his early-OE
   corpus but not of Old English as a whole. The dative's shape — stem
   *heg-* + *-e*, the exact outcome the protected oblique stem predicts —
   is however attested only in one late, single-tradition poetic text:
   it confirms the levelled paradigm's existence, not the pre-umlaut
   dating any early-apocope account requires for the levelling.
   Wright's *hīeges* remains a paradigm illustration; the genitive is
   still unattested.
2. Outside the Paris Psalter datives, every attested token is direct
   (nom./acc.) or syntactically dative without an ending (*mid hig*), or
   compound-internal. Verse now supplies direct-case tokens as well
   (PPs 71.16, 105.17; Andreas 38); PPs 146.9 *hig* is Krapp's
   emendation of manuscript ⟨hio⟩ and is not a token.
3. The WS/late-WS spellings ⟨hig⟩, ⟨hieg⟩, ⟨hyg⟩ show the i/ie vocalism
   that only an ēa-type diphthongal stem + i-umlaut produces regularly
   (geminate route, levelling, or Dahl's diphthongal parse —
   DAHL_REASSESSMENT.md); the Anglian ⟨heg⟩, the gospel-gloss ⟨heg, hehg,
   heig, hóeg⟩ (cycle 2) and Kentish ⟨hei-⟩ are compatible both with
   smoothed/Anglian ēġ from that stem and, in principle, with an
   ungeminated \*hewi > \*hei reflex. The dative *hege* shares this
   ambiguity at the segmental level (Anglian/poetic ⟨e⟩ vocalism).
4. Parallel copies (Dialogues C/O/H; the WS gospel MSS; **Ru2's John
   gloss, whose text-historical relation to Li is close but, per Kotake,
   possibly mediated rather than direct copying**) are single textual
   traditions, not independent lexical witnesses; so are the six Paris
   Psalter loci (ids 33–38), which count as one witness. **Farman's
   Rushworth Matthew gloss is an independent Mercian witness** (cycle 2).
5. Excluded homographs: plural pronoun *hig*; *hege* 'hedge, enclosure'
   (Minkova 2014: 64 lists its ME continuation "hay (now dial.)");
   *gærs*/*gers* translations (now including Li Mk 6:39 and Lk 12:28,
   verified); Rushworth *heggetgrese* (Mk 6:39) is a compound, not a
   bare token. Cook 1894 lemmatizes Li *heg* 'hay' separately from *heh*
   'high' and the hedge word, securing the lexical identity of the
   gospel tokens.
