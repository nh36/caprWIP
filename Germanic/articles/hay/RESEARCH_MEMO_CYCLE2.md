## Answers to the required questions

### A. Dahl's alternative: defensible vocalism without gemination?

**Yes, conditionally — and the final consonant is analogical by his own
statement.** Dahl's premise is that \*-awj- was syllabified with a
diphthong, \*-au̯.ja-, so that the \*w was never intervocalic and the
sequence was never input to gemination [@Dahl1938, pp. 81–86;
secondary, from the held extract, against his printed text]. Granted
that parse, the vocalism is regular: \*au > \*ēa by the ordinary
development, i-umlaut > WS *īe*, Anglian smoothing > *ē* — no special
rules. What remains analogical in his account is exactly one segment:
final *-ġ*, restored from the oblique cases, as he says explicitly; and
one patch: the missing †*hīeg* for 'hue' is explained by homonymy
avoidance, his only plainly ad hoc move. The condition is the
syllabification premise itself, which is a reconstruction (inferred),
in tension with the standard account in which \*wj geminated and the
gemination was then "reversible" [@RingeTaylor2014, p. 53]. Decisive
for the article: R&T's reversed geminate [auj], the surviving geminate,
and Dahl's never-geminated diphthong all converge on pre-OE \*auj, after
which the three derivations are segment-for-segment identical. Full
reconstruction and eight-point determination in `DAHL_REASSESSMENT.md`.

### B. The Lindisfarne *heig/heg* evidence

**Independently verified this cycle; it concerns the noun 'hay'; the
alternation is genuine but cross-verse.** Primary-verified against
edition page images: Li *gers ɫ heg* Mt 6:30 and *ofer hehg ɫ gers*
Mt 14:19 [@KembleHardwick1858, pp. 55, 117]; Li *gærs ɫ heig micil*
Jn 6:10 [@Skeat1878, p. 55]; Ru1 (Farman, Mercian) *londes hóeg* Mt 6:30
and *on hóeg* Mt 14:19, both with printed acute accents; Ru2 (Owun)
*heg ɫ gers micel* Jn 6:10. Li Mk 6:39 and Lk 12:28 have *gers/gærs*
only (negatives, OCR-verified). All tokens gloss Latin *faenum*; Cook's
glossary lemmatizes them under one strong neuter, distinct from *heh*
'high' and *hege* 'hedge' [@Cook1894, s.v. *heg*] — the cycle-1
conflation suspicion is resolved. What the evidence establishes: Jones's
report is accurate in substance; every Li token is a double gloss with
'grass'; the variation is between verses, not within one passage; all
tokens are direct-case with no overt inflection (Claim A extended); and
Ru1 supplies the first identified *accented* tokens — a potential
quantity lead (see E). Caveats (unverified/blocked): the edition's
⟨hehg⟩ vs Cook's silent *heg*, and the scribal vs editorial status of
Farman's acutes, both need facsimile checks. Full dossier:
`LINDISFARNE_EVIDENCE.md`.

### C. The *glīg/glēo* doublet

**No — it is not independent evidence for a regular ungeminated
development.** Examined closely (`COMPARATIVE_CONTROLS.md`), the doublet
is consistent both with Campbell's verdict that the g-forms are
re-formations from the oblique stem [@Campbell1959, §411; secondary]
and with Dahl's regular double development. It therefore cannot
discriminate. Its real value is indirect: the WS-only distribution of
the g-forms (always ⟨i⟩ before ⟨g⟩) confirms oblique-to-direct *-g*
restoration as a live, repeated process in exactly this word class,
lowering the cost of the one analogical step that every surviving
account of 'hay' except B2 requires.

### D. OHG *hewi/houwi* and the comparative evidence

Gothic *hawi* and ON *hey* secure an **ungeminated direct cell for
Proto-Germanic** (inferred from primary handbook data; secondary for
the readings). They do not secure a consonantal \*hewi stage for
pre-OE — the stage Dahl denies, noting that no w-form of any OE \*-awj-
noun is on record (a negative that both research cycles' collations
confirm for 'hay'). OHG *houwi* beside *hewi* is routinely read as the
geminate stem levelled into the direct cell, but that direction of
levelling presupposes the early-apocope chronology; under the opposite
chronology *houwi* is regular and *hewi* innovative, and Dahl's
Excursus derives the OHG forms without gemination [@Dahl1938,
pp. 107–109; secondary]. The continental doublet shows that stem
redistribution happened; it cannot fix its direction. Levelling in both
directions within OE ja-stems is independently documented
[@Hogg1979Analogy, pp. 68–73; @Adamczyk2018; primary-verified against
held PDFs], so R&T's levelling is typologically cheap though its donor
paradigm is unattested in OE.

### E. Independent evidence for the long vowel

**None identified; quantity is a convergent reconstruction, not an
observation.** Spellings cannot mark length before /j/; dictionary
acutes are editorial; Minkova's merger argument voids the ME/ModE
reflexes [@Minkova2014, p. 178]; no verse token exists; Jones's
transcriptions are internally inconsistent. The one new prospect is
Farman's two acute-accented *hóeg* tokens (primary-verified as printed),
but they are unusable until (i) the accents are confirmed scribal and
(ii) Farman's accentual practice is calibrated against etymological
quantity in the manner of Mokrowiecki [@Mokrowiecki2015]; Jones's
short-word ⟨wég⟩ acute warns against shortcuts. Ritt 2000 (ILL pending,
not consulted) is the natural control for the Li acutes. The security
of the reconstruction is exactly the security of the disjunction of
viable derivations, all of which predict a long nucleus.

### F. Does the absence of overtly inflected tokens weaken the analogical account?

**Cycle-2 status, superseded by the cycle-3 collation.** At the source
cutoff for this memo, no genitive, dative in -e, or unambiguous plural
had been identified; the six new gospel tokens were all direct-case.
Cycle 3 verified Paris Psalter dative *hege* ×3 (101.4, 101.9, 102.14),
so the general claim that no overtly inflected form exists is withdrawn.
The remaining gap is chronological: those eleventh-century forms do not
demonstrate an oblique donor stem available before i-umlaut. This late
attestation bears on the existence of an inflected cell, not on the
competing derivations' timing. See `RESEARCH_MEMO_CYCLE3.md`.

### G. Which derivations remain viable, and where do the others fail?

Viable: **B2** (late apocope; fully regular, no analogy for this word);
**A2** (early apocope + pre-umlaut stem levelling to \*hēaj-; regular
thereafter; one word-specific analogical assumption); **Dahl** (no
gemination; regular vocalism granted the diphthongal parse; analogical
final -ġ). Failed: **A0** (untouched early-apocope form \*hewi > \*hei:
cannot produce WS ⟨ie/i/y⟩ vocalism at any stage — fails at i-umlaut
input shape); **A1** (A0 plus independently motivated later changes:
Hogg's hiatus resolution yields *hē*, destroying the final segment;
resyllabification to /ej/ supplies neither length nor WS *īe*; no
attested OE change bridges the gap — fails for want of any regular
lengthening/diphthongization of /e/ before /j/ feeding *īe*). Precise
stage-by-stage statements in `DERIVATIONS.md` and §5 of the draft.

### H. Can 'hay' discriminate the chronologies?

**No.** All three viable derivations pass through pre-OE \*auj and are
thereafter identical; the word is regular under late apocope, repaired
by well-paralleled levelling under early apocope, and outside the
gemination input class under Dahl. A form compatible with every
ordering evidences none. The discriminating evidence lies outside the
glide class: light C+j neuters (*nett*, *bedd*, *cynn*) versus the
*mene*-type ungeminated relics [@Stiles2013; @Boutkan1995]. The frozen
CAPR A/B2 captures (387/396 vs 388/396, shared inputs; the Model A
direct-cell mismatch a deliberately uncertified residue) formalize the
same non-discrimination; they are explicitness checks, not historical
evidence.

### I. Strongest defensible original contribution

Three, in descending order of novelty:

1. **The convergence argument** (§5): the demonstration that geminate,
   reversed-geminate, and Dahl's never-geminated derivations converge
   on pre-OE \*auj, so that the WS vocalism requires a diphthongal
   nucleus, not gemination — correcting a recurring overstatement in
   the handbook tradition and striking 'hay' from both chronologies'
   witness lists on principled grounds.
2. **The verified gospel-gloss corpus** (§3.1, CONCORDANCE ids 26–32):
   the first locus-identified, image-verified collation of the Li, Ru1
   and Ru2 hay tokens, including the previously uncited accented Mercian
   *hóeg* tokens and the double-gloss structure, resolving Jones's
   unlocated report.
3. **The quantity audit** (§4): the explicit demonstration that OE vowel
   length in this word rests on no direct observation, with Farman's
   acutes identified as the only prospect of derivation-independent
   evidence.

## Changes to the thesis

Cycle 1 ended with a negative verdict framed around the geminate
requirement ("WS *hīeġ* requires the geminate-shaped stem"). Cycle 2
**retains the negative overall verdict but changes its ground**
(assignment outcome **D**, combined with non-discrimination): Dahl's
alternative is conditionally defensible, the geminate requirement
weakens to a diphthongal-nucleus requirement, and the word is shown to
be structurally incapable of testing the chronology rather than merely
untested. Cycle-1 claims, as adversarially reviewed
(`ADVERSARIAL_REVIEW.md`): A confirmed and extended; B and C restated
in narrower, defensible form; D confirmed with scope fixed; E not
established as stated (attestation symmetry only).

## Recommendation

The article's thesis should be: *the Old English word for 'hay' is a
dependent of the gemination–apocope debate, not a witness in it; its
every attested property is a shared prediction of the surviving
derivations, and the chronological question must be decided by the C+j
neuters and the ungeminated relics.* The manuscript (Sections 1–8 now
drafted) is a complete argument in provisional form. It should be held
at draft status until Ritt 2000 and Dal 1934 arrive and the two
facsimile checks are made; thereafter it needs one editorial cycle, not
a third research cycle, unless the Farman accents calibrate — in which
case §4 gains the article's only direct quantity evidence and should be
rewritten around it.
