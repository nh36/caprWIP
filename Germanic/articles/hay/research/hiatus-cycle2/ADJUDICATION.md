# Old English hiatus: second-cycle adjudication

**Decision.** Use the foot guard as the isolated research substrate.
Prefer a later resolution window for short unlike front hiatus where
early resolution would wrongly feed high-vowel loss. Retain nuclear
incorporation and consonantal desyllabification as distinct hypotheses
for the edge. For nonfinal cases, no independently verified comparison
selects direct contraction over a serial path. **T5m** has the greatest
explanatory scope, not the status of an adopted historical model.
Regular short *hej* is the best economical working account of written
Vespasian ⟨heg⟩ **if** early apocope gave inherited *he.i*; that preference
does not determine the written witness's quantity, prove its ancestry,
or extend unchanged to Farman or West Saxon.

The [stress record](STRESS.md) explains the independent substrate decision;
[‘couch’](COUCH.md) assesses the nasal evidence and complete grammatical
cells. [Stack comparisons and source checks](STACKS.md) lay out the
physical orders, historical graph, alternate inputs and adverse
predictions. The [unscored benchmark](benchmark.tsv) is not a production
target list. No rule, overlay, target or article revision is authorized
by this record.

## Twenty-four answers

### Stress

1. **Cause.** The root acute survives entry in A/B2 prefixed
   ‘call’, and the foot marker identifies an initial pretonic
   syllable. Fronting and umlaut leave a plain root *e*; after
   *w*-loss, the old SC083 body treats it as unstressed even
   though the remaining foot boundary says otherwise. It
   deletes the root *e*, not the second vowel. This is
   loss of local stress representation and failure to use
   residual context, not a historical stress shift.
2. **Extent.** The independently reconstructed prefixed present
   and participle and the later-entered past demonstrate it.
   The frozen 394 archived and 396 active derivations per model,
   including all eight genuine SC083 weak-tail firings in each
   panel, are unchanged under the repair. That bounds the
   observed defect; it does not certify every possible CAPR input.
3. **Repair.** Apply `FootCtx` around the existing operation
   (S1). Explicit tier S2 diagnoses wider representation needs
   but fails the independent ‘world’ alignment control, so it
   is not a replacement or a comparable full-corpus substrate.
4. **Late contraction.** Viable for A/B2 prefixed ‘call’ with
   the repaired root still present. The early and late local
   arms yield *ġecēþ/ġecēd*. Canonical has a different earlier
   nucleus; its failure to take the same short-input rule is
   not a residual stress bug.
5. **Chronology.** The former late-arm failure does not support
   an early-contraction edge. Early unlike-vowel resolution
   can feed unwanted HVD in the supplied ‘flock’ cell; late
   resolution preserves the ending. The difference is real in
   the trace but depends historically on the vowel/ending
   reconstructed for that cell. Identical-vowel collapse may
   have preceded unlike resolution; no exact date follows.

### ‘Couch’

6. **Etymology.** A feminine ‘something spread out’ derivative
   of short *straw-* with an *in*-type nasal suffix is preferable.
   Wright’s feminine derivational classification, the wider
   Germanic verbal family, and other nominal *-en* formations
   support different parts of the argument. They do not prove
   the exact short *i* and singleton *n* in Ringe–Taylor's
   *strawin-* analysis.
7. **Attestations and grammar.** Sweet’s four Vespasian loci
   all print ⟨strene⟩, not a secure bare *stren*.
   The object cells are accusative; 131:3 likely has an
   English dependent genitive, with feminine agreement also
   compatible with a dative reinterpretation. Tanner 10,
   f. 13r.1 directly attests accusative ⟨streoƿne⟩.
   Exeter’s dative plural ⟨streonum⟩ may instead denote
   ‘treasures’. Feminine gender and strong OE inflection
   are better supported than an original stem class.
8. **Nasal hiatus.** *streina* (accusative projection) and
   *streinu* (unattested nominative) give a motivated
   comparison before *n* if the short derived stem and ordinary
   loss before weak *i* are accepted. The manuscripts do not
   directly establish hiatus, quantity or suffix segmentation.
9. **Conditioning.** It is positive **conditional** evidence
   for including *n* in a single nonfinal resolution. A model
   restricted to stops or dental obstruents needs a second
   explanation for this lexeme. The nasal does not license
   liquids, sibilants or all other consonants.

### Historical phonology

10. **Narrowest comparison.** The positively motivated
    environments are *d* and *þ/ð* in ‘call’ (and *d* in
    qualified ‘strew’/‘flock’). The narrowest single tested
    set covering the preferred ‘couch’ history is *d, þ/ð, n*.
    It is an empirical comparison class, not a demonstrated
    phonological natural class. There is no independent warrant
    here for adding *t* or all coronals.
11. **Mechanism.** Direct contraction is sufficient for the
    nonfinal endpoints, and should remain the minimal
    comparator. Independently motivated nuclear incorporation
    has the widest constructive reach because of the back
    vowels, but that precedent does not prove atomic *ei̯* in
    short front hiatus. Gliding and assimilation also give the
    same full ‘call’ and ‘couch’ outcomes under the matched
    scope. A retained intermediate, a pre-merger consonantal
    contrast, or weight independently determined before later
    resolution would separate them.
12. **Palatal type.** Yes. Weak *i* can yield a nonsyllabic
    vowel or approximant; Jones’s [j] denotes a fricative,
    not automatically that new glide. After merger a
    consonantal rule reaches the secondary palatal of ‘rain’
    when it did not before. Unconditioned fricative assimilation
    also threatens ‘sail’ and ‘way’; *sēl* was not verified and
    *wē* is an adverse prediction. Independent *cǣġ* already
    refutes using long-vowel final loss as a general explanation.
13. **Quantity.** Long-vowel absorption can converge with
    high-vowel apocope or syncope without adding length, as in
    ‘sea’ and ‘law’. Lengthening before desyllabification can
    instead feed HVD and prevent gliding; later lengthening
    can overapply to already established *ej*. Short /hej/
    and conditional long /heːj/ remain possible. Vowel length,
    syllable weight and type of palatal must be separate axes.
14. **Back vowels.** Yes, through a shared type of mechanism,
    not the same input domain. Early loss before *u* followed
    by nuclear incorporation matches *clēa/cnēo/trēo* and the
    ‘threat’/‘few’ controls. Endingless ‘knee’/‘tree’ can
    converge by *w*-vocalization. The ‘three’ holdout limits
    the current template/realization; oblique ‘claw’ still
    requires separate treatment.
15. **Origin of hiatus.** It matters through independent
    chronology and available representations, not an invisible
    provenance tag. Early *w*-loss before *u*, later *w*-loss
    before weak *i*, and intervocalic *h*-loss after ordinary
    syncope are distinct sources. Unmarked identical nuclei at
    the same time and place should behave alike unless an
    independent feature distinguishes them.
16. **Order.** Yes, especially HVD relative to front resolution
    and whether weak *i* is still present or has lowered when
    *h* is lost. For Anglian ‘strike’, unlowered *e.i* and
    hypothetical lowered *e.e* converge, but historical
    grammars reconstruct the unlowered input. ‘River’ may not
    be an inherited oblique at all. The sparse evidence favours
    separate windows over a universal order, not freely
    adjustable dates to rescue mismatches.
17. **Compounds.** A real morphological and prosodic
    distinction is independently motivated; a particular
    hiatus rule at every first-member edge is not. Explicit
    probes separate absolute edge, first-member boundary,
    ordinary internal adjacency and weak or locally accented
    last members. Kentish ⟨hei weg⟩ (Dahl prints 859) is
    first-member evidence, not a quantified simplex or a
    complete reconstruction of compound stress.

### CAPR and ‘hay’

18. **Identified deficiencies.** S1 isolates the pretonic
    root scope error. The frozen ‘see’ input preserves medial
    *hw*, so never feeds intervocalic loss; after private
    cluster reduction, a separate contraction gap remains.
    Early back-vowel incorporation is missing for the
    tested bare/plural histories. Admission rejects some
    justified later inputs, distinct from their phonology.
    The private atomic-*ei* and unaccented-*aeu* issues are
    representation limits, not new historical sound laws.
19. **Greatest scope.** T5m maintains the S1 stress domain,
    integrates the independently early back-nucleus histories,
    distinguishes medial from initial *hw*, and tests a later
    common window for front nuclei with narrow nonfinal merger.
    It reaches the named stem and ending predictions without
    changing the wide corpus surfaces. That last result is a
    safety check, not historical validation in a sparse corpus.
20. **Obstacles.** Early *streide* (if correctly identified)
    and poetic *strēd* are not explained by a uniform merger;
    ‘wealth’, ‘three’, Anglian smoothing, the complete ‘claw’
    paradigm, and uncertain ‘flock’ endings remain unresolved.
    There are not enough independent short *e.i* environments
    to establish the class or timing. Direct applicability of
    de Chene to weak-vowel hiatus, Opalińska’s original
    treatment and the wider Aldred distribution remain open.
21. **What inherited ‘hay’ predicts.** Conditional on early
    apocope yielding *hawją > hawi > hæwi > hewi > he.i*,
    the stacks predict retained hiatus (T1/T2), short
    /hej/ (T3), /hei̯/ (T3N/T5m) or /heːj/ after a
    separately motivated lengthening (T4). Direct edge
    contraction gives /heː/ in T2’s matched edge experiment;
    this cannot alone yield written ⟨g⟩ under a consonantal
    interpretation. TD instead starts with a differentiated
    long nucleus and predicts loss and then requires restoration.
22. **Preference and analogy.** Short /hej/ is a positive,
    conservative working preference for the early **Vespasian**
    direct history: it preserves the inherited mid vocalism,
    needs neither merger before a consonant nor extra
    lengthening, and produces an overt palatal without
    importing a stem. It is not demonstrated against a
    retained complex nucleus, conditional long /heːj/, or
    transfer. West Saxon high vocalism motivates investigating
    protected material under early apocope independently.
23. **Discriminating evidence.** Seek an independently
    quantified free direct form, securely analysed early
    metrical hiatus outside poetic archaism, an independently
    inherited final short-V + *j* comparison, and better
    dated dental/nasal and non-dental controls. Verify
    ‘flock’ tail, exact identity of early *streide*, Vespasian
    ‘couch’ leaves and an extra-English nasal cognate.
    Direct original source access is preferable to treating
    an index snippet as a new quantitative datum.
24. **Further CAPR audits.** Separate SC083’s foot-scope audit,
    noninitial intervocalic *hw* and later contraction (include
    initial *hw* negatives), back-vowel incorporation and
    inflectional levelling, and stage-aware phonotactic
    admission. A future independent audit should test the
    actual generalized hypotheses on new lexical evidence;
    none warrants transferring a production certificate.

## Witnesses and consequences for the paused article

| Witness | Consequence of the wider model |
|---|---|
| Kentish ⟨hei weg⟩ | A first-member structure can preserve a palatal or offglide where a free word might behave differently. The spelling is compatible with several quantities; it does not decide the precise compound boundary policy. |
| Vespasian ⟨heg⟩ | A short mid vowel with a retained palatal is an economical regular direct outcome. Orthography does not give a new length measure or exclude alternative histories. |
| Aldred ⟨heg/heig⟩ and Owun ⟨heg⟩ | The Lindisfarne variants are directly verified; the latter hand is a separate Rushworth comparison, not another Aldred token. Written *ei* may reflect a changed nucleus or a spelling before a consonantal palatal. Neither evidence set itself identifies shortness or long quantity. |
| Farman ⟨hóeg⟩ | Positive but local length evidence. A subsequent long direct history or transfer is possible; unchanged short survival is not the best account of this token. No reason to impose its quantity on Vespasian or to invent a dialectal split purely to fit the stacks. |
| West Saxon ⟨hig/hyg⟩ | The inherited short mid pathways do not predict the high vowel. Under early apocope, a protected source remains positively motivated; the source and specific analogical operation remain distinct inferences. |

For **CAPR**, the strongest actions are separate audits with hard
negatives and frozen inputs; phonological compatibility is not
permission to change targets or source classifiers. For the **article**,
the new arguments are the verified ‘couch’ correction, the bounded
nasal comparison, the timing-sensitive HVD result and the stronger
case for keeping whole-stem ancestry distinct from vowel quantity.
They are research inputs for a later editorial decision. Nothing
here reopens the article, its abstract, tables, frozen derivations
or PDF.
