The falsifiable question is whether an independently controlled history
of word-final short stressed /e/ plus unstressed syllabic /i/, or
independent evidence for short /ej/ in a secure early direct singular,
makes regular survival preferable. A secure early long nucleus would
exclude unchanged short /hej/ for that witness. A long nucleus would
not by itself prove replacement. The inherited path to hiatus is
conditional on A: \*hawją > \*hawj > \*hawi > \*hæwi > \*hewi > \*he.i.

**Assessment.** Dahl does propose a bare \*hē intermediate, but from his
own long diphthong and final-vowel loss. None of these checked ‘hay’
passages gives bare \*hē from contraction of A's short stressed /e/ and
unstressed final /i/. That is a candidate inference from controls, not
an inherited consensus. The conventional Anglian macron in *hēġ* is
explained by competing etymologies, not independent calibration of the
earliest spellings. This does not show the long reconstruction is wrong.
Retain the distinction in C01 and leave E33–E34 unestablished. Dal,
Wallenberg and Brooks and Kelly were not acquired.
