# R7: Intervocalic loss, later contraction, and its limits

## Identity and question

Research-only, `research/hay-article`, predecessor `8ba5b981`. SC085 (`OEHLoss`) and SC086 (`OEContraction`) supply the frozen controls. Do independently attested *h*-loss forms require the same operation as the post-*w*-loss series, and can a narrower *hw* treatment, regional smoothing, and different sorts of contraction coexist without overgenerating?

## Current state and source evidence

SC085 retains singleton intervocalic loss, with a pretonic protection and *x/ç* coverage; SC086 is a separate operation. Source distinctions are those in the existing [SC085 adjudication](../../../../docs/sound_changes/audits/sc085-adjudication.md), Campbell (1959: 186–187 §§461, 464–465), and Ringe & Taylor (2014: 314–318 §6.9.3). The stage start of a local entry is not a new account of its earlier derivation.

- **Wealth.** Ringe & Taylor (2014: 315–316) reconstruct dative *fehiwi > fehē > feohe > fēo*. Sweet (1885: 175 l. 5) prints *mid uncre claene feo* in the Codex Aureus inscription, supported by parallel *mid clsene golde*. This is dative ‘property, payment’ with a front weak vowel in the reconstructed input; it is not *feorh* ‘life’ or evidence for general postconsonantal lengthening. The written token does not directly mark the length. Southern *fēo* and the Anglian smoothing comparison do not make like-vowel *e.e* the only explanation.
- **See.** Ringe & Taylor (315–316) independently posit *sehwan > seohan > sēon*. Their Mercian *ge-sian/ge-sēan* and the front-vowel subjunctive *ge-sē* (compare southern *sēo*) motivate smoothing before loss: the retained diphthong would otherwise tend to persist after contraction as in the southern forms. The actuator `HwMed` is a private approximation to removing the labial from a medial cluster, not a reconstruction that initial *hw* underwent the same fate.
- **Strike and draw.** The infinitives *slēan* and *tēon* use broken or smoothed nuclei plus a following back vowel, not the short dental class indiscriminately. Ringe & Taylor (315, 317) reconstruct unlowered *slehiþ* for Mercian *slēþ*; a separately assumed early lowering to *sleheþ* would yield the same endpoint through *e.e*. The corresponding southern syncopated cells retain *h* because there is no intervening weak vowel left to trigger the same loss. ‘Draw’ *tēo* (WS) and *tio* (Kentish) in the subjunctive likewise support contraction with a front inflectional vowel; do not identify this with the distinct raised/smoothed ‘educate’ history (pp. 315, 317).
- **River.** Campbell (1959: §235.3 and pp. 254–255 §628.4) records widespread *ēa* including inflected uses, but southern gen./dat. *īe*, occasional *ē*, dative plural *ēam/ēum/ēaum*, and remodelled forms including genitive *ēas* and plural *ēan*. [Bosworth–Toller, supplement s.v. *eá*](https://bosworthtoller.com/43189), gives *þǽre ié* in the Danai phrase and separates obliques. Ringe & Taylor (318) allow inherited *eahi > iehi/ehi* with a root-noun ending, or forms built to contracted *ēa*. The fuller comparative neuter/feminine case genealogy is not uniquely recoverable from the English forms. Comparative regular dative *eahē* can yield *ēa* (Ringe & Taylor 315); it cannot simply be substituted for an inherited front-vowel ending when testing *ē/īe*. The reconstructed short mid nucleus is contingent, but the existence of independently attested remodelling elsewhere in the paradigm is not proof that the mutated oblique is remodelled.
- **Fringe and clay.** Sweet (1885: 104, 107), checked against edition images, prints Épinal and Erfurt 1066 *uulohum* versus Corpus 2122 *uuloum*, glossing *uillis/villis*. Pheifer (1974: §72(a), entry and note 1066) identifies the dative plural of ‘fringe’ and discusses why Latin ‘with fleece’ may have prompted the gloss. The letter *h* is genuinely absent in the Corpus edition. *Wlōm* remains an unattested contraction prediction (Ringe & Taylor 314), not a third historical stage observed in the record. The inherited SC085 clay comparator separately connects loss with *þō*.
- **Postconsonantal control.** Goering (2026: 306, 313–314) supports a short root after postconsonantal loss in *feore* (< *feorhǣ*); its heavy scansion in *Beowulf* 1293a can preserve earlier prosody. The distinction is physical: removing *h* after *r* does not create adjacent vowels. A postconsonantal outcome is not evidence that an intervocalic contraction operates after a consonant.

## Historical analysis and verdict

**RETAIN** narrow medial *hw* reduction in the research substrate and the distinction between ordinary intervocalic and postconsonantal loss. **SPLIT** front-broken-plus-front contraction and identical-vowel contraction from the provisional short *e.i* mechanism. **DEFER** the generality of the new front-broken comparison, the inherited origin of the river obliques, and the historical identity of the *w*- and *h*-generated short-hiatus changes.

Two edges are independent of ‘hay’: **regional smoothing before intervocalic loss and contraction** (the southern/Mercian contrast in front-vowel cells), and **loss before contraction** (the written uncontracted intermediate and the source-backed sequence, including the independent clay comparison). Neither the executable position nor a shared endpoint establishes when unlike *e.i* from *w*-loss was resolved relative to *h*-loss. A common late mechanism is feasible if early *e.i* remains syllabic or remains identifiable after a light first step. The surviving ‘flock’ ending supports delaying heaviness, not delaying until *h*-loss. ‘Strew’ and ‘fringe’ attest different conditional windows of persistence; they do not prove one common historical operation. No new edge is registered.
