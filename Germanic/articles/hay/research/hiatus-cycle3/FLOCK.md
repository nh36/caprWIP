# R3: ‘flock’, stem history and the surviving ending

**Superseded quantity attribution.** The direct page-image check in
[Q1a](../hiatus-cycle4/FLOCK_QUANTITY.md) confirms printed *awidī* and
*eïdī*, not short final *i*. In the original record below, the short
input must be read as a sensitivity case. The attestation and
morphological evidence stand; the chronology inference does not follow
from the published reconstruction.

**Question.** Does attested *ēde* positively require late
resolution? That inference needs an independently motivated
short high ending in the **attested cell**, eligible for loss if
resolution makes the relevant syllable heavy. An old long vowel,
an already nonhigh vowel, or analogical renewal could break the
inference without disproving *w*-loss in the stem.

This is a bounded edition and dictionary inventory, not a new
exhaustive DOE search. No charter, manuscript accent or other
unseen reading is used. The secure English neuter agreement,
and comparative Gothic and OHG, warrant reconstructing a neuter
formation; real feminine usage and genitives in *-es* are reasons
not to project a monolithic late-English paradigm back into PWGmc.

## Comparative formation and alternatives

- **Root.** Kroonen (2013: 45, s.vv. *awi-*, *awidja-*) derives
  the independently short *awi-* ‘ewe’ from the inherited
  i-stem (compare Latin *ovis*, Sanskrit *ávi-* and OHG *ouwi*).
  The collective suffix is *-dja-* in his analysis, with
  *kudja-* as an internal comparison. This identifies the
  **medial** *i* and the labial better than the ending.
- **Originally neuter dental collective.** [EWA II, s.v. *ewit*](https://ewa.saw-leipzig.de/articles/34800/en)
  distinguishes OHG *ouwiti* (neuter ja-stem, two Bavarian
  gloss manuscripts; Gl. 1.647.18) from *ewit* (probably
  masculine a-stem; cf. Rhein Franconian *euuida*, Gl. 2.21.32).
  EWA connects the first with Gothic *aweþi*, genitive *aweþjis*,
  and OE *ēow(o)de*; it makes the OHG masculine potentially
  secondary under influence of *ewist*. Ringe & Taylor
  (2014: 259) instead take *ewit* as without the *-ija-* suffix
  and leave Gothic *ē* unresolved. Gothic supports a
  dental + *j* formation, not the exact English stem vowel
  or a final short *i* at English HVD. The suffixal comparison
  goes back at least to the morphological literature cited by
  EWA (Wilmanns II §§264–265; Kluge, *Nominale Stammbildung*,
  §70); these original passages were not independently checked.
- **W-less English direct form.** Ringe & Taylor explicitly
  give PWGmc *awidi* (not *awidī*) and the path *awidi > æwidi >
  eidi > ēde* (pp. 259, 318). This is an independently published
  short-final analysis, not a change made for parser admission.
  The details linking the deeper collective to this early direct
  allomorph remain an assumption of the comparison. Campbell
  (1959: 100 §237.1b, pp. 166–167 §406), Luick (§243c,
  pp. 219–220), and Sievers–Brunner (1965: 111 §129 n. 2,
  p. 142 §173e) support the conditioned loss/contraction
  account, not a separate attestation of the tail or a dated
  HVD edge. Wright & Wright's §624 classification is cited by
  Bosworth–Toller; it is not additional primary evidence.
- **W-retaining English.** Ringe & Taylor (p. 259) explain
  West Saxon *eowede* through remodelling on *eowu*: transfer
  of ‘ewe’ to a class with nontriggering endings preserves the
  donor's *w*. The base is therefore available, and matching
  *eo* as well as *w* makes renewal plausible. The
  early Mercian w-retaining forms show it is not simply a
  contrast of regional sound laws. They do not prove this
  exact analogy, nor exclude replacement of *w* alone or
  different inherited allomorphs in some cells.

## Cell predictions and sequencing

| Input proposition | Direct cell | Obliques and what they test |
|---|---|---|
| Ringe–Taylor short final high vowel, *awidi* | Supply *ewidi* before *w*-loss, and compare unchanged *eidi*, the two serial intermediates, and *ēdi*. If final *i* is short and still present at HVD, the result distinguishes an already heavy stem from retained syllabic hiatus (or an independently light complex nucleus). | Genitive *-es* and dative plural *-um* have separate endings and cannot test loss of this short final *i*. In English these are directly evidenced in the w-bearing family; no corresponding w-less ending is inferred from them. |
| Deeper inherited neuter ja-stem *awidja-* (or *awidija-* notation) | The short *awidi* cell is a serious hypothesis, not a mechanical substitution for any full PGmc input. Earliest comparison must record how and when the overt final vowel became the short high vowel; a geminated, protected, or later remodelled form is not the same input. | Keep genitive and dative plural as independent protected forms unless the suffix reduction and later endings are explicitly motivated. A palaeographic ⟨-e⟩ or ⟨-æ⟩ does not directly recover their prehistory. |
| Shorter consonant-final *ewit* type, or late transfer to a different class | An inherited endingless equivalent would not directly yield the attested English direct vowel; it would need later renewal or a different stem choice. Late w-retaining or feminine forms are not a positive example of early *e.i* followed by loss. | Feminine agreement in some witnesses motivates treating these as a real alternative later English history, not transferring the class wholesale to early neuter *ēdæ*. |
| Long *ī* or already lowered *e* | Use as sensitivity controls, **not** attested alternative early endings. Neither necessarily participates in the same high-vowel loss; a surviving ending alone would no longer date resolution. | Evidence for one of these earlier alternatives must come from morphology or independently dated reductions rather than a better fit. |

**R3 verdict.** The short-*i* direct cell has independent published
support and the surviving ending belongs to a defensible direct
singular, not just a normalized headword. That strengthens the
test against early **heavy** resolution. It does not show that
every nuclear incorporation produces heavy weight, that a short
ending was never renewed, or that the w-bearing and w-less forms
must be direct outputs of identical stems. Quantitative chronology
belongs to the separate R4 assay; no production edge is proposed.
