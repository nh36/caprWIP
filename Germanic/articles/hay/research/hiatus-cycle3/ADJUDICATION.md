# Old English hiatus: third-cycle comparative adjudication

**Correction to the cycle's chronology.** [Q1a](../hiatus-cycle4/FLOCK_QUANTITY.md)
verifies final *ī* in Ringe & Taylor. It withdraws the preference for
heavy resolution after high-vowel loss **on the evidence of ‘flock’**.
The preserved adjudication below reflects the previous reading; the
long-ending control permits either chronology. This does not select an
edge policy for ‘hay’.

**Correction to the h-loss comparisons.** [Q1b](../hiatus-cycle4/H_LOSS_QUANTITY.md)
supersedes historical deductions based on the short roots of ‘receive’,
‘cover’ and ‘rug’. [Q2a](../hiatus-cycle4/SOURCE_ENTRY_AUDIT.md) distinguishes
the long-tail ‘higher’ stage and the conditional ‘river’ comparison from
the reduced and sensitivity entries.

**Decision.** Prefer resolution to a heavy output **after high-vowel loss** for short unlike front hiatus where the ‘flock’ ending provides an independent test. Retain *d, þ/ð, n* as the best-supported working nonfinal scope, not a fully established sound law. Direct contraction is the least elaborate account of the convergent nonfinal endpoints, not the only historical account. Nuclear incorporation is independently motivated for early back vowels, not thereby demonstrated for short front hiatus. Under early nasal apocope and inherited *he.i*, short /hej/ remains the economical working explanation of Vespasian ⟨heg⟩. Even the best-supported broader stack does not independently select the final gliding policy that produces it.

## Fifteen questions

1. **Can the three mechanisms be matched?** Yes. [R1](STACKS.md) reproduces accepted T5m and separates common infrastructure from three front mechanisms. [R9](REVISED_STACKS.md) matches their inputs, *d, þ/ð, n* domain, and placements in canonical, A and B2. It first compares equally late D/J/N, then adds a post-HVD window or an edge operation independently. For a live post-loss witness, ‘strike’, D reaches `*s*l*ē*θ` directly; J passes through `*s*l*e*j*ʲ*θ`, and N through `*s*l*ėi*θ`. The latter two end at the same state as D. The private provenance tags are not historical phonemes.

2. **Does one account for a greater range of independently established developments?** Not for the matched short nonfinal *e.i* comparisons. All three cover the source-oriented ‘call’, ‘flock’, ‘couch’ and ‘strike’ comparisons when the necessary input and workable window are present, and all have the same adverse ‘strew’ results. Direct contraction is sufficient for those endpoints; no endpoint warrants ranking a serial intermediate above it. Nuclear formation has the broadest independently motivated use across the whole investigation because the early back-vowel series needs a way to make complex nuclei. This does not imply a common date, stress domain or front-vowel history.

3. **Does ‘flock’ support resolution after HVD?** Yes for the transition to **heaviness**, conditionally on Ringe & Taylor’s short-final *awidi* (2014: 259, 318) and regular rather than renewed retention. [R3](FLOCK.md) establishes the comparative lexical and collective evidence and the neuter direct cell; it does not infer previous ending length from *ēdæ/eode*. With injected *e.wi.di*, a heavy result before HVD gives *ēd*; delaying that result gives *ēde*. An early **light** complex nucleus can leave the ending intact; an independently long or already lowered ending would also remove the diagnostic. The poorly distinguished full-input alternatives and the w-bearing Vespasian cells remain separate. The [isolated replay](CHRONOLOGY.md) preserves these results and supersedes the first R4 receipt, whose component filenames overlapped.

4. **Does ‘strew’ support persistent unlike hiatus?** Yes, positively for a possible verse realization, not unconditionally for the spoken history. [R5](STREW.md) shows that Épinal has *stridae*, Erfurt *streidæ*, and Corpus *streide*, with different Latin gloss forms and a viable *stregdan* alternative. Munich Old Saxon *streidun* supports a separate continental w-less history but does not date English loss or distinguish a hiatus from an offglide. Sievers (1893: 132 §84.8 n. 5) and Fulk (1992: 79, 115 §§88, 120) motivate two positions in *Beowulf* 2436b, while monosyllabic scansion is not formally impossible and performance can preserve an older option. Early written ⟨ei⟩ does not decide syllabicity. Corrected R4 and R9 agree: all matched late arms remove separate *e.i* in the local short preterite, and in R9 all change the corresponding A/B2 participial projection. The late Owun plural has a contracted-looking stem but a different attested ending from the assay's local plural projection.

5. **Can identical and unlike hiatus be placed at different dates?** Prefer allowing different dates, not requiring a single event. Identical *i.i* in ‘new-’ and in the comparable ‘glee’ reconstruction can coalesce without a change of quality; identical *e.e/i.i* after *h*-loss is also an independent contraction comparison. The retained ‘strew’ evidence warrants an unlike-hiatus persistence option. The sources do not independently place completion of identical front coalescence before the relevant ‘strew’ layer. A shared late event for some unlike sequences is possible: Ringe & Taylor (258–261, 314–318) independently place late *w*-loss before general syncope and intervocalic *h*-loss later. ‘Prepare’ establishes a feeding distinction with syncope, ‘flock’ independently favours delaying heaviness, and ‘strew’ allows persistence. Those constraints make convergence feasible without dating it or proving the same operation.

6. **Does ‘couch’ remain compatible?** Yes, conditionally, and it remains the strongest short *e.i* case for adding *n*. The [second-cycle dossier](../hiatus-cycle2/COUCH.md) supports a short *straw-* derivative with a nasal formation, feminine gender, and a final vowel in the securely analysed accusative cells. R9 yields local *strēne* and full A/B2 *strēne* under each mechanism; canonical *strīene* reflects a different earlier vocalism. The evidence does not establish a short singleton suffix or original stem class directly, and the attested *streoƿne* shows a separate w-bearing history or cell. Do not treat the derived short nasal input as certain or the bare nominative as attested.

7. **Do early back-vowel developments extend?** In part. [R6](BACK_VOWELS.md) keeps early *w*-loss and *u*-incorporation for the direct ‘claw’, ‘knees’, ‘trees’, ‘threat’ and ‘few’ comparisons, not for the labial-retaining obliques. ‘Three’ independently involves ending raising; incorporation and realization are separately needed to project Hogg’s *þrīo* in the assay. R9 shows that the tested blanket realization also changes two distinct corpus lexemes and that the transient *æu* bridge affects established fronted outputs. Neither joins the common stack. The independently source-backed *clawe* treatment needs the earlier pre-*w* vocalism and careful treatment of frozen proxies; the experimental closed-*um* solution incorrectly contracts retained-*w* ‘knee/tree’ controls. Inflectional extensions and renewed *w* cannot be inferred merely from endpoint fit.

8. **Can later *h*-loss and contraction be represented coherently?** Yes, with distinct scopes, not one quantity-producing loss rule. [R7](LATER_H.md) separates medial *hw* reduction, Anglian smoothing, ordinary intervocalic loss, and broken-nucleus versus like-vowel contraction. It retains all five initial-*hw* controls, reaches southern *sēon* and source-oriented *fēo*, and preserves the written ‘fringe’ intermediate rather than inventing attested *wlōm*. The southern/Mercian contrast in front-vowel cells grounds smoothing before loss and contraction independently of ‘hay’; the attested loss intermediate and ‘clay’ comparison ground loss before contraction. ‘Draw’ reaches its outcome from a long broken nucleus, not short *e.i*. The distinct postconsonantal *feore* comparator need not lengthen (Goering 2026: 313–314). R9 retains failures for short *æ.i* ‘receive’, the back ‘rug’ probe and the later-entered ‘cow’ probe; coherent separation is not complete coverage.

9. **Is the stress/quantity representation sufficient?** For local comparisons, not for all serial histories. [R2](REPRESENTATION.md) isolates the loss of `*ᶠ` at `OEHLoss`, preservation of explicit acute in a private nuclear path, independently assignable light/heavy weight, and the indistinguishable rendering of two different *ei* structures. S1 protects the marked prefixed root, but after *h*-loss a plain root no longer has the same diagnostic. The strictly late arms therefore miss full A/B2 ‘call’ where the two-window arms succeed; this is not a historical order demonstration. The temporary R9 consonantal and nuclear states test source identity only across adjacent operations, and S2 is not adopted.

10. **Does palatal origin materially discriminate?** Yes as a constraint on generalizing the serial operation, not as evidence selecting it. An injected old approximant can be consumed by ordinary untagged assimilation before the palatal merger while a secondary *ʝ* is inaccessible; after merger both can be consumed. The revised J test isolates only a newly formed approximant, and N isolates newly formed nuclear material rather than consuming inherited *ei*. The independent *cǣġ/cǣġe* and ‘way’ retentions rule against indiscriminate palatal loss. ‘Rain’, ‘thane’, ‘maiden’, ‘wagon’ and ‘sail’ furnish secondary consonant or cluster histories; their variation is not evidence that genuine short *e.i* must follow the same course. It would take independent evidence of a surviving segmental, prosodic or temporal distinction to justify a historical origin-sensitive rule.

11. **What is the best nonfinal conditioning?** Keep the narrow working domain *d, þ/ð, n* for stressed short *e.i*. It has positives in independently reconstructed dental cells and a conditional nasal witness. Morphology supplies attested cells and historically available environments; it is not a condition on regular sound change. [R8](ENVIRONMENTS.md) does not establish equivalent short inputs before *t, l, r*, sibilants, labials or velars. Long comparators before *r/s*, palatal clusters and synthetic probes do not count as short unlike hiatus evidence. A broader non-sibilant coronal hypothesis is testable but presently less well supported; the narrow list is not proof of a historical prohibition outside it.

12. **Is the final/nonfinal contrast productive?** It is an independently sensible distinction between an open final environment and one followed by a consonant. Keeping *j* or an offglide at absolute edge while subsequently absorbing it in a narrow nonfinal environment is phonologically coherent; it is not an empirically established blanket rule. Conditional short inherited *ehi* in ‘river’ favours edge contraction rather than J if that prehistory is right; the documented remodelled forms do not prove that the mutated oblique is remodelled too. Independently long inputs can simply absorb a final high vowel, and unambiguously retained *j* is a different history. R1's isolated probes distinguish a compound-member boundary from an absolute edge and ordinary internal position. The late full pipeline does not accept the member marker, and neither a productive member policy nor a prevocalic extension is established.

13. **Does the preferred broader stack independently predict regular *hej*?** **No.** Under early apocope, the inherited conditional chain *hawją > hawi > hæwi > hewi > he.i* is the input to the comparison. The full A order and local projections in all orders exercise its last step. The best-supported medial histories leave it disyllabic at the absolute edge unless a distinct edge treatment is added. D edge yields *hē*, J edge short *hej*, and N edge a short complex nucleus; the common and N outcomes can both print *hei*. Long *hēj* requires a separately motivated intermediate length change or a long stem source; it is not obtained simply by accepting the shared chronology. Canonical and B2 full forms do not independently generate the short direct precursor. J remains the conservative reading of the written mid vowel plus a palatal under the conditional inherited history, but no wider independent edge control has selected it over the nuclear alternative or supplied an independent length measure.

14. **Which deficiencies warrant CAPR audits?** Separate audits should address SC083 weak-domain anchoring and persistence of stress through SC085; origin- and accent-safe short nucleus representation, with a proper alphabet for weight tests; independent medial *hw* reduction with retained initials; SC086 front-broken-plus-front and marked identical contraction, retaining the non-intervocalic negative; and early back-nucleus realization, restricted transient-token handling, and retained labials. Stage-aware admission should separately address later historical inputs the full proto parser cannot encode. The faulty numbered component mapping was an assay infrastructure problem corrected here, not a historical law. None of these discoveries licenses a changed production registry edge, capture or target.

15. **What evidence could change the decision?** Secure comparative morphology for the final vowel of ‘flock’, or evidence of renewed endings, could change the HVD preference. A secure identification and contemporary syllabification of an early *streide* token, or additional verse with unambiguous scansions, could constrain the persistent unlike-hiatus window. A securely inherited, independently short *ehi* or another short final *e.i* comparandum could decide whether absolute J can be general; a nonfinal in-domain pre-merger consonantal witness, independent intermediate weight, or secure short unlike hiatus outside the dental/nasal scope could distinguish mechanisms or extend scope. Independent Anglian quantity and securely dated free versus compound witnesses could shift the ‘hay’ preference. Access to the full de Chene dissertation/article, Opalińska’s originals, Jordan 120–123 and Ross–Stanley Book II remains useful, but a citation's availability is not itself new historical evidence.

## Implications for the paused article

| Witness | What this cycle does and does not add |
|---|---|
| Vespasian ⟨heg⟩ | A short mid vowel with consonantal offglide remains a conservative direct history under the stated assumptions. The assay supplies a regular path, not a reading of quantity, syllabification or stem ancestry. |
| Aldred ⟨heg/heig⟩ | The verified variants are compatible with different encodings of the palatal or nucleus. They do not independently decide that distinction or length. Owun’s ⟨heg⟩ is a separate Rushworth comparison, not another Aldred token. |
| Farman ⟨hóeg⟩ | The accent evidence favours length locally. It cannot be imposed on Vespasian, and the wider experiment does not decide between a later direct length/retention history and transfer from a protected stem. |
| West Saxon ⟨hig/hyg⟩ | The direct mid-vowel trials do not derive this high vocalism. Under early nasal apocope it is positive evidence for considering protected vocalism, not a quantity observation or identification of a particular analogical operation. The frozen full-input outputs are not proof of stem transfer. |
| Kentish ⟨hei⟩ in *hei weg* | First-member evidence, not an absolute simplex or a vowel-length measurement. The separate [Canterbury dossier](../CANTERBURY_CHARTER_PROPOSAL.md) retains the probable same-hand ⟨heg⟩ before *f* versus ⟨hei⟩ before *w* comparison and the independent topographical/semantic alternatives. Neither the palaeography nor this experimental run selects an inherited direct stem. |

For **CAPR**, the strongest conclusion is the independent order constraint on heavy resolution, with separate follow-up audits rather than adoption of a complete stack. For the **article**, the better case is for explicitly distinguishing quantity, shape and ancestry; for giving the ‘flock’ and ‘strew’ discriminators their qualified force; and for leaving edge gliding an economical conditional explanation rather than a prediction independently established by the wider phonology. This document proposes no article edits and leaves the Canterbury proposal unchanged.
