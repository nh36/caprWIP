# R6: Early back-vowel holdouts

## Identity and question

Research-only, `research/hay-article`, predecessor `e5e8b7ad`. The frozen substrate and common placements are those of R1. Can the proposed early short vowel + *u* operation explain ‘three’ and the labial-retaining paradigms without changing the historical cause that supplies each input? The diagnoses below distinguish nuclear formation, realization, consonant loss, and morphological renewal.

## Current state and source evidence

- **‘Three’.** Hogg (1992/2011: 58 §§3.8, 3.19(3), 64–65 §3.34), Campbell (1959: 47 §120.3(c)), and Ringe & Taylor (2014: 175) connect the neuter direct form with contraction following the development of the ending, not *w*-loss: PGmc *þrijō > þriu > þrīo*. Compare Gothic *þrija*, Old Norse *þrjú*, Old Saxon *thriu*, and OHG *driu*. The local input *þri.u* isolates the proposed intermediate, not a reconstruction that the full parser is known to admit. [Bosworth–Toller](https://bosworthtoller.com/32044) records both normalized *þrīo* and *þrēo* and Northumbrian variants; *þreo* in the common receipt is not a failure to print an attested letter sequence, but its **short** `*eo` and levelled first component fail to instantiate Hogg's chain.
- **‘Claw’.** Hogg (1992/2011: 58 §3.19(1), 78 §5.10) gives distinct early direct and protected histories. Campbell (1959: §598.2) and Hogg & Fulk (2011: 118–119 §3.94) separate inherited nominative *clēa*, retained-*w* *clawe* in the accusative and other obliques, backformed nominative *clawu*, and extensions of *clēa* to originally protected cells. The independently attested donor *clawa* also supports restoring *w* (Ringe & Taylor 2014: 150, 173), not undoing the old sound change. Hogg & Fulk cite *clawu* at Ælfric, *Grammar* 55.11; late WS *clēa* nominative plural and *clēum* dative plural in *Alexander* 468, 473; Mercian *clēa* accusative plural at Vespasian Ps 68:32 (Hogg & Fulk's numbering 68.31; Sweet 1885 glossary p. 463). *Clēa* also appears as a levelled dative singular in Charm 12.7. *Clēam* is reported with explicit editorial qualification by Hogg & Fulk (119 n. 5); *clam* in *Phoenix* 276 is not synonymous with a demonstrated regular *clēam* path. The early *clauuo* of Épinal/Erfurt 29 and Corpus 211 glosses *arpago* ‘hook’, so the early spelling supports the *w*-bearing shape but is not by itself a complete noun paradigm or an unambiguous anatomical use. The *clawe : scalpo* gloss is a verb, not the noun accusative.
- **‘Knee’ and ‘tree’.** Ringe & Taylor (2014: 61, 174), Campbell (1959: §§120.3, 146, 279, 584), and Hogg & Fulk (2011: 89–91 §§3.45–46) distinguish the final-labial vocalization of the endingless singular from loss before raised *u* in the direct neuter plural. Recovered *w* in a later direct cell is supported by available inflected forms. In the obliques, *cnewa* and *trewum* supply independent evidence of labials, not of inherited long *ēo*. Equally, written *cneowum/treowum* need not signal a regularly broken short diphthong. Hogg & Fulk (90 n. 2) explicitly allow *cnewum* followed by analogical *cnēowum*, rather than short breaking before *w*. The same output spelling can therefore reflect different stem histories.
- **Independent nuclear comparison.** Hogg (99–101 §§5.41–44) derives *io, eo, æw* (the last written ⟨ea⟩) from the respective earlier nuclear *iu, eu, æu*, noting lowered second elements and later mergers vary by dialect. His etymological-form interpretation is not a universal spelling prescription. The common early incorporation still has two distinct sources—intervocalic loss before *u* and emergence of *u* by final labial vocalization or raising—and neither implies the later loss before weak *i* (Ringe & Taylor 2014: 258–261).

## Diagnosis

The private receipt holds the early substrate constant, splits insertion of atomic *iu* from realization of that token, tests plain and acute abstract controls, and displaces the shared *aw* proxy only in named counterfactuals. It includes local retained, contracted and remodelled cells even when a spelling happens to converge. No broader applicability or chronology is inferred from a changed FST output.

## Historical analysis and verdict

**RETAIN** early *a.u/e.u* incorporation as a productive and economical interpretation of the common direct forms, alongside the equivalent earlier nuclear starting point. **SPLIT** *i.u* incorporation from long *io* realization in the historical comparison: the holdout tests both, and the realization is independently motivated beyond this lexical item. Do not rank later front-hiatus mechanisms from this back-vowel result. **DEFER** generalized retraction, the historical treatment of protected *ew*, the closed-*um* scope, and any production realization change, pending independent controls and dialect-specific evidence. Donors for renewal are independently attested; the occurrence of some renewal does not imply it in every protected cell.

Ordering claims here are limited to feeding: raised *u* and deletion/vocalization in the applicable histories must supply the material for incorporation, and incorporation supplies the nuclear input for later realization. The local experiments illustrate these edges but do not independently date the relevant operations by reference to *h*-loss or the late weak-*i* series. No registry verdict or propagation applies.

**Propagation and residue.** Only this research directory changes. No frozen capture, benchmark identity, production target, or PDF is touched. A production audit of the long-*aw* proxy, a dialect-sensitive realization audit, and the status of short breaking before retained *w* are possible separate inquiries; the present comparisons do not authorize any of them.
