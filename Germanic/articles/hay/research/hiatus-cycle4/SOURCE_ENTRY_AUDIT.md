## Diagnosis

The targeted comparison is the immutable SC047 frozen cascade and the cycle-three common substrate, each in canonical, A and B2 order. The [replacement receipt](source-entry-traces.json.gz) records pre/post anchors, all changes, complete output and per-build provenance. These are stage entries, not corpus selections or a new general firing census; there is no rule edit or new claim of corpus-wide scope.

- **‘Pillow’:** Ringe & Taylor p. 260 print Latin *pulvīnus* and reconstruct *pulwīn > pylwī > pylī*. The original local `*p*y*l*w*i` is a short-quantity proxy; both frozen and common rules delete *w* from the corrected long-vowel `*p*y*l*w*ī*. The old short probe and the corrected long probe converge at *pyle*, but only the latter separates labial loss, later shortening and lowering. The success is about a postconsonantal position and a fully unstressed high vowel, not hiatus. The separate earlier full-entry admission failure is not reversed by a successful late injection.
- **‘Prepare’:** The publication distinguishes West Saxon *ġierwidǣ* and Mercian *ġerwidǣ* (pp. 258, 260); both corrected entries lose *w*, and produce *ġierede* and *ġerede* respectively. The Mercian local entry has no prefix. The printed infinitive supplies the protected comparison. The valid chronology argument concerns creation of a light syllable before general syncope, not contraction; neither the original split `*i*e` encoding nor pre-lowering the ending is necessary for the stage-local loss.
- **‘Betray’:** The long source tail changes the frozen outcome: the lowered proxy gives *lēide*, whereas *lēwidǣ* gives *lēde* without the common-substrate absorption component. The common substrate gives *lēde* with either input. The output no longer demonstrates a need for that new component. It does not choose between syncope and contraction, as the root was already long (pp. 259, 318).
- **‘Strew’:** Pre-loss *stræwidǣ* produces uncontracted *stræide*; changing just the tail of the old *e*-root proxy does not force contraction. The corrected input retains hiatus but exposes a gap between frozen *w*-loss and the source's *e*-quality at that stage. This is not a new historical raising proposal or a failure of hiatus retention. The written and metrical comparisons retain their earlier lexical and transmission qualifications.
- **‘Higher’:** Both quantities for the final vowel give laryngeal loss. The common substrate absorbs *i* after long *ē* in either test, but with the printed long tail leaves *hērā*, not *hēra*. This is a separate weak-ending realization gap, not evidence for short-root *e.i* before *r*. The strong ‘nearest’ comparison likewise has an independently long root.

## Q2b — Early back vowels and additional laryngeal-loss entries

The ledger now also covers Ringe & Taylor pp. 150, 173–175, 314–317 and Hogg (2011) pp. 58, 78, 99 (held edition SHA-256 `f8e6d35e5ff1c6ca3a4c26f425a8fb38462d7a96a5255d4f16f4f9337b28a773`; sheets 71, 91, 112). The new [receipt](back-h-entry-traces.json.gz) has the same frozen and common comparators. It compares narrowly scoped stage entries, not new rule proposals. Its command is the Q2a command with `--back-and-h` added after `source_entry_check`; the Q2a receipt remains bound to the earlier runner at `8f884b3f`.

- **‘Claw’ is a disagreement between authorities, not a reason to discard the short experiment.** Hogg gives short *a* in the direct form and the protected accusative. Ringe & Taylor print long *ā* in both: direct *klāwu > klāu > clēa*, protected *klāwā > clāwe*. The short direct entry gives *clēa* in the common substrate. The archived long control already gave *clāw*; replay verifies that it reaches high-vowel loss with *w* retained. Injecting *klā.u* after loss yields *clā*, so neither omitting the long-vowel loss nor supplying it alone implements the Ringe–Taylor path. This is a genuine gap for the long reconstruction. Matching Hogg's output is not grounds for preferring his quantity. The protected short retraction experiment cannot stand in for the long protected history.
- **‘See’ retains the historical regional contrast, but the result is not an exact executable demonstration for both dialects.** The printed Mercian input after smoothing is *sehæ*. In the stage-local palatal-`ç` encoding, both the lowered *e* and printed *æ* tails permit loss. Only the lowered probe reaches *sē*; the corrected vowel input leaves *seæ*. The southern *seohæ* gives *sēo* in the common stack. This does not undo the source argument for Anglian smoothing, or the independent full-entry medial *hw* result in the older receipt. It identifies the missing exact-input Anglian contraction.
- **‘Clay’ and ‘shoe’ expose two other reasons for mismatches.** For ‘clay’, *þōhæ* reaches the attested uncontracted stage *þōæ* but not *þō*; the earlier *e*-tail proxy does. This preserves the written evidence for loss before contraction but withdraws a claim that the exact source sequence was already modeled. The long back-vowel ‘shoe’ result *scōs* on a stage-local unpalatalized input tests Ringe & Taylor's nominative plural; the separate *sċēos* probe has a broken nucleus and is not their example. The local consonant spelling is not evidence against the source's printed *sċōs*.
- **‘Strike’ does not require adding a final *i* to the immediate source stage.** The page gives the full earlier *slahiþi*, but prints final-less *slehiþ* for the Anglian stage. The later full-cell injection is a deliberately different test. It gives *sleiþi* beside *sleiþ* on the common path before a tested D/J/N resolution; it is not a correction to that matched mechanism experiment.

**REFORMULATE the historical coverage of the early and late arms; RETAIN the observations.** The older short ‘claw’ result remains valid for Hogg; the long alternative and the exact Anglian ‘see’ and ‘clay’ inputs reveal gaps, not dating evidence. No new chronology or analogical history is inferred. No production or corpus source is changed. Secondary-palatal, dialectal and witness-level comparisons still require their own source checks.

## Q2c — Palatals and the meaning of printed diacritics

Direct images checked: Campbell §§243–245, 267 (sheets 123, 132); Hogg 1979:105 (17); Ringe & Taylor 235, 286 (250, 301); Jones 19–20, 55, 149–150 (33–34, 69, 163–164); Sweet 207, 263, 335 (221, 277, 349); Dahl 96–97 (118–119). One legacy holdout cites “Jones 1995” for ‘maiden’: the identified comparison is Jones **1989**, p. 55. The frozen table is preserved.

- ‘Key’ still supplies the independent long-vowel retention control. Hogg's *kaijai* is a protected dative; Ringe & Taylor's *kāju* is a direct cell with the earlier diphthong already monophthongized and a final *u* still present. Neither is an input containing short *e.i*. Hogg's inherited [j] is not Jones's fricative [ǰ]. The source forms and the archived inputs agree, so no rerun is needed.
- Jones's comparison of short [heǰ]/[heiǰ] on p. 149 and long [heeǰ]/[hiiǰ] in the table on p. 150 is real. His p. 55 associates the relevant orthographic alternants with Northumbrian tradition. The short form is a published analysis with a **fricative already present**, not an independent analysis of a newly consonantalized vowel or a measurement of Vespasian quantity. Sweet prints plain *heg* in the accusative at Ps 103:14, and plain *weg* for both nominative and accusative at Ps 17:31, 33. That makes ‘way’ a good same-tradition spelling comparison with independently reconstructed short quantity, not proof that the convention could only render a short vowel.
- Campbell's source assigns preconsonantal *ġ*-loss to specific following consonants; Jones's ‘wagon’ comparison instead uses velar [ɣ] and the ‘ask’ series includes different grammatical cells. Their normalized long loss outcomes do not license a general law targeting absolute-final inherited *j* or syllabic *i*. In ‘clay’, the *clæg* cited by Jones is not the independent *þō* item. Keeping these records separately indexed prevents accidental merging by English gloss.

**RETAIN** the distinct inherited and secondary palatal controls. **REFORMULATE** the role of the short Jones analysis: it makes short mid vocalism a scholarly precedent, but is not direct evidence for its date, source or consonant production in Vespasian. No experimental trace was changed; the new rows correct source interpretation only.
