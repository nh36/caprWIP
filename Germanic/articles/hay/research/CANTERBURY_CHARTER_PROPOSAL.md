# Canterbury charters: proposed revision after Brooks & Kelly

**Status: proposed, not implemented.** Branch `research/hay-article`.
Compare the Cycle 22 publication (`bf9194f3`) and the experimental
closeout (`b83edf49`). Follow the [revision protocol](../REVISION_PROTOCOL.md)
and [worked charter example](../HEIWEG_DEMONSTRATION.md).

**Problem and verdict.** Cycle 22 had the earlier edition reading,
the Lowe-based hand/date evidence and a general meadow-road comparison,
but not the modern edition's treatment of the three charters. Would
that treatment establish the highway etymology, or make the spelling
a diagnostic reflex of inherited ‘hay’? **Retain** the qualified
‘hay-way’ preference; **reformulate** its evidential basis and the
documentary and topographical account; **defer** a phonological or
ancestral assignment. The new comparison is more local than S 445,
but its lexical interpretation is not wholly independent. The
topography makes the highway rival stronger, not the identification
of *hēah-* more secure.

### Same hand, different environments

The pertinent written environments are *heg-forde* before **f**
and *hei-weg* before **w** (printed ⟨ƿ⟩). The shared hand, probable
for the S 328 insertion and explicit for the S 1196 bounds, reduces
one source of spelling variation. If both first elements mean
‘hay’, it places ⟨heg⟩ and ⟨hei⟩ in one local scribal repertoire.
It is not a minimal pair: the following consonant, lexicalised name,
document and textual layer all differ. Different treatment of a
palatal before *f* and *w*, purely graphic variation, or different
first lexemes are all open. There is no matched series here to
choose among them or to recover quantity, syllabification, or the
phonetic value of either spelling. Keep S 1195 *dei* as a comparison
for the availability of ⟨ei⟩ with a palatal, not a quantity calibration
or a reading key. S 296 shows why *hege* must not be assigned to
‘hay’ from letters or meadow context alone; conversely it supplies
no local ⟨hei-⟩ enclosure parallel.

### Road, transaction and textual layers

- **A plausible major road, not a lexical gloss (pp. 751–752).**
  The editors connect Scufeling ford with Shelford Farm and a Stour
  crossing. On that reconstruction the eastern bound follows the
  Roman road now represented by the A28 toward Sturry and Fordwich.
  They compare the public street on the eastern side of their
  no. 54 (823) and put the dung-site on the king's street beside
  the same Roman route. The Stour and road run obliquely to the
  compass bounds; roughly parallel boundaries need not enclose the
  same meadows. This makes the Cycle 22 conditional identification
  of the two royal-road expressions more concrete and gives the
  ‘highway’ rival a genuine local basis. A major road could also
  carry hay; the borough water meadows are listed as appurtenances,
  not proof that this road was named for hay or primarily served
  those meadows. Downgrade the meadow context to compatibility.
  Neither the road's importance nor the modern phrase ‘king's
  highway’ supplies the still-missing local *hēah-* > ⟨hei-⟩
  comparison before *w*.
- **Document date is not the date of every layer (pp. 747, 749–750).**
  The heading gives 859 with a possible earlier range (c. 853 × 858);
  the written indiction fits 859, but Æthelwulf died in 858 and
  Æthelbald's designation as the king's son sits awkwardly with his
  accession in 855/6. The editors consider a witness list suitable
  to 853 × 855 and propose, tentatively, reuse of an earlier royal
  landbook for the private sale, which could itself be from 859.
  The witness list is **the same hand** in smaller script with a
  thinner nib; a separate writing stage and recopying are possible,
  not demonstrated. This clarifies, rather than removes, the
  Lowe/Cooper caveat. Do not assign either a precise date to the
  ⟨hei⟩ spelling or the bounds to the putative earlier model.
  The attribution also does not make the personal name *Deimund*
  a matched control for the compound.
- **The Latin access clause is secondary (pp. 748–749, 752).**
  After the anathema, another hand in darker ink added *communi uia*;
  that reviser also supplied a missing word earlier. The editors
  read the clause as shared access through one gate, plausibly on
  the track linking the Roman street and the ford and serving the
  divided estate. The track is a topographical hypothesis, but the
  second-hand attribution is the editors' explicit palaeographical
  judgment. Neither the clause's position nor its apparently shared
  access makes it a Latin gloss for the English eastern bound, or
  evidence for ‘high’ or ‘hay’. Cooper's exclusion remains a
  methodological judgment, not a lexical argument by default.

**Experimental comparison and invariants.** The second-cycle
[adjudication](hiatus-cycle2/ADJUDICATION.md), especially answers
17 and 21–24, allows retained hiatus, a glide, or a complex
nucleus under different experimental assumptions; some arms also
reach long results, with or without a palatal. First-member position
does not identify the free-word edge or the inherited source of a
compound stem. The new charter evidence supports running distinct
boundary comparisons in principle, not choosing a front-hiatus
mechanism, chronology edge, or chronology of *hay* and *bed*. The
conditional short /hej/ preference for early Vespasian ⟨heg⟩ is not a
reading of Kentish ⟨hei⟩; Farman's local length evidence and the
West Saxon high-vowel argument are untouched. Protected material
remains possible, with no new positive evidence of transfer here.
No SC is reassigned: executable identifier, new traces and firing
census, production propagation and regression changes are not
applicable. Keep the manuscript, all tables and frozen derivations
and targets, production models, publication PDF and Cycle 23 pause
unchanged. If review later authorizes revision, update the affected
argument, plan and apparatus together before implementing the
scoped prose increments and publication checks.
