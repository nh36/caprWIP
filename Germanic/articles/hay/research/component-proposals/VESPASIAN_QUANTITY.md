# C4 — Vespasian ⟨eg⟩ does not distinguish inherited quantities

## Result

Vespasian ⟨leg⟩ ‘flame’ supplies the independently long mid-vowel
comparison missing from the earlier sample. Three direct singular
tokens in one lexeme are confirmed on Sweet's edition page images:
Ps 28:7 ⟨leg⟩ : *flammam* (accusative); 82:15 and 105:18 ⟨leg⟩ :
*flamma* (nominative). See pp. 222, 307 and 341, not their running
headers (which can refer to the next psalm). None prints a length
mark or an additional vowel letter. Compare direct short ⟨weg⟩
‘way’ and ⟨deg⟩ ‘day’ from the same gloss. This is positive evidence
that **the spelling ⟨eg⟩ alone does not distinguish short from long
mid vowels in a word-final palatal environment**. It is not positive
evidence that the vowel of ⟨heg⟩ was long.

## Why the control is independent

Ringe & Taylor (2014: 209, 246, 287) give PNWGmc *laugiz* > *lēaġi*
> Mercian and Northumbrian *lēġ*, alongside West Saxon *līeġ*.
Old Norse *leygr*, with its own mutated diphthong, is a comparative
control, not an inference from Vespasian ⟨e⟩ or an editor's macron.
Breaking of syllabic *e.i*, desyllabifying *i*, and analogy with
protected ‘hay’ are not premises. Historical quantity comes from
the inherited diphthong and the Anglian reflex of its mutation, not
from counting letters.

The final palatal is secondary, from dorsal *g* palatalized in the
environment of the former front ending. That is also the origin of
the palatal in ‘way’, whose short root has independent comparative
support (Gothic *wigs*). The phonetic strength of the comparison
must not be overstated: Ringe & Taylor (203–204, 280, 296) separate
[ʝ] from original *j* and do not date their merger to this gloss
from the graph alone. Thus the panel matches vowel letter,
historical quantity contrast, word position and reconstructed
palatal class; it does not match *demonstrated* synchronically
identical approximants. ⟨geres⟩ ‘year’ is an inherited-*j* control
(Ringe & Taylor 150), but with initial *j*, not a final match.

## Scope, comparisons and exclusions

The [versioned supplement](vespasian-quantity-v1.tsv) records ten
new positive cells: three ‘flame’, four ‘sound’, one ‘year’ and two
‘say’. It separately records a two-token length-marking control.
It supplements rather than replaces the
[frozen 16-token Vespasian sample](../REGULAR_SURVIVAL.md#r3-witness-specific-orthographic-controls):
12 short controls (two families) and four high-vowel long-source
controls (three lexical selections, not three unrelated roots).
Those include direct and inflected forms. ‘Flame’ adds a new
matched long family; ‘sound’ adds final, dative and genitive
examples with a historically different **rounded** long vowel
(Ringe & Taylor 209, *swōgiz* > *swœ̄ġi* > Mercian *swœ̄ġ*).
Do not count ⟨oe⟩ as two syllables or use this as a second ⟨eg⟩
match. The preconsonantal ⟨segde/segdon⟩ controls spelling, not
secure preservation of [j] before /d/ (Ringe & Taylor 212, 280).
A matched long-mid preconsonantal token and a matched final
inherited-*j* token are not established by this investigation.

The added screen covers Sweet 188–420 (PDF sheets 202–434):
word-bounded ⟨leg, lege, leges, leger, swoeg-, sweg-, segde,
segdon, geres⟩ in the PDF text layer, with Sweet's glossary
cross-check for ‘flame’, ‘sound’ and ‘year’. Latin *lege/leges*,
forms of ‘lay’ and derived ‘inflame’ or ‘lightning’ are not simplex
‘flame’ hits. The three simplex ‘flame’ and four ‘sound’ cells
are the aligned positive hits in this screen; ‘year’ (genitive
only) and ‘say’ are comparison selections, not lemma counts.
OCR silence is not a claim of exhaustive recovery. The control
⟨god⟩ ‘God’ and ⟨god⟩ ‘good’ in Ps 13:1 matches even the verse:
the inherited contrast *gudą* versus *gōdaz* yields respectively
short *o* and long *ō*, both unmarked in the edition. It tests
non-obligatory marking, not the palatal environment.

The primary witness is Cotton MS Vespasian A I. Editions and
original image are distinct verification tiers: image collation
here is of Sweet's printed pages, not manuscript leaves. The
[ARCA record](https://arca.irht.cnrs.fr/ark:/63955/md021c18dr7x)
points to holdings; target leaves were not accessible through
the published links consulted. The Kuhn (1967) facsimile is
identified, not newly collated. Do not infer the total absence
of a manuscript accent from the edition or pool these copies
with Farman's later, locally informative acute practice.

## Editorial consequence

Replace the old *sample-level absence* of a long comparator in
the prospective argument, not in the protected article or
earlier frozen panel. Ordinary Vespasian ⟨heg⟩ supports a mid
vowel plus a written palatal in the direct singular, and its
shape is compatible with regular short survival. The same
notation also represents independently long *lēġ*. No positive
quantity preference follows from the present Vespasian evidence;
the choice must rest on competing historical derivations and
independently calibrated evidence. This finding neither transfers
Farman's length evidence backwards nor selects the ancestry of a
potentially long vowel. Manuscript collation could refine the
statement about marks; it is not a substitute for reconstructing
the consonant and the lexical history.
