# Scholarly priority — component and historical review supplement

This supplements, rather than rewrites, the
[fourth-cycle priority register](../hiatus-cycle4/SCHOLARLY_PRIORITY.md).
Priority here means priority among consulted or explicitly
attributed sources, not an exhaustive first-discovery claim.
The [source-entry audit](../hiatus-cycle4/SOURCE_ENTRY_AUDIT.md)
continues to govern input quantities.

| Work | Established precedent or evidence | This assignment's contribution and limit |
|---|---|---|
| [SC083](SC083.md) | Ringe & Taylor 228 distinguishes shortening, unrounding and raising of an earlier weak sequence; Campbell §74 n. 5 independently warns against assuming prefixes are always unstressed. | **Implementation correction:** preserve a serialized root head across one pretonic syllable. No discovery of a new historical reduction or claim that the same serialization covers arbitrary prosody. |
| [SC085](SC085_HANDOFF.md) | Ringe & Taylor 258–261 distinguishes loss environments and later syncope. There is no phonological precedent needed for the name or lifetime of an invisible computational marker. | **Equivalent implementation refactor** at the existing boundary, proved over the relation and replayed over three frozen captures. A marker surviving into another consumer would be a different claim. |
| [Medial labiovelars](MEDIAL_HW.md) | Ringe & Taylor 49, 178–179, 312–316 and 344 separate initial, medial, singleton, geminate and voiced environments; the filter and strainer citations add cell-specific evidence. | **Newly applied comparison:** split singleton and geminate ablations, and retain later contraction and *io/eo* as distinct problems. An executable reduction is not a date or a proposed universal deletion. |
| [SC086](SC086.md) | Campbell §§235–238; Ringe & Taylor 314–318 already distinguish hiatal loss, contraction, long-plus-short interaction and contraction following dorsal loss. | **Formalizations and new comparisons:** isolate five candidate environments. The broken-front contrasts are supported locally; wide non-firing and fringe retention do not establish conservation in other unattested or untested environments. |
| [Labial stems](LABIAL_STEMS.md) | Hogg §§3.19, 5.10 and Ringe & Taylor 150, 173–175 disagree on ‘claw’; Campbell §598.2 and Hogg & Fulk §§3.45–46, 3.94 explain cell transfer and retention. Luick's metrical comparison and Kroonen's different ablaut analysis do not measure ancestral English quantity. | **Newly applied comparison:** independent short and long branches, separating labial loss from realization and separating true direct singulars from plural or renewed attestations. No discovery of labial loss or general long-vowel feeding. |
| [Early nuclei](EARLY_NUCLEI.md) and [representation](STAGE_ENTRY_REPRESENTATION.md) | Ringe & Taylor 173–175 and Hogg §§3.19, 5.155–160 distinguish early sources of *iu* from later regional lowering or merger. | **Implementation diagnosis and formalization:** an atomic collapse can produce a correct late spelling for the wrong early state. Local typed routes and the consumer census do not establish a general chronology or make the existing model stage-aware. |
| [Final desyllabification](FINAL_DESYLLABIFICATION.md) and [chronology](FINAL_HIATUS_CHRONOLOGY.md) | Campbell §410 offers consonantalization before a vowel; Hogg §§5.131–137 offers nucleus formation, including final cases; Ringe & Taylor 258–261, 313–318 differentiates labial and dorsal loss. | **Newly applied comparison; unestablished generalization:** no independently established absolute-final short *e.i > ej* or date of resolution between the two loss classes. Absolute-final position could matter, but its effect must be established independently. |
| [River](RIVER_ANCESTRY.md) and [Vespasian](VESPASIAN_QUANTITY.md) | Hogg & Fulk §3.127 and the early oblique evidence support inherited root-noun morphology. Sweet pp. 222, 307 and 341 print unmarked ⟨leg⟩ ‘flame’; Ringe & Taylor 209, 246, 287 and ON *leygr* supply the long comparison independently of the spelling. | **Newly applied adverse controls:** genuine inherited ‘river’ obliques are preferable to an otherwise unnecessary early renewal, and unmarked ⟨eg⟩ is compatible with length. Neither control proves long ‘hay’, and three ‘flame’ tokens do not make three lexical histories. |
| [Comparative ‘hay’ history](HAY_COMPARATIVE_HISTORY.md) and [editorial proposal](HAY_EDITORIAL_PROPOSAL_V2.md) | Campbell §579.4 supplies the direct *hewi* premise; Dahl 96–101 starts with a diphthong and consonantal palatal; Jones 149 starts with a palatal consonant, not final syllabic *i*. Ringe & Taylor 246 gives the regional outcomes. | **Conditional historical synthesis**, already proposed in CAPR before this assignment: early apocope yields a regular short-hiatus direct route; final gliding and identification with the surviving written Anglian form are not established. The Canterbury dossier revises interpretation, not this proof burden. |
