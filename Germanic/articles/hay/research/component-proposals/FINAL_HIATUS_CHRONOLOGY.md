# C3 — A window for final hiatus resolution, not an ordering result

## What can be ordered independently

Ringe & Taylor (2014: 258–261) place late medial *w*-loss before
unstressed *i* after mutation but before general syncope. Their
strongest diagnostic is ‘prepare’, not ‘hay’. In the infinitive
West Saxon *ġierwan*, Mercian *ġerwan*, earlier *i* in *-CijV-*
had already syncopated before the *w*-loss could apply. In the
finite and past cells, *garwiþi* and *garwidē* retain the vowel
that conditions loss. Removing *w* makes the preceding syllable
light, bleeding subsequent syncope: compare Mercian *ġeġereð*
and *ġeġerede*. Old Saxon *gerwian* and *gerwida* and OHG
*garewen* and *garota* help control the morphological comparison.
The timing of the move of ‘ewe’ out of the *i*-stems supports the
lower bound after i-mutation on the authors' analysis (259–260).

Ordinary intervocalic *h*-loss is later than general syncope on
their reconstruction (260, 313–318). Their independent tests
include preservation where syncope had already made the dorsal
preconsonantal (the West Saxon ‘cover’ contrast, 317) and Anglian
monophthongization feeding loss with different subsequent
outcomes before front and back weak vowels (316). The latter
establishes an additional bound, not a calendar date for loss in
every dialect. These are historical inferences with independent
comparative premises, not consequences of the stage names.

The resulting interval is real on this chronology: late *w*-loss
may supply short *he.i* before ordinary *h*-loss supplies short
*e.i* from mutated *ehi* ‘river’. If a general **word-final**
consonantalization of short unstressed *i* operated inside the
interval and ceased before *h*-loss, the distinct outcomes would
require no permanent memory of the lost consonant. But the
independently motivated inequalities date the **creation** of
hiatus, not its final resolution.

## Tests of the intervening event

| Comparison | Positive evidence | What it does not order |
|---|---|---|
| ‘Strew’, Pheifer (1974: 899, 1910); Campbell (1962: §237.1b); Ringe & Taylor (2014: 259, 318) | Early Mercian ⟨streidæ/streide⟩ supports retention after *w*-loss; the meter at *Beowulf* 2436 supports two syllables on their reading. The claims rest on different evidence, not identical copies, but the same morphological comparison. | The vowel pair is nonfinal. The poem's manuscript date does not date the phonological event, and contractions and archaisms need not be synchronous. | Vespasian and regional geography; [C4](VESPASIAN_QUANTITY.md) | The gloss already has direct ⟨heg⟩; in the same witness short *weg* and long *lēġ* are both written ⟨eg⟩. Ordinary Anglian and high West Saxon forms cannot simply be treated as one unchanged surface. | A mid-vowel spelling is not evidence that a short syllabic *i* had become /j/ before ordinary *h*-loss. Farman's later acute cannot supply the missing early terminus. |

Ringe & Taylor (318) explicitly entertain a single **later** episode
of unlike contraction, with persistence of *w*-loss hiatus into the
period after *h*-loss; they also allow earlier resolution of identical
sequences. The strong same-origin negative ‘strew’ is therefore
already accommodated by a published account. Separating final
desyllabification from nonfinal contraction is an independently
intelligible prosodic hypothesis, but nobody's evidence cited here
requires the final operation to fall in the interval.

## Verdict and discriminators

**RETAIN** the distinct chronology of *w*-loss and *h*-loss;
**DEFER** the edge placing final consonantalization between them.
There is an intelligible way for short direct ‘hay’ and inherited
contracted ‘river’ to coexist without origin-sensitive phonology,
but no independent discriminator currently puts final short unlike
hiatus on the early side. The key evidence would be a second
securely short, final pair whose consonantal reflex predates
ordinary *h*-loss; or a genuinely attested short-ending
interaction in a structurally matched history that distinguishes
resolution before and after the late loss. A securely dated
orthographic or metrical distinction between a nuclear offglide
and a coda, with independent quantity calibration, would also
matter. Another compatible model ordering, or another later
⟨eg⟩ spelling, would not.
