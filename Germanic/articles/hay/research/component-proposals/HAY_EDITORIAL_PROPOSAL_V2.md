## Proposed argument

Start with the genuinely inherited direct versus protected
distinction, not the reading of a vowel letter. If the
continental singleton forms continue the direct stem, early
nasal loss independently predicts English *hawi*. Regular
fronting, i-mutation and late *w*-loss take that stem as far as
short *he.i* without any contribution from an inflected stem.
The early Vespasian direct singular is grammatically the right
place to look for this continuation.

Consonantal desyllabification would preserve both elements as
short *hej*, and known cases of desyllabification make the
operation historically motivated. But no exact independent
final short front comparator dates it, and prefer inheritance
of the ‘river’ oblique, where comparable final hiatus has a
different outcome. The rise of *w*-loss hiatus before
*h*-loss hiatus is independently supported; placing
consonantalization **between** them is an open hypothesis.
Neither long final *ī* in ‘flock’ nor nonfinal retained
‘strew’ fixes that placement.

The short form would fit ordinary Vespasian spelling.
Independent short ⟨weg⟩ and long ⟨leg⟩ ‘flame’ actually show
the same unmarked ⟨eg⟩ in comparable direct cells, so the
spelling supplies **comparability, not a length preference**.
The palatals of the controls are historically secondary and
the exact phonetic merger date is not established by this
comparison. Treat Farman's marked forms as later local
evidence tending toward length. Explain explicitly why the
verses and later merger do not retrodate that quantity.

Early direct inheritance need not bring the protected stem
into Anglian, but protected *hēaj-* independently predicts
long mid *ē* there and high *īe* in West Saxon. The positive
western high-vowel evidence therefore motivates protected
**type**, not a particular transfer date or inheritance
mechanism. Keep short nuclear incorporation, direct length
plus palatal, contraction plus palatal supply, protected
substitution, late protected inheritance and Dahl's separate
history distinct. The choice between them cannot be a count
of the distinct graphs they can print.

**Strongest presently publishable claim:** independently
supported comparative and phonological premises yield a
regular, untransferred direct stem through short hiatus; an
economical and historically defensible final resolution would
give the ordinary Anglian form without whole-stem substitution.
The crucial last development and early quantity remain
conditional. The Anglian form need not count as an analogical
cost for early apocope, but does not demonstrate that order or
explain independent English geminates.
