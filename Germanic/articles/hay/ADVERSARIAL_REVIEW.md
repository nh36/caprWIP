# Adversarial review of research cycle 1 (research cycle 2)

Method: each principal cycle-1 claim is attacked with the new evidence
of this cycle (verified Lindisfarne/Rushworth tokens, the full
reconstruction of Dahl's analysis, the comparative-controls study) and
assigned one of: **Confirmed / Confirmed only in a narrower sense /
Not established / Contradicted by additional evidence.**

## Claim A — "No pre-i-umlaut donor stem is identified"

**Assessment: the general absence of an overtly inflected form is
contradicted; the donor's early availability remains unestablished.**

- Three Paris Psalter dative singulars *hege* (PPs 101.4, 101.9, 102.14)
  are manuscript-verified in BnF lat. 8824, s. xi med. (CONCORDANCE.tsv
  33–35; `RESEARCH_MEMO_CYCLE3.md`). Their Latin parallels and syntax
  identify the noun ‘hay’. They establish an overtly inflected cell, not a
  pre-i-umlaut stem.
- The six gospel-gloss tokens examined in this earlier cycle (Li Mt 6:30,
  Mt 14:19, Jn 6:10; Ru1 Mt 6:30, Mt 14:19; Ru2 Jn 6:10) are direct-case
  forms without overt endings. Hogg's statement that inflected forms are
  absent from "my material" remains a statement about his own corpus.
- The wider surviving OE record has not been exhaustively searched. The
  bounded Lowe charter search found no diagnostic ninth-century Kentish
  occurrence; this does not restore the former general absence claim.
- The noun's mass-noun use helps explain why oblique forms are scarce.
  The late dative is compatible with an oblique paradigm, but does not
  establish the date or historical availability of its stem as an analogical
  donor. See Claim E for the separate comparison of assumptions.

## Claim B — "The long vowel has no independent evidence"

**Assessment: Confirmed only in a narrower sense.**

- Correct core (restated): no *decisive* evidence establishes /eː/ in
  the ninth-century direct singular. The bounded matched Farman
  calibration completed in cycle 5 makes his two printed acutes
  suggestive of his own practice only; it is not a population rate or a
  ninth-century calibration. Direct manuscript confirmation is
  unavailable online. Ritt (2000) concerns Lindisfarne, not Farman.
- Too categorical as originally worded, on two counts. (i) *New lead*:
  the two Rushworth1 tokens print acute accents (⟨hóeg⟩ ×2, Kemble–
  Hardwick 1858, pp. 55, 117, checked against three editions). The
  bounded matched sample now provides limited calibration of Farman's
  practice, but it is small and selected; the manuscript acutes remain
  unverified directly. They are suggestive witness-specific evidence,
  not decisive evidence for earlier quantity. (ii)
  *Reconstructive evidence is not zero evidence*: the long nucleus
  follows from independently established sound laws on **all three**
  live derivations (geminate resolution, levelling before umlaut,
  Dahl's diphthong) — every viable route yields a long nucleus. What is
  missing is evidence independent of *any* derivation, not evidence
  independent of the *geminate* derivation specifically. The cycle-1
  wording tied quantity to the geminate route alone; that was too
  narrow.

## Claim C — "West Saxon *hīeġ* requires the geminate-shaped stem"

**Assessment: Confirmed only in a narrower sense.**

- What survives: WS ⟨ie/i/y⟩ requires an ēa-type diphthongal nucleus
  feeding i-umlaut, and that nucleus is **unreachable from the
  consonantal-w direct forms \*hæwi/\*hewi** by any independently
  supported change (the A0/A1 negative findings stand; Hogg's hiatus
  resolution yields *hē*; resyllabification yields non-WS shapes).
- What falls: the equation of "diphthongal nucleus" with "historically
  geminated stem". Dahl's derivation (DAHL_REASSESSMENT.md) produces
  the same nucleus with gemination never applying, via the regular
  \*au > ēa change under a diphthongal parse of \*-awj-. Ringe–Taylor's
  own "reversible gemination" (p. 53) shows even the geminate route
  passes through \*auj — from which point all accounts are
  observationally identical in English.
- Revised claim: *WS vocalism excludes an ungeminated consonantal-w
  source for the direct cell; it does not discriminate geminated,
  levelled, or diphthongal-inherited origins of the ēa-type stem.*

## Claim D — "No regular phonological chain produces WS *hīeġ* from the inherited early-apocope direct form"

**Assessment: Confirmed, with its scope made precise.**

- As a claim about the early-apocope **consonantal-w** direct form
  (\*hawi > \*hæwi > \*hewi), it stands: the reachable regular endpoints
  remain *hē* (hiatus resolution, vocalism wrong for WS, final
  consonant lost) and *hei/hej* (A0/resyllabification, vocalism wrong
  for WS). Failure to derive the vocalism and failure to derive the
  final consonant are distinct failures, and the early-apocope form
  fails on both for WS.
- The precision now required: the claim excludes *that starting point*,
  not every non-geminate history. Dahl's starting point (\*hauja̯ with
  a diphthong) is a different inherited representation, and from it a
  regular chain to the WS **vocalism** does exist — though not to the
  final -ġ, which even Dahl takes from the paradigm. No account on the
  table derives the complete attested WS form without either gemination
  or one analogical step.

## Claim E — "The competing reconstructions are evidentially symmetrical"

**Assessment: Not established (as a claim of equal evidential standing);
what is established is the narrower attestation symmetry.**

- True component: the protected analogical donor (*hīeġes* etc.) and
  the inherited ungeminated direct form (\*hewi) are both unattested in
  the collated English record. As a statement about *attestation*,
  symmetry holds.
- But evidential standing involves more than attestation, and there the
  accounts differ:
  - *Comparative support*: the ungeminated direct cell is directly
    attested in Gothic (*hawi*) and arguably OHG (*hewi*); the
    reconstructed OE donor obliques have exact comparative and
    system-internal parallels (every scholar reconstructs them with the
    same vocalism). Slight edge: both well supported, differently.
  - *Sound-law support*: the geminate route to the attested WS form is
    regular throughout (B2); the early-apocope route requires levelling
    (A2) or fails (A0/A1). The levelling itself is typologically cheap
    (Hogg 1979: both directions attested in the masculine ja-stems;
    OHG *houwi* as a possible parallel act) but is an extra assumption.
  - *Coverage*: both A2 and B2 cover the attested WS + Anglian +
    (new) Northumbrian/Mercian material without remainder; Dahl covers
    it with one timing assumption plus segment-analogy; A0/A1 cover
    only the non-WS spellings.
  - *Assumption count*: B2 needs its chronology (late \*-ą loss) and
    nothing word-specific; A2 needs its chronology plus one
    word-specific levelling event dated before i-umlaut; Dahl needs a
    class-wide reparse plus segment-analogy plus the *hue* patch.
- Conclusion: the hypotheses share an attestation gap but are **not**
  evidentially symmetrical in assumptions or coverage; cycle 1's
  symmetry wording conflated the two. The corrected formulation
  belongs in DRAFT.md §3.2 (made in this cycle).
