# Adversarial review of research cycle 1 (research cycle 2)

> **Cycle 7 status:** This file preserves earlier adversarial conclusions;
> its categorical statements about Model A and a shared long-vowel
> prediction are not current. Eadwine's ⟨hieg⟩ readings are now
> manuscript-verified but remain dialectally and textually unassigned.
> Read `QUANTITY_EVIDENCE.md` and `RESEARCH_MEMO_CYCLE7.md` for the
> current, non-adjudicative status.

Method: each principal cycle-1 claim is attacked with the new evidence
of this cycle (verified Lindisfarne/Rushworth tokens, the full
reconstruction of Dahl's analysis, the comparative-controls study) and
assigned one of: **Confirmed / Confirmed only in a narrower sense /
Not established / Contradicted by additional evidence.**

## Claim A — "No pre-i-umlaut donor stem is identified"

**Assessment: the general absence of an overtly inflected form is
contradicted; the donor's early availability remains unestablished.**

- Three Paris Psalter dative singulars *hege* (PPs 101.4, 101.9, 102.14)
  are manuscript-verified in BnF lat. 8824, s. xi med. (CONCORDANCE.tsv
  33–35; `RESEARCH_MEMO_CYCLE3.md`). Their Latin parallels and syntax
  identify the noun ‘hay’. They establish an overtly inflected cell, not a
  pre-i-umlaut stem.
- The six gospel-gloss tokens examined in this earlier cycle (Li Mt 6:30,
  Mt 14:19, Jn 6:10; Ru1 Mt 6:30, Mt 14:19; Ru2 Jn 6:10) are direct-case
  forms without overt endings. Hogg's statement that inflected forms are
  absent from "my material" remains a statement about his own corpus.
- The wider surviving OE record has not been exhaustively searched. The
  bounded Lowe charter search found no diagnostic ninth-century Kentish
  occurrence; this does not restore the former general absence claim.
- The noun's mass-noun use helps explain why oblique forms are scarce.
  The late dative is compatible with an oblique paradigm, but does not
  establish the date or historical availability of its stem as an analogical
  donor. See Claim E for the separate comparison of assumptions.

## Claim B — "The long vowel has no independent evidence"

**Assessment: Confirmed only in a narrower sense.**

- Correct core (restated): no *decisive* evidence establishes /eː/ in
  the ninth-century direct singular. The bounded matched Farman
  calibration completed in cycle 5 makes his two printed acutes
  suggestive of his own practice only; it is not a population rate or a
  ninth-century calibration. Direct manuscript confirmation is
  unavailable online. Ritt (2000) concerns Lindisfarne, not Farman.
- Too categorical as originally worded, on two counts. (i) *New lead*:
  the two Rushworth1 tokens print acute accents (⟨hóeg⟩ ×2, Kemble–
  Hardwick 1858, pp. 55, 117, checked against three editions). The
  bounded matched sample now provides limited calibration of Farman's
  practice, but it is small and selected; the manuscript acutes remain
  unverified directly. They are suggestive witness-specific evidence,
  not decisive evidence for earlier quantity. (ii)
  *Reconstructive evidence is not zero evidence*, but the earlier claim
  that every viable route yields a long nucleus is withdrawn. B2, A2,
  and Dahl's diphthongal parse each make a long-vowel prediction under
  their own assumptions; the regular direct outcome under A0/A1 remains
  unresolved. Because the Eadwine readings are not assigned to a dialect
  and the applicability of West Saxon ⟨ie⟩ developments is open, they do
  not categorically exclude Model A.

## Claim C — "West Saxon *hīeġ* requires the geminate-shaped stem"

**Current status: conditional; the former categorical exclusion of Model
A is superseded.**

- Conditional point: if a particular ⟨hieg⟩ witness is established as
  West Saxon and its ⟨ie⟩ is shown to result from a specific historical
  source, that source may constrain the input. The two Eadwine readings
  are now manuscript-verified, but neither their dialect nor the
  applicable source is established. The earlier conclusion that A0/A1
  categorically fails against the attested record does not follow.
- What falls: the equation of "diphthongal nucleus" with "historically
  geminated stem". Dahl's derivation (DAHL_REASSESSMENT.md) produces
  the same nucleus with gemination never applying, via the regular
  \*au > ēa change under a diphthongal parse of \*-awj-. Ringe–Taylor's
  own "reversible gemination" (p. 53) shows even the geminate route
  passes through \*auj — from which point all accounts are
  observationally identical in English.
- Revised claim: the relationship between ⟨hieg⟩ and West Saxon
  vocalism, and the regular direct development under Model A, remain
  questions for the next adjudication. This source-preparation cycle
  does not choose among them.

## Claim D — "No regular phonological chain produces WS *hīeġ* from the inherited early-apocope direct form"

**Current status: historical claim, conditional and not adjudicated.**

- The previous A0/A1 negative result assumed that the relevant
  ⟨hieg⟩ was West Saxon and that the cited hiatus and resyllabification
  analyses exhaust the possible developments. Neither assumption is
  established by the current word attestations. Hogg and Campbell
  document multiple sources of ⟨ie⟩ and distinct dialectal conditions;
  whether any applies here requires a separate historical argument.
- The claim that Model A fails for the actual ⟨hieg⟩ observations is
  therefore not established. The regular outcome of the inherited direct
  form, including its quantity and the relevance of hiatus resolution,
  remains open.

## Claim E — "The competing reconstructions are evidentially symmetrical"

**Assessment: Not established (as a claim of equal evidential standing);
what is established is the narrower attestation symmetry.**

- True component: the protected analogical donor (*hīeġes* etc.) and
  the inherited ungeminated direct form (\*hewi) are both unattested in
  the collated English record. As a statement about *attestation*,
  symmetry holds.
- But evidential standing involves more than attestation, and there the
  accounts differ:
  - *Comparative support*: the ungeminated direct cell is directly
    attested in Gothic (*hawi*) and arguably OHG (*hewi*); the
    reconstructed OE donor obliques have exact comparative and
    system-internal parallels (every scholar reconstructs them with the
    same vocalism). Slight edge: both well supported, differently.
  - *Sound-law support*: the geminate route to the attested WS form is
    regular throughout (B2); the early-apocope route requires levelling
    (A2) or fails (A0/A1). The levelling itself is typologically cheap
    (Hogg 1979: both directions attested in the masculine ja-stems;
    OHG *houwi* as a possible parallel act) but is an extra assumption.
  - *Coverage*: both A2 and B2 cover the attested WS + Anglian +
    (new) Northumbrian/Mercian material without remainder; Dahl covers
    it with one timing assumption plus segment-analogy; A0/A1 cover
    only the non-WS spellings.
  - *Assumption count*: B2 needs its chronology (late \*-ą loss) and
    nothing word-specific; A2 needs its chronology plus one
    word-specific levelling event dated before i-umlaut; Dahl needs a
    class-wide reparse plus segment-analogy plus the *hue* patch.
- Conclusion: the hypotheses share an attestation gap but are **not**
  evidentially symmetrical in assumptions or coverage; cycle 1's
  symmetry wording conflated the two. The corrected formulation
  belongs in DRAFT.md §3.2 (made in this cycle).

# Adversarial review of the cycle-8 argument (cycle 8)

The cycle-8 claims under attack: (i) regular early-apocope outcome of
the direct cell is \*\*hewe; (ii) `OEWLossBeforeI` is refuted; (iii)
'hay' requires whole-stem levelling under early apocope and is regular
under late apocope; (iv) all derivations compatible with the attested
record have a long nucleus.

## Objection 1 — "eowu is not a clean control: its w could be analogically restored"

Final postvocalic w is indeed often analogical (snāw-type, Hogg–Fulk
§7.72). But eowu's w is *medial* (before the ō-stem ending), not final;
there is no w-less byform †ēo(u) on record; and R&T (5.1.2, 6.6.2,
6.9.4) and Campbell (§402: "\*aui > OE eowu") both treat the w as
continuous. More decisively, *cleweþa* has no paradigm cell from which
w could be restored — its w is suffix-internal. The objection fails
against the pair; it would need both words to be independently
remodelled, with no motive for the second.

## Objection 2 — "w-loss might be conditioned on word-final -wi#, which cleweþa does not test"

*eowu* tests exactly that environment: PWGmc \*awi was word-final -wi#
(nom.sg.) before the morphological shift, and R&T's \*ǣwi > OE \*ewi
carries the w through that stage. The crāwan : crǣwþ alternations add
w before umlaut-triggering i in verbal inflection. No environment
remains in which the rule could hide.

## Objection 3 — "OHG hewi may be an OHG innovation, not the inherited PWGmc direct cell"

R&T call *hewi* "the inherited nom.-acc. sg." (pp. 53, 173) and Gothic
*hawi* independently attests the ungeminated direct shape. Moreover the
point is model-internal: under early apocope the direct cell loses its
\*j before gemination can apply, so \*hawi is forced by the ordering
itself; OHG merely confirms it. (Under late apocope the objection is
moot — the direct cell is \*hawwj-/\*hauj- anyway.)

## Objection 4 — "The diphthongal parse could extend to \*hawi: [ha.wi] > [hau̯.i]"

No independent example of such nuclearization before a syllabic vowel
exists; the attested \*-awi- words (*eowu*, *cleweþa*) show the
consonantal development; R&T's reversible gemination (p. 53) concerns
\*[aww'] from \*awj, i.e. prevocalic j-cells only; and Hogg–Fulk §7.69
deny that OE ever acquired the /ei̯ ~ æi̯/ diphthong class such a parse
would need downstream. The rescue has no legs.

## Objection 5 — "\*\*hewe may simply have been lost or replaced lexically"

Possible, and unfalsifiable — but it concedes the claim: if \*\*hewe
existed and was replaced by the oblique stem, that *is* the required
levelling. The cycle-8 argument does not assert \*\*hewe never existed;
it asserts no attested form continues it.

## Objection 6 — "mene cuts the other way: if \*mani survived unlevelled, why not \*hawi?"

The strongest objection, and it is accepted — as a fact about
levelling, not about the phonology. \*manją and \*hawją are parallel
inputs, and the record splits: *mene* preserves the regular
early-apocope direct shape (if inherited; Stiles 2013), while 'hay'
shows only the oblique-stem shape. Under early apocope this means
levelling ran oblique→direct in 'hay' but direct→oblique (or nowhere)
in 'necklace' — possible (Hogg 1979 documents both directions in the
hyll/hyse classes) but word-by-word. Under late apocope the burden
flips: *hīeġ* is regular and *mene* needs Boutkan-type remodelling.
The honest summary, adopted in the article: **under either chronology
exactly one member of the \*manją/\*hawją minimal pair requires
analogy.** 'Hay' adds real weight to the late side of the scale; it
does not tip it alone.

## Objection 7 — "The quantity conclusion smuggles the old categorical claim back in"

No: the cycle-4 claim was that all viable accounts predict length,
asserted while the regular direct development was unknown and \*hei was
a live candidate. Cycle 8 closed the gap in that argument rather than
re-asserting it: the short-vowel candidate now has a definite shape
(\*\*hewe), and that shape is excluded by every attested token on
surface-observable grounds (monosyllabicity, absence of w). The
conclusion is conditional in exactly one way: it concerns the attested
word, and says nothing about what an unattested survival of \*\*hewe
would have sounded like.

## Standing after review

Claims (i), (ii) and (iv) survive unqualified. Claim (iii) survives
with the Objection-6 qualification built into its statement: 'hay'
joins the C+j nouns as an analogical liability of early apocope, while
*mene* remains an analogical liability of late apocope; the pair is
now the sharpest known minimal contrast in the debate.
