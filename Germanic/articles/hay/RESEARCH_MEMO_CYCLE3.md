# Research memorandum, cycle 3 (24 November 2026)

Scope: adjudication of the cycle-3 questions (Paris Psalter *hege*
datives; poetic quantity evidence; Farman's accents and ⟨oe⟩; the Li
Mt 14:19 *hehg* discrepancy; the Ru2–Li relationship; the A2/B2/Dahl
comparison) and the consequent revision of `DRAFT.md`. The frozen SC047
models and PR #21 are untouched. Dal 1934 and Ritt 2000 remain
ILL-pending.

## 1. Principal adjudications

### 1.1 The Paris Psalter datives are accepted as genuine overt
inflections

The three *hege* tokens (PPs 101.4, 101.9, 102.14) are accepted as
dative singulars of 'hay', on converging evidence at every level this
cycle could reach:

- **Lexical**: each faces Latin *faenum* in the psalter's parallel
  layout, in mowing/withering similes ('I am like hay that withers when
  mown by a man's hands'); the hedge word, pronoun and 'mind' homographs
  are contextually impossible.
- **Grammatical**: each is complement of *gelīc*/*anlīce*, which govern
  the dative; 101.4 adds a resumptive dative *þam þe*, and 102.14 an
  agreeing dative participle *māwenum* — the -e is inflection, not an
  orthographic flourish.
- **Textual**: Krapp's text (ASPR 5, pp. 72–73, 75) carries no
  emendation at any of the three loci.
- **Manuscript**: all three were verified in images of BnF lat. 8824
  (fols. 116r, 116v, 118v; Gallica canvases f241, f242, f246): ⟨hege⟩
  plainly legible each time, unaccented.

By contrast PPs 146.9 *hig* is **rejected as a token**: Krapp's
apparatus (p. 146) reads "146,9,2 hig] hio" — the manuscript has
⟨hio⟩ and *hig* is editorial, exactly as the DOE's angle brackets
indicated. PPs 71.16 *heg* and 105.17 *hig* and Andreas 38 *hig* are
accepted as direct-case tokens (edition level).

**What the datives do and do not show.** They retire the cycle-1/2
claim that no overtly inflected OE token exists; the dative cell of the
paradigm is attested with the stem shape (*heg-* + *-e*) that the
protected oblique stem predicts. They do **not** supply what Ringe and
Taylor's analogy needs, namely a protected oblique stem demonstrably
available *before i-umlaut*: the Paris Psalter is an eleventh-century
copy of a late, Saxonized-Anglian poetic text (Fulk 1992: 410–414), and
by that date every surviving account — geminate, levelled, or Dahl's —
predicts precisely *hēġe/hege*. The tokens are real and
non-discriminating.

### 1.2 The poetry supplies tokens but no quantity evidence

The cycle-2 statements "no verse token" and "metre is silent" are
withdrawn as factually false and replaced by a demonstrated negative:
the verse tokens exist (six in PPs including the datives; one in
Andreas) and are metrically unusable. The monosyllables stand in lifts
where a closed syllable is heavy regardless of vowel length; the
disyllabic *hege* could in principle feed resolution-based tests, but
Fulk (1992: 410–414) shows the metrical psalms are too irregular,
formulaic and dialectally filtered to sustain quantitative inference,
and Minkova (2003: 23–25) independently cautions that resolution is the
metrical equivalence least directly grounded in the ambient language.
Toswell (1990, chs. 1, 5–6) supports the same transmission picture
(single late tradition; close relation to the glossed psalters; padded
verse technique).

### 1.3 Farman's accents and ⟨oe⟩

- The acute on *hóeg* (Mt 6:30, 14:19) is printed by three editions
  made independently from the manuscript (Kemble–Hardwick 1858, Skeat
  1887, Tamoto 2013); editorial invention is effectively excluded,
  though the manuscript itself cannot be checked online (Digital
  Bodleian's 49-folio selection omits both pages).
- A calibration of Farman's accented types in Matthew (~90 types,
  collated from Tamoto's edition) shows the acute falls predominantly on
  etymologically long nuclei but is sporadic and not exceptionless
  (secure short exceptions: *wég* Mt 7:14, *stéfn* Mt 2:18, *mónige*
  Mt 16:9). Verdict: **probabilistic evidence for a long nucleus**,
  which all surviving derivations predict — hence non-discriminating.
- ⟨oe⟩ is adjudicated an **inverted spelling of /eː/**: Kuhn (1945)
  establishes that the unrounding of /ø(ː)/ was complete in Farman's
  dialect, with inverted spellings of the ⟨oele⟩ type; Farman writes
  the \*au+i class with plain ⟨e⟩ ~140 times. A genuine rounded vowel
  in this one word has no support.

### 1.4 The Li Mt 14:19 discrepancy is resolved

Skeat (1887: 123) prints *ofer heg\* ɫ gers* with the apparatus note
"\*hehg, *alt. to* heg": the Lindisfarne gloss wrote ⟨hehg⟩ and
corrected it to ⟨heg⟩. Kemble–Hardwick (1858: 117) printed the original
spelling; Skeat, Cook and the DOE print the correction. Both readings
are genuine stages of one gloss; neither bears on quantity. The
cycle-2 instruction not to assert ⟨hehg⟩ as a definitive manuscript
spelling is superseded by this apparatus-level resolution.

### 1.5 Ru2 and Lindisfarne

Kotake (2016: 377–396) shows that Owun's direct copying from the
Lindisfarne manuscript is a premise, not an established fact; the
non-R–Y readings may reflect a common source or intervening copies.
The dossier now states the dependence as "textually very close, whether
by direct copy or close congener"; for the hay evidence the consequence
is unchanged — Owun's Jn 6:10 ⟨heg⟩ is a respelling within one textual
tradition, with residual value only as his own orthographic act.

### 1.6 The A2/B2/Dahl comparison (deliverable D)

Full table in `DERIVATIONS.md`, "Cycle-3 comparison". Outcome: the
enlarged record changes the bookkeeping, not the standings. The new
datives are predicted by all three accounts; the accent evidence
supports the long nucleus all three predict; the ⟨hehg⟩ resolution and
⟨oe⟩ adjudication remove two apparent anomalies without favouring any
account. B2 remains the only derivation needing no analogy for this
word; A2's levelling is typologically supported and now has an attested
(late) donor cell but still no pre-umlaut dating; Dahl's route remains
conditionally defensible with an analogical final consonant. The word
still cannot discriminate the chronologies.

## 2. Withdrawn and retained statements

Withdrawn from the cycle-2 record (and from `DRAFT.md`):

1. "No overtly inflected OE token of 'hay' is identified" — false as a
   claim about Old English; true only of the pre-cycle-3 corpus.
2. "No verse token of the noun has been identified, so metre is
   silent" — tokens exist; the metre is unusable for demonstrated
   reasons, which is a different (and stronger) statement.
3. "No accented-token calibration exists for our witnesses" — Farman's
   practice is now calibrated.
4. ⟨hehg⟩ as an unresolved edition discrepancy — resolved.

Retained: the central negative conclusion (the word is a dependent of
the gemination–apocope debate, not a witness in it); the WS-vocalism
constraint (an ēa-type diphthongal nucleus feeding i-umlaut is
required); the non-evidential status of the frozen model scores.
