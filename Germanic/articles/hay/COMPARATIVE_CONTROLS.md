# Comparative controls for the 'hay' problem (research cycle 2)

Question: which other lexemes genuinely discriminate between (i) early
apocope with ungeminated direct forms, (ii) late apocope with geminated
direct forms, and (iii) Dahl's diphthongal no-gemination route — and
which are compatible with several analyses? Sources: the held originals
of Dahl 1938, Campbell (as excerpted in the SC047 dossiers and cycle-1
matrix), Ringe–Taylor 2014, Hogg 1979 (analogy), Adamczyk 2018, Stiles
2013, and the frozen PR #21 model captures (predictions only, not
evidence).

## 1. The *glīg* ~ *glēo* doublet

**Reconstruction.** Conventionally \*gliwą (neut.), with an i-grade
glide sequence \*-iwj-/-iuj- in the derived cells; Dahl's parse is
\*gliuja- with a genuine \*iu diphthong, exactly parallel to his
\*hauja̯. Campbell operates with \*gliwi as the expected "undisturbed"
(ungeminated) direct development [§579(4)] and calls attested *glīg* a
"re-formation" [§411].

**Attestation (as reported in the consulted scholarship; not newly
collated here).** The forms divide into an i-type (*glīg*, *glī(e)g*)
and an eo/iw-type (*glēo*, *glīw/glēow*). Dahl documents a clean
distribution: the g-forms are **strictly West Saxon**, and in them the
vowel is **always ⟨i⟩ before ⟨g⟩, never ⟨io/eo⟩**; the w/eo-forms
carry the back vocalism. Grammatical-cell coverage is not fully
recoverable from the consulted reports; no claim is made here about
inflected tokens of either variant.

**The two analyses.**

- *Campbell*: \*gliwi would be the regular ungeminated outcome; *glīg*
  is a re-formation (i.e. analogical), *glēo* shows the w-retaining
  development with back reflex.
- *Dahl*: both variants are regular outputs of one diphthongal stem
  with different umlaut fates: \*gliuja- > \*glīe (umlaut by stranded
  \*-i) → *glī(e)g* with -g reintroduced from the obliques; or \*gliu >
  *glio(w)* unumlauted, with -w- from the oblique cases. The WS-only,
  always-⟨i⟩ distribution of the g-forms is his supporting observation:
  umlaut of the iu-diphthong is exactly what WS, and only WS, spells ⟨i⟩
  here.

**Does the doublet supply an independent parallel for 'hay'?** Only a
conditional one. What it demonstrates on *either* analysis is (a) that
this glide class could split into an umlauted g-variant and an
unumlauted w-variant, and (b) that the final -g of the umlauted variant
is secondary (both scholars restore it from the obliques). What it
cannot do is certify a *regular* ungeminated route to a g-form: the one
candidate regular witness is precisely the form Campbell calls a
re-formation, and Dahl's regularity claim for it depends on the same
contested diphthongal parse as his hay derivation. The doublet is
therefore **consistent with Dahl, consistent with re-formation, and
decisive for neither** — but it does independently confirm that
analogical -g restoration in this class is a real, repeated process,
which lowers the cost of the one analogical step every account of 'hay'
except plain B2 requires. A fuller manuscript-level collation of the
*glīg/glēo* cells remains a research desideratum (recorded in
UNRESOLVED.md).

## 2. Old High German *hewi* ~ *houwi*

**The facts.** OHG attests both *hewi* (matching Goth. *hawi*) and
*houwi*; Middle Dutch attests *hoye/houwe*. These are attested doublets,
while their identification as direct/oblique stems or as
inherited/levelled forms is historical analysis. Old Norse *hey* and
Gothic *hawi* agree on an ungeminated direct singular outside West
Germanic.

**Direction of levelling.** Ringe–Taylor take *hewi* as the inherited
direct singular and *houwi* as remodelled from the obliques — but that
direction is partly a consequence of their chronology (early apocope
creates the ungeminated cell which *houwi* then replaces). On the
opposite chronology (Dahl, Boutkan), *houwi*-type forms are regular and
*hewi* must be the innovation — and Dahl's Excursus duly derives OHG
*houwi/gouwi/gistrouwi* by gradual \*i > \*u assimilation after the
unmutated diphthong and explains *hewi* via \*haui with later diphthong
modification (differentiation setting in later on the continent), with
OFris *hā*, OS *gō* straight from -aui-. Each side can generate the
other's "inherited" form by a mechanism of its own. Boutkan likewise
assigns Middle Dutch *hoye* to an oblique stem and *houwe* to an old
nominative stem (1995: 212–213); those stem labels and any direction of
levelling depend on his historical analysis. The direction of levelling
is thus **not independently established**: it is inferred within
whichever chronology one assumes. Gothic *hawi* and ON *hey* secure an
ungeminated direct cell for *Proto-Germanic*; they cannot secure a
consonantal-w \*hewi stage for *pre-Old-English* specifically, which is
exactly the step Dahl denies ("no recorded w-forms" in the OE -auj-
nouns). An OHG form is not an OE attestation.

**What the comparative evidence does establish.** (i) The inherited
PGmc direct singular was unlike the obliques (ungeminated vs geminated
or diphthongal); (ii) the continental languages attest doublets with
different stem shapes, but whether a given form is inherited or levelled
depends on the historical analysis; (iii) nothing in the continental
record fixes which shape the *English* direct cell had on the eve of
i-umlaut or establishes a particular direction of English levelling.

**Reading of the table.** The genuinely discriminating items are the
light neuter C+j nouns (*net, bed, kin*), where the attested geminate
direct cells are regular only under B2 and require levelling under A;
and the ungeminated relics (*mene/meni*), which point the other way if
their transmission is inherited. The -auj-/-iwj- glide class — the class
'hay' belongs to — is **internally non-discriminating**: island, the
verbs, and (on inspection) glee and hay itself all reach the same
attested surface by geminate, reversible-geminate, levelled, or
diphthongal routes. 'Hue' is the only glide-class item that favours one
side (its w-form and missing g-form sit most easily with the
geminate/levelled accounts and require special pleading from Dahl).

**Consequence for the article.** The chronological argument cannot be
won inside the glide class. 'Hay' inherits whatever verdict the C+j
nouns and the relic evidence impose on the general chronology; it
cannot itself impose one. This confirms, now with the full comparison
class laid out, cycle 1's instinct that the word is a *dependent* of
the gemination–apocope debate rather than a *diagnostic* of it — and
Dahl's analysis, if admitted, formalizes that dependence (see
DAHL_REASSESSMENT.md §4).

## 4. Cycle-9 revision: the w-loss controls (supersedes the cycle-8 §4)

The cycle-8 version of this section misused *eowu* and *cleweþa* as
proof that no loss of \*w before \*i existed, and concluded that the
regular early-apocope direct cell was \*\*hewe. Ringe & Taylor treat
the w-loss directly (2014: 257–61 §6.7.1: regular, post-i-umlaut,
pre-syncope); *eowu* retains w only because 'ewe' was transferred to the
ō-stems before the change (p. 259) — R&T use it to *date* the change —
and *cleweþa* is their explicitly flagged lexical exception (pp. 260–1).
The corrected control set:

| Lexeme | Preform | Regular OE outcome | Attested | What it secures |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| glee | PWGmc \*gliwi | \*glii > *glī* (contraction) | *glī*, re-formed *glīg*, *glēo(w)* | **the structural twin of 'hay'**: light neuter ja-stem, direct cell survives w-loss + contraction, -g later restored from the inflected cases (Campbell §§411, 579(4)) |
| flock | \*awidī | \*ēidi > Angl. *ēde* | *ēde* (North., N. Merc.) | medial \*-awi- with w-loss and contraction to a long vowel |
| pillow | Lat. pulvinus → \*pylwi | \*pyli > *pyle* | *pyle* | postconsonantal w-loss; loanword, independent of Germanic reconstruction |
| sea, law | \*sāwi, \*āwi | *sǣ*, *ǣ* | *sǣ*, *ǣ* | final \*-wi after long vowel (apocope or contraction) |
| strew (pret.) | \*strawidē | \*strǣide > *strēde* | Cp. *streide*, Ru² *strēdun*, Beo 2436 *strēd* (disyllabic) | the persisting ⟨ei⟩ hiatus stage and its contraction |
| food, necklace | \*mati, \*mani | *mete*, *mene* | *mete*, *mene* | final-vowel treatment where no w is involved |
| ewe | \*awi → ō-stem | *eowu* | *eowu* | the dating of w-loss after i-umlaut (transfer precedes the change) |
| itch | \*klawiþō | \*\*clēþa predicted | *cleweþa* | R&T's flagged exception (lexical analogy with *clāwan* or suffix competition) |

With the w-loss admitted, the regular early-apocope direct cell of 'hay'
is \*hē (via \*hewi > \*he.i, contraction), on the *glī* model.

**Changed reading of the table (again).** 'Hay' does **not** move into
the *net/bed/kin* column. Those words' geminate direct cells require
whole-stem levelling under early apocope; 'hay''s early-apocope cost is
dialect-differentiated and smaller — Anglian *hēġ* is derivable as \*hē
plus the glīg-precedented restoration of -ġ, and only the WS vocalism
(*hīeġ*, matching the oblique stem) requires a larger analogical
operation (or the geminate route). The cycle-8 claim that the word
"testifies with the other light neuter ja-stems" overstated its weight,
as did the mene/hay "minimal pair" (under early apocope *mene* is
regular **and** Anglian *hēġ* is near-regular; the pair is not
symmetric). The §3 verdict that the glide class is internally
non-discriminating stands; what cycle 9 adds is that within that class
'glee' supplies a complete precedent for an inherited ungeminated direct
stem with later limited paradigmatic restoration.
