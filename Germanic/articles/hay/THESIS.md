# What the article argues

Consult this before any substantive revision. The source of claim strength
and dependencies is the [historical model](ARGUMENT_CYCLE13.md).

**Problem (C01–C03).** The relative order of nasal apocope and West
Germanic gemination determines whether the direct singular of a light
neuter ja-stem still offers prevocalic *j* at gemination. Under the
consonantal reconstruction, early loss gives ‘hay’ an ungeminated history
to *he.i* at hiatus; late loss yields the protected-type stem (conventional
West Saxon *hīeġ*). Ringe and Taylor get the English dialectal outcomes
under early loss by replacing direct *hæwi* with protected *hēaj-* before
i-umlaut. Is replacing the nucleus necessary in Anglian as well as West Saxon?

**Principal thesis (C10, C09).** The inherited direct stem is an
independently motivated potential source of the earliest Anglian mid
vowel. The vowel's origin is separable from the palatal's: even if final
desyllabification fails, contraction plus independently defensible
glide restoration could supply both without borrowing vocalism. West
Saxon high vocalism gives a positive reason for protected material under
early loss; it does not establish that operation in Anglian. This is a
criticism of obligatory whole-stem replacement, not proof that replacement
did not occur. Unlike the missing direct geminate of ‘bed’, the Anglian
vowel need not come from another stem. That changes the comparison of
the explanatory burdens, not the decision between chronologies.

**Stronger hypothesis (C11).** Some early direct forms may have survived
without analogy. The preferred ‘hay-road’ reading gives Kentish *heiweg*
an exact written counterpart to the hiatus-stage sequence (C04–C05),
not proof of hiatus, quantity or direct ancestry. Earliest Anglian
⟨heg/heig⟩ fits a short or long mid vowel plus a glide. The controls
motivate a mechanism for short /ej/ but do not establish its conditioning
or date in the target environment. Farman favours local length, not a
universal quantity. Distinguishing actual survival from restoration or
substitution remains a research objective.
