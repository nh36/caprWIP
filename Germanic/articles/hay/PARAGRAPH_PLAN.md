# Paragraph plan

Rows are the approved prose order, not baseline paragraph numbers.
R1–R3 have nonprinting anchors in the draft; the rest remain unimplemented.
Each row states the reader's question and the paragraph's job, what to argue
and why, and what it leaves for its successor. Preserve IDs when moving
rows. The **Claims** column is the bidirectional map to the
[historical model](ARGUMENT_CYCLE13.md); the **Block** column maps to the
[architecture](RHETORICAL_ARCHITECTURE.md). Reverse apparatus links
come from the [allocation plan](SCHOLARLY_APPARATUS.md); anchors are
maintained there only. Source loci and status travel with the referenced
propositions.

| P-hiatus-test | R5 | Frame: which processes can account for ⟨heg⟩ after *w*-loss? | C06, E13, H1, H2, H3 | Ordinary palatal spelling requires either inherited or secondary /j/; retained hiatus, /ej/, contraction alone or with restoration, and replacement have different predictions. | P-hiatus-retain | R5 | Control: had hiatus necessarily disappeared? | E11, E13, C06 | *Streide/strēidæ*, disyllabic *strēd* and reverse-order *sīem* permit persistence but not a date for final /e.i/. | P-hiatus-glide | R5 | Control: could the final vowel become the glide? | E12, E25, C06, H1 | *Clēa*, *cnēo/trēo* support post-loss desyllabification, not this front-vowel environment. Specify short stressed /e/, unstressed final /i/ and priority to contraction. | Gives a motivated short /ej/ prediction, not a law or date. |
| P-hiatus-contract | R5 | Control: could contraction instead preserve the vowel? | E14, E19, C06, H2 | Table 3 compares same /e.i/ in *gecēd, ēde, strēde* with different final sequences in *glī*, *sǣ/ǣ*. | P-hiatus-result | R5 | Compare: what would distinguish viable continuations? | C06, C07, C10, H1, H2, H3 | H1 retains both pieces, H2 keeps the nucleus only, H3 imports both. | P-morph-scope | R7 | Infer: which operations fit the datives? | E17, E18, E22, C08, H2, H3 | Contrast survival without spread, extension with retained *-e*, protected-to-direct vocalism, consonantal repair, and loss of *-e*.
