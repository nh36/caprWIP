## New evidence and corrections to review

- Three **candidate** overt oblique `hege` forms occur in one metrical Paris
  Psalter tradition: `PPsA5 [76900 (101.4)]`, `[77400 (101.9)]`, and
  `[80500 (102.14)]`. Syntax suggests dative but the lexical identification,
  ending, editorial reading and manuscript witness need verification. They
  do not themselves establish an analogical process.
- Additional poetic candidates include `PPsA5 [29700 (71.16)] heg`,
  `[90300 (105.17)] hig` and `AndA2.1 [1200 (33)] hig`.
  `PPsA5 [158200 (146.9)] <hig>` is editorially supplied: retain brackets,
  and do not call it a manuscript attestation. Earlier assertions in
  `DRAFT.md`, `RESEARCH_MEMO_CYCLE2.md` and related cycle-2 evidence files
  that no poetic tokens or overtly inflected candidates were identified
  require reconsideration; those historical documents are intentionally
  unchanged in this intake.
- Rushworth Mt 6:30 and 14:19 correspond to existing concordance **29 and
  30**, not new witnesses. DOE writes `hoeg`, while Tamoto's printed
  edition pp. 19 and 47 visibly has `hóeg`. No manuscript image was inspected.
  `on hoeg` may be syntactically dative but has no overt case ending; revisit
  the concordance's accusative label rather than asserting a new case.
- At Lindisfarne Mt 14:19 DOE has `heg`, Cook has `heg`, and
  Kemble–Hardwick p. 117 prints `hehg` (concordance 28). This is an edition
  discrepancy, not an adjudicated manuscript reading. At Jn 6:10 the DOE
  `heig` corresponds to concordance 26.
- Do not multiply independent witnesses from search hits, biblical parallels,
  glosses of the same Latin passage or three lines of one poetic tradition.
  Hedge, *hige* ‘mind’, pronouns *hig/hyg*, ‘high’, place names and uncertain
  compounds require distinct treatment. Search totals, displayed excerpts
  and verified lexical tokens are different quantities. Five searches
  (`hieges`, `hiege`, `hehg`, `hegg`, `hyeg`) returned zero under settings
  that cannot be reconstructed; this is not proof of historical absence.
- Fulk pp. 410–414, Toswell chs. 1, 5 and 6, and Minkova §2.6 supply controls
  on transmission, metre and circularity. Tamoto pp. xcix–c is not by itself
  a vowel-length calibration. Kuhn p. 634 discusses acute marks in
  Lindisfarne, not a Farman-specific calibration. Kotake's tentative
  reconsideration of Owun's source (pp. 377–396, esp. 394) qualifies simple
  claims that Owun directly copied Lindisfarne.

## Questions for specialist adjudication

1. Do the three Paris readings, after edition, manuscript, grammar and
   transmission checks, supply the protected oblique stem postulated by
   Ringe and Taylor?
2. Do Farman's accents support long vowel quantity, and how reliably?
3. Can Paris Psalter metre discriminate the proposed reconstructions given
   its irregularities, textual transmission and the risk of circularity?
4. Can dialectal or textual differences account for `hege`, `heg`, `hig` and
   `hóeg`, without treating normalized editorial forms as manuscript data?
5. Does any verified evidence change the comparison of Model A and B2?

Do not treat these questions as decided by ingestion. Review edition and
manuscript images, independence, grammar and counterevidence before proposing
changes. Ritt 2000 and Dal 1934 remain unavailable pending ILL; distinguish
reports by Dahl or Hogg–Fulk from direct inspection of Dal.
