# Approved reader sequence

Approved in principle; R1–R6 are implemented through Cycle 19 (see the
[decision record](REVISION_PROTOCOL.md#decision-record)). The
[historical model](ARGUMENT_CYCLE13.md) orders reasons;
the [thesis](THESIS.md) fixes their force. This document orders what
readers need to know to see why each reason matters.

| Block | Entry knowledge, question and stakes | Evidence and argument | Objection to address | Exit knowledge and next need | Comparative technique |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R0 Abstract | Only the title. What does ‘hay’ add to the chronology dispute? | State the predictions and distinguish inheritance of a vowel from inheritance of the whole stem. | Do not imply that ⟨hei⟩ settles either chronology or ancestry. | Reader can distinguish the result from the stronger hypothesis; the opening explains their historical stakes. | Synthesis: separate the two claims in the first encounter. |
| R1 Introduction (exordium, narratio, partitio) | Reader knows ordinary comparative method, not the controversy. Why should a case assignment or a mid vowel matter? | Contrast the orders, explain the ja-stem trap with ‘bed’ and ungeminated comparanda, then introduce the two ‘hay’ predictions, Ringe–Taylor's intervention, and the recorded forms. State the problem as necessity of replacement. | Anglian and West Saxon variation is not itself an anomaly: the substituted stem can yield both. | Reader knows what observation might discriminate and why analogy is an issue; now verify what the two orders actually predict. | Ball and Stiles: relate the lexical problem to exact relative ordering; Versloot: state expectations before comparing witnesses. |
| R2 Derivations (narratio) | Reader knows why the orders matter. Where do they diverge, and exactly what does substitution replace? | Read verified Table 1 at the divergence, then the unchanged stages, protected line and explicit pre-umlaut substitution. | Consonantal analysis is a premise; early hiatus is not an attested endpoint and late *hīeġ* is regional notation. | Reader has separate predictions for untreated direct, protected, and substituted stems; can now assess the written match. | Versloot: like-for-like predictions; Laker: separate chronological steps from the extra operation. |
| R3 Charter and composition (confirmatio with confutatio) | Reader expects *he.i* only conditionally. Does the exact Kentish letter sequence belong to ‘hay’, and if so what does it test? | Give Sweet's reading; build the positive case from spelling and context; give the ‘highway’ objection its strongest form; resolve the lexical preference separately from phonetics and composition. | Route status, transmission and missing phonological comparator are different issues; *dei* is not a hiatus or long /eːj/ control; a generalized protected stem can also fit. | Reader understands why ‘hay-road’ is preferred and why that matters even without direct ancestry. A secure simplex is needed to test the direct singular. | Laker: isolate the inferential gap in the rival; Ball and Stiles: let the witnessed contrast control the explanation. |
| R4 Simplex record (confirmatio) | A plausible early compound cannot identify a grammatical cell. Which simplex witnesses bear on the early Anglian direct form and the regional comparison? | Put the Vespasian and Gospel anchors, grammatical status and verification level ahead of the fuller distribution; separate late Eadwine spellings from the normalized form. | Do not count dependent glosses or corrections as independent, or use manuscript provenance as a dialect diagnosis. | Reader now has the actual Anglian and West Saxon targets. Neither is a phonetic transcription; which continuations are historically motivated? | Ritt: begin with what editorial or manuscript inspection actually shows. |
| R5 Hiatus and palatal (confirmatio with confutatio) | Direct development reaches *he.i*, while Anglian letters usually suggest /Vj/. What supplies each component after *w*-loss? | Establish the input from positive loss controls; compare retained hiatus, desyllabification, contraction, restoration and substitution by position, vowel order and historical source. | No exact final front-vowel control; the lack of a phonemic /ei/ diphthong does not rule out /e.i/ or /ej/; a secondary glide must have a source. | Reader has two motivated inherited-nucleus paths with different quantitative predictions. The next question is whether witnesses discriminate. | Versloot: compare the same diagnostics; Laker: disentangle nucleus and glide rather than requiring adjacency. |
| R6 Quantity and quality (confirmatio with confutatio) | Short /ej/ and long /eːj/ have different histories. Where can length actually exclude unlengthened survival? | Contrast ordinary spellings, Ritt's logic of marking, Farman's own controls, West Saxon quality and Eadwine, then metre and Middle English. | Absence of a mark is not shortness, and Farman is not Aldred or earlier Mercian. High versus mid is not a measurement of length. | Reader can weigh the hypotheses witness by witness; orthography alone cannot identify a donor or direction. | Ritt: spell out the prediction before giving accent counts. |
| R7 Morphology (confirmatio with confutatio) | More than one history can supply the Anglian vowel. What independently motivates the source and direction of levelling? | Pose the donor question, establish both directions with Ball and Stiles, then test them against the reconstructed and attested cells and the limits of ‘glee’. | Mass semantics is not a frequency count, a possible donor is not a demonstrated transfer, and ending erosion differs from spread of a stem. | Reader can compare the scope of each operation; the synthesis can now combine, rather than recite, these findings. | Ball and Stiles: use distribution to argue direction, not just classification. |
| R8 Synthesis and consequence (peroratio) | Reader knows the spelling, phonological and morphological discriminators. Which inferences survive if gliding fails, and does ‘hay’ still count like ‘bed’? | Compare H1–H5 against the same constraints; combine surviving alternatives, regional contrast and continental trade-off, then answer the opening question. | Neither analogy-free survival nor the general chronology follows; if both inherited-nucleus paths fail, narrow the contribution. | Reader leaves with a resolved question about necessity and an open one about actual descent. Bibliographic and witness detail remains independently checkable. | Laker: negate necessity without claiming to prove a rival; Ball and Stiles: return to the original chronology. |
| R9 Appendix | Reader has seen abbreviated witness references. Can the distribution and exclusions be audited? | Provide parallel loci, editorial and manuscript status, damage and exclusions. | Parallel glosses do not supply independent innovations; editorial supply is not a token. | Reader can reproduce the evidential distinctions without internal records. | Ritt: retain provenance and separate observation from inference. |

The largest proposed moves are to put the chronology before the first
attestation, bring composition into the charter argument, put the
quantity test before its controls, and put the direction-of-levelling
question and published parallels before the case census. The
[paragraph plan](PARAGRAPH_PLAN.md) specifies how to make these moves
without changing the historical thesis or losing apparatus.
