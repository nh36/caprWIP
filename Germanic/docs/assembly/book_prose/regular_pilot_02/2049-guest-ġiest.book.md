Campbell and Ringe and Taylor treat this as an ordinary i-stem whose West Saxon development produces _ġiest_, while non-West-Saxon evidence preserves forms of the _gest_ type [@Campbell1959, 91; @RingeTaylor2014, 296]. Bosworth-Toller and Clark Hall indeed record a family of spellings such as _gist_, _gest_, _giest_, and _gyst_, so the issue is not whether the West Saxon form exists, but which member of that attested set the book should foreground [@BosworthToller1898, 387; @ClarkHall1960, 149]. The derivation itself is regular: brightening and i-mutation reshape the stem, and West Saxon palatal diphthongization produces _ie_.

Dialect note. Anglian _gest_ remains genuine Old English evidence, but it is a parallel dialectal comparator rather than a correction to West Saxon _ġiest_ [@RingeTaylor2014, 296; @BosworthToller1898, 387].
