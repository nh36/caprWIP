Kroonen's _\*bērō-_ and the Old English evidence _bar_, _bær_ point to an uncomplicated feminine noun continued here as normalized _bǣr_ [@Kroonen2013, 717; @ClarkHall1960, 32; @BosworthToller1898, 73]. The only real editorial issue is spelling: the dictionaries transmit _bær_ or _bar_, while the book form _bǣr_ simply marks the long vowel explicitly. Once that normalization is stated, the derivation remains regular: final long _ō_ raises, long _ē_ lowers to _ǣ_, and apocope yields the expected Old English form.
