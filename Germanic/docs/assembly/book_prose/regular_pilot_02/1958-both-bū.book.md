The regular item here is not the better-known masculine dual _bēġen_ but the inherited neuter dual _bū_. Kroonen's paradigm under _\*ba-_ includes precisely that unextended _\*bō_ form alongside the wider dual set, and the Old English grammars likewise preserve neuter _bū_ beside masculine _bēġen_ and feminine _bā_ [@Kroonen2013, 47; @SieversBrunner1965, 324; @Campbell1959, 295; @Fulk2018, 223]. That makes _bū_ the cleanest regular comparator for this entry: it is attested Old English, and it does not depend on the more debated extended formation behind _bēġen_. Campbell and Brunner both treat final stressed _ō > ū_ as an ordinary West Germanic-to-Old-English development, with _bū_ among the standard examples [@Campbell1959, 122; @SieversBrunner1965, 69].

Comparison note. The historical discussion of _bēġen_ and the continental forms behind Modern English _both_ belongs to a related but different problem. Orel still cites an older _\*bō-j-_ explanation, while Fulk reports Seebold's preference for _\*bō-þ-_ [@Orel2003, 45; @Fulk2018, 223]. Those formations matter for the wider history of the numeral, but they do not displace the regular comparison _\*bō > bū_ used here.
