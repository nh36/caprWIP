Kroonen and Orel both treat _\*faran_ as the inherited strong verb, and Campbell uses it as a standard example of A-restoration [@Kroonen2013, 149; @Orel2003, 132; @Campbell1959, 61]. The Old English evidence is equally simple: dictionaries distinguish the strong infinitive _faran_ from weak _færan_, while forms such as _fære_ and _færð_ belong to the present tense, not to the citation infinitive [@ClarkHall1960, 113; @BosworthToller1898, 250]. That is enough to explain why the regular comparison stays with _faran_. After Anglo-Frisian brightening, A-restoration before single _r_ returns the stem vowel to _a_, and the later tail reductions yield the expected infinitive.
