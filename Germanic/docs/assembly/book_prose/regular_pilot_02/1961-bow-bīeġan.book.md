Kroonen's weak causative _\*baugjan-_ and Ringe and Taylor's fuller chain to West Saxon _biegan_ place this verb firmly within the ordinary bend-family rather than among analogical repair cases [@Kroonen2013, 75; @RingeTaylor2014, 94]. Old English dictionary evidence is straightforward, and the spelling _bīeġan_ here is simply a normalized representation of the attested weak verb [@ClarkHall1960, 34; @BosworthToller1898, 102]. The regularity lies in the sequence after pre-Old-English _\*bēagjan_: palatalization of _gj_ and i-mutation produce the West Saxon infinitive, so the prose only needs to identify the causative lexeme and confirm that the output belongs to the expected verbal line.
