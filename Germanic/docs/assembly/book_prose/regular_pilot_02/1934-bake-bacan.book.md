Orel's _\*bakanan_ and Campbell's standard examples of A-restoration both point to the ordinary weak verb continued by Old English _bacan_ [@Orel2003; @Campbell1959, 61; @RingeTaylor2014, 270]. The dictionary evidence is uncomplicated: _bacan_ is the attested infinitive headword in Old English [@BosworthToller1898, 72; @ClarkHall1960, 19]. What matters here is the regular phonology. After Anglo-Frisian brightening, A-restoration before single _k_ returns the stem vowel to _a_, and the later weak-verb tail reductions produce the expected infinitive.
