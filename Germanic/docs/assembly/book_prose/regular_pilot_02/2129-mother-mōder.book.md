The compact issue here is not whether the lexeme is right but which Old English form best represents the regular nominative line. Kroonen and Orel give the Proto-Germanic r-stem as _\*mōdēr-_ or _\*mōdēr_, while Old English reference works more often print _mōdor_ or _modor_ and show oblique _mēder_ elsewhere in the paradigm [@Kroonen2013, 368; @Orel2003, 274; @ClarkHall1960, 219; @Campbell1959, 598; @RingeTaylor2014, 312]. The trace therefore supports a regular nominative _mōder_, not the later levelled dictionary headword. That is the point of compression in this entry: the philology lies in paradigm history, not in an irregular sound change.

Comparison note. The familiar _mōdor_ should remain visible in fuller editorial treatment, because readers will expect it, but it does not replace the regular nominative reflex represented here.
