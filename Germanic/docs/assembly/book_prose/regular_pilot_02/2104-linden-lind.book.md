Kroonen's _\*lindō-_ and the Old English dictionary evidence both point to the ordinary noun _lind_ 'linden, lime-tree' [@Kroonen2013, 337; @ClarkHall1960, 198; @BosworthToller1898, 630]. The derivation is correspondingly brief: Northwest Germanic final _ō_ raises to a _\*líndu_ stage, and high-vowel apocope gives _lind_. No further explanation is needed beyond noting that Clark Hall's adjectival _linden_ 'made of linden-wood' belongs to a different lexical use, not to the noun chosen here for direct comparison [@ClarkHall1960, 198].
