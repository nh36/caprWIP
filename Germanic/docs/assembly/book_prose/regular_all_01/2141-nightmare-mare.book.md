The selected Old English form is weak feminine nominative singular *mære* 'nightmare', normalized from Corpus Glossary *maere* under Latin *incuba* [@Lindsay1921, 96, I225]. Hall distinguishes the short-vowel weak noun from strong *mær*; neither is the adjective *mǣre* 'famous'. The variant *mare* is attested in *Bald's Leechbook*, with oblique *maran* in *Leechbook III*. Hall explains the back root in *mare* as levelling from obliques such as *maran*, not regular restoration in the nominative [@Hall2007, 301–302, §2, and 313–314, table 1].

Ringe and Taylor's printed list includes *mære*, and their nominative passes through front-root \emph{*mærǣ} beside the oblique stem \emph{*mærōn-} [@RingeTaylor2014, 192]. The unchanged selected input \emph{*márōn} currently yields *mare*, a mismatch against selected *mære*: the cascade's retained nasal ending still triggers restoration. Model B yields *mære* in an unadopted research experiment; no production sound rule is changed here. The English label 'nightmare' indexes the attested Old English simplex.
