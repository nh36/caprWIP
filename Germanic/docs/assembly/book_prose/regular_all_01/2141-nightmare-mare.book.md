The selected Old English form is weak feminine nominative singular *mære* 'nightmare', normalized from Corpus Glossary *maere* under Latin *incuba* [@Lindsay1921, 96, I225]. Hall distinguishes the short-vowel weak noun from strong *mær*; neither is the adjective *mǣre* 'famous'. The variant *mare* is attested in *Bald's Leechbook*, with oblique *maran* in *Leechbook III*. Hall explains the back root in *mare* as levelling from obliques such as *maran*, not regular restoration in the nominative [@Hall2007, 301–302, §2, and 313–314, table 1].

Ringe and Taylor's printed list includes *mære*, and their nominative passes
through front-root \emph{*mærǣ} beside the oblique stem \emph{*mærōn-}
[@RingeTaylor2014, 192]. From unchanged selected input \emph{*márōn},
the corrected cascade now yields *mære*. The final nasal bimoric vowel
merges early with oral \emph{*ō} in \emph{*ā}, then fronts to
\emph{*ǣ}; the ending no longer triggers root restoration.
The earlier implementation's *mare* output matched an attested levelled
variant, not this regular nominative history. The English label
'nightmare' indexes the attested Old English simplex.
