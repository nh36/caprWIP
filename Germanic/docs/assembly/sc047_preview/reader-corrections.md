# Proposed editorial corrections to SC010, SC041 and SC047

These replacements are for review only. They do not alter the canonical
reader, chronology or book. Rule blocks and references to executable
positions elsewhere in each chapter are unchanged unless noted.

## SC010

Replace the paragraph beginning “OE *nett* … fixes the order”:

> OE *nett* ‘net’ illustrates the difference between two histories of the inherited direct singular. If loss of the nasal ending strands word-final \emph{*j}, which then vocalizes before SC010 West Germanic j-gemination, PGmc \emph{*natją} gives regular \emph{*nete}; if the vowel protects the glide through gemination, the direct derivation gives *nett*. The attested geminate constrains the order only if it continues the inherited direct stem rather than a geminated stem extended from another cell. The executable order produces the attested endpoint, but cannot independently decide that premise [@RingeTaylor2014, pp. 44–47; @HoggFulk2011, pp. 19–22, §2.23].

## SC041

Replace the paragraph beginning “The broader apocope also stranded glides”:

> Loss of the exposed short oral vowel could strand a glide, as in the masculine nominative singular of light ja-stems. Both the Ringe–Taylor and Dahl–Hogg–Fulk reconstructions put this oral loss before West Germanic j-gemination; they disagree about whether short nasal endings also fell before it. This distinction does not follow from the position of the present bare-vowel deletion fragment. In the executable cascade the fragment follows SC010 West Germanic j-gemination, and its environment is narrower than the historical reconstruction [@RingeTaylor2014, pp. 44–47; @HoggFulk2011, pp. 19–22, §2.23].

## SC047

Replace the paragraph beginning “The executable position is later than that historical argument would suggest”:

> This executable fragment removes the nasal ending in its present prosodic environment after SC010 West Germanic j-gemination. Its position does not constitute evidence for the date of historical nasal loss. In particular, because SC041 Loss of final bare \emph{*a} also follows gemination in the executable sequence, the sequence does not implement the published split chronology. Moving the nasal fragment alone also removes triggers still visible to u-lowering and exposes final consonants at a different point in the cascade. Such dependencies constrain the model, not the dating of the historical changes. The secondary final \emph{n} of *dōn* ‘do’ must likewise be exposed only after SC023 Loss of final \emph{n} in n-stems has ceased to operate. The English nasal-loss edge remains open [@RingeTaylor2014, pp. 44–47; @HoggFulk2011, pp. 19–22, §2.23].
