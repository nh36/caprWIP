## 3. Literature

Campbell and Ringe and Taylor support the *widow* material behind SC039
and the low-stress *world* material behind SC040
[@Campbell1959, §218; §§338--339; @RingeTaylor2014, p. 322; §6.3.3].

The later lowering and root-u preservation are described in Campbell

back-vowel system precedes the later u/o development
[@StauslandJohnsen2015, pp. 21--22]; the preceding long-vowel shortening
and resolution are discussed on pp. 28--31. This is the source-backed
chronology already adopted in `audits/sc021-adjudication.md`, section 9a.

SC041 retains the separate support of Campbell's account of unaccented
final-vowel loss and Ringe and Taylor's discussion of word-final short
low vowels [@Campbell1959, pp. 137--138, §331; p. 143, §341;
@RingeTaylor2014, pp. 44--47]. The former60--61 locator counted PDF
sheets, not printed pages. Campbell's retrospective §341 reference
does not date the earlier protected-a loss to OE medial syncope.
Ringe and Taylor include both short oral a and nasal a; Fulk likewise
distinguishes their loss, irrespective of preceding syllable weight,
from subsequent high-vowel reductions [@Fulk2018, p. 91, §5.6].
The handbooks do not supply a single three-change textbook chapter.

The initial LH accommodation versus a later heavy head after initial H
is supported by McCully and Hogg and Goering
[@McCullyHogg1990, pp. 330--333; @Goering2023, pp. 79--84,
§§4.5.1.1--4.5.2]. It distinguishes the actual short-diphthong world
representation from eorþ.cun.de. Hogg's caution about circular tertiary
stress remains important [@Hogg1996, pp. 8--11]; neither a suffix identity
nor successful final spelling substitutes for phonological structure.
