# SC055-SC056: Umlaut Core and Palatal-Diphthongization Follower

## 1. Role in the book

SC055-SC056 is now the strongest immediate promotion candidate exposed by the
SC053-SC058 split review. It would be the first clearly central Old English
vowel-change report in the production layer after the earlier corridor and
singleton promotions.

That makes it an important test chapter. Unlike SC066-SC068, which is a narrow
late weak-tail corridor, this unit would center on a major textbook rule and a
tight right-hand follower. The book value is obvious, but the chapter still
needs careful framing because the historical weight of the two members is not
equal.

## 2. Name and basic formulation

- **change_ids:** `SC055`; `SC056`
- **display_names:** `OE I Umlaut`; `OE Ws Palatal Diphthongization`
- **rule_names:** `OEIUmlaut`; `OEWsPalatalDiphthongization`
- **current_orders:** `55`; `56`
- **cards:**
  - `Germanic/docs/sound_changes/order_tests/chronology_cards/SC055-oe-i-umlaut.md`
  - `Germanic/docs/sound_changes/order_tests/chronology_cards/SC056-oe-ws-palatal-diphthongization.md`

Working formulation: the report would treat `SC055` as the narrative center and
`SC056` as a narrower West Saxon follower on the chapter's right edge. The pair
works structurally because the cards form a tight local link, but the prose must
make clear that the follower is not equal in historical weight to the umlaut
core.

## 3. Traditional description and literature

The literature dossier points in a consistent direction.

Campbell, Hogg, Luick, Ringe and Taylor, and Fulk all support the centrality of
i-umlaut. That is the chapter's secure historical core. The same sources also
recognize West Saxon palatal diphthongization, but usually as narrower,
dialect-specific, or embedded in a broader palatal chronology rather than as a
second coequal law with the same chapter weight as i-umlaut.

This means the eventual report should be confident about `SC055` and more
careful about `SC056`. The handbooks support a companion section, but they do
not automatically support giving both rules equal narrative billing.

## 4. Formal implementation

In CAPR prose, the pair is straightforward.

1. `OEIUmlaut` fronts and raises vowels under a following `i/j` trigger and
   supplies the main historical relation for outputs such as `cȳ` and other
   later umlaut-sensitive forms.
2. `OEWsPalatalDiphthongization` applies West Saxon palatal-triggered
   diphthongization on the right edge of that zone, yielding the local card
   relation seen in `gift` and `sheath`.

That is useful formal material for the book, but it needs one explicit caution:
CAPR's local `SC055 < SC056` order is not identical to the broad textbook
chronology for all palatal-diphthongization material.

## 5. Place in the cascade

In the assembled half, this pair sits in a good narrative position.

1. It follows `SC052` OE Velar Palatalization.
2. It also follows the residual `SC053` / `SC054` bridge material in current
   order, though that material should remain outside the promoted core for now.
3. It precedes later weak-tail, apocope, and syncope developments, especially
   the already promoted `SC063` and `SC066`.

That placement would let the chapter function as an early Old English vowel core
before the book returns to later weak-tail reduction.

## 6. Order-testing evidence

The chronology cards give the pair a clean local profile.

1. `SC055` must follow `SC052`, with `cow` and `lung` as the key earlier-side witnesses.
2. `SC055` must precede `SC056`, with `gift` and `sheath` as the key later-side witnesses.
3. `SC056` must follow `SC055`, again with `gift` and `sheath`.
4. `SC056`'s later side is not a positive historical boundary; it is only a no-break-before-runner-boundary result.

This gives the book a good chapter skeleton, but not a license to rewrite the
entire handbook chronology as if it were derived directly from the cards.

## 7. Interpretation for the book

If promoted later, the report should keep `SC055` as the narrative center.
`SC056` is best treated as a right-edge follower or dialectal companion rather
than as a coequal headliner.

That would let the chapter make a stronger methodological point: a major
historical rule can be anchored by local computational evidence without being
reduced to that evidence. The cards show where CAPR's current serial
implementation is tight; the handbooks explain why i-umlaut itself is historically
central.

## 8. Relation to neighbouring changes

1. **SC054 OE W Loss Before I** is probably bridge material rather than part of the core chapter, even if it receives a short note later.
2. **SC057 OE J Cluster Coalescence** still looks better placed for later reconsideration with the `SC049-SC052` palatalization/fronting region.
3. **SC063 OE High Vowel Apocope** and **SC066 OE L Adjacent Syncope** are later promoted rules whose chronology already depends on the earlier umlaut setting established by `SC055`.

The pair is therefore strong partly because it sits between earlier
palatalization and later weak-tail reductions without trying to absorb either
neighboring cluster whole.

## 9. Remaining uncertainty

1. The final chapter title is still open: **OE I-Umlaut**, **Umlaut Core**, and **Umlaut Core and Palatal-Diphthongization Follower** are all plausible.
2. The scope and narrative weight of `SC056` are still unsettled.
3. It is not yet decided whether `SC054` deserves a bridge note in the eventual report.
4. The report will need to decide how explicitly to foreground West Saxon specificity.
5. It is still open whether `SC057` should be mentioned in passing or left entirely for the later palatalization/fronting review.
