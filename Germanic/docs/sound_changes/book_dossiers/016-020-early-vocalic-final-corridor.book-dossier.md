# SC016–SC020: early vowel and ending corridor

## 1. Current component identities

This current dossier supersedes its earlier SC016-before-SC017 spine.
SC016 is the late West Saxon spelling operation `OEWsPalatalGlide`, not
an early inserted phonological glide. SC017 `PNWGmcULowering` supplies
the lowered vowel subsequently written in *ġeoc* 'yoke'
[@RingeTaylor2014, pp. 5, 129; @Fulk2018, p. 56].
The historical SC016/017 memo and its measurements remain intact,
with a separately identified SC017/019 qualification.

The executable corridor retains SC017 → SC018 → SC019. SC018's
stressed-monosyllable operation is separate from final unstressed oral
raising; its lack of a close displacement boundary is not evidence that
the change was absent [@Campbell1959, p. 47, §122].
SC020 is the separately adjudicated West Germanic loss of final
unstressed *z*, not an unconditional deletion of any final *z*
[@RingeTaylor2014, pp. 44–45, 212].

## 2. Lowering domain and the nasal vowel

The subsequent rainbow adoption extends SC017 to subordinate root
*ù > ò*, without changing its placement or plain/acute clauses.
Ordinary *boga* has the same early vowel history (Colman2011
pp.95–96; RingeTaylor2014 pp.27–29). Applying it to reconstructed
*régna-bùgô* is CAPR inference. The retained grave feeds linking
syncope; rainbow's already lowered vowel is not evidence for late
SC040. The principal source/qualification account is the SC017 reader
and `audits/rainbow-2148-adoption.md`. No SC019 chronology claim is
strengthened by this extension.

Stressed *u* lowers before a following non-high vowel unless blocked
in the relevant nasal or intervening-j environments. Initial *j* is
not the intervener that blocks yoke [@Campbell1959, p. 43, §115;
@Fulk2018, p. 56]. The implemented first-nucleus matcher excludes
a nasal immediately after the target and *j* throughout its consonantal
interval; it must not be paraphrased as excluding all nasals anywhere
in that interval.

Bare final nasal *ǭ* is now admitted by the input grammar, and its
general long/back/vocalic/non-high memberships are correct. Ringe's
nominative *wullō* and accusative *wullǭ* substantiate this distinct
domain [@Ringe2017, pp. 242–243]. The repair does not merge oral *ō*,
nasal *ǭ*, and trimoric *ô*, nor broaden final oral raising.
All consumers of the seven modified classes are enumerated and tested
in the new technical receipt; all selected derivations are preserved.

## 3. Preferred chronology and its actual force

Fulk's *skulō → scolu* argument supplies the clearest direct lexical
case for lowering before final raising [@Fulk2018, p. 56]. With the
selected inputs, the same sequence gives *nosu* 'nose', *sċofl*
'shovel' and *sorg* 'sorrow' without additional oblique-root
generalization. CAPR provisionally prefers that economy. It is not
an inference from an output score or a proof that no alternative
paradigm history existed.

Nose is already remodeled before the selected history: *nus-* is a
secondary zero grade beside *nas-* [@Kroonen2013, p. 383].
Luick infers a high-root shovel doublet beside *scofel*; that supports
possible alternation, not the direction or date of levelling
[@Luick1914, pp. 105–106, §78]. Sorrow's comparative formation supports
the inherited stem, not a recorded early English analogical operation
[@Kroonen2013, p. 493]. These individual distinctions matter more
than counting nominally independent witnesses.

## 4. The coherent alternative and analogy

Stiles explicitly places final *ō > u* before a-umlaut
[@Stiles2012, p. 45]. With correct nasal conditioning, this sequence
gives wool's regular nominative *wullu* and accusative low-root
*wollǭ*, ultimately *wull / wolle*. Ringe and Taylor's proposed
generalization of nominative *u* could then give the attested high-root
paradigm; their corresponding love explanation has the same structure
[@RingeTaylor2014, p. 32; @Ringe2017, pp. 242–243].

Earlier raising conversely requires oblique-o generalization to explain
the low-root nominatives used by Fulk and the selected comparison words.
Generic support for levelling comes from early *Holtijaz* and the
daughter/ox paradigms, not direct attestation of each proposed operation
[@RingeTaylor2014, pp. 33–34; @Stiles2012, pp. 47–48].
Fulk's continental *skola* comparator is not an untouched nominative:
he also describes accusative-to-nominative ending extension in those
ō-stems [@Fulk2018, pp. 154–155].

The preferred account therefore explains several low-root forms with
fewer unsupported extra operations, but leaves wool and love as genuine
high-root residues. Ringe and Taylor's wool account is plausible but
unproved, not refuted. No analogy is encoded as a sound law.

## 5. Runic bounds, attribution and precedents

*Horna* about 400 attests lowering; *waritu* about 450 attests final
oral raising. Neither supplies a lower bound for the other change's
onset [@Fulk2018, pp. 56, 82]. Stiles's conditioned and analogical
Runic examples, and his distinction between realization and
phonemicization, prevent those dates becoming a premise-free strict
pairwise order [@Stiles2012, pp. 47–48, 54–60].

Fulk cites RT's lowering/final-*az*-loss discussion, not an express
rejection of their wool account [@Fulk2018, p. 57, n. 1].
Luick's relevant discussion is §§77–79, especially §78
(printed pp. 105–106), not §§105–106. His rejection of a labial
protection rule uses low-root counterexamples, not this exact
comparative-order argument [@Luick1914, pp. 104–106].
Campbell and Bülbring remain text-checked with the documented
original-image certification limits [@Campbell1959, p. 43, §115;
@Bulbring1902, pp. 45–46, §116].

No checked predecessor was found making precisely CAPR's comparative
cost argument. The bounded later-response search found no verified
specific rebuttal or defence; general reviews are not substitutes.
No novelty claim is made. Fulk also calls *scolu* 'host' a Latin
loan elsewhere [@Fulk2018, p. 47, n. 1]; that passage must not be
silently identified with, or treated as independent support for,
his inherited *skulō* example on p. 56. The preference rests on the
explicitly stated inherited analysis and the other conditional
comparisons, not on assuming every occurrence of the spelling is
the same untouched lexical history.

## 6. Final consonants, quantities and later apocope

In *rástōz*, *z* protects *ō* from SC019. SC020 subsequently exposes
the surviving long vowel, which proceeds through SC042, the long-final
fronting support, SC072 shortening and SC073 merger to *ræste*
'rest'. Moving raising after the consonant loss incorrectly raises
that newly final vowel. The independently established SC019/020
relation is retained unchanged.

Nasal *ǭ* does not raise at SC019. Its later SC072 → SC073 reduction
gives *e*, a telescoped serialization rather than a claim that nasality
survived historically until that executable position. Ordinary SC063
apocope must be distinguished from the earlier prosodic SC098 loss and
short nasal-tail SC047 loss [@RingeTaylor2014, pp. 57–60, 284–289].
RT's bimoric genitive *-ōz* and Fulk's trimoric *-ôz* remain alternatives,
not interchangeable quantities [@RingeTaylor2014, pp. 59, 299;
@Fulk2018, p. 155]. The incomplete trimoric input/nucleus pathway is
explicitly deferred.
