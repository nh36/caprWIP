# SC053-SC054: Pre-umlaut Bridge and W-loss

## 1. Role in the book

This unit is a small chronological bridge between the promoted SC052 velar-palatalization hinge report and the promoted SC055-SC056 umlaut-core report. Its main editorial role is to keep two ordinary FST changes explicit in the assembled sound-change half without pretending that this is a large textbook chapter.

The asymmetry is the key fact. SC053 is residual and boundary-limited. SC054 is the narrow but real positive member, because the `sea` derivation gives it a historically intelligible place before the umlaut core. That makes the unit book-useful, but still modest.

## 2. Name and basic formulation

- **SC053:** OE Post Velar W Loss
  - **FOMA rule:** `OEPostVelarWLoss`
  - **Current role:** residual `*ngw > *ng` cleanup with no positive first-break boundary in either direction
- **SC054:** OE W Loss Before I
  - **FOMA rule:** `OEWLossBeforeI`
  - **Current role:** narrow pre-umlaut glide-loss bridge with two real but witness-limited boundaries
- **Combined unit label:** Pre-umlaut Bridge and W-loss
- **Present editorial judgment:** keep together provisionally because the changes are adjacent, but leave open a later reduction to an SC054-centered note

## 3. Traditional description and literature

The literature strongly supports only one half of the pair. SC054 is real handbook material. Campbell, Ringe and Taylor, and Luick all support the development behind `sǣ` 'sea' from earlier `*saiwi-` / `*sawi`, which makes "loss of `w` before unstressed `i`" a genuine historical bridge before later umlaut outcomes [@Campbell1959, §406; @RingeTaylor2014, §6.7.1; @Luick1914, §187].

SC053 is weaker. Ringe and Taylor provide the narrow comparative derivation `*singwan > singan`, which matches CAPR's `*ngw > *ng` simplification [@RingeTaylor2014, §6.4.2]. But this is not a large handbook headline, and the chronology card finds no positive first-break boundary on either side. The pair is therefore practical rather than traditional: SC054 has the better chapter claim, while SC053 mainly needs to remain visible.

## 4. Formal implementation

In CAPR terms the pair is compact:

```text
SC053: *w -> 0 || *n *g _
SC054: *w -> 0 || EnglishStarVocalic _ *i .#.
```

That coding difference matches the book problem. SC053 is a local `*ngw` cleanup. SC054 is a better-known glide-loss rule whose output feeds later fronted vowel results. The formal prose should keep that asymmetry visible.

## 5. Place in the cascade

SC053-SC054 belongs immediately after SC052 and immediately before the promoted SC055-SC056 umlaut-core report. This is exactly the kind of small bridge stretch that the assembled half needs in order to remain strictly chronological while still giving every ordinary FST change explicit prose.

The rightward chronology of SC054 does not change that placement. Its later positive boundary happens to be SC063, but that should be handled by cross-reference rather than by dragging SC054 into a non-contiguous chapter.

## 6. Order-testing evidence

- SC053 has no positive later boundary.
- SC054 must follow SC020, with `sea`.

- There is no internal reciprocal SC053-SC054 pair.

The key consequence is that this unit is promotable, if at all, only as a modest bridge. SC054 carries the positive chronology; SC053 is present because it is an ordinary FST change and because it occupies the same local stretch of the cascade.

## 7. Interpretation for the book

The most plausible eventual treatment is still a short adjacent bridge report. That version would say plainly that SC053 is residual and implementation-heavy, while SC054 is the narrow source-backed member that prepares the way for the umlaut core without becoming part of it.

But a second shape remains possible: the final prose might instead center almost entirely on SC054, with SC053 reduced to a very short prefatory note. The dossier work should keep both options open rather than forcing a larger chapter than the evidence can support.
