# SC006 Early i-apocope — book dossier

## Historical phenomenon

SC006 isolates the early loss of final `*i` after unstressed syllables before later i-umlaut can operate.

## Relevant CAPR rule

- `PWGmcEarlyIApocope`
- deletion of final `*i` in remote unstressed syllables, with optional final `*z`

## Example lexemes

1. `thousand`
2. `bore (3sg)`
3. `have`
4. `learn (3sg)`
5. `lick (3sg)`

## Source support found so far

1. Sievers/Brunner treats the early loss of final `-i` after unstressed syllables as established by its failure to trigger later i-umlaut [@SieversBrunner1965, §§145--146].
2. Ringe and Taylor give the `*jugunþi > *juguþ > OE geoguþ ~ iuguþ` pathway explicitly [@RingeTaylor2014, p. 141].
3. Campbell cites the same `dugup / geogup` area of evidence [@Campbell1959, §332].
