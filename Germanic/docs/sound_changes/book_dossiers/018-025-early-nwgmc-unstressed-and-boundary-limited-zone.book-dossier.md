# SC018-SC025 book dossier: early Northwest Germanic unstressed and boundary-limited zone

## Role in the book

The current `SC018-SC025` row exists because the assembled sound-change half
needs a visible holding zone between the promoted pilot `SC016-SC020`
**Early vocalic/final corridor** and the promoted `SC026-SC027`
**Nasal spirant corridor**. This region is historically real enough to deserve
explicit prose, but it does not yet behave like a compact adjacent production
chapter.

That is why this dossier is a book-review document rather than a promotion
request. The live question is whether the row should remain one broad cautious
undertermination report, or whether it should later split into smaller notes.

## What the chronology cards currently show

The current cards divide the row into two editorial classes.

### Boundary-limited members

- `SC018` `NWGmcStressedMonosyllableORaising`
- `SC022` `NWGmcMnDissimilation`
- `SC025` `NWGmcLongENasalRounding`

All three are negative / boundary-limited on both sides. Their earlier search
stops at bundled `PWGmcChanges`, and their later search reaches the current
search boundary without a real break. The book should therefore keep them
visible without narrating positive local chronology that the current testing
does not show.

### One-sided later-facing members

- `SC021` `NWGmcUnstressedORaising` -> outward rightward boundary at `SC040`
  with `heaven`
- `SC023` `NWGmcNStemNLoss` -> outward rightward boundary at `SC047` with `do`
- `SC024` `NWGmcLongELowering` -> outward rightward boundary at `SC056` with
  `sheep` and `year`

These are the three members most likely to become future singleton notes,
because each has one real positive rightward boundary. But none of those
boundaries is internal to the row. All of them point outward to already
promoted later reports.

## Why this is not yet one production chapter

The book should avoid two different overstatements:

1. It should not turn bundled `PWGmcChanges` into a historical left boundary.
   That left edge is a runner limitation, not a positive chronology claim.
2. It should not pretend that SC040, SC047, or SC056 justify a non-contiguous
   merged chapter. Those are cross-references to later promoted units, not a
   reason to collapse the chapter sequence.

So the present review does **not** support promoting `SC018-SC025` as one
adjacent production report. There is no convincing internal local subgroup here
comparable to the already promoted SC029-SC030, SC039-SC040, or SC055-SC056
cores.

## Plausible future shapes

### Option A: keep one broad underdetermination scaffold

This is the safest current option. It preserves the chronological place of the
region between the SC016-SC020 pilot corridor and SC026-SC027 without forcing
an artificial internal story.

### Option B: split into boundary notes plus later singleton notes

This is the clearest later split if the project wants more explicit prose.

- boundary/context notes: `SC018`, `SC022`, `SC025`
- later singleton candidates: `SC021`, `SC023`, `SC024`

This split would respect both the source review and the order evidence. It
would also keep the outward SC040 / SC047 / SC056 relations as cross-references
instead of turning them into non-contiguous chapter mergers.

### Option C: one cautious underdetermination report

This remains possible only if the book explicitly presents the row as an early
Northwest Germanic zone with mixed evidence and no strong internal core. It
would have to be written as a cautionary holding chapter, not as a positive
adjacent cluster.

## Working editorial recommendation

Keep `SC018-SC025` scaffolded for now and treat this row as **dossier-prepared,
pending human chapter-shape review**. The dossiers now support a real
editorial choice:

1. keep one broad scaffolded underdetermination zone; or
2. split later into boundary-limited singleton notes plus source-backed
   singleton notes.

If the project chooses to promote anything later, the most defensible narrow
candidates are `SC021`, `SC023`, and `SC024`. If it chooses not to split yet,
the present grouped scaffold remains the safer architecture.
