# SC059-SC061: Back mutation and weak-tail bridge

## 1. Role in the book

This row is the compact seam between the promoted `SC055-SC056` umlaut core and
the later weak-tail reduction region that includes promoted `SC063`,
promoted `SC064-SC065`, the promoted `SC066-SC068` corridor, and the still-broad
`SC069-SC078` scaffold. The book therefore needs some local prose here
eventually, but not necessarily a finished three-rule bridge report.

The new literature dossier makes the internal hierarchy much clearer. SC059 is
the source-backed center: it is the only member with strong handbook support and
real chronology boundaries on both sides. SC060 and SC061 are narrower one-sided
notes. SC060 points leftward to the already promoted umlaut report through
`SC055 < SC060`; SC061 points leftward to the already promoted SC023 singleton
through `SC023 < SC061`. Neither currently behaves like the coequal second half
of a local reciprocal pair.

## 2. Hard structural constraint

Grouped production reports may cover adjacent changes only. Non-adjacent links
must stay as cross-references.

That means:

1. SC059 must **not** be merged backward into the promoted `SC046-SC048` corridor even though `SC048 < SC059` is real.
2. SC060 must **not** be absorbed into the promoted `SC055-SC056` umlaut core just because `SC055 < SC060` is real.
3. SC061 must **not** be pulled backward into the promoted SC023 singleton note just because `SC023 < SC061` is real.
4. SC059's later relation to `SC078` must remain a rightward cross-reference only rather than a reason to expand the chapter into the larger late-tail scaffold.

## 3. Member-by-member book reading

### SC059

SC059 is the clearest prose center in the row. The sources support back mutation
or back umlaut directly, and the card gives it a real two-sided window:
`SC048 < SC059 < SC078`. That makes it more than filler. If this row is ever
promoted, SC059 should carry the historical explanation.

### SC060

SC060 is real, but narrower. Its `might` / `night` evidence shows that it must
follow the promoted SC055 umlaut center, yet its later side is runner-bounded.
In book terms this makes SC060 look more like a later singleton or bridge note
than like a coequal member of a finished three-rule chapter. Conceptually it
belongs near the umlaut region by cross-reference, but in the assembled book it
still needs to remain in strict chronological position after SC059.

### SC061

SC061 is narrower again. Its `do` / `dōn` witness gives it one real earlier
boundary at SC023, but its later side is runner-bounded. That makes it legible
and worth keeping visible, but not obviously promotable as part of an automatic
three-change bridge. In book terms SC061 looks like residual weak-tail material
that may later become a short singleton note or remain scaffolded while the
larger late-tail clusters are sorted out.

## 4. What the sources now make clear

The dossier work reduces the uncertainty to a chapter-shape question, not a
source-gap question.

1. **SC059 is genuinely source-backed** as a back-mutation center.
2. **SC060 is historically plausible but dependent**: it belongs to the wider umlaut / palatal-vowel region more than to a self-standing SC059-SC061 trio.
3. **SC061 is historically legible but narrow**: the `do` witness is real, but the rule still reads like one-sided seam material rather than a chapter center.
4. **The grouped row is still useful architecturally** because it marks the turn from the promoted umlaut/back-mutation region into the later weak-tail cleanup zone.

## 5. Chapter-shape options

### Option A: one cautious grouped SC059-SC061 bridge

This remains viable if the book wants one compact local seam.

1. SC059 would carry the prose as the back-mutation center.
2. SC060 would appear as a short umlaut-side follower with explicit caution that `SC055 < SC060` is a cross-reference, not a merger signal.
3. SC061 would appear as a short weak-tail/`do` note with explicit caution that `SC023 < SC061` is likewise a cross-reference only.

This option works best if the final prose is comfortable admitting strong
internal asymmetry.

### Option B: split SC059 away from SC060-SC061

This is structurally credible because SC059 is clearly stronger than the other
two members.

But the current dossier does **not** find a strong internal SC060-SC061 core.
If the row were split, the likely result would not be a strong two-change bridge
on the right. It would more likely be a follow-up decision about whether SC060
and SC061 deserve separate singleton notes or continued scaffold treatment.

### Option C: later promote SC059 as a singleton and leave SC060 / SC061 scaffolded

This is the strongest alternative to the grouped bridge.

It matches the evidence profile very closely:

1. SC059 has the clearest historical center and the clearest two-sided chronology.
2. SC060 and SC061 are both one-sided and still mostly chapter-orienting rather than chapter-carrying.
3. The assembled half would stay strictly chronological without pretending the row contains a strong three-way core.

## 7. Promotion readiness judgment

The row is now dossier-prepared enough for a human chapter-shape review, but it
is **not** ready for automatic grouped promotion.

- **Most ready if the project wants a narrow next prose move:** SC059 alone
- **Most viable grouped option:** one cautious bridge with SC059 as the clear center
- **Not currently justified:** treating SC060 and SC061 as a strong internal pair
- **Not currently necessary:** doing substantially more source work before deciding

So the next real decision is now between **Option A** and **Option C**, not
between "promote now" and "search for basic evidence."
