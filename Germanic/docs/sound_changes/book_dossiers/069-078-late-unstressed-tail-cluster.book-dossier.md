# SC069-SC078: Late unstressed-tail cluster

## 1. Role in the book

This row is the broad late-vowel placeholder between the promoted
`SC066-SC068` weak-tail syncope corridor and the still-broad closing
`SC079-SC087` cluster. The assembled book needs something visible here, but the
new dossier work makes one thing clear: the current nine-change scaffold is a
visibility device, not a future production chapter in its present shape.

The internal hierarchy is now much clearer than it was in the bare scaffold.
`SC072-SC073` is the strongest local production-scale seam. `SC070-SC071` is a
smaller early bridge. `SC074-SC075` is a narrow medial-lowering bridge.
`SC069` and `SC076` are context notes of different kinds. `SC078` is the right-
edge weak-tail hinge and already points forward to the closing cluster through
`SC086`.

## 2. Hard structural constraints

Grouped production reports may cover adjacent changes only. Non-adjacent links
must stay cross-references.

That means:

1. The row must not be stretched backward into promoted `SC059`, `SC063`,
   `SC064-SC065`, or `SC066-SC068`, even though those chapters are the leftward
   narrative context.
2. `SC078` must not be silently merged forward into the closing `SC079-SC087`
   cluster just because its later boundary is `SC086`.
3. Any future chaptering must preserve strict chronological order inside the
   late-tail region rather than creating a non-contiguous "weak-tail mega-
   chapter."

## 3. Answers to the book-shape questions

### Is there a real local core inside SC069-SC078?

Yes, but not one single core. The row contains several smaller seams. The
strongest is `SC072-SC073`. `SC070-SC071` and `SC074-SC075` are smaller local
bridges. `SC069`, `SC076`, and `SC078` do not belong in one symmetrical shared
core with the middle members.

### Should SC069-SC072 form an early late-tail vowel-shortening/fronting subcluster?

Not as one finished production unit. `SC070-SC071` is a real local pair, but
`SC072` is stronger than both and already forms a tighter hinge with `SC073`.
`SC069` is broader and farther-reaching than the others. The best reading is
therefore: `SC069` as an opener/context note, `SC070-SC071` as a short bridge,
and `SC072` carried forward into the stronger middle core.

### Should SC073-SC076 form a medial/prefix unstressed-vowel reduction subcluster?

Also no, not as one report. `SC073` works best with `SC072`; `SC074-SC075` is a
small local bridge of its own; `SC076` is a source-backed but chronology-
negative prefix note. The grouped four-change option would blur three different
evidence profiles instead of clarifying them.

### Should SC078 split out as a weak-tail reduction singleton or bridge into the closing cluster?

SC078 is the clearest singleton or right-edge bridge candidate in the row. It
belongs to the late-tail cluster by strict order, but it also orients forward to
the closing cluster through `SC086`. The safest current reading is: keep it in
place, keep the forward link as a cross-reference, and decide its eventual prose
shape after or alongside a dossier pass on `SC079-SC087`.

### Which member or pair is the strongest first production target?

`SC072-SC073` is the best first subcluster candidate. It has the strongest
combination of source support and real local chronology. The best singleton
alternative is `SC078`, but that is slightly more dependent on how the closing
cluster is later framed.

### How should the book cross-reference earlier promoted reports?

Keep the earlier promoted reports as context only:

1. `SC059` remains the back-mutation seam immediately to the left of the late
   weak-tail region.
2. `SC063`, `SC064-SC065`, and `SC066-SC068` remain the immediate promoted left
   neighborhood inside the late weak-tail story.
3. None of those reports should be expanded into the present row, and none of
   them should absorb the row's members backward out of order.

### Does SC078 belong primarily with the late-tail cluster, or forward toward SC079-SC087?

Primarily with the late-tail cluster in strict order, but book-shape judgment
for its eventual promotion must account for the forward tie to `SC086`. In other
words, SC078 is still a member of the present region, but its eventual prose
should not ignore the closing cluster seam.

## 4. Member-by-member book reading

### SC069

SC069 is a broad opener. It is worth keeping visible because the change is
historically legible and the row needs a left edge, but it is not the place to
start promotion.

### SC070-SC071

This is the cleanest early local bridge in the row. It could later become a
short production note if the project wants a modest early late-tail bridge, but
it is not yet the strongest candidate overall.

### SC072-SC073

This is the best middle core. It has the strongest source support and the most
convincing local chronology. If the row is ever broken open by a small
subcluster promotion, this is the likeliest place.

### SC074-SC075

This is a narrow medial-lowering bridge. It is real and useful, but the
evidential base is too small to make it the first promotion target.

### SC076

SC076 should survive the later split as a singleton note. It is historically
justified by the prefix-vowel literature, but its chronology card is negative on
both sides, so it should not be forced into a grouped promotion for the sake of
tidiness.

### SC078

SC078 is the right-edge hinge. It is much stronger than a mere leftover, but it
also needs care because it points straight toward the closing cluster rather than
staying purely local.

## 5. Proposed split map

The most plausible later structural split is:

1. `SC069` — singleton scaffold opener/context note
2. `SC070-SC071` — short early fronting/shortening bridge
3. `SC072-SC073` — strongest late-tail shortening/merger core
4. `SC074-SC075` — narrow medial-unstressed-`i` lowering bridge
5. `SC076` — singleton prefix-reduction note
6. `SC078` — singleton weak-tail reduction note / right-edge bridge

This is the map that best respects both the source hierarchy and the current
chronology cards. A simpler three-way split (`SC069-SC072`, `SC073-SC076`,
`SC078`) is possible for rough planning, but it is less faithful to the actual
evidence.

## 7. Promotion readiness judgment

The row is now dossier-prepared enough for human chapter-shape review, but it
is not ready for grouped promotion.

- **Most likely next move:** split `SC069-SC078` structurally into smaller
  scaffold rows before promoting anything in the region.

  `SC072-SC073`
- **Best singleton alternative:** `SC078`, especially after or alongside
  `SC079-SC087` dossier preparation
