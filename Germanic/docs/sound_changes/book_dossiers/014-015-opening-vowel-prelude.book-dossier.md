# SC014–SC015: opening vowel prelude

## Ownership and disposition

SC014 and SC015 share an editorial source, not a historical identity.
SC014 remains unstressed *ai* monophthongization; SC015 is first-syllable
short-*i* lowering. The earlier description of SC015 as unstressed
front-vowel reduction was erroneous and is superseded.
The canonical decision is [sc015-adjudication.md](../audits/sc015-adjudication.md),
REFORMULATE,16 October2026. Earlier experiment conclusions and receipts
remain historical evidence in the investigation directories.

## SC014: unchanged separate account

`PNWGmcUnstressedAiMonophthongization` maps unstressed *ai* to long *ē*
in final and nonfinal positions. Selected dative *span* and *meed*
are its live applications. Its existing later SC072 shortening boundary
and lack of a positive earlier boundary are unchanged. The stressed
*ai* change remains the separate SC004
`EAFAiMonophthongization`.
Ringe and Taylor's discussion of unstressed vowel history remains
the comparative background [@RingeTaylor2014, pp. 37--41].
No new SC014 adjudication, rewrite or movement is made here.

## SC015: adopted historical account

The unified first-nucleus short-*i* rule lowers immediately before
inherited *r*, *st*, and preconsonantal/final *z*. It no longer tests
the next vowel, an arbitrary transparent coronal class or a preceding
velar exclusion. Acute and unmarked short vowels run in parallel;
long *ī* is excluded. Its exact reader definition must match
`germanic.txt` [@Hill2017, pp. 153--158].

The scientific argument is comparative rather than score-based:
the traditional account must explain retained high vowels in
participles, adverbs and *sk* formations, often through unspecified
levelling. Hill's consonantal generalization relates positive
correspondences without predicting early lowering in every such
negative class. It remains a working reconstruction, not a
conclusively demonstrated unified event
[@Lloyd1966, pp. 738--745; @Cercignani1979, pp. 72--82;
@Cercignani1980, pp. 126--134; @RingeTaylor2014, pp. 33--36;
@Fulk2018, pp. 55--59; @Hill2017, pp. 153--158].

## Evidential components and counterevidence

The inherited-*r* component has the strongest historical and phonetic
case. The *z* component has important pronominal and reward evidence
but confronts the inherited *twizna-* family. The general productivity
of *st* lowering is less firmly established beyond *nest*.
The executable approximation *z(C|#)* does not independently establish
the syllabification of every *zn* sequence
[@Hill2017, pp. 153--158; @Howell2024, pp. 81--82, 90--91].

Twine's borrowing proposal remains disputed and does not remove
continental high-vowel evidence. Numeral-adverb sibilants, extensions
and syllabification remain conditional controls. ON *beðinn/beðenn*,
not an attested *biðinn*, poses a real question about lowering versus
analogy. OHG *irh* has inherited and borrowed accounts. These are
substantive caveats, not licensed lexical exceptions
[@KlugeSeebold2011, p. 1021; @Orel2003, p. 414;
@Kilday2024, pp. 1, 4--5; @Noreen1923, pp. 326, 332;
@Seebold1970, pp. 94--95; @Fulk2018, pp. 58--59, 286;
@Hill2017, p. 156].

## Live evidence and lexical selections

The adoption receipts enumerate seven selected applications:
2095/2313/2314 learn cells,2124 meed,2137 nest,2250 thistle,2302 world.
These represent five lexical families, not seven independent etymologies.
Thistle's later mutation masks its intermediate lowering.
Wether's masculine *wéθruz* is unchanged and does not fire.

The three independently attested targets are *leornian*, *leorna*,
*leornaþ*; inherited inputs remain *líznōjaną*, *líznô*, *líznōθi*.
Crawford and Miller establish the grammatical cells independently
of the output. Historical *liorn-* alternatives and uncertain weak-III
morphology remain explicit [@Crawford1922, pp. 71, 351;
@Miller1891, p. 380; @Jasanoff1973, pp. 865, 868;
@TarsiLaker2026, pp. 42--43].

## Chronology and placement

Retain the physical position and qualified `pnwgmc/pan_pnwgmc`
working placement, confidenceB. Hill's *hir > her > hēr* orders
the rhotic component before monosyllabic lengthening only under
his particular historical premises; it does not date *z* or *st*
[@Hill2017, pp. 156--158; @RingeTaylor2014, pp. 36--37].

The former SC015→SC036 independent edge is removed. Its *world*
result depended on the rejected following-vowel rule and the
selected mixed-stage input. The source-supported already-*wer-*
compound is not independent proof of that edge.
The PWGmcChanges boundary is retained as a runner limitation,
not a historical before-relation
[@Orel2003, p. 462; @RingeTaylor2014, p. 326].
