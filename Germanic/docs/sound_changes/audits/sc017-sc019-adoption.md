## Historical analysis

Retain the current broad Northwest Germanic stage/scope; no whole-event
restaging or source-unsupported split follows from distinguishing
conditioned realization and phonemicization. The vowel repair makes
the exceptionless condition general; it is not a wool-specific law.

The preferred order regularly accounts for selected low-root
nose/shovel/sorrow and Fulk's inherited scolu without additional
oblique-o generalization. This is more economical under the stated
inputs, but the evidential costs differ: nose is already remodeled;
Luick's inferred shovel doublet supports possible alternation, not
its direction; sorrow's comparative stem does not independently
attest early English levelling. Fulk's scolu argument depends on an
unremodeled inherited nominative root. OS skola is not pristine
nominative evidence because Fulk155 describes accusative-ending
extension. Counting matching outputs cannot replace these arguments.

Earlier raising yields regular wool *wullu / wollǭ* and love's
corresponding high/low opposition. RT32's nominative-u generalization
could account for their high-root attestations. Under that chronology,
oblique-o generalization could instead give the low-root comparison
words. Early analogy is independently credible (RT33–34; Stiles47–48),
but neither direction is directly proved for these early paradigms.
RT's wool account is therefore plausible but unproved, not contradicted.

Horna about400 and waritu about450 separately attest lowering and
raising (Fulk56,82). They give no lower bound on the other's onset
and no premise-free strict relative order. Do not conflate written
o in another ending, nasalization, bimoric/trimoric quantity, phonetic
realization, phonemicization or inscription affiliation.

The registry retains measured reciprocal SC017/019 forms, directions,
witnesses and group, but reclassifies them `technical_computational /
boundary_observation`: they are conditional computational dependencies,
not independently demonstrated historical order. SC017→late SC016
feeding remains separate. Both SC019/020 records are byte-for-byte
