## Source assessment

Ringe and Taylor2014 pp.44-48, section3.1.2, treats loss of oral and nasal
unstressed short low vowels as West Germanic, before j-gemination; p.47
uses i-stem/ja-stem reanalysis, mettas beside mete, and OHG beti as evidence.
Glide nuclearization and later paradigm extension obscure regular reflexes.
Printed45n4 explicitly qualifies absolute dates from inscriptions.
Fulk2018 pp.79-81 section5.2 distinguishes earlier unprotected loss from
protected short vowels and nuclearized glides; pp.91-92 section5.6
explicitly denies a preceding-weight condition on West Germanic a/anasal
loss. Its subsequent high-vowel loss is a different phenomenon. Campbell1959
pp.137-140 section331 distinguishes protected-a loss and surviving genitive
-as; the old reader's p.166 section403 concerns nasal loss before fricatives,
not the proposed secondary nasalization. Hogg2011 pp.62-64 sections3.27-32
separates final-consonant loss, earlier vowel loss and the later z-fed loss.

Ringe and Taylor pp.152-154 section5.1.2 conditions nonfronting of
unstressed a on a tautosyllabic nasal, not word-final n alone. Original
printed153 (PDF168) was image-checked: bindaną > bindan > bindąn;
bindand- has remodelled PWGmc bindandī > bindąndī > bindændi > bindende.
The internal coda therefore matters, but i-mutation subsequently removes
the low back quality. Their guma paradigm has regular an in acc.sg. and
nom.pl., extended to other cells; gumena involves syncope, restoration of
a from paradigm material and dissimilation. This is positive paradigmatic
evidence, not permission to repair any failed regular trial by analogy.
Fulk2018 pp.168-172 sections7.30-33 offers a partly different history:
acc.sg. inherited un (compare Ruthwell galgu), with an extended from
other cases; nominative plural an is a concrete donor. Keep those
reconstructions distinct. Neither guma nor reconstructed alternate cells
are new selected corpus entries in this pass.

Hogg2011 pp.214-217 sections6.1-6 explicitly puts coda nasalization before
first fronting, contrasts heterosyllabic nasals and notes secondary/tertiary
stress complications. Ringe and Taylor pp.189-194 sections6.3.1-2 explains
stressed restoration and paradigm alternations; pp.222-223,236-237,251-252
section6.6 explicitly describes foot-local iterative i-mutation. These
are not evidence for the serialized adjacency of CAPR operations.

Ringe and Taylor pp.319-327 section6.9.4 and Hogg2011 pp.149-157
sections5.103-106 distinguish dialects and vowels: in West Saxon e is
back-mutated by following u, not a. Thus stelan and wefan do not by
themselves establish nasalization before back mutation. Ringe and Taylor
p.325 explicitly contrasts Mercian weofan with WS wefan. Nasal quality
can still affect CAPR's broader low-front-vowel trigger, to be tested
separately. Campbell pp.85-86 sections205-206 describes the general
domain, not a uniform vowel-independent WS trigger. Hogg and Ringe/Taylor
report Ball and Stiles1983 pp.5-28, especially11-14, for glossary chronology.
The Ball/Stiles original is not held or newly verified. Stiles1983
pp.415-418 author-deposited abstract was read: secure early combinative
wudu is Northumbrian; the Mercian glossaries show both mutation types
together. Full original not obtained; do not upgrade this to manuscript
collation. Versloot2026 pp.105,111-113,120-121 argues for a different
fronting/mutation chronology; its broad alternative cannot be equated
with moving one CAPR rule. SC045/046 remain settled for this pass.

Faulkner2020, author preprint sections2-3, pp.3-7, directly consulted:
19,315 infinitives; 96.1% written an overall, very consistent written an
even during inferred phonetic reduction. Manuscript dates, editorial
normalization and conservative orthography limit early phonetic inference.
Written en alone neither dates early apocope nor disproves the reconstructed
nasal stage. Fulk2010 pp.126-142 and Goering2016/2023 are reused through
the accepted source-critical high-vowel-deletion dossier: quantitative HVD
is distinct, not an explanation for loss of short final a/ą.
