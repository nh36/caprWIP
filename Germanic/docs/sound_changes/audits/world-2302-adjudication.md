### Paradigm and dated analogy analysis

This is a partial comparative paradigm, not an invented attested *world*
paradigm. Fulk2018 §§7.22–23 pp.161–162 describes feminine i-stems:
OE often retains endingless accusative, while accusative *-e* is borrowed
from ō-stems; the old genitive and dative have their own disputed histories.
For *world* specifically Campbell supplies that accusative evidence.
The comparative nominative *-iz*, genitive *-aiz* and a proposed dative
*-ī* must not all be treated as the same umlaut-triggering cell.
In the complete current model third-nucleus short *i* is lost by
`PWGmcEarlyIApocope` before umlaut. Consequently the unmutated nominative
cannot by itself date the reclassification before umlaut. The generic
pre-umlaut substitutions in Campbell §610(4) concern several nouns;
they are not a world-specific dating proof.

For ō-stems Fulk2018 §§7.14–15 pp.154–156 distinguishes nominative
*-ō > -u*, accusative nasal *-ǭ > OE -e* in CAPR's spelling,
and genitive *-ôz* (his reconstruction) versus *-ōz* (Ringe–Taylor's).
OE generalised genitive *-e* is analogical in the former analysis, potentially
regular in the latter. A feminine class is therefore not one interchangeable
ending. These general endings establish morphological contrasts, not actual
unattested forms of *world* or an analogical donor cell.

The additional six schematic full-start comparisons record:
short-i `*wír-aldi → wereald`; long-i dative `*wír-aldī`, genitive
`*wír-aldaiz` and trimoric `*wír-aldôz` are rejected by the input grammar;
nasal accusative `*wír-aldǭ` and bimoric genitive `*wír-aldōz` give
`werealde`. These are diagnostics of coverage, not proof that those forms
existed or that every oblique was irregular. Ordinary documented Unicode
spellings are used; the parser lacks these full word shapes despite
recognising the vowel symbols elsewhere.

Positive evidence for remodelling is the comparative class divergence and
the scholarly *weraldiz >— weraldu* account, not simply a failed transducer
prediction. However, Campbell prevents claiming that the entire lexeme lost
all i-stem inflection. No single analogical source cell or direction of
within-paradigm levelling is established. A regular surviving ending is a
candidate for future fuller paradigm work, not a fabricated fully regular
selected cell. Fulk2010's conservative-Mercian method supports this caution;
its neuter *hēafud* paradigms cannot simply be transferred to this feminine.

If an analogical input is selected, its defensible interval is relative,
not a calendar date: after formation of an inherited i-stem compound and,
in the u-conditioned analysis, before the particular post-reclassification
*-u* path feeding weak medial vocalism.
Reassignment could supply an already productive *-u* after SC019, or an
earlier *-ō* before SC019. No independent evidence chooses between those
placements for this word. Nor does lack of nominative umlaut settle it.
Retaining `early_analogy` describes preparation of the selected input before
its modelled regular tail, not a proven pan-Germanic date or wholesale
replacement of the documented OE paradigm.

This does not establish that the selected conservative cell requires an
analogical stem. Campbell's endingless accusative is the best actual
conservative-cell lead; its stem history still needs assessment. The
comparative i-stem nominative is the best presently accepted full-start
experimental comparator. No complete source-backed input/output cell pair
with a demonstrated uninterrupted regular history is selected in its place.
Parser rejection of the schematic nasal accusative is a coverage gap,
not evidence against its historical regularity.

## Historical analysis

### Prosody, linking vowel and dialect

Fulk2018 pp.36–38 separates protective true compound secondary accent from
weakening in obscured compounds. Goering2023 §4.5.1.2 pp.79–82 argues
for initial trimoraic `(we-ruld)`, not a light initial foot followed by a
secondary foot `(we)(ruld)`. Apocope of *weruldu* would occur under either
analysis; it does not decide between them. Medial reduction/loss and the
inflected poetic forms support ordinary unstressed behaviour.
The actual verses are Beowulf3068a *þurh hwæt his worulde ge·dāl* and
2343a *worulde līfes*. His `xworolde brēac` and `xcyninga bearn` are
hypothetical unmetrical controls, not manuscript readings.

This permits an earlier secondary accent but neither reconstructs its PGmc
acoustic degree nor independently dates its loss before SC036. Goering's
c.600 and c.650–750 framework is his approximate footing chronology,
not a date of deaccentuation for this compound. Hogg1996 pp.3,9–11
warns that an unexplained “tertiary stress” can merely name the outcome;
Minkova1996 pp.20–29 cautions against equating metrical ictus and lexical
stress, without rejecting secondary stress or all metrical evidence.
Her tentative stressability hierarchy is not permission for grammatical
conditioning of ordinary sound laws.

The current grave marks the second root, not the lost linking vowel.
Removing it from the input has no effect on the selected world final,
but global early removal damages another compound. Delaying raising until
after linking syncope and stripping preserves all finals; this demonstrates
a marker-compatible serialization, not an independently dated historical
deaccentuation law. There is no independently supported new conditioning
domain and additional witness set warranting such a production law.

Literal *wíra-àldō* yields `weorauld`, not `weorold`. The
consonantal right context of SC037 does not delete a vowel immediately
followed by the second root vowel. The synthetic inserted-consonant
control does syncopate. Hiatus resolution and consonant-separated linking
syncope are different hypotheses. The compact input presupposes a reduced
compound stem; comparative compact forms make it plausible, but no identified
sound law independently derives its absence of *a*. This is an acknowledged
coverage gap, not a newly established PGmc contraction.

OE *weorold/worold*, *weoruld/woruld*, Kentish *wiarald* and later
*world* constrain different dimensions: initial diphthongisation/rounding,
medial weak *a/o/u*, and subsequent vowel loss. Ringe–Taylor p.326
derives the exceptional WS diphthong by combinative back umlaut; it is not
breaking of the initial *e*. The i-stem diagnostic instead exposes fronting
and breaking of the **medial** vowel in the current executable.
Kentish medial *a* and lack of the stronger-stress breaking outcome,
and Goering's ordinary weak medial behaviour, constrain prominence without
forcing every dialect through medial *u*. The source-reported forms are
attested variants, not fresh manuscript collations in this investigation.
No dialect-specific production branch or late-world syncope is added.

### Historical phenomenon versus executable alternatives

The source-led account begins with an inherited compound, weakening of its
second member and weak-vowel retraction/rounding. Partial class mixing is
independently supported, but it need not precede that vocalism. Campbell
§338 and Hogg §§6.4–7 distinguish weak retraction/rounding from ordinary
breaking and protective secondary stress; Luick §113 explicitly questions
whether Bülbring's following-*u* explanation covers the whole development.
None establishes a uniform *o/u* boundary or a non-circular phonetic
definition of the often descriptive “tertiary” stress category.

Under the unchanged executable the inherited i-stem loses short *i* at
exec8, loses final *z* at23, and misses SC036 at45. Stripping at47 gives
medial *a*, fronting at51 gives *æ*, breaking at54 gives atomic *ea*:
`wereald`. The decisive mismatch is not rejection or obligatory umlaut.
A private pre-*lC* raising candidate, without following-*u* requirement,
after51 instead gives *æ → u*, back mutation and later medial *u → o*:
`weorold`. Inserting it while retaining the existing proxy elsewhere
changes no selected final, including soul. This disproves computational
necessity of the analogical ending; it does not prove the candidate law.

The six requested families and14 new configurations are compared in the
[experiment report](world-2302-experiments/README.md). The further
rounding-only comparison matters: *o* introduced early later becomes *a*
through `OEFinalUnstressedOLowering`, exec84. Its failure is not evidence
that a historical rounded *o* stage was impossible. Further raising to *u*
before back mutation succeeds, but no independent source dates that exact
split sequence for this conservative cell.

A bounded semantic discrepancy was also discovered in `OEBackMutation`.
The current comma-separated definition shares its contexts: acute/plain
*e* can mutate before liquid + *o* or *a*, despite the apparent *u*-only
branches. Explicitly composing isolated *u*-only branches removes that
behaviour while preserving the broad *æ* branch. The control changes no
selected final. With the early *o*-only candidate it gives *werald* rather
than *weorald*. Therefore the executable does not establish that only *u*
can feed initial back mutation. This finding neither changes that rule nor
reopens its historical adjudication; narrower ordinary WS conditioning and
the exceptional treatment of *world* remain source questions.

The strongest inherited candidate is consequently:
inherited compact i-stem → early final-*i* loss → weak medial retraction/
rounding and, conditionally, further raising → initial back mutation →
late medial lowering → *weorold*. The compact junction and the exact
rounding/raising domain still require independent justification. A formula
changing every weak *a/æ* before *lC* does not distinguish ordinary weak
vowels from former secondary-stressed vowels. Synthetic noncompound and
twofold-shaped probes expose that breadth; *twifald/twifold* is not an
attested *twifuld* or proof of a universal earlier high stage. This is a
historical conditioning problem, not merely a missing parser shape.
