## Question

Hypothesis before experiments: ordinary unstressed fronting, a positive
representation of pretonic versus foot-headed syllables, regular raising
after a palatal fricative, and later unstressed merger explain more
source-backed forms than the compressed proxy. No sound law may test
grammatical prefix identity.

Confirm with complete-cascade predictions for believe, the sourced
clustered-prefix comparison, and Hogg's prefixed/unprefixed shaft pair,
while preserving stressed palatal-diphthongization witnesses. Refute a
proposed raising law with a securely weak, early non-high vowel sharing
its phonological environment after the relevant palatalization; a stressed
vowel, a different dialect/stage, or mere alternative analogy is not that
counterexample. Compare the staged account without raising as well.

## Literature

Acquisition and bibliographic integration are recorded in
`docs/references/README_resources.md`, `Germanic/docs/REFERENCES.md`
and `docs/refs.bib`. Full sources, not abstracts, were obtained.

- Hogg, *Grammar*, §5.48 n.1: the held 2011 reprint's printed p.104,
  PDF sheet117, image-checked. A palatal directly before a stressed vowel
  begins a stress foot; word-initiality is unnecessary. Both *sċeaft*
  and *ġesċeaft* diphthongize after the root's sh-sound, not after the
  latter's initial j-sound. The main section distinguishes original,
  palatalized-velar and sk-derived palatals and different vowel histories;
  its foot statement does not erase those distinctions.
  Cite `HoggGrammar1992`, never `HoggPhonology1992`, for this passage.
- Hogg §§6.50–6.54, held pp.237–239: merger/lowering includes weak
  pretonic be-/ge-, while following palatals protect suffixal i.
  Early Mercian glossary i-spellings (§6.53) prevent interpreting Berg's
  "northern" summary as an exclusive Northumbrian distribution.
- Minkova and **Z. L. Zhou** 2022, **533–558**, especially pp.535–536
  (PDF sheets3–4), image-checked: root stress, unstressable weak
  prefixes and metrical evidence. Their noun/verb stress distinctions
  concern reconstructed inputs and prosody, not grammatical conditioning
  of vowel laws. The article does not establish pre-OE raising chronology.
  The full published article is CC BY4.0.
- Riegger, Bögel and Walkden 2024, pp.2065–2068: three manuscript
  studies and a separate modern German experiment. OE ge- may group
  with preceding material; morphological and prosodic boundaries need
  not coincide. Most tokens retain ordinary grouping. The modern
  auxiliary/negation results differ; modern German duration does not
  date English vowel changes. Citation-word modelling cannot pretend
  to reconstruct every sentence-level refooting.
- Berg 2014, pp.60–61, especially p.60 (PDF sheet72), image-checked:
  Pforzen ga- limits an early common-WGmc gi- claim. His preference
  for bi-analogy rests here on the stated unattractiveness of phonological
  development, not positive substitution evidence. This is not a
  counterexample to a later English change after palatalization.
  The complete 104-sheet thesis was obtained from the verified Oslo/NVA
  record (handle10852/39907), after the supplied CORE link failed.
- Ringe and Taylor 2014, pp.334–335: regular raising of pretonic
  unstressed front vowel after a palatal fricative is explicitly possible;
  the alternative influence of bi- is a suggestion, not demonstrated
  substitution. They supply early gi-, later ge- and the clustered
  comparison. Their adjacent-palatal suffix examples support phonetic
  plausibility, not an independently attested pretonic non-ga witness.

Source authors' interpretations remain distinct from CAPR's inference:
regular raising is the preferred working hypothesis absent a phonological
counterexample or positive analogy evidence, not a demonstrated unique
event with a precisely known date.

## Historical analysis

- Historical stage/scope: retain English pre-OE onset with OE completion;
  do not turn continental gi- into proof of one pan-WGmc raising.
- Regular phonological conditioning: test raising of a short non-high
  front vowel immediately after a palatal fricative in a weak pretonic
  syllable before a positively headed foot. No morpheme/category test.
- Counterexamples and falsification: test stressed ga-, non-palatal
  pretonic vowels, genuine stressed roots, clustered onsets and
  palatal-diphthongization positives. No independently sourced non-ga
  positive sharing the entire proposed early environment has yet been
  identified; an artificial same-environment string is not an attestation.
- Positive evidence for analogy: none in the consequential passages
  consulted. Berg's preference and possible bi-influence do not discharge
  that burden. Analogy remains possible, not the default explanation.
- Proxy/domain distinction: the incumbent environment is not the
  historical domain; a general stress-foot representation must be
  specified and tested independently of grammatical prefix recognition.
- Chronology: the earlier model-local SC035/SC107 dependency is not a
  historical date. Raising, if adopted, presupposes its palatal trigger;
  preserve the independently supported ordinary-PD-before-mutation chain.
  No experimental change alone licenses a new historical edge.

## Verdict

Registry-verdict: SC035=REFORMULATE/REORDER; SC056=REFORMULATE; SC073=REFORMULATE; SC076=REFORMULATE

Adopt model C: ordinary pretonic fronting, regular raising of short
pretonic ae immediately after the palatal fricative, and later lowering
of weak i to e. The wider historical development is not a morpheme-conditioned
operation. Exact raising date and independent lexical breadth remain less
secure than the attested gi/ge sequence.

The complete isolated comparison preserves every final output in selected389
and the unchanged migration-aware legacy380 population. Intermediate changes
are confined to begin1943 and believe1944. Both staged models correctly
derive the source-backed clustered climb comparison and Hogg's prefixed
shaft comparison; the incumbent gives giestigan and geasceft respectively.
The positively specified long-stress control retains long i, whereas an
otherwise similar stress-erased input is deliberately not newly classified
as weak. This is a falsifiable improvement over the earlier ga-based flag.

Model B explains fronting, stress-foot diphthongization and eventual e,
but does not supply the attested early i. Model C supplies it through
Ringe and Taylor's explicitly possible regular conditioning. Neither the
consulted Berg passage nor the competing bi suggestion gives positive
substitution evidence; no phonological counterexample was identified.
The absence of an independent non-ga positive limits confidence in the
generalization, not its formal domain. C is therefore the preferred
regular working reconstruction, not a uniquely proven microchronology.
The previous provisional preference for compression is superseded.

restriction is replaced by headed-foot initiality. SC073 adds positively
identified pretonic ae to its existing post-tonic merger. SC076 replaces
