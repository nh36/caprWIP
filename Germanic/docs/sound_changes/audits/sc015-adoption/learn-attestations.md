## Alternative vowels, morphology and scope

The former selections *liornian*, *liorna*, *liornaþ* remain recorded
alternatives. Campbell (1959, pp.48,59,125, §123 n.2, §154 n.3 and §296) and Sievers–Brunner
(1965, §84 and §417 n.10), discussed in the existing lexical memos,
document *liorn-* alongside *leorn-*; *io* is not uniquely Northumbrian,
and early West Saxon also has *liorn-*.
The earlier row2314 memo constructed a normalized third-singular
*liornaþ* from the stem and paradigm; it did not verify a specific token.
Preserving that selection as historical evidence does not justify falsely
upgrading it to a newly inspected manuscript attestation.

Jasanoff (1973, pp.865,868) and Tarsi–Laker (2026, pp.42–43) motivate
weak-classIII prehistory and later remodelling; the exact inherited
paradigm, mutation distribution and levelling remain uncertain.
The finite rows retain `late_analogy` for that family history, not because
their PROTOFORM selects a finite cell. Their selected vowel is now
derived by SC015, not by a newly posited analogical vowel replacement.

The precise target migration changes2095 `liornian → leornian`,2313
`liorna → leorna`,2314 `liornaþ → leornaþ`, the corresponding TOKENS,
and the finite IPA vowel. Infinitive IPA and all ALIGNMENT values remain
independent comparison data. All inherited inputs, stages, contexts,
IDs, other languages and other lexical selections remain unchanged.
