# Source-critical diagnoses of the nonhigh conflicts

## Find2011: the record does not establish a regular masculine accusative

The selected input is `*fúnðanǭ`, target *fundene*. The mature article,
ledger and memo describe it as a wholly regular masculine accusative
singular, although the row is classified `late_analogy`.

The actual cited phrase, verified in the original dictionary Supplement,
is “Beón þá herigeata swa fundene” (Toller 1921, p.219, *findan*, sense
II.9, with citation Ll. Th. i.414,4 note). This is a plural passive
construction: the heriots are to be supplied/arranged thus. Whatever
precise gender is assigned to the plural subject, this quotation does
not exemplify the claimed masculine accusative singular. Thorpe's cited
page/manuscript has not been separately collated, so no exact witness
date or dialect is inferred from the modern dictionary's publication.
The holding's generic filename conceals its identity: its title and
copyright pages identify the Supplement, first edition 1921, not the
1898 main dictionary currently cited in the article.

There is a second, independent morphological issue. Fulk2018 §9.9 p.218
says past participles decline as adjectives. His strong adjective table
§9.2 p.209 reconstructs masculine acc.sg. *-anōⁿ* (CAPR *-anǭ*).
Applied compositionally to the participial stem *funðan-*, that supplies
an uncontracted morphological comparison *funðan-anǭ*, not the currently
selected *funð-anǭ*. Conversely *funðan-ǭ* can represent a feminine
ō-stem accusative shape; that does not make the cited plural sentence
evidence for that cell. A contracted/allomorphic earlier form would
require its own evidence. The experiment does not assume that an
uncontracted comparison proves every prehistoric participial allomorph.

Most decisively, Hogg1992 §§6.15–16, pp.221–222 explicitly contrasts
syncopated masculine accusatives *ġebundne, ġeworpne* with analogically
restored *ġebundene, ġeworpene*, noting the particular frequency of
restoration in participles. Campbell §341 p.143 likewise lists
*ġebundne*, and §343 p.144 independently discusses restoration in
inflected participles. Campbell §334 p.141 establishes medial fronting,
not exemption from earlier nonhigh syncope.

## West2282: neither the target nor still-heavy ending is secured

The source claim must be separated into three statements:

1. Old English has directional *westan, westane*. Clark Hall p.350
   gives both, glossed ‘from the west’. These are actual dictionary forms.
2. Orel p.459 reconstructs *westan-* with a final *e* bearing both a
   breve and a macron. Original high-resolution inspection resolves
   the mark: it is not merely plain *ē*, nor OCR's isolated *ě*.
   The notation does not by itself establish the long quantity
   selected by CAPR as the only historical possibility.
3. The exact target *westene* was chosen as a generated normalization
   in the archived local slice. The cited Clark Hall entry does not
   establish that exact form. This is not a claim that no manuscript
   could ever contain it; it is a failure of the present source support.
   Dictionary occurrences of *westene* meaning ‘desert’ are a different
   lexeme and cannot validate the directional adverb.

Fulk2018 §11.2 pp.237–238 gives directional *-nē*, comparing *aftana*
and OE *æftan*, and cognate directional formations. Campbell §§677–678,
p.280 and Brunner §321 describe from-place *-an* and fuller *-ane*
byforms. They establish the formation and alternants, not the exact
quantity and ordering needed to preserve medial *a* in the CAPR
three-syllable representation.

Fulk2018 §5.3 pp.82–83 only tentatively derives endingless locatives
from loss of bare final bimoric *ē*, with alternative explanations.
He distinguishes originally protected endings and explicitly flags
difficulties with weak preterites. That is not a demonstrated general
directional-adverb *ē*-apocope rule. The current population also contains
final *ē* in *wúndōdē* and *θánxtē*: indiscriminate deletion would
silently reopen their endings. No such law was invented here.

distinction that would protect it while deleting soul's medial vowel.
Component probes preserve medial *a* in closed-final *westan* and in the
short-ending sensitivity *westane*. These are transparent weight tests,
not demonstrations of the earlier chronological route to the attested

claim; categories 2 and 3 (quantity and chronology) remain genuinely
open. There is no positive evidence for an adverb exemption, no proved
new general sound change, and no basis to declare the new law either
refuted or vindicated by this corrupted comparison. Resolve an attested
target and its earlier ending stratum before adoption. Retain the
current record untouched in the meantime, not as certified science.

## Hammer2053: the additional light-root diagnostic

Hogg §§6.14–15 explicitly includes light-root nonhigh deletion:
*awles, fæġnes*. The heavy-initial fragment therefore cannot be a
complete nonhigh law. G changes only that restriction and additionally
predicts *hamres* for selected `*xámaras → hameres`.

The live row and mature article already carry `late_analogy`, while
their prose and ledger claim a straightforward regular selected genitive.
That documentary discrepancy must not be hidden. Campbell §574.3,
pp.226–227 describes transfer of a parasitic vowel to inflected forms,
especially after short roots before *r*, and includes *hamor* among the
relevant nouns; Hogg §6.16 supplies the general restoration mechanism.
This is positive support for an analogical route to a nonsyncopated
inflected form, not a lexical exception to syncope. It is not, by
itself, a complete source-checked derivation or an attestation of the
particular experimental *hamres*.

The exact retained genitive is independently real: Toller Supplement
p.66 has *beátendes hameres*, glossing *tundentis mallei*, An. Ox.480.
The mature record's locator 78 is this holding's PDF sheet, not its
printed page. Neither that locator nor the analogy claim is corrected
in production as a side effect of this investigation.

Disposition: a credible restoration/formation issue needing a bounded
lexical adjudication if G is pursued. The change is not discarded
because the old output matched, and the class label is not substituted
for historical evidence. No hammer SOURCE is edited and no wider new
syncope replacement is launched to repair it in this assignment.
