## Historical analysis

- Stage: preliterary/early Old English for the relevant syncope, compared
  with later inflectional levelling. No new absolute dating is established.
- Scope: the Old English development; this is not a new branch-topology
  or common-West-Germanic judgement.
- Conditioning: short, unstressed, open-medial vowels; preceding and
  following weight must be evaluated before later shortening and loss.
  Case, adjective class, participial status, adverb status and word
  identity are not sound-law conditions.
- Counterexamples: west's historical weight/chronology is unresolved;
  the initial fragment misses light-root positives; G's wider domain
  introduces hammer. The full uncontracted participial probe remains
  *fundenene*, demonstrating the fragment's limited arity, not a new
  attested form. Warmth's current-source comparator fails independently
  of new nonhigh syncope; see `results.md`.
- Analogy: Hogg §6.16 explicitly gives restored *ġebundene, ġeworpene*;
  Campbell §343 independently describes restoration in participles.
  Campbell §574.3 describes transfer of a parasitic vowel to inflected
  forms, especially after short syllables before *r*, and lists *hamor*.
  These support possible historical dispositions; they do not themselves
  authorize a selected-input migration. Fulk's *-iþō* argument has
  positive light-root evidence, not merely a failed FST.
- Proxy relation: C is a heavy-initial fragment, not the whole handbook
  nonhigh law; G expands root weight but remains a bounded fragment.
  The existing high-vowel restrictions remain technical proxies.
  SC040's later computed prominence is not imported into earlier syncope.
- Chronology: i-umlaut before the high-vowel deletion relevant to the
  umlauted abstracts is independently supported by those forms; the old
  displacement evidence is retained. Nonhigh deletion before the
  relevant later unstressed shortening is source-supported, but the
  precise shared site immediately before SC063 is CAPR serialization,
  not an independently established edge. Fulk2010 n.20 prevents claiming
  a universally demonstrated identical nonhigh/high rule. No registry
  chronology edge is added or reclassified.
