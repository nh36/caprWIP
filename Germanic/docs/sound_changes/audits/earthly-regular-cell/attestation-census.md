## Question

- **Hypothesis or suspected problem (falsifiable):** Ciszek-Kiliszewska
  (2015: 489–490) reports six DOEC tokens of *eorþcund* (against 761 of
  *godcund*). The two prior-audit dictionary loci
  (`rainbow-earthly-secondary-stress/lexical-evidence.md`) are not a
  complete concordance. Hypothesis: further *eorþcund* (not *eorþcundlic*,
  not *eardcund*) tokens, with recoverable grammatical cells, are
  independently discoverable from dictionary citation networks already
  held in this repository's source library, without new DOEC access.
- **What would confirm/refute it:** confirmation = a distinct, dated,
  citable edition/manuscript locus with a genuine *eorþcund-* (not
  *-cundlic*, not *eardcund-*) inflected spelling. Refutation = no further
  loci recoverable by bounded, documented search, leaving the census
  honestly incomplete.

## Current state (before this research)

`rainbow-earthly-secondary-stress/lexical-evidence.md` had recovered only
two loci directly from Bosworth–Toller: *eorþcunde* (Gen. 1627) and
*eorþcundre* (An. Ox. 691), and explicitly flagged *eardcundes* (Rtl.
33, 36) as a different first constituent, not an *eorþcund* token. It
recorded that the DOEC URL linked from the dictionary redirected to the
Toronto library homepage, and that a bounded search for *eorþcundes*,
*eorðcundes*, *eorþcundne* found no primary-text occurrence. This
census is additional work, not a repeat of that search.

All three loci below were checked directly against the live

### 1. Genesis A 1627 — *eorþ-cunde* / *eorð-cunde*

- **Dictionary locus:** Bosworth–Toller online
  [entry 9582](https://bosworthtoller.com/9582), s.v. *eorþ-cund*:
  "Se rinc ageaf eorþ-cunde eád. ... Cd. 79; Th. 98, 8; Gen. 1627."
  Also repeated under *eád*
  [entry 8279](https://bosworthtoller.com/8279), which gives *eád*'s own
  dictionary head as **"EÁD, es; n."** (neuter, genitive *-es*), with
  cognates *ód*, *ôt*, *auðr* also listed. **Correction:** this
  research's earlier draft additionally asserted that those cognates are
  themselves "neuter/masculine n-stems" — that characterization is
  **withdrawn**: it was not independently verified against the
  individual Old Saxon/Old High German/Old Icelandic grammars and is not
  needed for the point actually established, which rests only on B-T's
  own explicit "n." tag for the OE headword *eád* itself.
- **Edition checked directly:** Thorpe, Benjamin. 1832. *Cædmon's
  Metrical Paraphrase of Parts of Holy Scripture*. London: Society of
  Antiquaries. Public-domain Google-digitized copy,
  <https://archive.org/details/caedmonsmetrica01thorgoog> (OCR'd `_djvu.txt`
  stream fetched directly, 2026-11-01). The page there carrying Thorpe's
  own page-number "98" (matching the dictionary's "Th. 98, 8" pointer)
  gives the Old English couplet (OCR'd blackletter, þ/ð/ƿ imperfectly
  rendered but legible):

  > "þa fe rinc ageaf · eorð-cunde eáð(/eád). sohte oðer lif."

  i.e. "then the prince gave up earthly {eád}; sought another life."
  This independently locates the passage in the edition's OCR, supporting
  *eád* as the direct object of *ageaf* ('gave up, surrendered') and
  the absence of a determiner or possessive governing the adjective/noun
  phrase. The OCR appears to give *eorð-cunde*, but the exact printed
  þ/ð glyph and hyphenation have not been checked against a page image.
  An OCR decoding is not proof of an editor's normalization.
- **Not independently checked:** the Junius 11 manuscript image itself,
  and Krapp's ASPR *Genesis A* (1931) critical text and apparatus for
  line 1627, or Thorpe's original printed page image. Only Thorpe's
  digitized edition OCR and the two dictionary
  quotations of it were read. A possible manuscript-vs-Thorpe-vs-Krapp
  spelling/punctuation divergence (e.g. þ vs ð, hyphenation, whether
  Krapp prints *eorðcunde* as one word) is **not resolved** here; this is
  a residual access gap, not a claim that Krapp's text was consulted.
- **Grammatical cell (this research's conclusion — see morphology.md):**
  weak neuter accusative singular, agreeing with neuter *eád*, licensed
  attributively without an overt determiner by the attested OE poetic
  convention for substantival/non-specifier weak adjectives.

### 2. An. Ox. 691 — *eorþcundre* (glossary; exact edition/manuscript not independently established)

- **Dictionary locus:** Bosworth–Toller online
  [entry 44148](https://bosworthtoller.com/44148), s.v. *eorþ-cund*
  (Supplement): "Ealre eorþcundre gesceafte totius terrestris (i.
  terrene) creaturae, An. Ox. 691." Also directly confirmed in the
  physical Supplement OCR at `bosworth_toller_anglo_saxon_dictionary.vision.txt:65416`.
  "An. Ox." is B-T's standard sigil for texts printed in the *Anecdota
  Oxoniensia* series, which includes glossarial material edited by
  Napier among others. **Correction:** this research's earlier draft
  additionally attributed the specific text to "Ælfric's (or Ps.-)
  Antwerp–London Glossary" — that attribution is **withdrawn**: it was
  not independently verified against a B-T sigla list or the *Anecdota
  Oxoniensia* volume itself, and no such list was located in the OCR
  texts held in this repository (see the access-log note on Rtl. below
  for the same limitation applied to a different sigil). The genre
  (glossary, glossing a Latin lemma) is clear from the dictionary's own
  Latin apparatus ("totius terrestris (i. terrene) creaturae"), but the
  exact edition/compiler/manuscript behind "An. Ox. 691" is **not
  established** by this research and should not be asserted. The
  specific volume/page behind "691" was **not** independently retrieved
  (no holding in this repository's library; not located online in the
  time available). This locus is therefore checked only at one remove,
  through the dictionary's direct quotation, not against the underlying
  edition itself.
- **Source-critical flag (independently re-verified from the dictionary's
  own structure, not merely the web front end):** the Supplement's
  physical entry for the headword *eorp-cund* reads, verbatim and in this
  order:

  > "eorp-cund. Add:—Eardcundes aldores terreni parentis, Rtl. 33, 36.
  > Ealre eorpcundre gesceafte totius terrestris (i. terrene) creaturae,
  > An. Ox. 691. [Goth. airþa-kunds.]"

  i.e. the dictionary's *own print layout* files *Eardcundes aldores*
  (Rtl. 33, 36 — sigil identity corrected below, glossing Latin
  *terreni parentis*) as the **first** citation under the *eorþ-cund* headword, immediately
  before the genuine *eorþcundre* citation, with a single shared closing
  etymological bracket. This is not a scraping artifact of the website:
  it is how Toller's Supplement itself groups the two forms on the page.
  *Eardcundes* is **not** an *eorþcund* token (different first
  constituent, *eard-* 'native land/dwelling' vs. *eorþ-* 'earth'); it
  is kept here only as a documented dictionary-structure explanation for
  why it must be excluded from this census. This does not explain the
  missing three DOEC tokens. Confirmed separately at
  `bosworth_toller_anglo_saxon_dictionary.vision.txt:31831`
  ("eorp-cund. Add:—Eardcundes aldores ... Ealre eorpcundre gesceafte
  ... [Goth. airþa-kunds.]" followed immediately, as a **separate**
  headword, by "eorþcund-lic; adj. ... Past. 78, 18").
- **Grammatical cell:** strong feminine genitive singular, agreeing with
  *gesceafte* ('creation'), within a partitive/quantifying construction
  governed by *Ealre* ('of all'). This is the hardest-to-derive cell
  (competing *-izōz*/*-aizōz* histories; see morphology.md) and is **not**
  recommended here as the regular cell to adopt.

### 3. Rule of St Benedict (OE), Schröer's edition, R. Ben. 139, 27 — *eorþcundum* (NEW — not in the prior two-locus record)

- **Dictionary locus:** found independently in this research, not
  recorded in the prior `rainbow-earthly-secondary-stress` audit. Live
  bosworthtoller.com full-text search (2026-11-01) for the inflected
  form *eorþcundum* returns, s.v. *grædiglice* (Supplement, sense "with
  manifestation of strong desire, eagerly"):

  > "Hí swíþe grǽdilíce (inhianter) eorþcundum lustum filigaþ, R. Ben.
  > 139, 27."

  and, in fuller context, s.v. *sceamleáslíce* (Supplement):

  > "Hí swíðe grǽdilíce eorþcundum lustum filigaþ and oft swíðe
  > sceamleáslíce on manna gesyhþe, R. Ben. 139, 28."

  These are **the same single occurrence** of *eorþcundum*, quoted twice
  under two different headwords (hence the apparent line-number
  discrepancy, 139,27 vs. 139,28 — the sentence spans the cited lines and
  each headword's own keyword falls on a different line); this is one
  token, not two. Independently confirmed in the physical Supplement OCR
  at `bosworth_toller_anglo_saxon_dictionary.vision.txt:77974` ("Hi swipe
  grædilice (inhianter) eorþcundum lustum filigaþ,") with the citation
  "R. Ben. 139, 27" on the following OCR line.
- **Source:** "R. Ben." is Bosworth–Toller's standing abbreviation for
  the Old English prose *Regula Benedicti* (Rule of St Benedict),
  edited by Arnold Schröer, *Die angelsächsischen Prosabearbeitungen der
  Benedictinerregel* (Kassel, 1885–88; reissued with an appendix by
  Helmut Gneuss, 1964). **Not** held in this repository's reference
  library; the edition itself was not independently retrieved, only the
  dictionary's quotation of it. This is a genuine access gap: the exact
  manuscript basis (B-T's "R. Ben." vs. "R. Ben. I." = the interlinear
  version) behind this particular line was not confirmed beyond the
  dictionary's citation sigla.
- **Syntax:** *fylgan*/*filigan* ('follow') governs the dative in Old
  English; *eorþcundum lustum* ('earthly lusts/desires') is the dative
  object of *filigaþ*. *lust* is independently attested as a masculine
  a-stem noun (Bosworth–Toller s.v. *lust*: nom./acc. pl. *lustas*,
  dat. sg. *luste*); *lustum* is accordingly its dative plural, and
  *eorþcundum* is the strong adjective agreeing with it in the same
  case/number, with the ordinary OE syncretic *-um* surface ending
  (masc./neut./fem. dat. pl. are not distinguished).
- **Grammatical cell:** strong dative plural. The surface ending *-um*
  is gender-syncretic in OE (masc./neut./fem. dat.pl are not
  distinguished), but the adjective's gender **agreement target is
  recoverable**: it agrees with *lustum*, the independently-attested
  masculine a-stem noun *lust* (see below) — so the cell is best
  recorded as **masculine dative plural by agreement**, not as an
  ungendered "n/a" cell. This is
  a genuinely new, independently recovered attested cell beyond the
  prior audit's two loci, in a **prose** text (Genesis is poetry; the
  Oxford locus is glossarial). Its surface ending is independently attested for *godcund* at
  *godcundum* (Gú. 501; Cri. 638, already in the prior audit's
  documentary table), i.e. the attested OE spelling *-um* is not
  unique to this lexeme. **Note (see morphology.md for the current
  qualification):** this does not mean the full PGmc *\*-aimiz* → OE
  *-um* phonological derivation is an already-settled, modelled
  history — per the parent's replay, *\*-aimiz* does not currently
  derive *-um* (it yields *-em*). The attested-synchronic-spelling
  claim made here is independent of that unresolved derivation
  question.

### Excluded family members (checked, not counted)

- *eorþcundlic* (distinct suffixed lexeme, excluded per scope):
  - "þá wilnunga ðissa eorðcundlicra ðinga terrenarum rerum cupidinem,
    Past. 78, 18" — King Alfred's *Pastoral Care*, Sweet's EETS edition
    (held reference: `docs/references/stausland_johnsen_2015...` discusses
    this text's vowel reduction but not this line; the Sweet edition
    itself is not held here). Confirmed at B-T Supplement
    `bosworth_toller_anglo_saxon_dictionary.vision.txt:31831-31832`, and
    independently matches Clark Hall's concise dictionary entry
    (`clark_hall_concise_anglo_saxon_dictionary.vision.txt:13413`):
    "eorðcund, eorðcundlic (CP) sprung from the earth, earthly" — "(CP)"
    = *Cura Pastoralis*, i.e. exactly this *Past.* 78,18 locus, confirming
    Clark Hall's citation is for the *-lic* form (and possibly also
    intends it to cover the bare *eorðcund* headword — Clark Hall's
    compressed format does not disambiguate this; it is not read here as
    an independent *eorþcund* attestation beyond the three above).
  - "eordcundlice men magon gewiordan hiofonwlitige, Först. Verc. 115,
    11." — Förster's edition of the Vercelli Homilies/Book. Not held
    here; dictionary quotation only.
- *eardcund(es)* (distinct first constituent, excluded per scope):
  "Eardcundes aldores terreni parentis, Rtl. 33, 36." **Siglum
  correction (reversing this research's earlier, insufficiently checked
  draft):** the earlier draft of this file asserted, on the user's
  working assumption, that "Rtl." = the Rushworth Gospels gloss. That
  attribution is **withdrawn as unverified and probably wrong**. No
  formal B-T abbreviations/sigla list was located in either OCR text
  held in this repository (`bosworth_toller_anglo_saxon_dictionary.vision.txt`,
  `legacy/anglosaxondictio00tolluoft.txt` — both searched for a
  prefatory "List of Books Quoted"/abbreviations section; none found).
  Absent that formal key, two independent pieces of internal textual
  evidence instead point away from the Gospels and toward a liturgical
  ritual/collectar text: (a) other "Rtl." citations in the same
  dictionary are liturgical-prayer language, not Gospel narrative,
  e.g. "Âcennendum parentibus suis, Rtl." (dat.pl 'to the parents who
  bore [him/her]', s.v. *â-cennend*,
  `bosworth_toller_anglo_saxon_dictionary.vision.txt:1027`) and
  "Acennic and hehstald genetricis et virginis, Rtl. 69, 9" ('born of a
  mother and a virgin' — Marian/liturgical phrasing,
  `bosworth_toller_anglo_saxon_dictionary.vision.txt:1037`); the Latin
  phrase targeted by the *eardcundes* gloss itself, "terreni parentis"
  ('of an earthly parent/forebear'), is a devotional/commemorative
  formula of the same kind, not a phrase that occurs verbatim in Gospel
  narrative. (b) Clark Hall's own printed abbreviations list (held at
  `clark_hall_concise_anglo_saxon_dictionary.vision.txt:353`) defines a
  closely analogous sigil: "DR the Durham Ritual, ed. T. Stevenson
  (Surtees Society), London, 1840" — i.e. *Rituale Ecclesiae
  Dunelmensis* (the Durham Ritual/Collectar, Durham Cathedral MS A.IV.19,
  a Latin liturgical book with an Old English interlinear gloss). Clark
  Hall's sigil is "DR", not "Rtl.", so this is **not** a direct proof
  that B-T's "Rtl." names the identical edition, but it independently
  confirms that a Durham Ritual/Collectar text, abbreviated along these
  lines and exactly matching the liturgical register of the other
  "Rtl." citations above, is a standard citation target in this
  reference literature, which the Rushworth Gospels (continuous Latin
  Gospel text with interlinear OE gloss, not a prayer-book) is not a
  good fit for. **Net assessment:** B-T's "Rtl." almost certainly
  denotes a Durham Ritual/Collectar-type liturgical text rather than the
  Rushworth Gospels, on textual-register and partial-sigla-key grounds,
  but this is not confirmed by a found formal B-T abbreviations list and
  should be treated as probable, not certain. Either way, **this does
  not affect the main exclusion finding**: *eardcundes* remains a
  different first constituent (*eard-* 'native land/dwelling' vs.
  *eorþ-* 'earth') and is excluded from the *eorþcund* census regardless
  of which text "Rtl." denotes. The specific edition itself (whichever
  it is) was not independently retrieved; not held in this repository.

## Bounded-census outcome against Ciszek-Kiliszewska's six tokens

Three genuine *eorþcund*-spelled (not *-cundlic*, not *eardcund-*) loci
are now recovered and independently source-checked by this research:
Gen. 1627, An. Ox. 691, and R. Ben. 139, 27/28. This is **one more**
than the prior audit's two, but still short of Ciszek-Kiliszewska's
reported six. This research does **not** offer a reconciliation of the
discrepancy: the excluded *eorþcundlic* and *eardcundes* loci documented
above are kept in this census only as documented exclusions in their
own right, not as a basis for speculating how Ciszek-Kiliszewska's count
of six might be reached by including them. Her DOEC wildcard query
string and the DOEC concordance itself were not accessible (see below),
so no reconciliation of the six-vs-three discrepancy can be confirmed or
even responsibly hypothesized from the dictionary citations alone.

**No further distinct search route was found productive in the time
available.** The DOEC (Dictionary of Old English Corpus, University of
Toronto) is a subscription resource; the dictionary's own outbound DOEC
links (`tapor.library.utoronto.ca/cgi-bin/doecorpus/...`) were attempted
and timed out / are not independently reachable from this environment.
No paywall bypass was attempted. This leaves the census **honestly
incomplete** at three recovered loci versus six reported tokens. The
remaining reported tokens are unrecovered; no inference about their
readings or lexical identity is licensed.
