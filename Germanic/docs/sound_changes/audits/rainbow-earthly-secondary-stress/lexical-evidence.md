# Lexical evidence and grammatical cells

Documentary occurrences, dictionary headwords, comparative reconstructions,
and synthetic forms are separate evidence classes. Accent marks supplied
by grammarians are not manuscript records of stress. No item below is
an authorization to add a production corpus row.

## The six earthly tokens: what was and was not recovered

Ciszek-Kiliszewska (2015: 489–490) reports six *eorþcund*
tokens and 761 *godcund* tokens in her DOEC survey. Table 1 groups
them by lexeme, not by inflected spelling or individual locus. Her
wildcard method expressly includes inflections. Neither her count nor
the table's uninflected headword verifies a manuscript nominative.

The two direct earthly loci above were recovered from the dictionary.
The *eardcundes* citation is kept separately. A bounded search for
*eorþcundes*, *eorðcundes*, and *eorþcundne* did not recover
a primary-text occurrence. Search-engine generated paradigm descriptions
and Wiktionary are not attestations. The old DOEC URL linked by the
dictionary redirected to the Toronto library homepage rather than
returning a concordance. The other four tokens in the six-token report
therefore remain unrecovered; two dictionary loci must not be passed
off as the six actual tokens.

The predicted *eorþcundes* and *eorþcundne* are possible
accidental gaps. That classification makes them eligible for an
honestly labelled reconstruction test; it neither makes them attested
nor proves their regular derivation.

## Experimental inputs and cell authority

The machine receipt records the accepted/rejected status and all stages
of each exact serialization. The stop-`d` diagnostic is never silently
substituted for the source-backed `ð`.
Its retained machine key `earthly-voiceless-dental-diagnostic` is a
misnomer: the actual `d` is a voiced stop, not a voiceless dental.
The explicit input, not that label, determines the experiment.

The private input extension adds only the whole adjective endings
*-es*, *-asa*, *-anōn*, and *-ōn* after an existing strong heavy
stem, including the existing compound grammar. It does not change
the production parser, nasal-loss laws, or dental representation.
An accepted string whose ending fails downstream is recorded as such.

## Prosodic and segmental controls

The component tests contain source-backed word shapes (*weruld(es)*,
*æþeling(as)*, *cyningas*, *rīdende*, *wesende*, inflected
*heafod*, and *godcund*) separately from explicitly synthetic strings.
The synthetic high-vowel, *j*, and later-position nasal tests isolate
the early rule's context; they do not supply documentary evidence.
Likewise the paired schematic *berun/berum* strings test the dental/
labial distinction, while Hogg's attested grammatical *-un > -on*
provides its independent empirical basis.

The weak-LH controls retain the original contrast between short
primary-accented first syllables and heavy-initial, grave-bearing,
primary-medial, weak-initial, and non-*lC* negatives. Full selected
candidate/corpus traces additionally include *gold*, *hund*, *full*, *wulf*, *hēafod*,
*heofon*, *nama*, and *wundude*. Existing mismatches are labelled,
not made into new successful controls by changing their targets.
