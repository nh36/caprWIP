## Historical analysis

Historical stage/scope: ordinary nonhigh and high losses are prehistoric
Old English developments; the distinct later light pre-l development has
consonant and dialect/date qualifications. PGmc b distribution is inherited
material, not a newly dated sound law at its computed realization site.

The general rule deletes short unstressed i/u/y after a heavy first syllable
when a singleton following consonant plus vowel establishes an open medial
syllable. Nonhigh a/æ/e loss has the same bounded openness representation,
without root or final weight conditioning. Root prosody is protected by
FootCtx. Later light pre-l deletion targets i and retains d/θ/f-l environments
while those consonants remain distinct. No morphological class, word
identity, optionality or lexical diffusion is used as a sound-law condition.

The conventional apocope correction retains final high vowels in H+L
trisyllables. The bounded working account follows the conventional
Campbell/Brunner/Hogg analysis of the retained-final-high contrast, rather
than selecting serialization by corpus score. Hogg's documented paradigm
levelling provides an independent alternative explanation for restored
vocalism, but does not prove that every conservative Mercian form is
analogical. Neither Fulk nor Ringe–Taylor is claimed to be disproved;
the heads control tests the adopted conventional history, not unanimity
across dialects. Fulk's positive paradigm evidence
and Campbell's lār-type analogy are retained for endingless abstracts; a
secure vocalic accusative is selected instead of forcing the old nominative.

Earlier light-root contractions such as Sigfrið and Hyglāc remain substantive
counterevidence to an exhaustive heavy-only account. Formation date, first
stem, linking vowel and prosody are not sufficiently discriminated to adopt
an additional regular earlier law. The present general operation does not
claim to explain those contractions.

Mutation-before-general-high-loss is independently demonstrated by the
umlauted selected root of filth. General syncope-before-preconsonantal
degemination is demonstrated by spindle. Nonhigh/apocope/high serialization
is the conventional source-supported working account with its dispute
explicit. Weak unrounding preserves height and commutes with bounded general
high loss; gaduling's d/l protection permits retention on either side of
light pre-l deletion, but does not prove universal commutation.
Labial realization after nonhigh loss is a model-local dependency, not a new
strict historical SC114-before-PGmc edge. The old proxy/marker dependencies
and spindle's former SC066 edge remain superseded experiments.
