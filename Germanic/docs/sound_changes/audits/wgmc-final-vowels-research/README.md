The recommendation below to reconsider the old mare selection is

## Executive conclusion

RECOMMEND CONDITIONAL ADOPTION of Model B's bounded phonological
correction, not of its automatic production deployment or any baseline
migration. Final nasal bimoric o must cease to function as a separate
back-vowel trigger before English fronting/restoration. Restricting
following-vowel stop-g palatalization to original high front vowels and
allowing posttonic intervocalic loss of both velar and palatal h resolves
the toe and tongue conflicts without lexical exceptions. The residual
front-root flask and mare nominatives are not a reason to reject the
merger: the sources explicitly reconstruct them and identify later
paradigm levelling.

The result is not “all 394 outputs preserved.” Model B changes two
finals: flask `flasce → flæsċe` and nightmare `mare → mære`.
It preserves the other 392 finals, including toe and tongue, and
preserves all eight inherited mismatches. The altered regular
nominatives must be distinguished from the accepted later lexical
forms before adoption. Flask already has an analogy classification;
nightmare's present regular classification requires reconsideration.
No accepted input, target, classification, baseline, production FST,
registry, or generated book artifact has been changed here.

One important original-source correction strengthens the recommendation.
Ringe–Taylor 2014:55 explicitly says there is no indication of inherited
word-final nasalization surviving in PWGmc. Their 44 preserves nasalized
long vowels before h in stressed roots. Final nasalization and root
nasalization therefore cannot be treated alike. Existing statements that
their 54–55 supports continued final nasal contrast into PWGmc need
correction in a subsequently authorized implementation. Their 58–60
and 299–300 supply the quantity-sensitive merger and later outcomes.

There are real limits. The inherited restoration implementation does not
satisfy Hogg's sp exclusion; Ringe–Taylor and Campbell formulate a broader
sC environment. A literal extension of Hogg's preceding-i nasal-mediated
palatalization changes seven additional corpus forms and creates
unresolved downstream interactions. Neither disagreement is settled by
counting target matches. Versloot's conditional-fronting alternative
remains a serious historical alternative, but no whole-system
implementation of it is claimed. This report does not pretend to have
authenticated inaccessible DOE records or every manuscript-specific note
in Pheifer's edition.

## Four independently reconstructed lexical histories

### Flask

Ringe–Taylor 192 reconstructs `flaskā > flæskǣ` in the nominative,
beside an oblique stem with back ō. Restoration in the oblique gives
the back-root donor. Campbell 61 §158 explicitly places `flasce`
after inflected `flascan`; Luick 153 §161.3 likewise identifies the
donor relation. The Bosworth–Toller Supplement 223 supplies actual
flascan/flaxan and flascena/flaxena evidence in Gregory's Dialogues,
66.3–10 and 141.25–30. This is positive evidence for the paradigm,
not an analogy invented to preserve a target.

Model B's regular path is:

`fláskōn → flaskǭ → flaskā → flæskǣ → flæʃǣ → flæʃæ → flæʃe`.

The printed nominative `flasċe` in Ringe–Taylor has a palatal mark.
Plain sc spellings and later x spellings do not prove an original
velar pronunciation of the nominative. The sources establish root-vowel
levelling much more directly than the complete consonantal history of
every variant. Both the reconstructed front-root stage and attested
back-root lexical forms are legitimate, but not interchangeable
measurements of one purely regular nominative derivation.

The mature entry currently describes the selected weak feminine as a
regular path to flasce while also citing its analogical donor. That
explanatory conflict is identified here, not silently rewritten.

### Nightmare

The regular weak nominative follows:

`márōn → marǭ → marā → mærǣ → mæræ → mære`.

The oblique `maran` supplies the back root for later `mare`.
Hall 2007:300–302 and 313–314 distinguishes the weak noun from strong
`mær`, identifies the incuba gloss tradition as one witness family,
and discusses both the riding-mare passages and Leechbook III's maran.
The early gloss spelling mera is not secure evidence for a masculine
mara paradigm. The short-root `mære` is not long-root `mǣre`
'famous'. Orel's comparative headword does not change that distinction.

Both mare and mære have historical standing. Under the preferred
traditional reconstruction the former is a later lexical/paradigmatic
outcome, not a regular result of retaining final nasal o until restoration.
The present regular classification should therefore not be preserved by
keeping an independently falsified vowel trigger.

### Toe

Sweet 1885:39, Corpus 141, actually prints `allox: tahae`;
the original page was image-checked. Ringe–Taylor 314 interprets
the stage as tāhæ and contrasts later tā. It is an intermediate-stage
witness for an existing lexeme, not a new lexical admission.

Model B gives:

`táixōn → taixǭ → tāxǭ → tāxā → tāxǣ → tāçǣ → tāçæ →
tāçe → tāe → tā`.

The earlier prototype stopped at tāhe because the existing h-loss
network consumes x but not ç. No new toe-specific contraction is
necessary. The a/ae spelling does not itself measure the precise
quantity of the reconstructed final vowel.

### Tongue

Model B gives:

`túngōn → tungǭ → tungā → tungǣ → tungæ → tunge`.

The ng cluster is not an intervocalic singleton g. A following
nonhigh front ǣ does not meet the medial original-i condition
in Ringe–Taylor's stop account. Positive `lenġu`, `lenġ`,
`strenġ`, `genġe` controls prevent the opposite overcorrection
of banning ng palatalization. The newly generated tunġe is not
justified merely by the later e. This correction leaves the original
high-front conditioning productive.

Kroonen's 526–527 and Orel's comparative weak formation supply
lexical context; medieval plain g does not, by itself, date or disprove
every palatal stage. The preceding-i question is kept separate below.

## Negative and ordering controls: do not flatten the results

B and C each pass all 18 initially pinned component expectations.
The supplementary restoration/sk/citation set passes eight of nine
in each model. Its sp negative fails in baseline, B, and C:
`flæspōn → flaspōn`.
This is an inherited source-dependent discrepancy, not a regression
caused by the merger and not a passed historical control.

Hogg 93–94 §5.35n2 specifically cites inflected æspan/æpsan and
hæpsian for nonrestoration before sp, and questions ft evidence.
Campbell 61 §158 uses general fC/sC language and permits
analogical removal of an earlier restored a; Ringe–Taylor 189
likewise writes sC. The exact discriminating follow-up is the
original attestation and paradigm of æspan/æpsan versus ascan/flascan,
not an arbitrary restoration permutation or an unsourced lexical
exception. No SC046 source definition was changed here.

The h-loss/contraction ordering controls pass in both revised models:
normal order gives tā; reversing only those components leaves tāe.
The key-shaped high-front control likewise distinguishes
palatalization before versus after mutation. These are independently
motivated local ordering assays, not new promoted chronology edges
or arbitrary cascade permutations.

## Exact conceptual proposal and approval-dependent adoption plan

The experimental insertion and identity replacement are laboratory
machinery. Production need not acquire an extra historical law or
an arbitrary new cascade position. Its smallest candidate is:

1. Extend the existing SC042 rewrite centre from oral `*ō` to
   `[ {*ō} | {*ǭ} ]`, keeping the current finality/quantity guard
   and post-SC020/pre-SC108 position.
2. In the stop network, replace the following `EnglishStarFrontVowel`
   condition on g by original high-front i/ī, including existing
   stress-encoding equivalents. Keep the singleton-fricative network
   and its independently governed merger distinct.
3. Give singleton posttonic intervocalic x and ç the same loss,
   using the existing stress-foot helper. Preserve geminates, initial
   h, protected h, and pretonic stress-foot onset.

The evaluated h proposal is:

```foma
define AFH [FootCtx([
    [{*x}|{*ç}] -> 0 || EnglishStarVocalic _ EnglishStarVocalic
])];
```

The exact experimental definitions and short-name recipes are in
the capsule. SC108, trimoric o shortening, and æ/e merger remain.
SC023 remains a citation adapter. The delayed nasal branch in
SC072 becomes redundant for the tested final domain, but must
not be removed globally without a separate remaining-domain proof.
The current comment justifying retained nasalization by flask's
target cannot remain as a historical explanation.

Before any authorized production adoption:

1. Approve treatment of the two regular-versus-remodelled nominatives,
   especially nightmare's classification and flask's consonantal
   interpretation. Do not silently retarget either entry.
2. Make the three edits as one coherent, source-documented package.
   Preserve and explicitly qualify the sp disagreement; settle it
   separately from any claim of complete restoration correctness.
3. Recheck all 394 selected inputs, all 17 citation witnesses,
   zero missing/multiple forms, exact inherited mismatches,
   and every predicted changed intermediate/final. Add the source-bound
   clay/hiatus and positive/negative consonant controls.
4. Use the normal scientific finalization and generated-output owner
   only after approval. Perform the required strict source/runtime
   certification then; this research run is not that certification.

For further discrimination obtain Pheifer 1974's original notes on
Epinal3/1066 and the corresponding Corpus readings, the modern
facsimile/codicological account in Bischoff et al.1988, and the actual
ng/ncg tokens invoked by Hogg §7.17 and Brunner §215. Those specific
items can refine dating and consonantal eligibility. They are not a
reason to preserve the demonstrated false final-nasal trigger.
