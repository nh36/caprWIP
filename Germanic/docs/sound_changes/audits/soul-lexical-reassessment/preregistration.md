## Source-bound expectations, before execution

- Find: Strunk (1904), p.15, Juliana 334–335, prints
  `gif we gemete sin on moldwege / oþþe feor oþþe neah fundne weorþen`.
  His glossary p.82 expressly parses `fundne, 335` as `ptc npm.`.
  Fulk (2018), §9.2 p.209 and §12.30, supplies strong masculine nominative
  plural *-ai and participial *-an- ~ *-in-. Ringe–Taylor's account of the
  inherited participial alternant supports the voiced dental and zero-grade
  root. Thus *fúnðanai is a compositional reconstruction, not a quoted
  whole-word reconstruction. Test it, nominative *fúnðanaz, and the complete
  masculine accusative hypothesis *fúnðananǭ. The old *fúnðanǭ does not
  contain the full uncontracted masculine accusative morphology. Do not repair
  that by assuming unattested haplology. Retained *-in- alternatives remain
  distinct from analogical restoration of a vowel after syncope.
- West: Orel p.459 distinguishes neuter *westan from a derivative with
  breve/macron over final e. Test literal *wéstan, the usual nasal-vowel
  representation *wéstą (a notation/morphology hypothesis, not a new loss
  rule), and both long and short directional endings. The supplement has
  actual adverbial west in Orosius 6.37, S.296.7. No directional westene has
  been independently established. Later manuscript westan/westane does not
  date inherited final-vowel shortening.
- Hammer: Orel's printed p.158 (PDF sheet 197), Kroonen p.206, and
  Wright–Wülcker (1884), I p.272 line 36, `Malleus, hamer`, independently
  establish the reconstruction and attested citation gloss. Test nominative
  *xámaraz as well as current genitive *xámaras. Do not treat predicted
  hamres as attested. Campbell §574.3 and Brunner §245 discuss secondary
  vowels and their transfer; those accounts are not automatically a proof
  that every inherited medial vowel was originally lost.
- Soul: preserve the preceding source verification of accusative sāwle and
  *sáiwalǭ. Do not restart the entire nominative controversy.

Root acute accents are CAPR's input convention, not diplomatic accents in the
comparative dictionaries. Reconstructions in candidates.json distinguish
whole inherited hypotheses from deliberately incomplete or sensitivity probes.

## Phonological comparisons

All variants retain SC063, SC065, SC066 and SC040 literally. The input-coverage
contrast separates parsing from sound-law effects.

| Model | Change |

| C-nonhigh | B plus the previous heavy-initial, three-syllable kernel |
| G-light-nonhigh | C without the initial-heavy restriction |
| H-local-nonhigh | G's following-heavy environment without its word-left anchor |
| J-open-medial | Traditional unrestricted open-medial nonhigh syncope, diagnostic |

H is a bounded implementation hypothesis, not an already demonstrated
historical law. It deletes short unstressed nonhigh vowels in open syllables
before a heavy following syllable, irrespective of total word length or initial
weight. It permits an eligible environment inside a longer word. The old
following-heavy definition is retained, not a borrowed SC040 foot oracle.
The source grounds are Hogg §§6.14–17, Fulk 2010's light-final retention, and
Fulk 2018 p.213's explicit light-root nonhigh syncope. A fully unconditioned J
must separately answer Fulk's retained-light-ending evidence.

The longer-word inherited prefixed find input is a morphological extension,
not itself a verified token; stage controls prefixed to independently verified
contexts test formal locality, not a claim that the prefix already had this
particular shape at the historical event. Closed medial syllables, stressed
vowels, long vowels and light following syllables are negative controls.
Awles is tested as a stage control unless its complete comparative formation
can be established independently. Full *fáganas and *fágenas, *þéudanas,
*gōdanǭ and *mánagas distinguish actual inherited/stem alternatives from
hand-specified late vowels. Their exact outputs are not presumed.

Sumer, wæter and ofer are full-input negatives where comparative sources
justify them; full *úberi is an explicit suffix-variant sensitivity probe.
Existing selected high-vowel cases and all actual corpus deltas are reported.
The earlier ending-unrounding experiment is not repeated or silently adopted.

## Second parser correction and stress refinement, before attempt 03

Attempt 02's recorded parses identify the defect: the productive grammar
permitted a coda-less short-vowel root followed immediately by a second vowel,
so `ái` could be parsed as `*á *i` instead of `*ái`. Native-parser priority
protected existing accepted words but not newly admitted diphthongal inputs.
Require a consonant between a short root vowel and the first weak vowel,
using the existing strong-heavy *parser* network (its name is not a historical
syllable-weight restriction). Spell the final diphthong mapping with the
native grammar's braces. Check parsing before compiling whole model networks.
No candidate has been changed. Both failures remain execution failures.

K uses the already independently justified early positive-primary-stress
context, SC113/FootCtx, not SC040's later prominence computation. Within its
root domain it protects the first vowel and allows an unbounded number of
intervening syllables before an eligible nonhigh vowel. This removes the
three-syllable limit without permitting deletion of the primary root of a
marked pretonic word. `ᶠgegedæs` is an explicitly synthetic primary-root
negative, not a reconstruction or attestation; H is predicted to violate it.
`ᶠgesāwalǭ` and `tādanalǭ` test marked prefix and longer posttonic locality.
The early context currently represents one pretonic syllable; K does not
silently claim a general multiple-prefix stress parser. Final-heavy versus
light-final conditioning remains an explicit historical hypothesis.
