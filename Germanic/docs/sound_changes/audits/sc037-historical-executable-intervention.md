## Question

defects be repaired generally, preserving the 391 selections, while obtaining
independent historical controls? Can comparative evidence independently date
*seleweard* before the relevant loss? Failure on stressed breaking, former

its formation date.

### Input grammar

Two separate native constraints reject Orel's sali-warđ formations:
first-member i is excluded; rð is absent from the second-root coda inventory.
Adding high vowels indiscriminately introduces an actual atomic-iu versus
split-i+u ambiguity and loses older canonical acceptance. The adopted
closed-token-root branch admits a/i/u/zero; the open-token-root branch retains
a/zero, conserving the atomic analysis. These grammar names are not a
syllabification: sal+i represents historically light sá.li.

The established ATT checker proves old canonical-domain inclusion and
functionality of the old/new canonical union, hence conservation of older
analyses. Raw native functionality was already false; this is not a claim
of raw-native functionality, whole-cascade totality or every possible
historical compound's coverage.

### Breaking, mutation and high-vowel handling

Plain medial i wrongly underwent breaking. Plain initial i can represent a
stressed root after an earlier accent-consuming change; acute í and explicit
subordinate ì are also eligible. The repair uses those distinctions and the
existing positive pretonic foot context. It preserves the high-front
following-vowel blocker, including ì. Fulwiht and nawiht supply independent
weak-i negatives; ordinary root and subordinate-stress breaking remain live.

The independently reconstructed ūtumist exposed the consonant-only mutation
context: it mutated medial u but could not transmit mutation to initial ū.
Hogg §5.76 and Campbell §203 independently prescribe that double mutation.
The fronting component now permits intervening plain u; a secondary-stressed
ù is protected and becomes the internal precomposed carrier ỳ under mutation.
Raising and diphthong contexts are not reordered or indiscriminately broadened.

The companion control then exposed weak y surviving as gædyling.
Hogg §6.10 independently requires early unrounding and eventual e. The
support component therefore unrounds retained weak medial y to i before the
existing i/e merger, protecting initial root y and subordinate ỳ. Its placement
after high syncope is a commuting technical serialization, not a new
unrounding-after-syncope historical edge.

## Historical analysis

The ordinary nonhigh and high processes are independently distinguishable
by quality, weight and relative chronology. Earlier name losses remain a
real historical problem, not a newly established third categorical law.
Stage/scope remain the existing Old English/English-specific judgements;
the late support identifier invents no independent historical metadata.

For seleweard, Orel pp.315,448 reconstructs sali-warđaz and sali-warđuz:
both have sali-, and differ in second-member a-/u-stem inflection. Kroonen
p.424 supports inherited s-stem history and secondary i-stem sali-,
including Old Saxon seli and Old Norse salr/salir. Runic sali-gastiR
corroborates an early combining sali-, not this guardian compound's date.
ON sal-vǫrðr and OE seleweard make inheritance a reasonable published
hypothesis; productive separate formation also remains viable, explicitly
reported by Orel through Carr. No uniquely shared innovation is required
by comparative reconstruction, but no independently discriminating
formation chronology has been recovered.

The selected attestation is Beowulf's object accusative singular seleweard,
Clark Hall p.260 citing line 667; Harrison–Sharp's numbering gives 668.
The ordinary a-stem accusative -ą is legitimate if Orel's a-stem hypothesis
is selected. Lack of an explicitly printed case reconstruction is not the
objection. In sá.li-wàrð-, the first syllable is light, i is inherited
stem material, and primary/subordinate root prominence is the conditional
compound hypothesis, not an independently dated fact about every name.
The grammatical form and segments can be justified more confidently than
the specific compound's pre-SC037 existence.
Confidence is high for sali- and ordinary a-stem inflection, moderate for
the published inheritance hypothesis, and insufficient for independently
fixing this compound before SC037. These are separate evidential judgements.
No forced admission follows from its now regular output.

Older light-root losses may involve earlier prosody/chronology or independently
varying first stems, but none of those possibilities supplies a universal
explanation. Existing pre-l deletion mechanically explains a conditional
hygi-lāk path; that is not independent proof of its date and does not
explain Sigfrið or every Hað-/Bad-/Cyn- contraction. No lexical exception,
personal-name grammar condition or unsupported analogical disposal is adopted.

Chronology: breaking before the relevant nonhigh loss and mutation before
ordinary high syncope are independently demonstrated in the cited sources.
SC038 marker lifetimes, the exact SC066/SC115 adjacency and the retained early
a holding site are model-local dependencies, not new historical edges.

## Verdict

Registry-verdict: SC037=RESTRICT/SPLIT/REORDER

The former high clause was historically overgeneral and its early placement
misrepresented independently sourced mutation-before-loss histories.
Retain the literal selected a fragment as a qualified holding proxy; separate
and place the bounded heavy/open high core after mutation. Adopt the
conservative high-stem/dental grammar, stress-sensitive i-breaking,
transmitted-u mutation and weak-y unrounding repairs. Do not invent an older-name
law or admit seleweard without its independent formation premise.
