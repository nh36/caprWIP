# SC025 / SC104 adjudication — the nasalized low-vowel complex

Registry-verdict: SC025=SPLIT/REFORMULATE; SC103=SPLIT/REORDER; SC104=SPLIT

## Identity

- **SC ids:** SC025 (`EAFLongANasalRounding`), SC103 (`PGmcNasalLossBeforeX`),
  SC026 (`EAFNasalSpirantLengthening`); new identity created by this
  adjudication: SC104 (`EAFNasalizedLowRounding`).
- **Executable identifiers:** Foma `define` names, not stage claims.
- **Date / branch / base commit:** branch `sc001-sc020-chronology-audit`, base
  commit `1893552f`.

## Question

Has CAPR telescoped one later development of the nasalized long low vowel into
three different earlier rules, and if so what is the historically correct
decomposition?

Before this pass CAPR produced \*ō directly in three places:

| rule | mapping | claimed stage |
|---|---|---|
| SC025 `EAFLongANasalRounding` | \*ā → \*ō / \_ N | eaf / north_sea_germanic |
| SC026 `EAFNasalSpirantLengthening` (low branch) | \*aNf/þ/s → \*ōf/þ/s | eaf / north_sea_germanic |
| SC103 `PGmcNasalLossBeforeX` (low branch) | \*aNx → \*ōx | pgmc / pan_germanic |

The last of these is chronologically impossible on its face: a Proto-Germanic
rule cannot deliver a rounded vowel that Gothic, Old Norse, Old High German and
Old Saxon do not have.

**What would confirm:** the sources treat the three as feeders of one later
change, and reconstruct a shared unrounded nasalized intermediate.
**What would refute:** the sources treat the \*ō outcomes as independent, or
deny a common intermediate, or make the intermediate consequence-free.

The hypothesis was confirmed.

## Sources consulted directly

Campbell 1959 §§119, 121, 127, 128, 132; Fulk 2018 §§4.1, 4.11, 4.12; Ringe
2006 vol. 1 §3.2.7; Ringe & Taylor 2014 vol. 2 §§5.1.1, 5.1.2; Sievers–Brunner
§§26, 64, 67, 69, 80 Anm. 1, 186.1; Luick §301.

Decisive passages:

- **Campbell §128 n. 1, p. 50** — *“This ō is derived from a nasalized, but not
  rounded, vowel ą̄, for when it is subject to very early shortening the result
  is a … not o (e.g. samcucu) … ą̄ from Prim. Gmc. ǣ became already in
  prehistoric OE identical with ō from Prim. Gmc. ō, and this change affected ą̄
  from Prim. Gmc. ą̄ (§119) and from Ingvaeonic ą̄ (§121) at the same time.”*
  This single passage settles the whole question: one later change, three
  feeders, and an intermediate that is demonstrably unrounded.
- **Campbell §121, p. 47** — the nasal-spirant law rejects the nasal *“with
  compensatory lengthening and nasalization of the preceding vowel to ą̄, ī̃,
  ū̃ … West Gmc. ą̄ > ā in OS only, while in OFris. and OE it remained
  nasalized, and was ultimately developed to ō.”* Also *“West Gmc. ī̃ and ū̃
  subsequently develop like original ī and ū.”*
- **Campbell §119, p. 44** — PGmc \*ą̄ *“became ā in Goth., North Gmc., OHG, and
  OS”*, rounded only *“in OE and OFris.”*
- **Fulk §4.1, p. 55** — *“The lengthened vowels may have remained nasalized for
  a considerable time, well past the close of the NWGmc. period, since ą̄
  produced this way developed to ō in Anglo-Frisian … and did not fall together
  with OE ā < ai.”*
- **Fulk §4.11, p. 72** — the nasal-spirant law *“produces ą̄, ī̃, ū̃. The first
  of these yields ō in Anglo-Frisian (as does ą̄ inherited from PGmc.), but
  either ā or ō in OS (whereas PGmc. ą̄ is always reflected as ā …).”*
- **Fulk §4.12, pp. 72–73** — \*ǣ before nasals *“reflected everywhere as ō,
  presumably from earlier ą̄”*, stated within the Anglo-Frisian section.
- **Ringe vol. 1 §3.2.7(ii), pp. 149–150** — PGmc \*hanhaną = \*[xą̄xaną];
  *“the low vowel was rounded, like other nasalized low vowels, in the
  northernmost dialects of WGmc (‘Anglo-Frisian’).”*
- **Ringe & Taylor vol. 2 §5.1.2, p. 142** — *“Rounding affected not only the
  nasalized low vowels discussed below, but also those in the list at the
  beginning of 5.1.1 and the examples of \*/anh/ = \*[ą̄x] inherited from
  PGmc.”*; *“Stressed low vowels were nasalized … in the northern WGmc
  dialects”*; the rounding was *“a parallel development of the diverging
  northern dialects.”*
- **Sievers–Brunner §26 p. 33, §64 p. 58, §67 p. 59, §69 p. 59** — OE ō from all
  three sources; nasalized ī merges with old ī *“nach Aufgabe der
  Nasalierung.”*
- **Luick §301.1, p. 276** — heads these *“der anglofriesischen Dialektgruppe
  eigentümliche Lautwandlungen”*; the nasalized vowel *“später seine Nasalität
  aufgab”*; the change applies in unstressed syllables too (\*jugūþ).

## Verdict

**Architecture B** of the four compared. Three historically distinct processes
create a long nasalized low vowel \*ą̄; one later Anglo-Frisian change rounds it
to \*ō.

Architecture A (the previous telescoped model) is rejected: it misrepresents the
Proto-Germanic outcome, hides a real feeding relation, and states three times
what the sources state once. Architecture C (partial unification) is rejected
because Campbell §128 n. 1 and R/T §5.1.2 both explicitly include the PGmc
\*/anh/ class in the same rounding. Architecture D (no explicit nasalized tier)
is rejected because the intermediate has independent consequences: it is
independently reconstructed, it survives a long interval, it feeds a later
change, and its nasality is precisely what distinguishes it from the later oral
\*ā < \*ai.

## Answers to the thirteen questions

1. **Is there one common later \*ą̄ > \*ō change?** Yes. Campbell §128 n. 1
   p. 50 states it directly; R/T §5.1.2 p. 142 confirms that the rounding
   covered the PGmc \*/anh/ class as well.
2. **What are its feeding sources?** SC103 (PGmc \*aNx), SC026/SC027 (the North
   Sea Germanic nasal-spirant law), SC025 (inherited long \*ā before a retained
   nasal).
3. **Geographical scope?** `anglo_frisian`. Old Saxon reflects the PGmc
   nasalized low vowel invariably as *ā* and the Ingvaeonic one as *ā* or *ō*
   according to word and dialect (Campbell §119 p. 44; Fulk §4.11 p. 72). The
   former CAPR justification for `north_sea_germanic` conflated two things:
   Old Saxon shares the **nasalization** (R/T §5.1.2 p. 142), not the
   **rounding**.
4. **Chronology?** After all three feeders; before the product merges with
   inherited \*ō *“already in prehistoric OE”* (Campbell §128 n. 1 p. 50), and
   therefore before i-umlaut; before \*ai-monophthongization, since the oral
   \*ā < \*ai escapes (Fulk §4.1 p. 55; Campbell §132 pp. 52–53; R/T
   pp. 169–170).
5. **Does SC025 represent nasalization, rounding, or both?** Both, previously.
   It is now split: SC025 nasalizes, SC104 rounds.
6. **Should \*ą̄ be explicit?** Yes, on all four of CAPR's criteria.
7. **Are explicit \*ī̃ / \*ū̃ needed?** No. Campbell §121 p. 47 says they
   *“develop like original ī and ū”*, and Sievers–Brunner §69 p. 59 that the
   nasalized ī merges with old ī once nasality is abandoned. No later rule
   depends on their nasality. **Modelling principle adopted:** represent an
   intermediate state explicitly only where its later development *diverges*
   from that of the corresponding oral segment. Nasalization is never added as
   phonetic ornament.
8. **SC026 output for underlying \*a?** \*ą̄.
9. **SC103 output for underlying \*a?** \*ą̄. \*ō would misrepresent Gothic,
   North Germanic, Old High German and Old Saxon *ā*.
10. **Where should SC103 sit?** At the head of the cascade, executable position
    1, ahead of SC096. See below.
11. **Independent confidences?** \*ē₁ > \*ā: **B**, unchanged, tied to the
    disputed \*ē₁ reconstruction. Nasalization before a retained nasal
    (SC025): **B**, since its input depends on that same pathway. Common
    \*ą̄ > \*ō (SC104): **A**, independently evidenced from PGmc \*Nx reflexes,
    from nasal-spirant reflexes, from retained-nasal forms, and by cross-dialect
    comparison. Confidence attaches to the claim being rated, so the uncertainty
    in one feeder does not lower the rating of the later rule.
12. **Does `youth` take the correct later path?** Yes — see below.
13. **Implications for SC028?** SC103 still creates the \*xst cluster that SC028
    consumes: \*fúnxstiz > \*fū́xsti > *fȳst*. The feeding survives the move to
    position 1 (SC028 is at 26). No part of SC028's own stage/scope has been
    decided here.

**Symbol note.** The transducer writes the long nasalized low vowel `{*ã}`
(U+00E3), a single Unicode codepoint. `ą̄` requires a base character plus a
combining macron; Foma's brace parser treats those as two separate sigma
symbols and the rule then silently never matches. The reader-facing chapters use
the linguistically correct `ą̄`.

**SC103's rule name.** `PGmcNasalLossBeforeX` names only the third of the rule's
three operations. The rule lengthens the vowel, nasalizes it, and then deletes
the nasal, which is exactly what Campbell §119 p. 44 and Fulk §4.1 p. 55
describe (*“compensatory lengthening upon loss of a nasal consonant”*). The
lengthening is not new in this pass — the previous version already mapped short
`{*á}` to long `{*ō}`. The display names now state all three operations; the
Foma identifier is left alone.

**SC025's rule name.** `EAFLongANasalRounding` no longer rounds. The identifier
is retained as a stable implementation name and a behaviour-neutral rename is
deferred to an isolated rename pass: the rename gate certifies that a pass
changed labels *only*, and it cannot certify a pass that also reorders a rule
and adds another. The brief's concern in §19 — that the rounding's canonical
identity should not be overloaded onto SC025 — is met, because the rounding has
its own identity SC104.

## SC103's position in the cascade

SC103 sat at position 23, after some twenty Northwest Germanic and Proto-West
Germanic innovations. That was an editorial holding-zone artifact. It is now at
position 1, ahead of SC096 `RootNounNomZLoss` (pre-PWGmc) and everything else.

Justification: the change is pan-Germanic, so nothing else in this cascade can
precede it. Verification: `foma` compiles clean, production-vs-sandbox
equivalence holds over all 385 rows, and both frozen fingerprints are
byte-identical (`outputs_sha256=1309dbc3…`, `legacy_subset_sha256=a72bdeb8…`).
The move is therefore output-neutral. Its only genuine antecedent requirement is
that the nasal and the \*x be intact, which holds at the head by construction;
its only genuine consequent requirement is that it precede SC028 (position 26)
and i-umlaut, both satisfied with a wide margin.

The intermediate positions 2–22 commute for this rule in the present corpus. No
finer order is claimed. SC022 and SC023 are also labelled `pgmc` and remain at
positions 22 and 23; their placement was settled by their own adjudications and
is outside this pass.

## Chronology tests

- **SC024 → SC025** (unchanged, independently demonstrated): displacing the
  lowering after the nasalization yields \*\*mānaþ, \*\*spān.
- **SC025 → SC104** and **SC026 → SC104** (executable feeding, technical):
  stating the rounding before either feeder strands the nasalized vowel and the
  cascade returns *no output at all* for `month`, `spoon`, `goose`. These are
  feeding dependencies, not historical intervals.
- **SC103 → SC104** (stage-entailed): a Proto-Germanic change necessarily
  precedes an Anglo-Frisian one. No corpus witness exists, since SC103's only
  firing takes the high-vowel branch.
- **SC025 → SC004** (revised): the demonstration is now run by displacing SC004
  *earlier*, before SC025. Twelve rows change, among them `stone` → \*\*stōn and
  `home` → \*\*hōm. Displacing SC025 *later* than SC004 no longer produces those
  forms; it merely strands the nasalized vowel, so the edge's witness formulation
  has been rewritten accordingly.
- **SC104 vs SC101 / SC004** (no claim): the input is segmentally nasalized, so
  the oral \*ā that SC004 creates from \*ai is outside SC104's domain by
  construction. Making \*ą̄ explicit has converted a stipulated ordering
  constraint into a segmental fact, which is exactly Fulk's observation (§4.1
  p. 55) that the two vowels did not fall together.

## `youth`

`youth` remains a good witness of a sound change whose output is later partly
obscured. \*júgunθ takes the high-vowel branch of the nasal-spirant law, giving
\*júgūnθ and then \*júgūθ; the lengthened vowel stands in an unstressed
syllable and its length is given up again by the later shortening of unstressed
syllables, so Old English shows *ġeoguþ*. Luick §301.1 p. 276 cites exactly this
form as \*jugūþ with the long vowel, and Sievers–Brunner §186.1 Anm. 3 p. 176
gives the parallel course of *beraþ* < \*beranþi through \*berōþ. The chronology
is source-supported and the current output is reached by the right mechanism. No
general re-adjudication of unstressed-vowel shortening was undertaken.

## What was preserved

- SC026/SC027 remain **one historical sound change** in two executable
  operations, with their shared `canonical_change_id`, their \*mf/\*nþ/\*ns
  domain, their executable (not historical) dependency, and `fist` excluded.
- SC101 and SC102 were not reopened. SC101's `- EnglishStarNasal` exclusion is
  now redundant, since it cannot see a nasalized vowel, and has been left in
  place as harmless.
- SC028 was not adjudicated. Only its feeding relation from SC103 was checked.
- The frozen cascade baseline was not rebaselined.
