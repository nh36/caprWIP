## Question

- **Hypothesis or suspected problem (falsifiable):** contextual genitive
  plural flascena/flaxena might distinguish inherited feminine ōn-
  from a masculine-source an- ending, independently of the nominative's
  root vowel. Inherited masculine guma, not originally neuter nama,
  must control the masculine route.
- **What evidence would confirm it; what would refute it:** require
  exact inherited endings, raw-entry attempts, named-stage execution
  and tongue/guma controls. A match contingent on an omitted vowel
  class, skipped syncope or supplied late ending refutes any claim
  to a complete regular inherited derivation.

Flask's root first fronts and then restores before either medial
ō or a. In the feminine route, `OEMedLongShort` gives medial o;
it does not become e. In the masculine-source route, a survives
`OENonHighSyn` when the immediately following nucleus is ô,
fronts under `OEUnstressedFrontingEarly`, and merges as e.
The final carrier gives a. That explains the apparent flascena/
tungena/gumena success, but does not validate its syncope.

The sensitivity changes only the immediate following-vowel class in
ordinary nonhigh syncope from `EnglishStarVocalic` to
`[EnglishStarVocalic|{*ô}]`. Its later-tail class already admits ô.
The changed relation now syncopates the masculine medial a, giving
flascna/tungna/gumna. Every other relation and selected input stays
fixed; all394 selected finals are identical to the reference.
This is an experimental representation repair, not an adopted new
historical sound law.

Literal PWGmc final ō is also tested instead of hiding the origin
distinction. At the named boundary the early merger turns it into ā,
then final fronting gives ǣ, and the final outcome is e:
flascone/tungune for the feminine routes and
flascne/tungne/gumne for the masculine ones. Some literal-ō
strings are admitted as raw inputs, but give flascn/tungn/gumn;
they have been fed at the wrong quantity/history, not successfully
executed as complete inherited nasal-trimoric genitives.
The raw nasal-trimoric forms and oral-shadow strings must not be
conflated in the admission report.

Source-motivated late *flaskæna/*tungæna/*gumæna entered before
`OEWeightMarkers` give flascna/tungna/gumna. Supplying *-ana
there is also still too early: the same syncope removes its medial
vowel. Entry of *flaskana/*tungana/*gumana after syncope,
before `OEEarlyOShortening`, gives flascena/tungena/gumena.
That last run explicitly presupposes analogical restoration of
the medial vowel. Reusing the later executable fronting relation
tests the downstream string, not an independently dated identity
between early fronting and Campbell's later -ana > -ena process.

## Historical analysis

- **Historical stage:** inherited PGmc paradigm contrasted with
  West Germanic and Old English remodelling; no new stage assigned
  from the executable entry point.
- **Historical scope:** class comparison, not a new tree/node claim.
- **Regular phonological conditioning:** general medial-vowel
  syncope and vowel fronting, with quantity distinguished; gender
  transfer is explicitly morphological analogy, not a hidden
  grammatical condition on either sound law.
- **Counterexamples and falsification:** the masculine-source final
  matches are lost when the following carrier is recognized by
  syncope. Even the independent guma match is sensitive. Literal
  ō loses the inherited final quantity distinction. Neither fit
  nor unchanged selected-corpus counts repairs those deficiencies.
- **Positive evidence for analogy:** the class-wide distribution
  of feminine -ena beside inherited ōn-, the source-reported
  syncopated forms and restored -ana forms motivate separate
  paradigm redistribution/restoration. That ending evidence does
  not independently prove nominative flask root-vowel levelling.
- **Relationship between phenomenon and proxy:** the present
  selected-input cascade is not a general inflectional generator.
  Its nasal-trimoric admission and quantity-shadow interfaces need
  separate validation before genitive-plural adoption.
- **Chronology evidence:** no new edge. The distinction between
  syncope and later restored -ana follows the cited source's
  analysis, not a claimed new displacement discovery. SC073–SC085
  remains a computational boundary observation; the conditional
  thoae interpretation and independent SC085–SC086 evidence stand.

## Verdict

- **Verdict:** DEFER inherited-cell/transponent adoption; RETAIN
  production and the selected nominative/comparator.
- **Machine-readable line:** n/a: cross-rule research recommendation,
  not a new canonical SC adjudication.
- **Justification:** the attested genitive plural genuinely distinguishes
  the inherited feminine stem from the masculine-source hypothesis.
  The latter can produce the expected medial e in the current
  diagnostic, but the apparent regular success depends on a
  following-vowel-class omission. Restored -ana after syncope
  offers a source-supported analogical account to investigate,
  not an already completed regular derivation or authorized
  replacement input. Keep root vowel and consonant separate.
