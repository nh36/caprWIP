## Historical analysis

Old n-loss is (pre-)PGmc, pan-Germanic, regardless of its identifier.
Its executable move is stage-entailed relative to early apocope.
The adopted northern route retains post-tonic ō before final n through
fronting, then shortens and lowers it late. The condition is segmental
and prosodic, never “feminine” or “plural.” Before m or nonfinal n the
tested early shortening remains.

Root fronting/restoration gives `flæskōn > flaskōn`; SC071 and SC100
give `flaskon > flaskan`. Root, ending and cluster evidence remain
separate. Hard sk is rendered sc; x/metathesis is not newly implemented.
The late mechanism is independently supported, but survival of ō in
these cells is the adopted synthesis, not a newly demonstrated
chronology edge. Generic paradigm convergence is positive evidence
for some analogy, not proof that these two attested cells were remodelled.
Accusatives, pre-m raising and genitive plural retain their prior residue.
