## Historical analysis

The current sources establish West Germanic endingless vocalization and
subsequent English diphthong development, with later paradigm levelling.
They do not establish the implemented unrestricted prevocalic-singleton
lengthening as one West Saxon sound law. The straw derivation currently
applies the long mapping before losing its final nasal vowel and retains
the `w`; that is not the cited regular endingless history.

The incumbent `SC047 -> SC034` record is labelled
`independently_demonstrated`, but its witness is displacement of
the old straw proxy. Both interventions have now been reproduced: early
loss of straw's following vowel disables the proxy. That is model-local
dependency, not a historical edge for a product incorporating levelling.
The approved regular branch no longer fires at SC034. Reclassify the
record as a superseded boundary observation; retain its frozen card.
Old dew/hew and reciprocal SC031 boundaries remain superseded under the
adopted SC031 account.

The endingless fragment belongs to the source's PWGmc final-vowel-loss
and vocalization history; its insertion after dental hardening is a
compatible serialization, not independently demonstrated relative dating
against that unrelated change. SC030/SC032 consume its ordinary `au`
product without new diphthong semantics. Its w-adjacent matcher identifies
the represented combined fragment, not the historical domain of general
final-a loss [@RingeTaylor2014, pp. 172-173].

Show's three selected cells remain computationally compatible with the
retained mapping, but their singleton dictionary inputs do not execute
Campbell and Brunner's inherited-geminate account. Intervening class-II
`ō` excludes direct root `aw+j` gemination; it does not explain inherited
root gemination or prove lengthening before any following vowel.
No source-supported new root input or general singleton lengthening law
has been established [@Orel2003, p. 337; @Kroonen2013, p. 442;
@Campbell1959, p. 45; @SieversBrunner1965, p. 49].
