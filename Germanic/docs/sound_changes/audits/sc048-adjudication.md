## Question

What does final n represent in this rule, and which chronology claims
survive independent source and implementation checks? Written an cannot
alone prove an intermediate nasal vowel.

## Current state (before any edits)

The registry called this an unadjudicated OE/English-specific/A change.
Its retained implementation is two composed replacements: short a and
secondary à become short ą immediately before word-final n. The literal
environment is narrower than a coda nasal and excludes m and internal
nasal codas. The selected endpoint target remains West Saxon.

The [SC047 memorandum](sc047-adjudication.md) identifies controlling sources,
preparations, unchanged assumptions and the common baseline. The executable
order is restoration, apocope, nasalization. Older contrary FST comments
are corrected without altering the relation.

## Diagnosis

The complete fresh census has89 applications, all ordinary unmarked short
a of selected -aną after SC047 has removed final ą. The n comes from
that same input sequence, not from late n insertion. The target a is
unstressed as encoded; the secondary à companion has no selected firing
but is retained, with an explicit positive diagnostic. Accented á and long
ā remain negative controls. The full159/89 census and all immediate and
downstream forms are in the common evidence record.

Bake and bind feed this rule; thatch/horn remove ą without supplying
a before n. Dōną is a counterfeeding-negative for old final-n loss.
Protected flaskōn and selected Flask's fláskōniz have long ō, not short
a; their adopted late shortening/lowering produces flascan without SC048.
Staged guman and gumin keep the proposed donor and remodelled recipient
distinct. The latter gives gymen, not an automatic analogical guman.

The old87 failures are one reversal, reciprocally indexed on two cards.
The current89 include sow and betray in addition. With apocope between
restoration/nasalization, the reversed outer order gives bacen/waden;
with apocope before both, it gives bæcan/wædan. These isolate ending
exposure and root-trigger quality, respectively.

Wider early coda nasalization reaches internal nd and final/internal m.
Without an i-mutation consumer it incorrectly preserves nasal ą in
bindandī and gives selected lungan. Adding that consumer recovers
bindende and lungen. Onset fronting and a nasal low-vowel restoration
trigger are independently specified companions, not consequences of
moving SC048 alone. The coupled alternative preserves all394 endpoints.

The literal current back-mutation relation accepts an a-trigger for e,
despite its written u-only e clauses: the parallel replacements share
contexts. Thus delayed SC048 still gives steolan/weofan in the live
implementation. Separating the five clauses into composition, with their
written environments unchanged, removes both deltas; the strict WS control
preserves all394 endpoints. This falsifies the preregistered prediction
about the literal relation, but confirms the dialect-specific objection.

Direct coda blocking without nasalization changes only those two outputs
under the literal back-mutation relation and changes none under the strict
WS diagnostic. Its constructed gumanô gives gumana rather than
gumena: the relations are not universally equivalent. That ô is a
protected-tail carrier, not the source's literal PWGmc quantity, so this
is a transducer diagnostic, not independent historical refutation.
All output sets, including initial raw-input rejections, remain archived.

## Literature

Ringe--Taylor2014 pp.152--154 explicitly gives bindaną > bindan >
bindąn and remodelled PWGmc bindandī > bindąndī > bindændi > bindende.
The latter is an internal-coda example, not final n. Fronting occurs
before an onset nasal; coda nasality prevents it until i-mutation where
that later condition applies. Hogg Phonology pp.214--217 §§6.1--6
also places nasalization before the relevant fronting and discusses
stress. Campbell pp.140--141 §§333--334 describes the coda/onset
contrast and participial paradigm extension. His §403 p.166 addresses
nasal consonant loss before fricatives, not an independent date here.

Ringe--Taylor153--154 explains the an-extension from regular accusative
singular/nominative plural in guma; Fulk2018 pp.168--171 instead gives
an older un accusative, with Ruthwell galgu as evidence, and an-extension
from other cells. The first experiment's gúmunų was an explicitly
offered NWGmc stage, not Fulk's PGmc reconstruction: his PGmc -on-um
is represented by already admitted gúmonų. The miscoded gúmammiz is
not the source's dat.pl.; Ringe--Taylor prints guma(m)maz, while Fulk
starts from -on-miz. The competing donor analyses are not conflated.

Ringe--Taylor319--327 and Hogg149--157 distinguish WS e/u from
non-WS e/a mutation. The Mercian weofan/WS wefan comparison is
Ringe--Taylor325; it does not independently establish a WS nasalization
cutoff before back mutation. Hogg's Ball/Stiles reports and Versloot's
alternative chronology receive their bounded assessment in the shared
source card. Faulkner's preprint pp.3--7 distinguishes consistent spelling
from inferred phonetic reduction. Original-access gaps are explicitly
listed in SC047 and the source assessment.

## Historical analysis

- **Stage/scope:** inherited northern WG unstressed coda nasalization
  before the relevant fronting, represented as early Anglo-Frisian/
  North-Sea-Germanic metadata; confidence B is independent. This is
  not a claim that every daughter implemented all environments identically.
- **Conditioning:** short unstressed low vowel before a tautosyllabic
  nasal, not an infinitive, noun or other grammatical category. Final n
  is a selected-input proxy. The coda approximation n/m before consonant
  or boundary is declared, not treated as a general syllabification theory.
- **Bounds:** apocope supplies final n in the infinitive series, while
  an internal nd coda needs no such feeding. Nasal nonfronting precedes
  ordinary unstressed fronting and, in bindandī, i-mutation. These
  source-supported bounds do not date every nasality interval absolutely.
  A universal nasalization-before-apocope or nasalization-before-WS-
  back-mutation claim does not follow.
- **Chronology roles:** SC047→SC048 is technical feeding for the
  retained n-final relation; SC046→SC048 is a technical trigger-quality
  dependency under the present encoding. Both directions of the old
  SC048/SC059 displacement evidence are retained as model-local records,
  not independently demonstrated historical chronology.
- **Analogy:** regular an cells provide the proposed source for other
  guma cases; the source authors disagree over which accusative was
  regular. Inflected participles provide the proposed en donor for
  endingless participles. Those explicit paradigmatic arguments, not
  resemblance or poor scores, warrant the discussion. No levelling rule
  is hidden in the transducer.

## Verdict

**REFORMULATE historical domain, stage/scope and edge interpretations /
RETAIN the final-n executable proxy and its current position.**

Registry-verdict: SC048=REFORMULATE/RETAIN

The source-backed phenomenon is broader than final-n nasalization, but
these89 applications are regularly in-domain. The wider coda candidate
needs separately source-supported consumers and trigger handling; it does
not beat all alternatives merely by equalling the selected score.
The retained narrow relation is explicitly a computational proxy, not a
claim that internal nasals failed to nasalize unstressed vowels.

## Residue

Actual nasality versus direct blocking remains inferential where both
produce the same observations. The constructed protected-tail control
distinguishes the tested relations but does not choose the history.
Actual PGmc gumanǭ gives gumne; newly admitted gumanų gives gumn,
and the dative controls also expose wider cascade limitations. No wrong
whole-word history is passed off as an established regular guma paradigm.
The source-shaped wider coda implementation and strict WS clause
separation are reviewable, unadopted alternatives. Full missing originals
and whole-paradigm modelling remain qualified. No new Mercian cascade or
next sound-change investigation is begun.
