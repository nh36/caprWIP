coverage census. Rust is a live application, not a phonological exemption.

new *rústaz lowers to *róstaz and instead fires SC020's final-z loss.

## Literature

Orel (2003, printed p. 309) actually gives *rustō ~ *rustaz, `sb.m./f.`,
with OE rust, OS rost and OHG rost. The conventional ō-stem and masculine
az-stem alternatives are real alternatives within Orel, not an exclusive
disagreement with Ringe. Existing image-checked evidence identities
`orel-core-2162-1` and `orel-core-2162-2` are preserved.

Ringe (2017, pp. 247-248; the actual *rustaz citation is on p. 248)
independently supports the masculine formation. His suffix/cluster-history
alternatives for st are a morphological question, not an explanation of
SC017's retained-vowel mismatch (`ringe-system-rust`).

Clark Hall (1960, p. 245) gives rūst m.; Toller's Bosworth-Toller Supplement
(1921, printed p. 691, original sheet703 visually verified)
records the noun and rúst/rust spelling alternatives. The supplement removes
the uncertainty sign beside the gender while retaining a quantity question.
The earlier p.677/main-dictionary attribution was erroneous: p.677 has or-
entries, while the held original is the separately catalogued Supplement.
Current citations are corrected without rewriting the old verification records.
These signs are preserved as source spelling, not silently interpreted as
a new quantity for CAPR's comparator rust.

Campbell (1959, p. 43, §115) describes regular lowering and retained-u
exceptions, including fugol, bucca and wulf; that paragraph does not itself
name rust. Ringe and Taylor (2014, pp. 27-34) discuss lowering and the
remaining unexplained retentions. OS/OHG rost furnish comparative lowering
context; they are not new runtime targets.

CAPR selects the masculine citation/input on morphological and comparative
grounds, retains Orel's feminine alternative, and supplies its own acute
stress notation. No sound law, vowel-quantity reinterpretation, analogy or
grammatical conditioner is adopted.
