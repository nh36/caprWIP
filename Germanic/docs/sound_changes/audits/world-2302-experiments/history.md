## Archived explanations and current limits

DEV_NOTES §§14.6 and16.6 record the successive transponents and older
parser failures. General early syncope produced large regressions in those
historical models (including epenthesis interactions); those counts are
historical receipts, not measurements of the current cascade.
The later notes' wish to derive more steps was computationally productive
but does not independently date the analogy or reconstruct the stress.

DEV_NOTES §17.11 recognises the selected input lacks a linking vowel,
but its suggestion that literal `a+à` automatically satisfies linking syncope
is refuted by the present component and complete-cascade tests.
The second root vowel is not a consonantal separator.

The early `prosodic_tier_research.md` proposal §§9–10 supplies the
design rationale for acute/grave and linking-vowel stress troughs. Its
multilevel stress assumptions are hypotheses, not independent word-specific
evidence. In particular, the current grave cannot simultaneously be cited
as proof of retained secondary prominence and as proof of its loss.
Goering2023's early-OE footing evidence concerns a later prosodic system;
it neither disproves an earlier compound accent nor supplies its exact
loss date. The proposed reclassification, composition resolution, loss of
transparency and later footing must not be collapsed into one event.

## Present disposition

Keep the production string to avoid a scientifically underjustified
stage/notation migration. State the compact stem, inflection and accent
assumptions openly. An earlier *-ō* input is computationally available,
not historically selected. Original *i* remains a supported comparative
tradition with an *e* alternative; it is not retained merely to make SC015
fire. No original source is claimed to print the exact CAPR selected form.
