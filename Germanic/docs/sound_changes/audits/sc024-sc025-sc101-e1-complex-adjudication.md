# SC024/SC025/SC101 — the *ē₁ complex: two sound changes, three executable rules

Registry-verdict: SC024=SPLIT/REFORMULATE/REORDER; SC025=REFORMULATE/REORDER; SC101=SPLIT

Status: adjudicated (this memo governs SC024, SC025 and SC101)
Scope: the complete history of Proto-Germanic *ē₁ in CAPR — the former
one-step SC024 `PNWGmcLongELowering`, the former SC025
`PNWGmcLongENasalRounding`, and the new SC101 `EAFLongAFronting`.
Supersedes in implementation: `sc024-adjudication.md` (whose historical
research this memo builds on, but whose decision to RETAIN the one-step
executable proxy is rejected; see §9).

Verdict summary:

- **SC024 = SPLIT/REFORMULATE/REORDER.** The old rule telescoped two
  historically distinct changes. SC024 now implements only the early
  Northwest Germanic lowering, stressed *ē₁ > *ā, unconditioned, as
  `{*ḗ} -> {*ā}` at cascade position 12.
- **SC101 = SPLIT.** New identity for the second half of the old
  telescope: the much later northern West Germanic fronting of
  non-nasalized *ā > *ǣ, `{*ā} -> {*ǣ} / _ [C − nasal]`, position 27,
  immediately before SC004 `EAFAiMonophthongization`.
- **SC025 = REFORMULATE/REORDER.** The nasal branch no longer bypasses
  the intermediate stage: `EAFLongANasalRounding`, `{*ā} -> {*ō} / _
  nasal`, position 26, consuming the *ā produced by SC024.

All 383 corpus surface outputs are unchanged; what changed is the
internal derivational history, which now passes through the
reconstructed intermediate *ā, and the chronology, which is now carried
by real cascade order instead of symbol separation.

## 0. What CAPR claimed before this adjudication

Before the first SC024 adjudication, CAPR presented a single
Proto-Northwest Germanic rule: *ē/*ḗ > *ǣ before a non-nasal consonant
(`pnwgmc`/`pan_pnwgmc`, confidence A, position 22), with a separate
PNWGmc nasal rule *ē/*ḗ > *ō before nasal (SC025, position 23).

The first adjudication (`sc024-adjudication.md`) discovered that this
telescopes two historically distinct developments and that the *ǣ
outcome is Anglo-Frisian, not pan-NWGmc — but it RETAINed the one-step
executable proxy, arguing that the `*ē`/`*ā` symbol separation encoded
the Campbell §132 ordering invisibly. That retention is what this
follow-up adjudication reverses: CAPR's modelling policy requires two
historical changes separated substantially in time to be two executable
rules whose ordering work is done by cascade position.

## 1. The adopted reconstruction

CAPR adopts the two-step reconstruction of Ringe & Taylor:

- **Change A (SC024): early Northwest Germanic stressed *ē₁ > *ā**, in
  all environments, nasal forms included. Pan-NWGmc; already reflected
  in Early Runic in the later second century AD.
- **Change B (SC101 / SC025): much later northern West Germanic
  treatment of the resulting *ā** — non-nasalized *ā fronted to *ǣ
  (Old English/Old Frisian front reflexes, sporadic Old Saxon ⟨e⟩),
  nasalized *ą̄ rounded to *ō (SC025).

### 1.1 Primary linguistic evidence

Kept separate from the scholarship interpreting it:

- **Gothic vs. NWGmc split**: Gothic ⟨e⟩ /ē/ in *ga-dēþs* 'deed',
  *slēpan* 'sleep', *mēna* 'moon', *jēr* 'year', *nēþla* 'needle'
  against ON á (*ráða, láta, mál, ár, nál, vápn*), OHG ā (*tāt,
  slāfan, jār, māno, sāmo*), OS ā (*dād, slāpan, jār, māno*). The
  lowering is common to all of North and West Germanic and absent from
  East Germanic.
- **Early Runic**: *mākija* 'sword' (Vimose chape, later 2nd c. AD)
  with ⟨a⟩ for the *ē₁ reflex; Opedal *swestar* with a-rune for the
  stressed reflex, supported by early Proto-Scandinavian loans into
  Sami showing /aː/ (Lid 1952: 238, apud Stiles 2017: 4). This dates
  the *ā stage absolutely and places it in the North.
- **Old English / Old Frisian front reflexes**: WS ǣ (*dǣd, slǣpan,
  ġēar* with later diphthongization, *sċēap*), Anglian/Kentish and
  OFris ē (*dēd, slēpa, jēr*), against OS/OHG/ON ā — the front outcome
  is geographically restricted to the North Sea coast.
- **Old Saxon variability**: sporadic OS ⟨e⟩ spellings for expected ā
  (Gallée's material, apud R/T), and variable OS rounding before
  nasals (*ōdar, sōd* vs. *quān, sān(o)*) — Old Saxon participates
  unevenly in both the fronting and the nasal rounding, which is why
  the scope of the later development is North Sea Germanic rather than
  strictly Anglo-Frisian.
- **Nasal forms**: OE *mōna* 'moon', *mōnaþ* 'month', *spōn* 'spoon'
  (OHG *māno, mānōd, spān*; ON *máni, mánaðr, spánn*) — the nasal
  environment yields *ō in the North Sea area, ā elsewhere.
- **þǣr / hwǣr**: the adverbs 'there', 'where' show the same front
  vowel, argued by Stiles (2004) to require the *ā detour in
  Anglo-Frisian rather than direct retention of a front vowel.
- **Non-fronting of ā < *ai**: OE *stān* 'stone', *hām* 'home', *lāþ*,
  *rāp*, *tācn*, *gāst* keep back ā although they satisfy the fronting
  environment — the ā from *ai must postdate the fronting.

### 1.2 Secondary scholarship

- **Ringe & Taylor 2014**: pp. 11–13 state the NWGmc lowering with the
  full witness list, print the intermediates *mānō, *mānōþ-, *spānuz —
  i.e. **nasal forms underwent *ē₁ > *ā too** — and restrict the change
  to **stressed** syllables (p. 13 with n. 3; unstressed *ē took the
  shortening path, *fadēr > *fader, p. 147). P. 12 cites Grønvik
  (1998: 87) for the dating value of *mākija*. §5.1.2 pp. 146–152
  describe the later fronting (non-nasalized, stressed, blocked before
  *w not followed by a high front vocalic) and the nasal rounding,
  with the OS evidence. Pp. 169–170 endorse Campbell's ordering
  argument (§132) as "cogent": fronting was well under way before
  *ai > ā was complete. P. 216 derives *sċēap* via already-fronted *ǣ.
- **Stiles 2004** (apud R/T pp. 13–14 and Fulk 2018 §4.6 n. 2): the
  þǣr/hwǣr argument for the *ā detour.
- **Stiles 2017** (Opedal *swestar*): the a-rune already writes the
  stressed *ē₁ reflex in the earliest inscriptions; unstressed final
  *-ē had shortened and merged with *-i (*faþiR*).
- **Campbell 1959** §§128–129 pp. 50–51: deliberately noncommittal on
  whether Anglo-Frisian ǣ/ē passed through *ā; §132 pp. 52–53: the
  ordering argument from *stān*-type non-fronting; §127 p. 50: the
  nasal rounding; §185 pp. 69–70: *sċēap, ġēar* by palatal
  diphthongization of the already-fronted vowel.
- **Fulk 2018** §4.6 pp. 60–61 (with notes): the strongest modern
  alternative — NWGmc *ǣ, retained unchanged in Anglo-Frisian, with
  backing to ā a separate North Germanic plus inner-West-Germanic
  areal development spreading from Upper German northward (1st c. to
  ca. 500); the *swā/sō coalescence argument; Angle/Frisian ethnonyms
  in ⟨e⟩; n. 2 counters Stiles 2004; n. 4 reads Thorsberg *-marir*
  (ca. 200) as already showing ⟨a⟩.
- **Retention tradition**: Bennett 1950, Grønvik (1981: 43–50;
  1998: 87–89), and older handbooks (Wright): Gmc *ē₁ was still ē or ǣ
  everywhere, with lowering/backing as late parallel developments.

### 1.3 Why the two-step analysis, and why confidence B

CAPR adopts the R/T two-step because (i) the Early Runic and Sami-loan
evidence puts [aː] on the ground in Scandinavia by the 2nd–3rd
centuries, i.e. an early *ā stage is directly attested in the North;
(ii) the þǣr/hwǣr evidence (Stiles 2004) is most naturally read as
front-vowel outputs of a back *ā input inside Anglo-Frisian; (iii) the
non-fronting of ā < *ai then follows from ordinary rule ordering
(Campbell §132), which a retention analysis must instead attribute to
accidental non-merger.

But Fulk's alternative is serious: the runic ⟨a⟩ spellings could
represent a low *front* [æː] for which the futhark had no better
grapheme, and the ethnonym and *swā/sō evidence is not trivially
dismissed. Campbell declined to decide. The reconstruction of the
intermediate value is therefore genuinely disputed, and all three
registry rows carry **confidence B**, not A. What is *not* disputed is
the surface comparative distribution (Gothic ē vs. NWGmc non-front ā
vs. North Sea front reflexes) — only the path between them.

## 2. Change A — SC024 `PNWGmcLongELowering`

- **Historical phenomenon**: stressed *ē₁ > *ā throughout North and
  West Germanic.
- **Conditioning**: none segmental — the change is unconditioned in
  stressed syllables. The nasal forms went through *ā (R/T's printed
  *mānōþ-, *spānuz); the old "before non-nasal consonant" restriction
  was purely an artifact of telescoping the later fronting into this
  rule. The genuine restriction is **stress**: R/T p. 13 n. 3;
  unstressed *ē shortened instead (*fadēr > *fader, p. 147; Stiles
  2017 on *faþiR*).
- **Stage/scope**: `pnwgmc` / `pan_pnwgmc`.
- **Chronology**: under way by the later 2nd c. AD (*mākija*); no
  in-cascade earlier boundary (runner-limited at bundled
  `PWGmcChanges`).
- **Executable**: `{*ḗ} -> {*ā}` (stressed tier only), position 12,
  at the head of the NWGmc block — before `MonosyllabicFinalZLoss`, so
  that secondary compensatory *ḗ created there cannot feed it, and
  before every rule that consumes *ā.
- **Census: 15 firings** — the 13 stressed oral roots (adder, bier,
  deed, eel, hair, let, meal, needle, read, sheep, sleep, weapon,
  year) plus month and spoon, which now correctly pass through *ā.

### 2.1 The five former unstressed firings

The old rule also changed *father, mother, sister, have, live*, whose
selected inputs carry **unstressed** plain `*ē`. These were never
witnesses of the stressed law (the attested OE outputs *fæder, mōdor,
sweostor, hæfþ, lifaþ* have short vowels). Restricting the executable
input to the stressed tier `{*ḗ}` removes them from SC024's domain;
they now take the independently existing §6.8.3 unstressed
long-vowel-shortening path (`{*ē} -> {*e}`), matching R/T p. 147
(*fadēr > *fader) — and their surface outputs are unchanged. This is a
conditioning repair, not a selected-input change. (A related corpus
issue — unmarked stress on some ō/ū-root protoforms — is recorded and
deferred; it does not affect SC024, whose input tier is fully marked.)

## 3. Change B — SC101 `EAFLongAFronting` (new identity)

- **Historical phenomenon**: much later fronting of non-nasalized
  stressed *ā > *ǣ in the northern West Germanic / North Sea Germanic
  dialect continuum (WS ǣ; Anglian/Kentish/OFris ē; sporadic OS ⟨e⟩).
- **Conditioning**: stressed, non-nasalized *ā; historically blocked
  before *w followed by anything but a high front vocalic (R/T
  p. 149). No corpus row reaches the *w environment, so the blocking
  is documented rather than encoded.
- **Stage/scope**: `eaf` / `north_sea_germanic` — not mechanically
  "Anglo-Frisian", because the sporadic OS ⟨e⟩ spellings show uneven
  Old Saxon participation.
- **Executable**: `{*ā} -> {*ǣ} / _ [EnglishStarConsonant −
  EnglishStarNasal]`, position 27, immediately before SC004.
- **Census: 13 firings** — the 13 stressed oral roots above.
- **Chronology**: must precede the completion of *ai > ā (SC004) and
  WS palatal diphthongization (SC056); see §5. The old sheep/year
  SC056 edge attaches **here**, not to SC024 — SC101 is the rule that
  supplies the fronted vowel SC056 operates on.

## 4. The nasal branch — SC025 `EAFLongANasalRounding`

- **Historical phenomenon**: rounding of nasalized *ą̄ > *ō in the
  same northern West Germanic area (OE *mōna, mōnaþ, spōn*; OS
  variably *ōdar, sōd* vs. *quān, sān(o)* — the OS variation shows
  the nasalization was there and the rounding dialectally uneven).
- **Reformulation**: input is now the historical *ā of that stage —
  `{*ā} -> {*ō} / _ nasal` — no longer a direct *ē > *ō bypass of
  Change A. Renamed from `PNWGmcLongENasalRounding`; stage/scope
  corrected from `pnwgmc`/`pan_pnwgmc` (wrong on both counts) to
  `eaf`/`north_sea_germanic`.
- **Relation to SC101**: complementary conditioned outcomes of the
  same later low-vowel development (nasalized → rounded, oral →
  fronted). The corpus cannot order them against each other and no
  claim is made; their mutual adjacency at positions 26–27 is
  arbitrary. An explicit nasalization microstep (*ā > *ą̄) is **not**
  modelled: CAPR's segment inventory has no nasalized-vowel tier and
  the scholarship does not force an independently *ordered*
  nasalization event between A and the rounding.
- **Census: 2 firings** — month (*mḗnōþz > *mānōþ- > mōnaþ), spoon
  (*spḗnuz > *spānuz > spōn), exactly R/T's printed intermediates.
- Nasal-spirant lengthening (SC026, goose-type *gansz > gōs) outputs
  `{*ō}` directly and never interacts with this rule.

## 5. Chronology: order now does the work

The previous architecture hid the Campbell §132 ordering inside symbol
separation (*ē vs. ā never merged because the fronting rule read `*ē`).
That trick is retired. The cascade now produces the correct non-mergers
by **position**, verified by displacement probes (full-cascade
recompilation with the rule moved, all 383 rows checked):

| Edge | Type | Witnesses | Displacement result |
|---|---|---|---|
| SC024 < SC025 | one-sided, independently demonstrated (feeding) | month, spoon | SC024 after SC025/SC101: **mānaþ, **spān |
| SC024 < SC101 | one-sided, independently demonstrated (feeding) | sheep, year, sleep | SC024 after SC101: **sċāp, **ġār, **slāpan |
| SC025 < SC004 | one-sided, independently demonstrated | stone, home | SC025 after SC004: **stōn, **hōm |
| SC101 < SC004 | one-sided, independently demonstrated | loath, rope, token, soul, ghost | SC101 after SC004: **lǣþ, **rǣp, **tǣcn, **sǣwol, **ġēast |
| SC101 < SC056 | broad/far, independently demonstrated | sheep, year | SC101 after SC056: **sċǣp, **ġǣr |
| SC024 earlier side | runner-limited at bundled `PWGmcChanges` | — | no earlier in-cascade break; absolute anchor is *mākija* (2nd c.), external to the cascade |

Interpretation discipline:

- The SC025/SC101 < SC004 edges are the executable expression of
  Campbell §132 / R/T pp. 169–170: *ā < *ai arose after the
  fronting/rounding of older *ā was under way. Historically the
  changes may have **overlapped** in time; a discrete cascade must
  serialize them, and this order is the one the evidence licenses.
- The SC101 < SC056 edge is the genuine historical content of the old
  SC024 → SC056 sheep/year edge: WS palatal diphthongization operated
  on the already-fronted vowel (Campbell §185; R/T p. 216). It is
  independently demonstrated (comparative WS forms), not merely
  stage-entailed.
- The feeding edges SC024 < SC025/SC101 are demonstrated inside the
  model and historically real (the intermediates *mānōþ-, *spānuz are
  R/T's own reconstructions), but their *tightness* is model-local;
  historically several centuries separate A from B.
- The absence of an earlier in-cascade break for SC024 is a runner
  boundary, **not** a positive lower boundary. The lower bound comes
  from external evidence only (*mākija*, Sami loans).

## 6. Executable implementation

In `Germanic/fsts/germanic.txt` (and mirrored in
`old_english_sandbox.txt`):

```
define PNWGmcLongELowering [ {*ḗ} -> {*ā} ];
define EAFLongANasalRounding [ {*ā} -> {*ō} || _ EnglishStarNasal ];
define EAFLongAFronting [ {*ā} -> {*ǣ} || _ [EnglishStarConsonant - EnglishStarNasal] ];
```

composed at positions 12 (SC024), 26 (SC025), 27 (SC101), with SC004
at 28, in both the production pipeline (`EnglishProtoToOE`) and the
trace pipeline. Sandbox stage bins:
`old_english_sandbox_after_pnwgmc_long_e_lowering.bin`,
`..._after_eaf_long_a_nasal_rounding.bin`,
`..._after_eaf_long_a_fronting.bin`.

**Surface neutrality**: after the split, the full mismatch report is
byte-identical to the committed baseline — all 383 corpus outputs
unchanged, both fingerprints unchanged. The change is to internal
derivational history and chronology, exactly as intended.

## 9. Why the previous RETAIN was reversed

The first adjudication weighed (a) surface-output fidelity, (b) the
symbol-separation encoding of Campbell §132, and (c) minimal churn, and
kept the telescope. The governing modelling principle now applied is
stricter: **if the preferred reconstruction contains two distinct
changes separated substantially in time, CAPR must represent them as
two executable changes** — separate identities, rules, stages,
censuses, chronology, and write-ups. The intermediate *ā stage is not
rejected or unreconstructible (it is R/T's central claim and is
directly supported by Early Runic evidence), so a compressed proxy is
not acceptable. The reversal costs nothing empirically (383/383
outputs identical) and gains: real feeding edges, order-encoded
non-merger with ā < *ai, historically correct internal derivations
(*mōnaþ via *mānōþ-), and the removal of five spurious census members.
