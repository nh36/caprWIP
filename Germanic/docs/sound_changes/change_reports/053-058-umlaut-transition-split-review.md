mixed to promote unchanged. `SC053` and `SC058` are negative or boundary-limited

## Possible split options

### Option A: Keep SC053-SC058 as one scaffold only

This remains useful for temporary visibility because it keeps the whole
transition zone present in the assembled half. It is a poor production shape,
however, because the unit mixes a major historical chapter candidate (`SC055`),
one tight follower (`SC056`), one narrow bridge (`SC054`), one likely
cross-cluster member (`SC057`), and two residual negative cards (`SC053`,
`SC058`).

### Option B: Promote SC055-SC056 as an umlaut/palatal-diphthongization core

This is the strongest immediate production path. `SC055` is historically
central, already sits in a growing reciprocal chronology network, and has a
clear right-hand local boundary in `SC056`. `SC056` is not equally central as a
chapter headline, but it is close enough to the umlaut core to work as a paired
right-hand section. This option still needs literature and book dossiers before
promotion.

### Option C: Split SC054 as a small W-loss-before-i bridge and SC055-SC056 as the core

This is structurally cleaner than forcing `SC054` into the same promoted chapter
as `SC055-SC056`. The `sea` evidence is real and worth preserving, but it is too
narrow to dominate the umlaut chapter. Treating `SC054` as a short bridge note
or later singleton would keep it visible without overstating its weight.

### Option D: Move or cross-reference SC057 toward a palatalization/fronting cluster

`SC057` has a positive earlier boundary across `SC052`, not across `SC055` or
`SC056`, and its representative failures are tied to the same broader
palatalization/fronting neighborhood as `SC049-SC052`. That does not require an
immediate row split in the scaffold, but it does mean `SC057` should be
reconsidered when the `SC049-SC052` cluster gets its own structural review.

### Option E: Leave SC053 and SC058 as residual scaffold notes

At present neither edge member has a positive current first-break boundary in
either direction. They therefore should not be promoted without either stronger
evidence or a separate literary reason to foreground them.

## Recommended next action

The next practical task should be to prepare literature and book dossiers for
`SC055-SC056` as the likely next promotion candidate. `SC053`, `SC054`, `SC057`,
and `SC058` should remain scaffolded for now. Within that residual set, `SC054`
can be kept in view as a possible short bridge note later, while `SC057` should
be reconsidered when `SC049-SC052` is reviewed as a palatalization/fronting
cluster. No promotion is recommended in this split-review task.
