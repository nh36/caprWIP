## Current scaffold problem

but it is not yet ready for promotion unchanged. `SC064` has real first-break
evidence on both sides, yet both boundaries are concentrated in the same
`fright` derivation. That makes the rule historically meaningful, but narrow.

The right edge is still worth taking seriously. `SC064`'s later boundary across

chronology already visible on the `SC072` card. Even so, the reciprocal relation
is still carried by the same `fright` witness, so it should not yet be treated
as a broad chapter-defining evidence base.

`SC065` is weaker. Its card has **no positive first-break boundary in either
direction**. The earlier side stops only at the bundled `PWGmcChanges` runner
boundary, and the later side reaches the current `SC087` runner boundary with no
real break. It should therefore not be treated as if the current card evidence
showed that `SC065` must follow a specific earlier stage or must precede `SC087`.

That leaves the pair structurally awkward but still useful. `SC064-SC065` sits
between the already promoted `SC063` **High-vowel apocope** chapter and the
already promoted `SC066-SC068` **Syncope and degemination corridor**. The pair
may therefore be better understood, at least for now, as a bridge note in the
late weak-tail zone rather than as an already settled full chapter.

## Possible split options

### Option A: Keep SC064-SC065 as one scaffold

This is the safest immediate choice. It keeps the immediate post-apocope tail
visible without pretending that the unit is already ready for production prose.
It also preserves the bridge between the promoted `SC063` singleton and the
promoted `SC066-SC068` corridor.

### Option B: Prepare a literature dossier for SC064-SC065 as a pair

This is probably the best next research move if the goal is to learn whether the
source tradition treats in-stem n-loss and medial syncope together as a real
post-apocope tail. The risk is that the dossier may confirm an uneven pair:
`SC064` has narrow positive card evidence, while `SC065` is still card-negative.

### Option C: Prepare SC064 alone as a narrow bridge report

This foregrounds the only member with positive chronology-card evidence. The
problem is that the evidence is extremely narrow and concentrated entirely in
`fright`. That may be enough for a later bridge note, but it is not yet an
obvious standalone chapter center.

### Option D: Absorb SC065 narratively into SC066-SC068 later

This may prove attractive if later source work shows that `SC065` functions
mainly as lead-in context for the already promoted syncope/degemination
corridor. Nothing in the current task justifies moving it now, but the option
should remain open.

### Option E: Leave SC065 as residual scaffold material

This may ultimately be the cleanest outcome unless literature review shows that
`SC065` has independent historical value despite the current negative card.
Right now the chronology evidence alone does not justify treating it as a strong
paired member.

### Option F: Treat SC064 as a cross-reference between SC063 and SC072

This option takes the `fright` evidence seriously without overinflating it.
`SC064`'s later boundary across `SC072` may make it more useful as a weak-tail
chronology note between the promoted `SC063` apocope chapter and the later
unstressed-tail region than as the center of a chapter in its own right.

## Recommended next action

Do **not** promote `SC064-SC065` now. The best next task is to prepare a
literature and book dossier for the **pair**, but with an explicit warning up
front that `SC065` is still card-negative and that the source work may end by
recommending a later split.

That dossier phase should ask three concrete questions:

1. whether the source tradition really treats `SC064` and `SC065` as one
   immediate post-apocope tail;
2. whether `SC064` is strong enough to stand later as a narrow bridge note on
   its own;
3. whether `SC065` belongs better as residual context between `SC063` and the
   already promoted `SC066-SC068` corridor.

Until that source work exists, the current row should stay scaffolded. The most
likely future outcomes are still open: a cautious paired note, a narrower
`SC064`-centered note, or residual treatment for `SC065`.
