# Hock1973: source integration for SC015

## Scope and result

This is a source review, not a second SC015 adjudication. It starts from
`update` at `014defd4253bd93ddc300532b603497ef91c9d5c` and preserves the
adopted `PNWGmcILowering`, registered REFORMULATE verdict, Northwest
Germanic scope/placement, selected inputs, targets and protected baselines.
No new sound law, chronology edge or lexical admission is proposed.

Hock is a substantial defence of vowel-conditioned lowering, not an
authority for rejecting it. His retention of an e/i phonemic contrast
does not contradict his acceptance of lowering before low vowels.
The reader should represent that argument and its later criticisms
explicitly rather than dismiss it by citation to phonemic distinctness.

## What Hock actually argues

| Pages | Argument and evidential status |
|---|---|
| 319--327 | Reviews the merger and non-merger proposals of Collitz, Twaddell, Fourquet, Marchand, Lehmann and Antonsen. Analogy between formerly conditioned variants requires a phonemic contrast at the time of remodelling, not necessarily throughout all earlier history. Hock rejects unsupported dialect-borrowing machinery and does not regard symmetry alone as proof. |
| 328--329, 331 | North Germanic breaking and Old English evidence preserve an e/i contrast before u. A later merger in that environment is confined to Old High German and Old Saxon. This rejects a complete ancestral merger without rejecting conditioned i-lowering before low vowels. |
| 330--334 | Criticizes claims that original e and ai categorically blocked lowering; discusses runic spelling, weak-verb suffix history and kinship nouns. These are substantive historical proposals, not new CAPR rules. |
| 334--338 | Answers Lloyd's proposed preservation of unstressed e and the nominal endings used to motivate levelling. Hock argues for early final-vowel loss and subsequent unstressed e-raising. The proposed endings and the relative chronology carry explanatory weight. |
| 339--341 | Compares unstable reflexes of original i and u. For class I participles, the proposed restoring model is especially the i-timbre of the present and avoidance of ambiguity with other classes, not merely the preterite plural. Zero-grade presents and class transfers are additional evidence for his lowering account. |
| 342 | Confronts the adverbs rather than ignoring them: proposes distinct formations, including an on-suffix and a comparative/deictic type, then analogical transfers. Old English heonan and neoðan are positive evidence in his argument. He explicitly does not claim to know every formation's details. |
| 343, 349 n. 29 | Proposes an inherited fish i-stem transferred to the a-stems after phonemicization, partly motivated by denominative verbs. His own note acknowledges that those verbs' vocalism would not itself be original under his account. The proposed stem transfer and its date therefore require independent evidence. |
| 343--345 | Acknowledges the weakness of derivational "quasi-paradigms" as sufficient restoring models; combines case forms and ultimately proposes nominative/vocative syncretism. Gothic supports the inherited existence of a vocative, but its survival and formal merger at the required Northwest Germanic date remain inferential. |
| 345--346 | Summarizes an ordered account of Germanic prehistory: final short-vowel loss, unstressed e-raising, conditioned stressed e-raising, i/u lowering before low vowels, phonemicization and subsequent levelling. Section 10.4 explicitly places e-raising before u later in the continental area. Nasal-conditioned raising is not independently ordered by the same argument. |

These are phonological laws followed by separately proposed analogical
changes in Hock's reconstruction. Reporting his analogical explanations
does not make grammatical category an exception to a CAPR sound law.
Nor does his discussion license lexical diffusion or arbitrary exclusions.

## Comparison with the existing authorities

| Authority | Agreement, disagreement and consequence |
|---|---|
| Lloyd1966 pp. 739--745 | Agrees that a complete merger is not established and that the morphological premises matter. Disagrees on the conclusion: Lloyd rejects general early a-umlaut of i, invokes systemic analogy and tentatively separates a rhotic lowering; Hock defends lowering, criticizes the case-ending argument and offers specific restoring models. They must not be cited as if they endorsed one account. |
| Cercignani1979 pp. 75--82 | Agrees on preserved opposition before u and in other environments. Explicitly challenges Hock's unstressed-e chronology and the vocative-syncretism solution (pp. 76--78), while allowing some participial analogy (p. 81 n. 52). Phonemic opposition and conditioned lowering are independent issues in this debate. |
| Cercignani1980 pp. 129--132 | Accepts a role for vowel-conditioned lowering but rejects uniform ancestral dating/conditioning and explicitly questions Hock's adverb and fish explanations (p. 131). The Norse preceding-velar restriction cannot be exported to West Germanic. His daughter-specific reconstruction also disagrees with Hock's common pre-Northwest-Germanic serialization. |
| Hill2017 pp. 149--158 | Accepts some nominal levelling and cites Hock for it (p. 149 n. 21), but directly rejects the class I present-model explanation: copying the infinitive's vocalism should copy long ī, not yield the observed short i (pp. 151--152). The distinction matters because Hock expressly proposes i-timbre, whereas Hill requires independent justification for selective qualitative remodelling. Hill offers r/z/st conditioning and dates the r-component conditionally; Hock does not independently support those environments. |
| Fulk2018 pp. 56--59 | Continues to defend vowel-conditioned lowering and analogical influence from the present; retains beðinn as a possible lowered participle and discusses the counterproposal. This keeps Hock's explanatory family alive, without validating every step in his 1973 chronology. Fulk's methodological Northwest-Germanic placement is qualified, not unanimous proof of common ancestry for every cited reflex. |
| Ringe--Taylor2014 pp. 34--36 | Distinguishes widespread wer/nest from southern West Germanic lowering and class-transfer cases. Page 35 n. 17 explicitly criticizes Hock's insufficient separation of evidence classes and notes that Old English eo for expected io is much later. Thus heonan/neoðan cannot, from their surface diphthong alone, establish Hock's ancestral i-to-e event. This is a material chronological control, not a reason to ignore his examples. |

## New comparisons, and what they do not prove

Hock p. 329 offers 'equal' as a tentative further lowering comparison:
OE ef(e)n beside ON jafn/jamn and the other Germanic cognates might
continue *imno-*. He acknowledges the disputed etymology and hypothesizes
mn-to-bn dissimilation before phonemicization of the lowered vowel to
remove the apparent nasal-cluster obstruction. If that reconstructed
high vowel and chronology were independently established, lowering in
this formation would fall outside r/z/st. It is therefore a conditional
counterexample worth recording, not an already demonstrated new
exception or a source-supported complete CAPR input. The absence of
counterevidence to a chronology is not positive proof of that chronology.

The adverb formations, the proposed fish i-stem, and the vocative-based
explanation add concrete hypotheses to the SC015 discussion. They are
newly read arguments, not newly established exceptions to the adopted law.
Each depends on a reconstructed input, formation date or later development
that must be independently assessed before becoming a CAPR control.

Especially, do not equate late Old English eo or later Norse e with
ancestral lowering: Ringe--Taylor p. 35 n. 17 and Hill p. 152 n. 24
explicitly supply later alternatives. Do not select a fish stem or
move a vocative merger merely to preserve a desired phonological account.
Hock does not resolve the already recorded *twizna-* counterevidence to
CAPR's sibilant condition; no discharge of that problem is claimed.

## Bounded editorial decision

Add the bibliography entry and a reader discussion of Hock's actual
alternative, Hill's quantity objection, the adverb dating control and the
independence of phonemic status from conditioning. Preserve all earlier
literature discussion and the existing r/z/st rule, stage, scope,
chronology qualifications and unresolved controls.

No new executable comparison is needed or claimed for this source
integration. Reading Hock is not a new source-bound certificate for
SC015's law. Any subsequent comparison must first establish the relevant
historical inputs rather than fit them to transducer outputs.

## Separate future question

If SC015 is expressly reopened later, ask whether independently supported
paradigm histories can establish restoration of short i-timbre without
copying present-stem quantity, and whether dated adverb or fish formations
actually discriminate the vowel-conditioned and consonant-conditioned
accounts. This is a bounded future decision, not an investigation begun
here and not a replacement for the existing SC041/042 or SC067/080 queues.
