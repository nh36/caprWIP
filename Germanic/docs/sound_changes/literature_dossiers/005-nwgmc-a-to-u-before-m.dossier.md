# SC005 A-to-u before m — literature dossier

## Historical phenomenon

This dossier concerns the raising of unstressed noninitial `*a` to `*u` before final `*m` in inflectional environments, currently represented in the inventory by the `shoulder` family and by the dat.pl.-type ending evidence discussed in the handbooks.

## CAPR rule

- change_id: `SC005`
- display_name: `NWGmc A To U Before M`
- rule_name: `NWGmcAToUBeforeM`
- FOMA definition: `*a -> *u` before ending-final `*m` with optional `*i`/`*z`

## Example lexemes

1. `shoulder`

## Source support found so far

1. Campbell describes a development in inflectional endings by which `a > o > u before m` [@Campbell1959, §331(6)].
2. Sievers/Brunner treats old unstressed `o` before `m` in endings as yielding `u` in Old English [@SieversBrunner1965, §44].
3. Fulk lists the development of early Proto-Germanic unstressed `*o` to `u` before `m` among the important North/West-Germanic shared innovations [@Fulk2018, §5.2].

## Missing source support
