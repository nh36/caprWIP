## Historical analysis: deer

### First-order attestation, edition and dialect

Bosworth–Toller supplement entry 42712 cites “Of camele ðǽm deáre,
Mt. p.19,11.” The underlying item is **Kemble and Hardwick (1858)**,
*The Gospel according to Saint Matthew in Anglo-Saxon and Northumbrian
versions*, printed p.19, prefatory capitulum LXXIX, the interlinear gloss
to Latin *de camelo*. It is not Matthew 19:11. The inspected original
prints *of camele (ðæm deare)* without the dictionary's editorial vowel
accents. The Latin-plus-Northumbrian prefatory material is the Lindisfarne
version, Cotton Nero D.IV, not a diplomatic West Saxon *dēore* token.
The copy includes a later Skeat appendix; that does not redate this main text.
Original: <https://archive.org/details/gospelaccordingt00kembrich>.

The main dictionary entry is 7606, not the supplement:
<https://bosworthtoller.com/7606>. It supplies the independent genitive
plural in Ohthere's account, Orosius I.1 §15, **Bosworth (1859), p.20,
lines25–27**, *tamra deora unbebohtra syx hund*. The partitive after
the numeral establishes the case. This is the West Saxon Orosius tradition,
not an Anglian ending imported into the production target.
Bosworth's pp.lxi–lxii and his “Notes, Various Readings, and Corrections”
opening explain the Cotton Tiberius B.I base and bracketed Lauderdale
substitutions; deora is not bracketed. Printed p.20 (scan leaf98, PDF sheet99) was also inspected as an image, not only through OCR. His nineteenth-century judgement
of “pure” dialect is not evidence that the manuscript lacks all variation.
Original: <https://archive.org/details/kingalfredsangl00boswgoog>.

Fulk (2018), pp.148–149, §7.8, gives genitive plural *-ôⁿ.
Hogg–Fulk p.15 uses the equivalent long-plus-nasal-vowel notation
(*stainōõ). CAPR's existing single-codepoint ô serializes trimoric
quantity but not a separate nasal feature: **the proposed source form is
*déuzôⁿ, not a claim that the source reconstructs an oral ending**.
The exact proposed PROTOFORM is *déuzô, target dēora, headword dēor.
No combining-diacritic Foma symbol is introduced.

The complete compiled genitive trace is:

and SC112; rhotacism *déurô; ordinary diphthong development *dēorô;
the long final carrier survives quantitative high-vowel deletion and
unstressed fronting; unstressed long-vowel reduction gives *dēora.

Gothic diuzam preserves the internal sibilant; OS dior, OF diār and
OHG tior reflect their own histories, including possible direct-case
levelling. They do not prove that the northern *z which became final

and OHG wir/OE wē comparison shows why the German outcome cannot
settle the English one.

**Decision:** propose genitive plural for review, preserve dēor as the
lexical headword and keep *déuzą as an explicit direct-case diagnostic.
Do not silently migrate the selected row or refreeze its baseline.
The original and hypothetical migrated scores are reported separately.

## Historical analysis: SC112 before early SC047

Only unchanged SC112 moves in the first new paired comparison. SC017
already precedes the proposed pre-gemination loss on both sides.
Without that move, exposed *dor becomes *dōr; with it SC112 sees
*dorą, does not fire, and loss subsequently gives short dor.
There and where still give *þār/*hwār and then þǣr/hwǣr. Fulk (2018), pp.56–57, §4.3 explicitly compares short OE/OS dor and OHG tor from PIE *dhuron. This is the selected neuter a-stem direct cell, not the distinct feminine u-stem duru discussed by Ringe–Taylor p.385; the latter cannot silently replace this control.
The full census returns exactly those two applications, with no new
ones or lost ones; the displaced control has the additional door firing.

All forty SC017 firing identities survive every configuration, including
Rainbow's secondary-stress lowering. The early SC047 population is160:
the original159 plus straw, whose ending is no longer consumed first by
SC111. This is a changed census domain, not an unreported new lexical item.

The compiled SC112 relation leaves disyllables, plain/secondary-stress
vowels, already-long vowels, z-final controls and still-suffixed dorą
unchanged. Its rule body has no “inherited” feature: origin is enforced
only by the placement before rhotacism and the relevant apocope.

**Decision:** SC112 can precede early SC047 in this representation and
restores door without changing its conditioning. This is a supported
executable prerequisite of that candidate, not an independently proven
strict historical SC112→SC047 edge. Hill supplies a serious broader
phonological argument; the deictic-stress and here-analogy alternatives
remain viable. No production move is needed while SC047 stays live.

## Historical analysis: stranded glides and SC111

The source-backed early-loss account predicts *sarwą > *saru and
*rīkiją > *rīkī. Nuclearization realizes these intermediate predictions,
and the latter continues to rīċe. Surviving medial j in *sagjas still
geminates; its s-bearing genitive continues to seġġes, not a nominative.

Saru is **not an unexplained historical failure**. Campbell §581,
Hogg §5.32(4), and Hogg–Fulk §§2.28,2.31 explicitly extend the breaking
diphthong of searwes-type cells into searu. The compiled w-bearing
control reaches searwes; the isolated direct branch regularly restores
saru. The model has no paradigm-transfer operation. That missing
implementation must not be disguised as evidence against early loss.

The oral-ending route is explicitly staged after z loss, rather than
implemented by a new global z/r order. Oral loss plus nuclearization
gives *gelu > ġielu, *kalu > calu and *falu > falu; the w-bearing
controls give ġeolwes, ċealwes and fealwes. Ball–Stiles supplies
ġeolw- as the breaking donor for yellow; the modeled direct ġielu is
not the source's final levelled ġeolu. Fealu likewise needs the
independently attested paradigm relation, not artificial preservation
of w in a direct cell. Calu is a useful successful direct control,
but the experimental ċealwes is not silently certified as a complete
calu paradigm: root quality/palatalization and dialect need further
evidence. No lexical exception or unverified levelling is installed.

Three selected ja-stem endpoints change when the full glide consequence
is included. Hay2061 first loses ą before gemination, then *xawj
becomes *xawi, missing the oblique/geminated branch and its umlaut
trigger; later w-loss and high-vowel deletion give hēa, not hīeġ.
Net2138 becomes *nati before gemination, then nete, rather than the
geminated/heavy nett branch. Hue2332 becomes *xiwi; w-loss gives
*xii and surface hii, not hīew. The last is itself an incomplete
regular direct-cell derivation, not an attested historical hii.
Campbell §§576,579 and Ringe–Taylor pp.173,238,246,250 positively
identify the alternative/oblique stems and levelling. Conversely,
Hogg–Fulk §2.23 and Boutkan pp.209–213 provide phonological
gemination-first alternatives. Aggregate fit cannot choose between them.

The archived staged *h*á*w*j*ą diagnostic uses non-native h at that
entry point and is rejected in every variant. It is retained honestly.
The actual hay comparison uses the correctly serialized canonical
xáwją and its complete compiled prefixes, not that invalid alias.

Straw's obstacle is separable. The additional, unadopted FW relation
accepts already-endingless aw# at the existing SC111 site as well as
the original awą. After early loss, it gives *straw > *strau >
*strēa and restores the baseline endpoint; it does not derive attested
analogical strēaw or move SC111. Aw-as, consonant+w, awj and long-āw
negatives are unchanged. Thus SC111's old matcher is not an immutable
historical argument against early apocope.

**Decision:** retain the current production SC047 placement. The
after-SC041 alternative still has no selected endpoint changes, but
does not implement early stranded-glide consequences. The best
pre-gemination package makes genuine intermediate progress, yet
needs explicit paradigm history and retains the three ja-stem
divergences; neither the early-both-loss analysis nor its late-nasal
competitor may be selected merely by preserving today's citation cells.

## Runic scope and chronological bounds

Stiles's comparative relative ordering, Boutkan's reconstruction and
an inscription's date are separate evidence. Versloot directly contests
the inference that all WGmc ending reflexes disappeared at one early
common cutoff. His skanomodu, welad(u) and other examples involve
different degrees of uncertainty in reading, morphology, phonetic
interpretation and provenance; do not turn every -u into retained
literal *a or an unambiguous length measurement. Conversely, late
Frisian vestiges cannot be dismissed simply because an OE cascade
already lacks those endings.

The positive bounds retained here are source-conditional: root
lowering can precede loss; stranded-glide nuclearization follows
loss; the early-loss interpretation orders that loss before
j-gemination, while the Hogg–Fulk nasal-ending alternative does not.
Earlier than OE unstressed-fronting stages is distinct from a
universal pan-WGmc date. No newly established calendar terminus or
general Frisian→OE chronological transfer is claimed.
