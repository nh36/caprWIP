## 5. Strongest quotations for SC019

1. **Luick 1921:** "auch möglich, daß auslautendes ō schon vor der Verkürzung ... zu u vorgerückt war und daher die Verkürzung zu u führte."
2. **Ringe and Taylor 2014:** "It is clear that PGmc word-final bimoric non-nasalized long *-ō became short *-u in unstressed syllables in PNWGmc."
3. **Fulk 2018:** "PGmc. *-ō became *-u in North and WGmc., and this is preserved as such (or lowered to -o) in OE after light stems ..."

Together these quotations show that SC019 is not a merely internal CAPR convenience. What is more model-specific is the exact stage label and its tight immediate placement between SC017 and SC020.

## 6. How SC019 changes the interpretation of the SC016/SC017 pair

The earlier pair report already suspected that SC017's right-hand side mattered more than treating SC016/SC017 as a closed two-rule unit. The SC019 dossier confirms that suspicion.

Three conclusions now look solid:

1. **SC016 remains the weakest literature-defined member of the corridor.** Its phenomenon is real, but it is still usually described under West Saxon palatal-glide/rising-diphthong behavior rather than as a standard named sound law.
2. **SC017 remains the strongest member.** It is the corridor's most standard handbook change and the best anchor for eventual book prose.
3. **SC019 is historically real but formally packaged.** The literature strongly supports final unstressed `*-ō > *-u`, but mostly as an ending-development story rather than as a named chapter-level law. That makes SC019 more literature-backed than SC016 in one sense, but still more formalized in CAPR than SC017.

This shifts the corridor architecture. The natural explanatory center is no longer "SC016 and SC017 as a neat pair." It is now:

1. SC016 as the left-hand entrance (`yoke`);
2. SC017 as the central standard rule;
3. SC019 as the right-hand hinge that turns the corridor toward final-vowel and ending structure.

## 7. How should SC016-SC019 be treated in the book?

At this stage, the best answer is **option 4: a broader corridor that also requires SC020 before prose drafting**.

Why not the narrower options?

1. **Not three separate mini-chapters.** SC016 is still too thinly literature-labeled for that treatment, and SC019's strongest literature is ending-centered rather than chapter-centered.
2. **Not merely SC016/SC017 paired plus SC019 separate.** SC017's later boundary and SC019's later boundary immediately open into the same final-vowel corridor.
3. **Not yet a finished three-subsection early-corridor chapter.** SC019's right edge is already local and reciprocal with SC020 in CAPR, but SC020 still lacks a matching literature dossier.

So the responsible current recommendation is:

1. treat SC016-SC020 as one early vocalic / final-vocalic corridor under study;
2. do **not** draft book prose yet;
3. complete SC020's literature dossier before fixing the eventual chapter architecture.

## 8. Must SC020 receive a literature dossier before drafting the early corridor?

Yes.

The reason is now stronger than it was in the earlier SC016/SC017 pair report:

1. SC017's later boundary with SC019 is historically plausible and literature-compatible.
2. SC019 itself is not a weak phantom stage; it is a real historical development formalized by CAPR.
3. But SC019's own later boundary with SC020 on `rest` is now the unresolved right edge of the same corridor.

If prose were drafted now, the chapter would either stop too early or treat the `rest` boundary as a bare computational curiosity. A literature dossier for SC020 is therefore the minimum missing piece before corridor prose can be written responsibly.
