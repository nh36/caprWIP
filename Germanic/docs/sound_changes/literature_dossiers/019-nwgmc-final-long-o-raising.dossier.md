## Explanatory economy

Lowering first regularly gives the selected low roots of nose2143,
shovel2185 and sorrow2200, as well as Fulk's inherited shoal example
(2018, p.56). Earlier raising protects those nominative roots and needs
oblique-o generalization to obtain the observed low roots. The
possibility is historically credible: RT's early generalizations
(2014, pp.33–34) and Stiles's Runic analysis (2012, pp.47–48) establish
the mechanism, but not each lexical application.

Nose is already morphologically remodeled (Kroonen 2013, p.383).
Luick's inferred *scufel* beside *scofel* (1921, pp.105–106, §78)
supports alternation, not its later direction. Sorrow's comparative
formation (Kroonen 2013, p.493) is not independent evidence of early
English oblique-to-nominative levelling. These qualifications prevent
the comparison becoming a bare 379/377 score or a count of pristine
inherited forms.

Earlier raising has a genuine advantage for wool and love: with nasal
*ǭ* correctly recognized, it produces a high nominative and low
oblique. Nominative-u generalization could account for their attested
high-root paradigms (RT2014, p.32). This is plausible but unproved.
CAPR prefers the economy of regular low-root derivation across the
other selected forms, while retaining these real residues rather than
claiming RT has been disproved.

## Runic dating and precise remaining premise

Fulk's *horna* about400 and *waritu* about450 supply separate positive
bounds (2018, pp.56,82), not proof of strict pairwise order. Quantities,
nasality, inscription affiliation, onset and completion remain separate.
The smallest unresolved premise is whether the low root in the inherited
ō-stem nominatives, above all *scolu*, is regular inheritance or an
oblique-root generalization. An independently possible analogy does not
prove the latter; lack of a demonstrated analogy does not prove the former.
