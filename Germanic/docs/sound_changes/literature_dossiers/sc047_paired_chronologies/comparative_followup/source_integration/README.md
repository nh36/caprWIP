# SC047 source integration and matched trials — open for review

## Identity and question

SC047, `OEHeavySyllableNasalApocope`; research branch
`research/sc047-labial-discussion`, following `934c04d8`. The production
reference is PR #20 at `1468c4ac`; the research line descends through
`489f7d42`. The prior published verdict and registry are not reopened
by a machine-readable verdict line here. The research question remains
open: does the short ending of a light direct ja-stem remain a vowel
when SC010 operates? The decisive form would have a securely identified
inherited direct cell and independently interpretable root quantity and
ending; neither a normalized headword nor a possible donor suffices.

## Current state and literature

The live model keeps SC047 after SC010. Its registered proposal is
PWGmc, pan-WGmc, confidence B; the executable removes final short nasal
low vowel without a root-weight condition. The current 394 inputs (386
matches, eight inherited mismatches) include deer genitive plural;
auxiliary case forms and probes are not selected. See the [paired
comparison](../../README.md), [source and token ledger](../sources.md)
and [Hollifield assessment](../hollifield.md) for published predictions,
attestation status and source limits.

*Mete* is an independent i-stem, not an inherited nasal ja-stem control.
*Mettas* diagnoses a doubled byform, not which case created it. Erdmann's
genitives establish that some light-looking stems have `*-ij-` in the
relevant cell: a proposed donor must independently preserve adjacent
`Cj`, as the local SC010 contrast tests. Sievers' loans show that the
doubling itself does not depend on a morphological alternation. *Here*
cannot control the presence of a geminate, since *r* is independently
exempt. The oral *sagjaz* test is a contrast with the nasal accusative,
not an inference from the English written nominative alone.

**Additional unscored witnesses.** Fulk §§5.2 and 7.11, Sandell §9.2
and Ringe–Taylor pp. 46–47 give *cynn* a comparative light ja-stem
reconstruction: compare Goth. *kuni*, ON *kyn*, and OS/OHG *kunni*.
Fulk prints OE *cynnes, cynne* and the [dictionary](https://bosworthtoller.com/7096)
documents *cynne* in edited contexts; Stiles cites OS gen. *kunnias*.
These are genuine outputs of protected cells. Ringe–Taylor p. 129
interpret *cyne-* in *cynedōm* as the old direct stem with nuclearized
*i*. Unlike another written *cynn*, it is positive evidence for a
singleton allomorph, but the argument depends on the compound's analysis;
it is not an independent free direct-cell token. *Hrycg* and oblique
[*hrycge*](https://bosworthtoller.com/19863) are attested, but the
oral masculine direct singular discussed by Ringe–Taylor p. 47
cannot separate A from B. Existing *dile* has an independently defended
i-stem source; *hyse/hysses* is confounded by stem-class reanalysis.
No additional lexical selection is justified by these probes.

## Trial definitions and falsification

**FII — candidate Early Old English stressed *i.i* contraction (no SC
number assigned).** Short stressed *i* plus short unstressed *i* becomes
long stressed *ī*. The candidate has no lexeme or morpheme condition
and is not orthographic normalization. In the direct early-loss
derivation of hue, SC054 creates the eligible hiatus after i-mutation;
the rule cannot supply the mutation trigger or delete a transcribed
*w*. Compare Ringe–Taylor pp. 250, 257–261, Hogg §§5.131–134, 5.149
and Campbell §406. Test directly after SC054, after
`OEWeakTailNasalLoss` (before late syncope and high apocope), and after
existing `OEContraction`. Equality across slots is negative evidence
for tighter dating, not a new chronological edge. The positive probes
are hue, *glī* and combining *nī-*; protected *hīewes, glīewes, nīewes*,
*sǣ*, nonhomorganic *e.i* and local stress/quantity controls test the
restriction. The local strings test the transducer, not independent
attestations. Ringe–Taylor p. 259 also give *ǣ* ‘law’, an independent
comparative negative for the specifically high-vowel contraction after
*w*-loss: its first vowel is not short *i*, like the long-vowel route
to *sǣ*. The historical interval after *w*-loss and before loss of
an eligible second vowel is plausible; an independently established
quantity witness separating contraction from later loss remains needed.
The tested rule scans for an adjacent sequence in the same word; its
historical evidence is limited to word-final hiatus and the comparable
compound element. Generalization to eligible nonfinal root/ending
hiatus is not independently established.

**FWA — targeted restriction of the SC034 proxy.** Campbell §§406,
579(4), Ringe–Taylor pp. 246, 250, 257–261 and Fulk §§4.10, 4.15
motivate keeping singleton `*aw` before the high-front vowel apart
from inherited *au* and from resolution of geminated `*awwj`.
The early direct cell has `*xáwi`; SC034 formerly supplies a long
diphthong here (`*xḗawi`), giving *hēa*. FWA tests blocking that
application, allowing the short brightened `*xæwi`, mutation to
`*çewi` and w-loss to `*çei`. Nothing in the existing late rules
contracts that sequence to an attested inherited direct form; the
proxy's *hei* is not evidence of such an attestation. The protected
stem is a separate derivation, and the selected *hīeġ* is not changed.
Ringe–Taylor's West Saxon *hīeġ* and Mercian/Northumbrian *hēg*
are given on the long-diphthong route, not as their short `*hæwi`
intermediate surfacing directly. The independently indexed compound
*hégsíþe* (Bosworth–Toller Supplement s.v. *híg-síþe*, *Gr. D.*
37.14) does not settle inherited vowel quantity in the free direct
cell. In OHG, the Notker *hewi*-type genitive and both direct types
likewise prevent treating the spelling classes as a preserved
complementary paradigm.
The staged controls include ‘flock’, preterites of ‘strew’ and ‘call’,
and non-i ‘show’; corpus controls include ‘strew’, ‘believe’ and ‘straw’.
Do not project this restriction to all inherited `*au` or adopt it
as a freestanding historical law from a currently inactive environment.

The matched A/B/C arms use FII at the feeding checkpoint and FWA in
SC034's position. The extra B/C geminate-only arms preserve only
stranded `wwj` until its existing consumers; the broader delayed
vocalization is not evidence that singleton `wj` has geminated. The
previous labial-block crossing changed ‘believe’ by pre-empting
prefix reduction and stranded ‘straw’ downstream of the consumers of
SC111. It is not adopted. Following C's northern order additionally
moves ordinary final *z* loss to keep it before removal of the newly
exposed oral ending; this is an explicit executable dependency, not a
new claim about the absolute date of final *z*.

## Diagnosis and research verdict

The [executed assay](provenance.json) ran 13 arms against the same 394
inputs and targets. Its identity reproduces all 386 matches and the
same eight mismatches. No arm rejected or returned multiple analyses
for any scored row. Counts describe consequences, not votes.

|---|---:|---|
| A | 381 | hay *hei*, hedge *heġe*, net *nete*, rest *rast*, hue *hī* |
| B | 382 | hay *hewe*, hedge *heġe*, rest *rast*, hue *hiewe* |
| C (northern proxy) | 383 | hay *hewe*, rest *rast*, hue *hiewe* |

| C, geminate-only protection | 385 | rest *rast* |

With no FII/FWA, A gives *hēa* and *hii*; B and C are unaffected by
the combined additions on the scored corpus. Contraction at all three
slots gives *hī, glī, nī*, preserves the protected and *sǣ* controls,
and produces no additional scored delta. The local Erdmann contrast
leaves each `*nij` unchanged and doubles only adjacent `*nj`;
these are staged phonological probes, not reconstructions of
the entire Old English forms. The *aw* staged controls change
*ēaide → eide*, *strēade → streide*, *ġeacēaide → ġeacide* and
leave non-i *sċēawien* unchanged. The *eide/streide/ġeacide*
surfaces are not certified historical outputs: the historical
analyses of the preterites and the dialectal contraction of
‘flock’ require independent checking rather than repair by analogy.

All 40 SC017 firing rows, both SC112 firings, and SC111's
`*stráu` were preserved. SC048 and SC059 remain in their existing
relative positions and use the same definitions. In the matched trials
‘believe’ still gives *ġelīefan*, ‘strew’ *strīeġan*, and ‘straw’
*strēa*. The [output table](outputs.tsv) records each selected
endpoint; [changed stages](changed-stages.tsv) records the shared
checkpoints for every changed endpoint, and the compressed capture
preserves the complete named witness traces, unscored checkpoints,
and whole-corpus before/after observations for the censused stages.
The opt-in run (not an addition to routine regression execution)
compiled 1,950 component queries in about 366 seconds, with a peak
compiler RSS of about 2.14 GiB. The receipt flags unrelated worktree
edits during capture; the pinned FST, corpus and runtime helpers are
verified against the archived identity and repeated reference arms.

The following causal divergences are distinct from the first differently

gemination effect of lowering.

The *cynn* probe isolates a second question after gemination. A gives
*cyne*; B and C do produce `*kúnnj` and reach `*kynn` after high
apocope, but CAPR's downstream `OEFinalGeminateSimplification` gives
surface *cyn*. The spelling *cynn* does not prove a phonetic final
geminate at that later date. Both histories agree on *cynnes/cynne*;
this is not evidence for changing the simplifier in an SC047 trial.

**Recommendation: retain production and keep the chronology open.**
A has the strongest **qualified comparative** case: *beti/peti* and
the *hewi* type agree with independent reasons to consider an old
singleton direct stem, and *mene* and *cyne-* are compatible but
morphologically less secure. B has the strongest direct regular account
of the English nasal neuters. C uses fewer stem transfers for the
northern diagnostic set alone but has not established its independently
needed southern post-gemination continuation or the direction of
transfer. Across the evidence there is no secure unique minimum of
unmotivated analogy: A's donor stem is regular and attested in several
paradigms, B requires an account of the continental singleton relics,
and C requires the additional regional phonology. Hollifield weakens a
literal appeal to survival of inherited nasality, but supplies no
chronology that separates A from C.

**Propagation: none.** FII is ready for comparative review as a
discrete candidate, not production adoption. FWA identifies a specific
overapplication of a technical proxy under the early hypothesis; it
does not independently justify changing production or supply a new
historical rule. No source-supported later change justifies forcing
*hei* into selected *hīeġ*. No change to the registry, reader chapter,
selected cells, historical edges, production cascade or frozen
scientific fingerprint is proposed.

**Residue.** The priorities are Dal's original argument from *beti*,
Dahl's directly documented case alternations, a secure simplex
singleton-plus-vowel nasal-origin cell in early OE independent of
compounds or stem-class transfer, and evidence for the chronology of
OHG singleton quantity against shortening/analogy. A quantity-sensitive
*i.i* witness that distinguishes true fusion from loss following
lengthening is more useful than another normalized *ī* spelling.
