## What the rereading establishes

- **Ringe–Taylor, pp. 44–48, 53, 173, 246–250, 258–261.** In the direct
  light cells, loss of the thematic vowel creates terminal *Cj*, and
  nuclearization produces *Ci*; this explains the ending, not just the
  ungeminated consonant. The corresponding obliques retain a vowel
  after *j*. Their *mete/mettas* argument uses partial reanalysis of an
  inherited i-stem as a ja-stem, not transfer from one inherited
  nasal direct cell to another. Their hay comparison assigns short
  `*hæwi` and the long diphthongal stem to different continuations.
- **Hogg–Fulk, *Morphology* pp. 19–25 §§2.20–2.33.** §2.23 explicitly
  prints pre-OE nominative `*sagi` and accusative `*saggi`, with
  neuter `*kunni` in both direct cases, but also recognizes the early
  alternative. The positive morphological parallel is ordinary
  a-stem nominative–accusative syncretism; the doubled accusative's
  antiquity in *seċġ* is the hypothesis, not a fact of the written
  paradigm. §2.24 n. 3 separately leaves the neuter **plural**
  `*kunnju > cynn` unresolved. §§2.30–2.33 have evidence for
  redistribution of a stem alternant, but *searu–searwes* involves
  vocalization and restoration, not the phonology of *nj*.
- **Campbell, §§120.2, 406–407, 576–582.** The direct `*sazi`
  and expected *cyne* explicitly require loss of both low endings
  before gemination; vowel-protected *secge* is phonological. §579(4)
  assigns hay and hue remodelled stem outcomes and distinguishes this
  from later restoration of final *w* from inflected forms. §406
  separately derives *sǣ* by w-loss followed by deletion of *i* after
  a **previously long** vowel. This is a credible mechanism for
  deleting a second vowel, not proof of prior lengthening of short *i*.
- **Boutkan, pp. 209–213, 435–436.** Distinguish common gemination
  before low loss, northern loss/simplification after gemination,
  and southern `-CC > -Ci`, after which the doubled stem can spread
  back to the direct cells. He also considers an alternative with
  high-vowel ending transfer from the heavy stems followed by
  restoration of the geminate; this is not the same phonology.
  His *mene* explanation instead transfers an i-stem ending;
  a gender/class switch has some positive support, but does not
  show that the lexical preform actually had *nn*. His reason for
  connecting low and high loss is the heavy *endi* type. Fulk
  §7.11 n. 5 counters with the independent late chronology of
  high-vowel deletion relative to i-mutation and with alternative
  heavy-stem histories. The northern CAPR proxy does not implement
  the southern proposal; fitting *beti* by writing an ad hoc
  `CC > Ci` rule would not test that proposal historically.
- **Erdmann, original PDF pp. 409–410; Sievers, original pp. 262–265.**
  Erdmann's *Deni(g)a* and *wini(g)(e)a* show that an oblique
  on a light root can have protected `*-ij-`, not adjacent `*-j-`.
  This defeats the claim that a geminating genitive plural is the
  automatic or universal i-stem donor for *mettas*, not the
  possibility of idiosyncratic adjacent *j* if independently attested.
  Sievers' loan *filiolus → filleol* and *lineola → linnol*
  support phonological gemination without inherited paradigm
  alternants. They do not give the date at which *-ą* was lost.

## Denasalization is not a deletion date

Hollifield I pp. 32–33 models early loss of inherited final *n* as
**creation** of a nasal vowel, distinct from the oral vowel apocope of
that section. II p. 169 proposes that inherited final nasality was
probably lost in pre-North/West Germanic; pp. 169–172 distinguish
renewed nasalization in `*-Vnz` from that earlier oralization.
He discusses the convergence of the short `*-az` and `*-aⁿ` sources
under bare *a* on p. 168. In contrast, p. 170 n. 86 and p. 171 steps
8–9 order **post-unstressed-syllable** syncope and final *z* loss:
the inscriptions do not date deletion after the single strong syllable
in *nett*. The analogical transfer of nasality in feminine plural
endings (I pp. 43–45; II p. 154, Table V discussion) is another
innovation again. It does not maintain an inherited short ending
until SC010 and is not CAPR's SC048 proxy.

For the matched light West Germanic cells, if the denasalized
reflex of `*-ją` is plain short `*-ja` **before** final *z* loss
and ordinary oral apocope, both source endings now have the same
phonological environment. They must behave alike by the time they
meet that deletion. If they both survive SC010, this is the
gemination-first prediction with oralization made explicit, not an
independent split. To strand oral *j* but make a **denasalized**
nasal-origin *a* survive, one would need evidence either for
denasalization *between* oral deletion and gemination, or for another
retained phonetic/prosodic distinction at the deletion. The former
does not follow from Hollifield's stated sequence; no evidence for
the latter was found for these paired cells. Later local apocope of
a previously oralized vowel is plausible; differential retention
of two now-identical short vowels is not a warranted fourth
executable arm. Original final nasal loss, remaining-vowel deletion,
glide vocalization and SC010 remain different hypotheses, not four
names for the same step.

## Edition loci behind the German forms

The [AWb *betti* article](https://awb.saw-leipzig.de/?sigle=AWB&lemid=B00752)
locates accusative *peti* at Steinmeyer–Sievers II 221.60, a ninth-century
Munich gloss (Clm 18550,1) on Gregory's *Regula pastoralis* II.5,
Latin *stratum*. Nominative *beti* IV 228.15 glosses *area*, a different
sense; *bedi* in the Fulda witness to Paulus Diaconus V.2 glosses
*culcitram*. Do not count different ‹p/b/d› conventions, gloss copies,
or every sense as independent chronologies. Edited Tatian *betti*
[25.2](https://titus.uni-frankfurt.de/texte/etcs/germ/ahd/tatian/tatia026.htm)
and [54.7](https://titus.uni-frankfurt.de/texte/etcs/germ/ahd/tatian/tatia055.htm)
and dative *bette* 54.2 and
[147.4](https://titus.uni-frankfurt.de/texte/etcs/germ/ahd/tatian/tatia148.htm)
give a doubled paradigm in
one East Franconian tradition. The AWb also gives undoubled dative
*pete* in Notker glosses at 40.4 next to doubled spellings; the
secondary spelling contrast is not an exceptionless case distribution.
The Tatian spellings were also checked in the edition; the glosses
were checked in the dictionary, not newly collated from Clm 18550,1
or the Fulda witness.

The [AWb *heuui, houuui* article](https://awb.saw-leipzig.de/?sigle=AWB&lemid=H02092)
indexes *heuui* against *fenum* (III 16.34). The Notker Psalter
tradition has nom. *heuue* 91.8, nom. *houue/houui* 36.2 and acc.
*houue* 146.8, **also** acc. *heuue* 103.14, gen. *heuues* 105.20
and dat. *heuue* 101.12. These do not form a clean direct–oblique
partition. At 146.8 manuscript branches even differ (*houue/heu*).
The article separately indexes Old Saxon genitive *hogias* glossing
Prudentius' *feni* (II 582.50 = Wadstein 96.15, Düsseldorf F. 1,
ninth/tenth century with uncertainty). That is an independent
protected hay stem, not a direct Old English donor or a witness
for which quantity came first.
