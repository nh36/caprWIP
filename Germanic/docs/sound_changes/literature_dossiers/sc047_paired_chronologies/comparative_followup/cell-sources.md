# Inherited cells and Old English witnesses

This audit concerns the comparanda, not the selected corpus. A dictionary headword, an editorially normalized form, a token with syntactic case, and a reconstructed donor are different kinds of evidence. Editions are cited below where the underlying hand has not been verified.

## Hedge: two formations

Orel (2003:150, s.v. *xaǥjaz*) adduces masculine OE *hege* and ON *heggr* ‘bird cherry’, alongside West Germanic hedge words (East Frisian, Middle Dutch, OHG). Kroonen (2013:198, s.v. *haga(n)-*) assigns ON *heggr* to masculine *hagja-* but groups OE *hecg* with feminine OHG *heckia/heggia* under *hagjō-*. Kroonen treats OE *hec* and Dutch *hek* separately, under a *hakkja/ō-* formation. The two relevant stems can thus be derivatives within the same enclosure/thorn family without one having been remodelled from the other in Old English. The evidence supports separate inherited formations, more strongly than it fixes their precise common-Germanic date of derivation.

[Bosworth–Toller, *hege*](https://bosworthtoller.com/18599), and its Supplement (p. 528, s.vv. *hege*, *hecg*) distinguish masculine *hege* in *of þām hege* (Kemble, *Codex Diplomaticus* III 78.21), *tō þǣm mǣrhege* and *þæs mǣres heges* (III 32.30), and feminine *hegge* in *æt þǣre lange hegge ænde* (III 385.7) and *hegce* in *in þā hegce* (III 52.25). These are charter-edition readings; the first two masculine examples are dative, the third genitive; feminine *in þā hegce* is accusative. The Supplement reassigns the doubled spelling in the Bamborough annal (547) from masculine *hege* to feminine *hecg*. That spelling by itself does not establish gender. Masculine *hege* inflects in the masculine strong series (compare plural *hegas*); feminine *hecg* has strong oblique *-e*, not weak *-an*. Compare the separate weak *hecge, -an* entry. The charter forms establish the distinction more securely than the annal, regardless of later overlap.

CAPR row 2069 pairs a masculine *ja*-stem nominative with the normalized feminine target *heġġ* (standard *heċġ*). For feminine *jō*-stem nominative and accusative compare the *-(i)jō : -(i)jǭ* contrast in Ringe (2017:225, §4.3.4). Neither long ending is the short *-ą* on trial.

## Rest: which oblique?

Kroonen (2013:405, s.v. *rastō-*; PDF p. 445) gives a feminine stem, comparing OE *ræst*, Gothic *rasta*, ON *rǫst*, OS and OHG *rasta*. Ringe (2017:225, §4.3.4, *gebō* paradigm) distinguishes nominative *-ō*, accusative *-ǭ*, genitive *-ōz* and dative *-ōi* (segmentation/reconstruction marked uncertain there). Ringe–Taylor (2014:61) derive *gebōi* > PWGmc *gebē*; their account of OE unstressed vowels distinguishes dative *-e* via *-ē* from accusative and genitive *-e* via PWGmc *-ā* (2014:299–300, §6.8.3). The common OE ending is not proof of a common PGmc ending.

[Bosworth–Toller, *ræst*](https://bosworthtoller.com/25735) gives nominative *sēo ræst* and dative *tō ræste* in the same prose *Boethius* passage (ch. 25, Fox 88.29–32), dative *tō his ræste* (24.4, Fox 84), and accusative *þā ēcean ræste* as the object of *geearnige* (*Blickling Homilies*, Morris 101.26; Princeton, Scheide Library MS 71). Locative *on ræste* is also in *Beowulf* 1298 and 1585 (Cotton Vitellius A.xv). A genitive is not impossible: *him ræste nēod*, *Guthlac B* 1068 (Exeter Book, Cathedral Library MS 3501; Thorpe 167.31), is a genitive-of-need candidate, as is *reste* dependent on *stōwe* in *Genesis* 1466 (Junius 11; Thorpe 88.17). Neither is the rationale offered by the selected row's prepositional citations. The dative is syntactically secure; the accusative also has independent attestation and tests a different inherited long ending.

## Hay and hue: direct and protected witnesses

For hay, Kroonen (2013:215, s.v. *hauja-*) compares Gothic *hawi*, ON *hey*, OE *hieg*, OFris/OS and OHG *hewi/houwi*. Campbell (1959:231, §579(4)) reconstructs unlevelled *hewi* as the direct preform; Ringe–Taylor (2014:53, 246) distinguish the short inherited direct route (including *hæwi*) from the long-diphthong English forms. [Bosworth–Toller Supplement, *hīg*](https://bosworthtoller.com/52468), gives *heg/hig* in Ps. 36.2, *heig* as object of mowing in *Gregory’s Dialogues* 36.2–29 (with *gærs* in the apparatus), and *mid hig* in the *Colloquy* (BT reference Coll. M. 20.1), whose preposition identifies an oblique use but whose noun lacks an overt ending. Neither *hīeġes* nor an independently parsed *hīeġ* plural was verified. The English evidence demonstrates the surviving stem, not the particular English donor cell.

The [OHG witness register](sources.md) (AWb IV, col. 1043) has nom.sg. *heuui* (Gl. III 16.34); Notker Psalter nom. *heuue* 91.8 and *houue* 36.2 (variant *houui*), acc. *houue* 146.8, gen. *heuues* 105.20. These are distinct witnesses/cases, not a synchronically complementary *hewi* direct versus *houwi* oblique paradigm. The distribution permits spread in either direction; it does not prove a direction for English.

For hue, Orel (2003:171–172, *xewjan*) and Kroonen (2013:224, *heuja-*) have e-vocalism, but give different derivational accounts (Orel compares *xawwanan*; Kroonen discusses Dybo shortening). Ringe–Taylor (2014:53, 250) use *hiwją* in the discussion of labial gemination. The assay's *xíwją* is the existing representation of the i-vocalism candidate, not a neutral representation of all three etymologies.

[Bosworth–Toller Supplement, *hīw*](https://bosworthtoller.com/52596), cites *hwītes hīwes* (variant *hiéwes*) from **Pastoral Care**, not Fox’s *Boethius*: Sweet (1871:86–87, l. 20) prints genitive *hwites hiwes* in Hatton 20 and *hwites hiewes* opposite in the Cotton text (Tiberius B.xi tradition). The flax is ‘of a white colour’; both forms occupy the same genitive singular. The same entry cites the direct *þæt æðeleste hiew* (Sweet 133.11; variant *hiw*, l. 23), Fox's *Boethius* 41.6 (254.24) *ungelīces hīwes*, and *hiowes* (Cockayne, *Narratiunculae* 15.32; hand not checked). The genitive provides an observed *hiew-* donor, not just a possible model output, but the Cotton/Hatton variation is not a chronological succession. The predicted identical direct plural is not needed as evidence and has not been independently identified here.

## Independent comparisons

**Searu.** In Campbell (1959:231–232, §§581–583), Hogg (1992, *Phonology*, §5.32(4)) and Hogg–Fulk (2011:22–26, §§2.27–2.33), deletion exposes *w* after *r* in the direct singular; syllabic *u* no longer provides the consonantal *rw* needed for breaking. The oblique *sarw-* keeps *rw*, so breaking gives *searw-*. Extension of its vowel to the regular direct *saru* explains *searu* without copying the whole ending. The case distinction is observed: [BT *searu*](https://bosworthtoller.com/27264), compare *his searwes áttre*, *Guthlac* 44.13 (BT edition reference, genitive before ‘poison’), and *þurh īdel searu*, Paris Psalter 138.17 (accusative after *þurh*). The Corpus Glossary *sarwo* illustrates a different transfer (restoration of consonantal *w*), not the copying of *ea*. Early *teru/smeru* in the glosses support the unbroken type. No English *saru* direct singular is asserted for this lexeme.

**Glee.** Ringe–Taylor (2014:53) contrast *gliwją* and *gliwjas*, with reconstructed singleton *gliwi* versus protected *[gliwwias]* and normalized *glīg : glīowes*. Campbell (1959:231, §579(4)) treats *glig* as re-formed, not an unmodified reflex of *gliwi*, and proposes transfer of *w* from inflected forms to explain many of the direct *gliow/hiow* forms. The early glosses are not a decisive direct witness: Pheifer (1974, §42 n. 2, no. 398) finds the spelling *gliu* in Épinal and Erfurt ambiguous (cf. Corpus *glio*, no. 825, and *gliowe*, no. 1112). [BT *glig*](https://bosworthtoller.com/17239) gives dative *mid þæm glige*, Sweet 183.25 (Hatton 20, fol. 35v; Cotton *gligge*), and *tō glige*, *Basil*, Norman 54.20. [BT *gliw*, *gleow*](https://bosworthtoller.com/17225) give genitive *his gliwes giefe*, *Maxims I* 172 (Exeter Book fol. 91v), and nominative *gleo*, *Beowulf* 2105 (Cotton Vitellius A.xv). Paris Psalter 67.24 *gleowe* glosses the instrumental association with musicians and is not a genitive. The witnesses establish a neuter with multiple stem shapes, including *g* in an oblique; the exact *glīowes* and a secure zero-ended *glīġ* token were not located in the checked editions. The normalized textbook pairing must not be presented as one witnessed and subsequently levelled paradigm, nor must all spellings be assigned to one dialect.

**Calibration.** *Mete : mettas* is directly witnessed within one Mercian gloss (Vespasian Psalter, Hymn 6.37), but concerns an i-stem and number-conditioned redistribution, not an inherited *-ją* singular. *Hyse : hysses* and plural *hyssas/hysas* show redistribution compatible with several histories, but the lost *Maldon* witness and uncertain inherited class prevent reconstruction of the direction from those forms alone. *Searu* provides the best independent account of a phonologically created alternant being extended; glee supplies a close labial comparison with less secure cell alignment. Neither establishes the direction of transfer in *nett*.
