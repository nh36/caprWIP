# SC047 comparative source appendix

Printed pages are used below. A starred form is a reconstruction, not an
edited reading. A cell or spelling cited by an author is not represented as
independently verified against manuscript images.

| Account and direct holding | Ordering, reconstructed environments, and regular outcomes | Levelling and discriminating weight |
|---|---|---|
| Ringe and Taylor (2014), pp. 44–47, 53, 173, 246, 250, 257–261 | Their economical ordering puts unstressed final `z` loss ahead of oral `-a` and nasal `-ą` loss. Both low vowels disappear before gemination; stranded light `-Cj` gives `-Ci`, heavy `-Cij` gives long `-ī`, and eligible obliques in `-CjV-` geminate. *Beti*, *mete* beside *mettas*, and the direct hay and hue intermediates motivate distinct continuations. | Geminated *bedd*, *cynn*, *hrycg*, and the remodelled labial stems take the stem found in vowel-protected cells, sometimes suppressing direct-cell `-i`. *Mete* is an inherited i-stem reanalysed in part, not a second inherited neuter ja-stem test. OHG *beti* is stronger evidence for the early analysis, conditional on rejecting secondary loss of a geminate. |
| Hogg and Fulk (2011), *Morphology*, pp. 19–22 §§2.20–2.24 | Oral `*-az` of masculine direct nominatives gives stranded `*-j` before gemination, hence `*sagjaz > *sagi`; nasal `*-ą` of the masculine accusative and both neuter direct singulars survives through it: `*sagją > *saggją > *saggi`; regular neuter *cynn*, and by the same phonology *nett/bedd*. Heavy `*-ij` and light `*-rj` need separate treatment. | Masculine nom. *seċġ* uses the inherited geminate of the accusative, consistent with direct-case syncretism in a-stems. The authors explicitly entertain Campbell’s alternative at pp. 20–21. Their split reduces the number of transfers in the English neuters, but does not itself account for the OHG singleton relics. |
| Campbell (1959), §120.2 p. 46, §406 pp. 166–167, §§576–582 pp. 229–232 | Both direct endings lost before doubling. `*sagjaz` and `*sagjam` give `*sagi`; protected dative `*-jai` and other surviving endings keep `j`. §579(4) contrasts expected ungeminated *hewi*, *gliwi* with labial diphthongs from doubled stems. §406 treats w-loss before unstressed i, with subsequent loss of i after a long vowel in e.g. *sǣ*. | *Seċġ*, *cynn*, hay and hue-type outcomes take protected stems. He additionally distinguishes extension of final w from obliques in diphthongal forms where final w would otherwise fall; this is not simply transfer before gemination. Neither modelled donor availability nor the final spelling proves the transfer. |
| Boutkan (1995), pp. 209–213, 419–420, 435–436 | Gemination first, then oral and nasal low-vowel loss. In his northern *segg* type, `-Cj > -Ci` follows low loss, and final geminates are subject to later reduction. His synopsis distinguishes northern outcomes from a southern type in which final `-CC` has a singleton-plus-i continuation. Also distinguishes `*ji > i` before gemination in weak verbs. | In the northern account *secg* does not need a donor. For *mene* he proposes degemination plus transfer of i-stem `-i`; masculinity in English is positive evidence of reclassification, not proof of when the simplex arose. In the southern account *beti* can follow a doubled precursor; *betti* is the one with reintroduced doubling. His alternatives for *mettas* include partial ja-stem transfer and a geminating i-stem genitive plural. His *auja* evidence is explicitly vulnerable to formulaic survival. |
| Fulk (2018), §§4.10 (pp. 71–72), 5.2 (pp. 79–81), 6.15–16 (pp. 126–130), 7.11 (pp. 151–153 and nn. 1–5) | §5.2 gives nuclearization after post-PGmc low-vowel loss (*kuni*, heavy *rīce*, *gearu*) and says geminate extension obscures it in West Germanic. Expects masculine `*sege` below *secg*, cites *mene*, *dili*, *beti*, *hewi* and inflectional alternations; distinguishes Dal’s reformed accusative from Dahl/Hogg’s inherited one rather than deciding between them in §7.11. Critiques conflation of low and high apocope in the Boutkan analysis. In §4.10 n. 1 inherited `*au` for *strew* is an alternative to consonantal `ww` followed by reversal. | Nominative *secg* gets a stem from other cells; later Saxon *seggi, beddi* can have ending transfer from heavy stems. The *strew* dispute concerns the source of a diphthong with protected j, not whether nasal loss strands that j. The normalized Anglian reflex does not distinguish the two `au` routes. |
| Stiles (2013), pp. 15–16, (3) A4–7 | A relative-chronology argument: polysyllabic final z loss, loss of final low vowel including from PGmc `*-an`, then `j`-gemination. Relic OE *mene*, OS *(hals-)meni* from `*manja` via `*mani`; compare protected OS gen. *kunnias* and Gothic *kunjis*. | OHG *menni* levelled, in this account. This is comparative evidence for early nasal loss because the proposed necklace input is neuter; if the singleton and vowel instead came from the independently suspected class transfer, the witness is no longer decisive. The OS compound also need not represent free-form phonology. |
| Versloot (2019), pp. 78–92, esp. pp. 79, 86–87 | Continental *kaba* has no written final z but retains a vowel. Runic Frisian forms, including *kabu*, are interpreted using late reduction/survival of low endings, not necessarily a literal phonetic [u]. Regional vowel balance is part of the analysis. | Does not reconstruct English *nett* or date its gemination. Morphology, rune interpretation and geography constrain any use of these forms: evidence for staggered regional loss is not evidence for an English split by nasality. |
| Hogg (1992; 2011 reissue), *Phonology*, §§2.78, 3.16–18, 4.10–14, 7.80–81 | Distinguishes geminate-derived diphthongization from hiatus resolution; §3.18 gives `*iwwj` and `*awwj` routes with differential loss of the two glides, with high-route j-loss at least after umlaut. Light pre-j doubling excludes r; weak-verb alternations independently establish the conditioning. §2.78 records both *bedd* and *bed* in very early glossaries. | Not evidence that a surviving word-final double spelling identifies the inherited direct stem. The protected labial history cannot be reduced to deletion of spelling `j`. Hogg’s 1979 pp. 68–73 case analysis is separately cited by Fulk; his 1992 phonology and the 2011 morphology volume are not the same source. |
| Luick, *Historische Grammatik*, §350 pp. 320–321 (held scan) | Relative separation of low and high apocope; the synopsis places loss of final `a` and `a` before `z` roughly in the second/third centuries and loss of Old English `i/u` at the start of the seventh. Luick explicitly qualifies the chronology of unstressed vowels. | The absolute dates are not adopted, and this does not by itself choose early nasal loss. Sievers (1892), pp. 262–265 is identified but not directly consulted here; Boutkan’s quote via Dal, p. 211, is only indirect evidence that compound boundaries can have earlier loss. |
| Ryan Sandell, *Foot Structure in Indo-European and Sievers’ Law*, §9.2 pp. 8–9, §10 pp. 9–10 | Teaching handout: loss of both low vowels after final z loss and before gemination. Protected OE *kynnes*, *fæstennes*, OS *kunnie*, *beddie*, OHG *kunn(i)e*. Contrasts heavy OE *wītes*, *æðeles* and OS *mākie*, *æðalie*; uses weak-verb alternations to motivate pre-gemination `*ji > i`. | Geminate stems levelled into direct *bedd*, *betti*. His printed `*[bedjaz]` next to *beti* is not a substitute for the independently reconstructed neuter direct `*-ją`; *heri* shows retained -i but r was exempt from gemination anyway. The handout clarifies predictions, not independent new attestations. Its heavy verbal `*-iji- > *ī` is not the stressed-root hiatus created by w-loss. |

## Lexical and attestation controls

| Group | Cell and witness | What it can establish |
|---|---|---|
| net | PGmc `*nátją` direct vs. `*nátjai`, `*nátjas` and `*nátjō`. The prior [paired dossier](../README.md) establishes an edited dative *nette* independently of the predicted *nettes*. | Neither a normalized direct *nett* nor a donor alone proves inherited direct quantity. The dative documents the protected stem; direct early *nete* is predicted, not an attested simplex of this word. |
| bed | PGmc `*bádją` direct vs. `*bádjas` and `*bádjai*. Hogg §2.78 cites Épinal/Erfurt gloss numbering 243 *bedd* and 971 *bed*. Sandell §9.2 supplies OS dative *beddie*; Fulk §7.11 distinguishes the poetic direct *bed* from later *beddi*. [DWEE s.v. *Bett*](https://dwee.saw-leipzig.de/etymology/Bett_1/de) attests the OHG doubled type *betti, petti* as a neuter ja-stem in glosses from the eighth century. | Edited glossary spellings show the variation the chronology must explain, not two independently preserved proto-cells. OHG *beti/peti* beside *betti* (separate evidence from Boutkan's citations of Braune–Eggers and Ringe and Taylor's of Dal) is a stronger direct-cell test but permits Boutkan’s southern alternative. The edited *beddes/bedde* below attest the protected stem without dating its extension. |
| food | PGmc i-stem `*mátiz`; *mettas* is not the inherited plural of a known PGmc light ja-stem. [Bosworth–Toller s.v. *mete*](https://bosworthtoller.com/22712) cites *mete* in John 4:34 and *mett* in Lindisfarne and Rushworth there, and plural *mettum* in Wright–Wülcker II 6.25. Ringe–Taylor p. 47 and Boutkan p. 211 report *mettas*. | The edition-based variant *mett* must not be suppressed. Protected j-bearing forms test a proposed source of the doubled stem, not an independently certified proto-form for *mettas*. |
| necklace | Proposed neuter `*mánją`, alongside protected `*mánjas`. Stiles cites OE *mene*, OS *(hals-)meni*, OHG *menni*; [EWA s.v. *hals*](https://ewa.saw-leipzig.de/articles/hals/en) verifies the OS compound. [Bosworth–Toller s.v. *heals-mene*](https://bosworthtoller.com/18448) cites *healsmyne*; it is not the simplex. | Inherited plain `n` plus final vowel would favour early nasal loss, but OE masculine class/gender and the OS compound weaken independence. A secure simplex attestation in a morphologically diagnostic context is still needed. |
| man/warrior | `*ságjaz` oral nom., `*ságją` nasal acc., `*ságjas` protected gen. Fulk §7.11 prints direct *secg* and inflected *secges*, *secge*, *secgas*, etc. | A and B predict a regular singleton in the oral cell, C a geminated stem; only B and C derive a direct nasal geminate. Syncretism makes the written nominative weak evidence: both a same-paradigm accusative and other cells can supply a donor. |
| dill / youth | Fulk §7.11 n. 5 cites Corpus Glossary *dili*, Early West Saxon acc. *dile*, OS *dilli*, and gen. *hysses* beside *hyse*. Boutkan pp. 210–211 notes *hyssas* beside *hysas*. | Useful targeted source leads, not admissions: independently establish *dill*'s gender and source cell, and distinguish i-stem gemination from inherited ja-stem gemination before using *hyse*. |

EWA I cols. 571–574, s.v. *betti*, independently lists single `-t-`
among the OHG variants. For the later German *Beet/Bett* contrast the
entry distinguishes direct `*badi` from oblique `*badj-` with doubling.
This strengthens the comparative alternation, while still not giving
the cell, locality, and writing conventions of every early token.

Pheifer (1974; 1998 reprint), pp. 14 and 50, confirms edited *bedd*
243 (Latin *culcites*) and *bed* 971 (*spatula*) in both Épinal and
Erfurt. Pheifer §49 treats both spellings together for i-mutation;
neither gloss identifies the inherited direct-cell allomorph. The
same glossary's *segg/secg* 463 glosses *gladiolum*, ‘sedge’, not the
masculine ‘warrior’ in the `*ságjaz` comparison. Do not silently
substitute that homograph. For net, the Paris Psalter's Psalm 89
has *on nette* in the bird/fowler context; its non-final double
consonant improves the evidence for that cell. The glossary's
*nettae* 702 is not used here as a direct citation for the fishing
net.

[Bosworth–Toller s.v. *bed*](https://bosworthtoller.com/39676)
also cites *beddes* (genitive, *Beowulf* 1791) and *bedde* (dative,
the *Collocutio*). These are independent edited attestations of the
protected vowel-bearing stem, not just predictions of the model.

The simplex [*mene, myne*](https://bosworthtoller.com/22609) is
independently attested in glossary editions. Pheifer's introduction
identifies Corpus Glossary C887 *maenoe* for Latin *crepundia*.
This verifies a singleton outside the Old Saxon compound, but the
gloss does not independently establish the original neuter direct
singular cell; the dictionary's masculine classification is for
the English word. That is precisely the ambiguity between an
inherited simplex and Boutkan's proposed reclassification.

**Highest priority from its bibliography:** Sievers (1892), *Zur
westgermanischen Gemination*, pp. 262–265, within
[*Grammatische Miscellen*](https://doi.org/10.1515/bgsl.1892.1892.16.235),
for the original distinction
between compound and free-form witnesses; Erdmann (1972), *Suffixal j in
Germanic*, pp. 407–415 (local text), for the independent reconstruction of
glide-bearing cells. In particular, Erdmann pp. 409–410 makes the
ungeminated OE genitives *Deni(g)a* and *wini(g)(e)a* evidence for
`*-ij-`, not `*-j-`: a useful guard against assuming Boutkan's proposed
geminating i-stem genitive plural for every i-stem. Dresher and Lahiri
(1991), pp. 251–286, is useful for
testing foot-sensitive protections, not an arbiter of which direct cells
actually geminated; Barber (2013) is background rather than a prerequisite.
Outside that bibliography, Dahl (1938), pp. 81–86, Dal (1934), especially
pp. 243–246, and the relevant OHG grammar/edition loci are more urgent
than further general debate about Gothic syllabicity. Dahl and Dal are
not conflated; the relevant original passages and token-level OHG
editions were not independently obtained here.
