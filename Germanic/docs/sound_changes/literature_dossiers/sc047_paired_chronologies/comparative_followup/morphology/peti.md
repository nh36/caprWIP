also gives *petti*, *betdi* and undoubled dative *pete* in the Notker
gloss tradition (40.4). The occurrence of `‹t›` in an oblique warns
against inferring a clean phonemic paradigm from grapheme counts.
The old codex reading matters because it verifies the early locus
and the spelling and eliminates editorial invention of *peti*, not
because one ligature measures consonant duration. Nievergelt p. 476
reports Simmler's (1974:124) reading of the single dental as inherited
singleton; that argument is mediated, not a fresh reading of Simmler.

## Common input and point of divergence

The comparative stem is PGmc `*badja-` (EWA `*bađja-`, Kroonen s.v.
`*badja-`); the two neuter direct singulars are `*badją` in CAPR's
notation. Gothic *badi* supports the class, not the West Germanic
deletion date. Use protected `*badjas` and `*badjai` for genitive
and dative. `*d` below covers the ancestral dental; umlaut before the
front vocalic or *j* and the OHG dental shift are local reflex steps
(notation abstracts away from their relative dating). The
spellings `b/p` and `d/t` are not separate chronological operations
at the apocope hinge.

| History | Direct regular derivation and possible secondary routes | Protected stem |
|---|---|---|
| **A** (Ringe–Taylor 2014 pp. 45–47, Campbell §576) | `*badją > *badj > *badi`: deletion before SC010 strands *j*, which syllabifies; there is no eligible medial sequence when gemination applies. The light root retains high *i*; umlaut and the dental shift yield *beti/peti* (compare weakly shifted/spelled *bedi*). *Betti* needs spread of the oblique double into a direct cell that still has *-i*, or later maintenance/restoration of that ending. Plain stem replacement, not a second phonological gemination. | `*badjas/*badjai > *baddjas/*baddjai >` umlauted and shifted *bett-*; compare attested dative *bette*. The direct plural in a long ending also leaves *j* protected at gemination. |
| **B** (Hogg–Fulk 2011 §§2.22–2.24, extrapolated here to OHG) | `*badją > *baddją > *baddj > *baddi`, if the stranded *j* syllabifies after the low vowel drops as in their English *saggi*. This gives a **double** dental with *i* if *i* survives in the southern history (*betti*), or a doubled endingless stem if lost. *Peti* still requires independent reduction before retained *i*, mere underrepresentation of /tt/, or a **regional** early nasal-loss order. If southern *j* instead drops after the geminate, *-i* also needs a separate source. Hogg–Fulk's English high-vowel loss is not automatically an OHG rule. | Same protected *bett-*; it is not a singleton donor. |
| **C** (Boutkan 1995 pp. 209–213, 435–436, applying his published *hrukk* example to bed) | `*badją > *baddją > *badd(j) > *badd`, then southern final `*-CC > *-Ci`, giving `*badi` and subsequently *beti/peti*. His main route **creates** *i* at cluster resolution rather than preserving a separately vocalized *j*. Alternatively `*badd > *bad` by final simplification, then transfer of high *-i* from *endi*-type heavy stems gives `*badi`; a second transfer restores doubles in *betti*. Both variants are explicit alternatives; neither is established by observing *peti* itself. | Protected *bett-* supplies the geminate which C must reintroduce in the remodeled *betti* type after the southern singleton step. |

Before gemination **A** already lacks the final low vowel; **B/C**
still have a vowel and double the dental. B and C do not differ at
this hinge for a *nasal* cell; the eligible masculine oral cell
(*seċġ*) is where the orders first separate them. After the
southern step C and A are observationally identical in this word.
If *i* is analogical instead, an actual heavy-stem source exists,
but a direction and timing for its transfer to this class must
still be established. The generic fact of final degemination cannot
by itself produce a vowel.

## What the EWA argument adds

Lloyd and Springer, [EWA I col. 572, *betti*](https://ewa.saw-leipzig.de/articles/12830/de),
derive modern *Beet* from reconstructed direct `*badi` and *Bett*
from the doubled oblique stem `*badj-`; the standard semantic/formal
split is probably Central German and is discussed by Kluge (21st
ed., pp. 59, 71; compare the [earlier translated entry](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/An_Etymological_Dictionary_of_the_German_Language/Annotated/Beet)).
The spelling-and-meaning split is positive evidence that different
stem shapes persisted long enough to be lexicalized. It is not an
independent observation of how the singleton was first created:
C produces the same preform, and the ninth-century *peti* already
means ‘bed’, whereas doubled OHG forms also gloss ‘garden bed’.
The modern division did not already characterize the oldest
witnesses. The EWA itself separates the inherited stem alternation
from uncertain semantic developments.

**Judgment:** *Peti* securely belongs to the inherited neuter and to
its accusative singular. Its reading and high ending are certain;
its interpretation as an **unremodelled** reflex of early loss is
well motivated but not demonstrated. An orthographic account would
need local evidence that `‹t›` can represent /tt/ plus a separate
account of *i*. A C-type reconstruction needs a dated southern
singleton-creating process or evidence for ending transfer. A uses
independently motivated nuclearization of stranded *j* and no new
southern consonant-to-vowel rule. This is affirmative Continental
evidence favouring A, not a demonstration of pan-West-Germanic
chronology. Dal 1934:243–252 / reprint 1971:65–73 is still not
available for firsthand collation; her proposed direction of
transfer is known from Ringe–Taylor p. 47 and Boutkan pp. 211–213,
not reconstructed from the dictionary as though it were her text.
