# Light ja-stems: what each attestation can decide

Direct means nominative/accusative singular for neuters; a masculine
nominative in `*-jaz` has an oral ending after final *z* loss, whereas
its accusative `*-ją` is the nasal test. Reconstructions below use
CAPR accent notation. Use `*-jas` genitive and `*-jai` dative as
vowel-protected probes (Hogg–Fulk §§2.20–2.24); neuter direct plural
`*-jō` is not a second nasal-direct witness. Predicted English forms
are outputs, not manuscript transcriptions. A = early loss of both
endings; B = split; C = Boutkan's **northern order proxy**, not his
southern development. Early material is not automatically exempt
from levelling.

| Word and inherited cell | Attestation and regular A / B / C | Donor, positive support and limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Bed**: light neuter `*bádją`; gen. `*bádjas`, dat. `*bádjai` | OE *bede / bedd / bedd*. The Épinal–Erfurt glossary has *bedd* 243 and *bed* 971 (Pheifer pp. 14, 50; distinct Latin lemmas); OS poetic *bed*, later *beddi*, protected *beddie* (Fulk §7.11). In OHG the old *peti/bedi* and *beti* type has the high vowel and singleton spelling; East Franconian ninth-century Tatian *betti/bette* has doubling. See [edition loci](../source_integration/source_audit.md#edition-loci-behind-the-german-forms). | OE gen. *beddes* in *Beowulf* 1791 and dat. *bedde* in Ælfric's *Colloquy* show an available doubled stem, common to all arms. OHG *peti* is a viable inherited direct survivor under A; B needs regional chronology or a secondary singleton, C can derive one from a doubled form only with its southern innovation. Later OS *beddi* may add an ending from heavy stems rather than retain the original direct ending. The OHG single-letter obliques and uncertain spelling-to-quantity relation prevent treating the direct pattern as pristine. |

The three histories match the observed OE paradigm once directional levelling is allowed; no independently dated *nete* is attested. |

## Candidate screen beyond the original set

| Candidate | Comparison, attestation and what is recoverable |
|---|---|
| **Fen** `*fánją` | A *fene*, B/C *fen* (earlier *fenn*); all *fennes/fenne*. Kroonen p. 128 compares Goth. *fani*, ON *fen*, OS *feni*, OHG *fenni*. The OS form has not been traced to a specific token. [BT](https://bosworthtoller.com/10296) has acc. *fenn* (*Orosius* I.1, Bosworth 15.19), *fen* (*Beowulf* 104), dat. *fenne* (*Beowulf* 1295), dat. pl. *fennum* (*Bede* IV.19). The latter cells show the possible donor; *fennas* reflects a changed paradigm. Final *n/nn* no longer discriminates the original quantity. |
| **Pledge** `*wádją` | A *wede*, B/C *wedd*; all *weddes/wedde*. Kroonen p. 564, Goth. *wadi*, OS *weddi*, OHG *weti/wetti*; Boutkan p. 201 lists *weti*, but the primary OHG singleton locus and its cell were not secured. [BT](https://bosworthtoller.com/34926) has nom. *wedd* and acc. *wed* in the same Ælfrician homily (Thorpe II 272.6–8), acc. *wedd* (Gen. 17.10; Deut. 9.9), gen. *weddes* (Gen. 9.12), dat. *wedde* (Gen. 17.13; Alfred 36). This is a second nonlabial lexical counterpart to *beti/betti*, not two English consonant quantities inferred from two spellings. Both direct inheritance in B/C and spread of the attested doubled stem under A fit the later English paradigm. |
| **Web** `*wábją` | A *wefe*, B/C *webb*; all *webbes/webbe*. Kroonen p. 563 and Hogg–Fulk §2.20 support light neuter ja-stem status. [*Godwebb*](https://bosworthtoller.com/17343), *Pastoral Care* Hatton 14.6, supplies an early compound direct form; the dictionary also has *godwebbe*. The A path changes at SC010 and later spirantizes the singleton; no secure oldest simplex–oblique contrast was established. The labial itself introduces extra downstream evidence requirements. |

**No securely decisive inherited nasal direct witness emerged.** The
cleanest English comparisons (*nett, bedd, wedd*) establish recoverable
doubled stems and real protected donors, but not whether the stems are
inherited in the direct cells. *Cynn* and *fenn* additionally lose final
quantity information; *mene* has class and compound confounds. The
Continental high-vowel singleton series is the most useful positive
evidence for early loss, most securely the OHG bed-type. Plausible
inheritance of a singleton is not demonstration that early
gemination could not have produced it. Count one class-wide potential
stem transfer, not one unrelated historical rescue per lexeme; the
existence of donors still does not prove that transfer took place.
