# SC047 primary-source review: early loss remains preferred, not adopted

## Identity, question and current state

SC047 (`OEHeavySyllableNasalApocope`), 2026-11-21,
`research/sc047-labial-discussion`, starting at `dd50f6ce`. Can the
old Continental direct form be explained by a regular inherited loss
before *j*-gemination, or does independently known southern phonology
derive the same two properties after gemination? A direct-cell
singleton **with** a high vowel matters, not a written singleton
standing alone. A post-gemination path needs independent evidence for
both the consonant quantity and the source of the vowel.

The registry already characterizes SC047 as the PWGmc, pan-WGmc
short-nasal counterpart of SC041 (confidence B). The live rule
deletes short final `ą` after a consonant with no syllable-weight
condition, at executable position 56 after SC010. The frozen
post-deer census has 158 firings; source compatibility is not
inferred from the rule name or place in the cascade. The existing
registry decision is reformulation with retention; this report
**defers the historical order and retains the executable rule**.
No registry verdict line, production propagation, or new

## Diagnosis and source gains

The [*peti* collation](peti.md) establishes the diplomatic
*p* + *et* ligature + *i* in the second-quarter ninth-century
Bavarian text glossary, its accusative status, and distinct Frankish
*bedi* in the same inherited **lexeme**. The modern *Beet/Bett*
division supports survival of two stem shapes, not the origin of the
shape contrast. The [English cells and search ledger](english-cells.md)
separate actual donors from structural donors and show that the
survey did not find a witness selecting among the nasal orders
independently of possible transfer. In particular, a doubled *wicg*
does not solve the problem because its doubled obliques are attested.
Dal and Dahl remain indirectly known; neither the available early
English *-i* forms (Hogg–Fulk §2.25) nor Boutkan's table supplies an
unambiguous matching nonlabial control.

The diagnoses are inherited from the pinned stage traces, not fresh
Foma execution. *Nett* is a direct nasal timing probe: A bleeds
SC010 by removing the following vowel, B feeds the geminated
stem before the vowel is lost, and C's northern-order proxy has the
same effect for this cell. *Bedd* and *wedd* reproduce that contrast
unscored. Their protected obliques feed gemination under all orders.
*Seċġ*'s oral nominative (and scored *hedge*) instead tests SC041:
early *-a* loss removes the gemination environment in A/B but not C.
*Rest* is an independently introduced long-ending/final-*z*
interaction. *Door* requires SC112 to see `*dorą` before the
experimental short-vowel loss; *there/where* and the 40 lowering
identities are invariants, not new historical edges.

### Boutkan's south is a separate hypothesis, not a corpus result

Boutkan (§3.2.2.2, pp. 209–213) treats the split as OE and **epic**
OS *segg* versus OHG and some **minor-text** OS *weggi* (e.g.
Petrus) and *dilli* (classification qualified). His common order is
medial `*Cj > CC` before low apocope. In the northern example the
non-doubled `*harj > *hari` syllabifies but *segg* does not gain
*i* after the geminate; later final simplification has its own
conditioning. In the south, his `*hrukjas > *hrukk > *hruki`
gives a plain C plus high *i*, after which *hrucki* can regain the
double from `*hrukkjes` and similar forms. The alternative on p. 213
instead uses `*hrukk > *hruk`, then transferred *-i* from heavy
stems, then an independently transferred doubled stem. P. 435
lists reduction in Ingvaeonic and `CC > Ci` and refilling of
direct-case geminates for “German,” rather than making it a second
common West Germanic event. The examples, including reported Dutch
doublets (p. 212 n. 71), establish distributions, not dated
changes or sharp boundaries covering all OS.

- **Bed, pledge and net.** The [bed derivations](peti.md) show
  exactly where C supplies the otherwise unexplained new *i*.
  Pledge is formally parallel: `*wadją > *waddją > *wadd >
  *wadi > weti`; restore *wett-* from a protected cell for *wetti*.
  But an OHG *weti* token and oblique donor are not secured in
  this set. Net likewise has `*natją > *nattją > *natt`; southern
  resolution predicts a singleton-plus-*i* stage before local
  shift, whereas attested OHG *nezzi* is the doubled type
  (Kroonen s.v., BT *nett*). It is not an independent confirmation
  of a singleton intermediate; the interpretation would need
  regular restoration or independent proof the process missed
  this segmental environment. Independent controls should compare
  uncontroversial final geminates and non-ja *-i* donors in the
  same region, not repeatedly count reflexes already at issue.
- **Hay and district.** Boutkan pp. 211–212 derives *gewi* from
  `*gawwj-` via `*gawi`, and *gouwi* with analogical *ww*;
  applying the same template to hay gives
  `*hawją > *hawwją > *haww > *hawi > hewi`. The restored
  *ww* type with the labial/diphthongal developments gives
  *houwi*. The A route directly gives `*hawj > *hawi` without
  the *ww* intermediate. Notker has both short and long direct
  spellings and short-type genitive and dative, so the comparative
  alternation does not observe either direction of stem transfer
  in an unremodelled paradigm. The mixed labial cases cannot
  validate a blanket consonant-to-high-vowel change.
- **Necklace.** Boutkan p. 210's northern `*manją > *mannją >
  *menn > *men >> *meni > mene` first simplifies a final double
  and then adds *-i* by i-stem reclassification. Shared oblique
  endings and OE masculine use support the move; no pre-transfer
  doubled English *men(n)* cell is established. A gets the
  singleton-plus-*i* without needing that transfer to create it,
  though later reclassification can still occur. The OHG
  *menni* type and the OS compound do not by themselves decide
  the free OE direct-cell history.

The heavy *endi* type motivates Boutkan's association of low
apocope with high apocope, but Fulk §7.11 n. 5 points out that
high-vowel deletion has independently late ordering constraints
relative to mutation; the two processes need not coincide.
Boutkan's *auja* on the Skodborg bracteate (§3.2.2.3 p. 213)
can support regional low-vowel survival if regular, but he
acknowledges possible formulaic survival, and *mäkija* is early
Runic, not an eligible West Germanic light direct cell. The
same source standard applies to A's compound relics. C saves
English transfers at the expense of an unverified, geographically
limited phonological operation or two separate morphological
transfers. That is not yet a better-supported comparative history.

Hollifield I pp. 32–33 and II pp. 168–172 distinguish inherited
final nasal loss, later loss of nasality (or renewed nasality in
some environments) and deletion of the **vowel**. No new evidence
was found that distinguishes the segmentally identical plain
short endings at the relevant deletion point. Keep the established
finding; a fourth executable chronology would supply the missing
conditioning by fiat.

## Implementation questions, not chronology votes

**Hue.** The published hypothesis is not “delete a letter in
`hii`.” Once loss of *w* before the weak high vowel gives a
stressed open *í* plus unstressed *i*, FII fuses the adjacent short
vowels `*í.i > *ī`. Compensation would act at loss of *w*
(`*í.wi > *ī.i`); compensation alone leaves two syllables, so
the monosyllable requires either concurrent loss of the second
vowel or subsequent high apocope `*ī.i > *ī`. In the latter path
the long first vowel supplies the heavy trigger; if the eligible
second vowel is already long or stressed, this particular
deletion is not predicted. Neither a *j*-interrupted sequence
nor a morpheme boundary automatically licenses fusion; stress
and prosodic adjacency must be established independently.

Ringe–Taylor §6.9.3 pp. 314–319 propose contraction across
hiatus from **non-w** sources, e.g. after intervocalic *h* in
*þohe > þoe > þō* (Épinal 3, Corpus 207), and in inherited
vocalic sequences such as *drȳ* from `*drui` and verbal
*frēo-* from `*frio-`. These make vowel coalescence independent
of *w*-loss a supported general mechanism; they do not instantiate
the same **short** `*í.i` input. Anglian `*gesihiþi` is a closer
non-w control: Ringe–Taylor p. 179 give Mercian *gesīþ* versus
Northumbrian *gesiid*, interpreting the latter hiatus as
analogically restored. The second vowel is in a verbal ending
before a consonant, and the history of breaking and syncope in
that dialect must be secured; it does not independently identify
the mechanism of the noun-final *í.i*. Mercian *fiil* (Corpus
Glossary 1234, Ringe–Taylor p. 306) comes from a cluster with
final sonorant after monophthongization, not from two vowels
separated by *w* or *h*. It is not an independent positive.
Conversely, Mercian *streide* attests nonhomorganic hiatus at
some point, but spelling alone does not establish first-vowel
length. *Sǣ/ǣ* could shed *i* after a vowel already long
(Campbell §406; Ringe–Taylor pp. 318–319). These comparisons
make broad compensation unattractive without identifying
short-nucleus fusion against restricted lengthening plus deletion.
The [pinned local contrast](hiatus.tsv) documents observational
equivalence for *hī, glī, nī-* after downstream deletion; no
new run would distinguish them on this evidence.

**Hay.** Ringe–Taylor pp. 53, 173, 246 posit separate
`*hawj > *hawi > *hæwi` and `*hawwj- > *hauj- > *hēaj-`
stems; the latter supplies West Saxon *hīeġ* and Anglian
*hēg* through mutation and dialect-specific developments.
The first is the light direct singular; for comparison a
vowel-protected genitive `*xáwjas` and direct plural `*xáwjō`
pass through `*wwj` and give *hīeġes/hīeġ* in the pinned
arm. The modelled genitive is not a manuscript token. Attested
*mid híg* (*Colloquy*, BT Supplement) places the long stem in
an oblique; Notker and the OHG glosses attest both direct types
and *heuues* as genitive (AWb IV 1043). This supports available
alternants, not their unambiguous original distribution. Fulk
§§4.10, 6.15 also allow inherited *au*, which need not pass
through restoration from an oblique geminate.

The narrow archived FWA restriction allows `*xáwi > *xæwi >
*çewi > *çei > hei` rather than promoting singleton *aw* to
a long diphthong at SC034. The model lacks a continuation
that would turn this short hiatus into a long nucleus plus
palatal; none is independently required by the attestations.
Thus the mismatch with the selected headword is not evidence
of a faulty *short-stem* sound law: the selected form can
reflect a distinct, possibly remodelled long stem. Only a
secure continuation of the short form would put an omitted
regular change at issue. The FWA-only contrast held *hue,
strew, believe, straw* fixed and left non-i *sċēawien*
unchanged; the early *hii* and scored *straw*'s inherited
baseline mismatch were not “fixed” by it. Local ‘flock’,
‘strew’ and ‘call’ preterites do change and need their own
dialect/ending analysis. FNS preserves both stranded singleton
`Vwj` and `wwj`; FNG protects only the geminate. Delayed
singleton treatment cannot silently represent immediate
vocalization. No production correction or labial block move.
