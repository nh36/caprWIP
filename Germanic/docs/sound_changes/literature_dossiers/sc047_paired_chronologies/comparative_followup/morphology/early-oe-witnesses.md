# Early Old English singleton–geminate witness register

**No A witness.** The best B evidence is the opposition *mete : mettas* in
the ninth-century Mercian gloss to the Vespasian Psalter, including both stems
on the same printed line in Sweet's edition. This is a genuine alternation,
but the independently supported i-stem ancestry makes it evidence for
morphological restructuring, not an inherited nasal direct cell.

This register extends the [earlier cell ledger](english-cells.md), which has
the full donor inventory and the archived predictions. All additions are
unscored. **A** = potentially decisive inherited direct cell; **B** = strong
observed morphological alternation, not necessarily a secure prehistoric
direction; **C** = relevant but confounded; **D** = not diagnostic. Text
forms below are edition readings unless a facsimile check is specified. A bare
Latin glossary lemma does not independently fix the Old English case or
number. Separate manuscript copies of the same inherited gloss are not
independent lexical experiments.

Ép = Épinal BM 72(2), c. 700; Erf = Erfurt Amplonianus 2°42, early ninth
century, copied on the Continent; Cp = Cambridge CCCC 144, ninth century
(mid-century in Herren–Sauer's introduction). These related Old English
glossary traditions carry layered linguistic evidence, not three clean
regional samples. VP = London, BL Cotton Vespasian A.i: ninth-century
Mercian gloss to an eighth-century Latin Psalter, not an eighth-century
English witness ([manuscript description](https://journals.lib.sfu.ca/index.php/asmmf/article/view/5493)).
The numbers after Sweet's psalm/hymn titles use his edition's numbering.
Herren et al.'s revised *Anetum* entry corroborates Pheifer §77 and no. 21.

## Inherited light neuter screen and separate controls

For the secure light ja-stems below, **A** predicts a vowel-final singleton
in the unremodelled singular; **B** predicts a doubled stem and eventual
loss of the high vowel. Both predict protected doubled donors. Written
final *C/CC* alone cannot distinguish inheritance from early levelling
or final simplification. PGmc stems and comparative support for the first
four are in the [cell ledger](english-cells.md); additional stems see
Kroonen s.vv. and Ringe–Taylor p. 213.

|---|---|---|---|

**Search boundary.** This is a targeted pass over the principal stems in the
previous ledger, the held Épinal–Erfurt edition and Corpus apparatus,
Herren–Sauer's sample and revised *Anetum* entry (with the
Corpus *dili* facsimile), Sweet's gloss and name-list loci, and
the named charter and comparative leads in Dahl and
Adamczyk. It is not an exhaustive DOE Corpus, charter, Kentish or
manuscript-image survey. Unchecked source leads are labelled as such.
Glossary *dili* is worth a focused etymological check, not a new experiment.
