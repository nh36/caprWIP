# English neuter ja-stems: cells, donors and exclusions

The stem reconstructions `*natja-`, `*badja-`, `*wadja-` and
`*kunja-` have comparative support independent of the English doublets
(Kroonen, s.vv.; Gothic *nati, badi, wadi, kuni*). The inherited
neuter nominative and accusative share `*-ją`. The table gives
**edited or dictionary-cited** spellings, not new manuscript
transcriptions; see the cited text loci. Missing attestations are
not claims of absence from all OE.

### The derivations and the morphological inference

In the [archived net and bed traces](../source_integration/diagnostics.tsv)
and [pledge traces](diagnostics.tsv), A loses `*-ą` and vocalizes
`*natj, *badj, *wadj` to `*nati, *badi, *wadi` before SC010.
The light root vowel fronts and mutates while *i* is available;
high *i* survives after the light syllable and later lowers to *e*.
In B the low vowel is still present at gemination:
`*nattją, *baddją, *waddją > *nattj, *baddj, *waddj >
*natti, *baddi, *waddi`. The mutated heavy stems lose *i* and
give *nett, bedd, wedd*. The C northern proxy gives the same
nasal-direct endpoints; Boutkan instead treats lack of
nuclearization in the geminate class as part of the northern
history. The endpoints alone do not distinguish those mechanics.

Under A, an **endingless doubled** direct stem is not obtained by
simply grafting `-es`, `-e` or `-um` onto the old direct form.
The transferred object is either the geminated stem before
high-vowel loss (so direct *i* now follows a heavy root), or a
doubled bare stem after high-vowel loss, potentially identical
to the direct plural. The documented obliques establish
availability, not when the transfer happened. A single class
pattern could serve all three; Hogg–Fulk §§2.19–2.24 show the
resulting EWS paradigm and discuss precisely this alternative,
not an independently observed diachronic transition in that whole
class. The proposed parallels are actual morphological phenomena
(direct-case syncretism in a-stems; shared oblique endings and
stem transfer across related classes), not proofs of
oblique-to-direct direction. Hogg–Fulk §3.41 do not treat
*hyse/hyssas* as a transparent example of inherited ja-stem
levelling. The Hogg source at §§2.23 and 3.41 is **“Analogy and
phonology”** (1979, *Journal of Linguistics* 15:55–85, pp. 68–73),
not his 1979 palatalization paper. The catalogue and indexed
extracts identify the contrasting models for *hyll/hyse*;
discussion here does not substitute those extracts for a full
collation of either Hogg or Dahl. Dahl pp. 81–86 are known
through Hogg–Fulk and Fulk; Dal's case and compound cautions are
known through the citations detailed in the
[source audit](../source_integration/source_audit.md). No class-wide
proof of one direction was found.

**Cynn.** `*kunją` in A gives `*kunj > *kuni > cyne`; B and the
northern C proxy have `*kunnją > *kunnj > *kunni > *kynn > cyn`.
The double is visible before final simplification, not lost at
SC010. Protected `*kunjas/*kunjai` give *cynnes/cynne* in all arms
(Fulk §7.11; [BT *cynn*](https://bosworthtoller.com/7096)).
*Cyne-* in *cynedōm* is evidence of an ungeminated form but not a
free direct cell; compound-internal apocope could independently
strand the glide earlier (Dal p. 244 n. 2 as quoted by Boutkan
p. 211). The Weser bone spelling `kunni` is early evidence for
regional doubling; an independent word boundary and its
grammatical cell are not secured. Thus neither the spelling
contrast *cyn/cynn* nor the compound gives the missing direct
chronology at the point of gemination.
Hogg–Fulk §2.25 also cite *cynnæs*, the genitive in Cædmon's Hymn,
giving an early dialectal donor beside the later *cynnes* type.

## Bounded inventory and exclusion ledger

Screened the simplex light-neuter list of Hogg–Fulk §2.20 in full,
their derived groups (§§2.21, 3.40), Boutkan's Continental direct-cell
table (p. 201) and discussions (pp. 209–213), and Kroonen's
comparative entries for the candidates below. Used dialectal
gloss/edition loci for the best-preserved contrasts. This is not
an exhaustive trawl of every text or spelling in the DOE. The
positive criteria were secure inherited `*-ją`, eligible *Cj*,
interpretable root quantity **and** final vowel, and either a
pre-transfer direct cell or evidence against the competing
interpretation of the paradigm.

| Candidates screened | Result |
|---|---|
| *Nett, bedd, wedd* | Secure class and eligible nonlabial root. English donors and direct forms firmly attested, but no independent direction of spread. OHG *peti/bedi* is the strongest positive singleton evidence; see the [primary collation](peti.md). Kroonen p. 564 and Boutkan p. 201 give *weti/wetti*, but no secure primary singleton locus, case and date were found for *weti*. It is a promising second lexical parallel, not equal in weight to *peti*. |
| *Cynn, fenn* | Historical root double later neutralized word-finally. Kroonen p. 128 reports OS *feni*, OHG *fenni*; OS *feni* was not tracked to a secure free direct cell. OE *fennum/fenne* are real donors. |
| *Nebb, webb* | Norse comparison and Kroonen's `*nabja-/*wabja-` support the class. [BT *nebb*](https://bosworthtoller.com/23529) shows nom. *nebb* (Exeter Book *Phoenix* 299), acc. *nebb* (*Riddle* 79.1), dat. *nebbe*; Alfred 48 even varies *neb/nebb/næb* by manuscript. Both have labial and quantity/transfer confounds; neither preserves the earlier direct shape. |
| *Wicg* | Useful nonlabial addition: Ringe–Taylor p. 213 explicitly posit `*wigja > *wigi` against its protected geminated stem (OS gen. pl. *wiggeo*). The [OE attestation ledger](https://bosworthtoller.com/35512) has direct *wicg* (*Elene* 1196), dat. *wicge* (*Beowulf* 234), gen. pl. *wicga* (*Beowulf* 1045), dat. pl. *wiggum* (*Andreas* 1097). The geminate stem is recoverable, but the donor is likewise independently attested and no free reflex of `*wigi` was identified. North Germanic *vigg* does not establish the West Germanic date. |
| *Mene*, Continental *bil*, OHG *chuti*, OS *kin/kinn(i)* | *Mene* has gender/class and compound-boundary problems; the strongest singleton examples of *bil/chuti* in this pass are table forms, not securely parsed primary paradigms. Boutkan p. 213 discusses alternatives *kinja-/kinwa-* and independent *nw* assimilation for *kin(n)* ‘chin’; not a secure like-for-like stem. OS *lilli* has a different original vowel quantity and *mūspelli* an opaque etymology/earlier double; neither can carry the test (Boutkan p. 213). |
| Hay, hue, and related labial stems | Comparatively real, but inherited *au*, *wj/wwj*, mutation and other changes introduce additional assumptions; long/short variants do not track direct and protected cells in the attested Notker paradigm. |
| *Fliċċe, styċċe*; *rīċe, wīte*; *-enn/-ett* formations and collectives | The first pair have earlier doubles (Hogg–Fulk §2.20 n. 2); the second pair are heavy *ij*-stems, useful vocalization controls but not light eligible roots. Derived *fæsten, wēsten, bærnet, þēowet* have unstressed suffixal geminate simplification and sometimes alternative suffixes (§3.40); collectives require compound/stem-weight assessment, not an inherited light simplex comparison. |
| Early *-i* spellings (Hogg–Fulk §2.25) | *Durheri/teblheri* end in the non-geminating *r* and occur in compounds; *mǣli/stǣli* belong to heavy stems. *Dili/dil* could be promising if the light ja-stem analysis were established independently; Hogg–Fulk favour the i-stem on later *dile* and cite Dahl p. 87 and Pheifer §77 for the opposing view. Isolated *geddi* is discussed as an apparent instrumental, not a parsed inherited nasal direct. |
| *Seċġ, hryċġ, weċġ, bridd, hyll, here, mete* | Masculine oral nom. and nasal acc. must not be treated as two nasal neuter direct cells. *Seċġ* is the useful pairing and *here* vocalizes but *r* is independently exempt. The i-/ja-stem history of *hyll* and inherited i-stem status of *mete* prevent counting them as independent neuter nasal cells. |

**Stop condition met:** no further primary candidate in these
groups combines secure nasal ancestry with independently recoverable
pre-transfer direct quantity. Existing data can test inheritance
against levelling, but not by holding the possibility of levelling
fixed for only one account. Keep all additional comparisons unscored.
