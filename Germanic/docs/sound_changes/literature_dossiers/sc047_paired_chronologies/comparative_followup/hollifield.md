# Hollifield (1980): final vowels, nasality and the limit of the chronology

Both parts of Patrick Henry Hollifield, “The Phonological Development of Final
Syllables in Germanic,” *Die Sprache* 26 (1980), 19–53 and 145–178, are held
as licensed loans. Part II identifies Part I in its title footnote. The
[intake receipt](../../../../../../docs/references/sc047_early_apocope/hollifield1980.tsv)
maps both sets of sheets, including the unnumbered titles and the Part II
volume cover. Page references below are printed pages. OCR served as a
navigation aid; the original page images control the crucial notation and
chronological summaries.

| Passage | Argument and applicability |
|---|---|
| I, pp. 19–33 | The comparative evidence for accent and inherited intonation bears on the quality of final reflexes. Hollifield's pre-PGmc sequence has final *m* becoming *n*, final *n* producing vowel nasality (with a monosyllabic restriction), and later apocope of short oral non-high final vowels in nonmonosyllables. Gothic verbal endings supply the evidence at this point. That first oral-vowel loss is not SC041: the post-PGmc *a* exposed by loss of *z* is not already final in its input. Do not infer loss of a mora from the intonation notation alone. |
| I, pp. 33–36; II, p. 169 n. 85 and p. 171 | Distinguishes North Germanic early loss of inherited short final *i* from its West Germanic preservation, and proposes fronting of **consonant-protected** short *a*. OHG *tages*, *fater* and *alles/elles* are evidence for vowel quality in retained syllables, not for the date of SC041. The contraction after loss of *i/y* next to weakly stressed back vowels on p. 36 is not the stressed-root `*í.i` of the hue trial. |
| I, pp. 37–46, especially p. 44; p. 52 correction; II, pp. 145–149 | Keep separate the reflexes of inherited long endings and the innovations that obscured them. In the a-stem accusative plural, `*-anz > *-ąz > *-ą`, with nasality protecting the vowel in Hollifield's account. Cotton *Heliand* adjectival plural *-a* and OHG noun *taga* versus adjective *blinte* are used to distinguish the generalized accusative and nominative endings. In the feminine accusative plural he proposes analogical reintroduction of nasality into a previously oral long ending in pre-OHG/pre-OS, not pre-OE, where fronting supplies a contrary test; compare OHG *gebā* versus adjectival *blinto*. His correction on p. 52 revises the intermediate of the feminine numeral, not the conditioning of short nasal apocope. These are evidence for ending history and real analogical generalization, not a donor analysis of *nett* or *bedd*. |
| II, pp. 150–166, esp. 154–155 and 174–175 addenda 7–8 | The Monacensis counts are divided by section, not pooled with Cotton. Contrast the almost general Cotton *-a* in specified cells with Monacensis variation (including *sie/sea* in the feminine singular and the atypical distribution in the weak declension). A preceding palatal element and possible dialect borrowing matter for interpreting that variation. Addendum 8 discusses corrections to Schlüter's counts and the limits of both his and Oppermann's classifications; addendum 7 distinguishes inherited and transferred endings rather than equating all written *-an*. These are later Old Saxon distributions and chronological consequences of particular reconstructed endings, not direct measurements of PWGmc nasal *a* after a light stressed syllable. |
| II, pp. 167–168; pp. 170–171 n. 86 | The order internal rhotacism → short-vowel syncope → final *z* loss is argued for **syncope following an unaccented syllable**, with *Aflims/Vatvims* still preserving the final consonant and *dōgur* and *lembir* showing the medial development. The inscriptions cannot be precisely dated. The environment is not that of bare `*-a` after the one accented syllable of the light ja-stem controls. Hollifield also discusses continental Malberg *focla* (possibly Latinized) and Weimar *leob*, *haribrig* (the latter with a qualification); these can bound regional bare *a* loss, not order it against SC010. |
| II, pp. 169–172; pp. 176 addenda 10, 13 | The prose **tentatively** removes original final nasality in pre-North/West Germanic, groups short `*-a` from both `*-az` and `*-aⁿ` in the later apocope discussion, and derives **new** nasality from `*-Vnz` before final *z* loss. This supplies a reason not to equate the origin of the nasal feature with the date of deletion. His lists explicitly give bare *a* loss in the pre-OHG and OS sections, but do not place gemination in those lists or establish the equivalent OE short-`*-a` order. The prose puts `*ji > *i` before consonant gemination (p. 169); this is not final `*j > *i` after loss of *a*. The *fridoo* correction strengthens an OHG long-vowel argument, not the chronology of short *a*; addendum 13 explicitly limits how much of the relative order can be recovered. |

**Implications for CAPR.** SC041 and SC047 may represent two distinct
input histories even if original nasal *a* was first denasalized and both
then underwent a single deletion. Hollifield's analysis, if extended to the
light direct cells under review, is less congenial to **nasality itself**
protecting them through SC010. It does not establish when the remaining
vowel disappeared, and neither A (both early) nor C (gemination first)
follows from it. Conversely, deriving gemination in a nasal-origin direct
cell does not show the vowel still carried the nasal feature. A theory with
early denasalization and shared apocope must independently explain how
nominal direct cells could still contrast if it predicts a split.

The renewed nasality from `*-Vnz`, and the later analogical extension of
nasality into another ending, are **not** CAPR SC048's final-*n* proxy.
Hollifield offers reasons to distinguish oralization, consonant loss,
nasal feature transfer and vowel loss; he gives no reason to move SC048
or SC059 along with SC047. His medial/final *z* evidence is germane to
the SC020 family and the deer contrast, but not an independent date
for the ordinary final-*z* operation against *j*-gemination, for
`PNWGmcFinalLongORaising` in *rest*, or for SC112 in *door*. Likewise,
the lowering in *fore* discussed on p. 145 n. 54 is not a calibration
of the tested SC017 census. No new registered edge is warranted.

**Other originals and access limits.** Erdmann (1972), pp. 409–410,
and Sievers (1892), pp. 262–265, were reread in the held originals.
Erdmann's ungeminated *Deni(g)a* and *wini(g)(e)a* support nuclear
`*-ij-` instead of *j* adjacent to the root consonant; they rule out
using a generic i-stem genitive as a known geminating donor for *mettas*.
Sievers' loan evidence makes gemination independent of paradigm
alternation, without excluding the later spread of a doubled stem.

the holdings or the catalogues consulted. HathiTrust's

is search-only; the [Dahl catalogue entry](https://books.google.com/books?id=tTF6oAEACAAJ)

der ia-Stämme im Westgermanischen,” *Norsk Tidsskrift for Sprogvidenskap*
7 (1934), 243–252 (or *Kleine Schriften*, 1971, 65–73), and Dahl,
*Substantival inflexion in early Old English: Vocalic stems*, Lund
Studies in English 7 (1938), 74–86. Dal's treatment of *beti* and the
accusative of *seċġ*, and Dahl's accusative and i-/ja-stem evidence,
remain mediated by the sources identified in [the comparative appendix](sources.md).
