## Historical problem

`SC039-SC041` sits at an awkward but important seam in the assembled half. On
its left the book has already promoted `SC035-SC037`, whose center of gravity
is SC036 and its low-stress medial-vowel material. To the right lie the
remaining brightening and restoration chapters, where SC046 is already promoted
inside `SC046-SC048`. The present row therefore has to do two things at once:
keep a real transition in view, and avoid pretending that three adjacent CAPR
rules automatically equal one neat textbook chapter.

The asymmetry inside the row is now fairly clear. SC039 and SC040 are the
strongest local pair: their reciprocal `widow` evidence makes a real adjacent
mini-corridor of low-stress `u` behavior. SC041 is different. It is
historically legible as part of early final-vowel loss, but its earlier
boundary reaches back broadly to SC020 and its later boundary points forward to
SC046. The real dossier question is therefore no longer "is there enough
source material?" but "should the future prose keep one cautious bridge with an
explicit SC039-SC040 core, or split later into SC039-SC040 plus an SC041
final-loss note?"

## Source dossier

### Campbell 1959

- **source_key:** `Campbell1959`
- **locator:** §218; §§338--339; §332; §341
- **terminology:** combinative `u`-umlaut; low-stress `weorold / weoruld`; unaccented-syllable `geogup`; loss of unaccented final vowels
- **short quotation:** "`wuduwe widow`"; "`weorold ... weoruld`"; "`geogup youth`"; "`@ of unaccented final syllables was lost in Gmc.`"
- **paraphrase:** Campbell gives the cleanest single handbook frame for all three members, but not as one ready-made chapter. In §218 he discusses the special `w`-conditioned or combinative `u`-umlaut material directly and cites forms such as `wuduwe`, `widuwe`, and related alternants, which makes SC039 historically legible rather than merely model-specific [@Campbell1959, §218]. In §§338--339 he treats low-stress vocalism in forms such as `weorold` and `weoruld`, including the development of later `u` in weakly stressed positions, which maps well onto the SC040 space [@Campbell1959, §§338--339]. In §332 he includes `geogup` among the unaccented-syllable developments, which supports the `youth` witness as part of the same broader low-stress and final-syllable zone [@Campbell1959, §332]. Finally, §341 gives the broad historical background for SC041 by stating that the relevant unaccented final vowel was lost in Germanic and then generalized farther in prehistoric Old English [@Campbell1959, §341].
- **conditioning:** `w`-conditioned or combinative environments for unstressed `u`; low-stress medial syllables; unaccented final syllables and later final-vowel erosion
- **chronology:** Campbell strongly supports SC039-SC040 as a real medial-vocalic region. SC041 is also source-backed, but as part of a broader final-vowel-loss history rather than as the third member of one tight local chapter.
- **examples:** `wuduwe`; `widuwe`; `weorold`; `weoruld`; `geogup`
- **cautions:** Campbell does not present one classical "SC039-SC041" chapter. His material supports a seam from medial vowel behavior into final-syllable loss, but the local unity is much stronger for SC039-SC040 than for SC041.

## CAPR formulation versus literature

CAPR sharpens one real but uneven region into three adjacent rules.

SC039 `OEWICombinativeUUmlaut` is the narrow left flank. Campbell's
`wuduwe / widuwe` material and Ringe and Taylor's `*widuwon-` derivation show
that the `widow` witness belongs to a genuine historical issue around
`w`-conditioned low-stress `u` behavior [@Campbell1959, §218;
@RingeTaylor2014, p. 322]. The chronology card is one-sided, but the one live
positive side is exactly the local one that matters: `SC039 < SC040`.

SC040 `OEMedUnstressedULowering` is the strongest member. Campbell's
`weorold / weoruld` discussion and Ringe and Taylor's `*weraldu > *weruld >
weorold ~ worold` derivation place it in the historical space of low-stress
medial vocalism, while the `geogup` / `youth` material gives its later
cross-reference a real source backdrop [@Campbell1959, §§338--339, §332;
@RingeTaylor2014, §6.3.3; @RingeTaylor2014, p. 267]. That makes SC040 the best
prose center if the row ever stays grouped.

SC041 `PWGmcFinalBareALoss` is historically real in a different register.
Campbell and Ringe and Taylor both support the broad early erosion of short low
vowels in final position [@Campbell1959, §341; @RingeTaylor2014, pp. 60--61].
But that historical support is less locally specific than the SC039-SC040
material. The rule reads less like the third member of one tight medial-vowel
chapter and more like the first explicit final-loss hinge that later interacts
with SC046.

The practical consequence is a grouped row with uneven internal weight: SC039 as
the one-sided left flank, SC040 as the source-backed local center, and SC041 as
the broader right-hand final-loss hinge.

## Chapter-shape options

### Option 1: keep SC039-SC041 as one cautious adjacent bridge

This remains viable if the book needs one compact seam between the promoted
SC035-SC037 report and the later brightening/restoration region.

- **Why it works:** the rules are adjacent, all three are ordinary FST changes, and together they narrate a real turn from medial low-stress vowel behavior toward final-syllable loss.
- **How it should be narrated:** SC039 and SC040 as the explicit local medial-vocalic core, SC041 as the broader right-hand final-loss hinge.
- **Main caution:** the prose must say openly that SC041 is weaker as part of the local core than SC039-SC040.

### Option 2: split later into SC039-SC040 plus a separate SC041 note

This is the strongest alternative and the one the current source pass modestly
favors.

- **Why it works:** the source record and chronology are both sharper for SC039-SC040 than for the three-change row as a whole. `Widow` gives a genuine reciprocal local pair, while SC041 points outward to SC020 and SC046.
- **Possible shape:** a compact SC039-SC040 medial-lowering report, followed by a short SC041 final-loss note or bridge.
- **Why it is not mandatory yet:** the present task is dossier preparation only, and the grouped scaffold still serves the assembled half well pending human chapter-shape review.

### Option 3: redistribute the row only by cross-reference

This is now the weakest option.

- **Why it is tempting:** SC040 points far right to SC072, and SC041 points both left to SC020 and right to SC046.
- **Why it is weaker:** every ordinary FST change still needs explicit prose somewhere, and the SC039-SC040 core is too strong to dissolve into pure cross-reference.
- **Main caution:** redistribution alone risks obscuring the row's real local seam without solving the need to write SC041 explicitly.
