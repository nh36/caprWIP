## Assessment for SC046

Dresher supplies a substantive third issue beyond choosing an early
sound-law chronology: how a later grammar can lose restoration while
retaining consonantal retraction. His distinction between raising,
restoration and consonantal retraction must survive a comparison with
restricted first fronting. The central inference depends on *hwalas*
remaining back and on the Corpus distribution of *a/æ/ea*, not simply
on the existence of *dagum*.

The analysis does not independently reconstruct every following vowel,
establish identical dialect histories, or remove the need to inspect
glossary transmission. The existing Seiler2019 card is the authority for
that transmission caution. Nor does Dresher's preferred evaluation
measure itself authorize a CAPR executable change.

Lass and Anderson (1975), pp.66–69, are deferred, not represented as read
or as evidence supporting either verdict.
