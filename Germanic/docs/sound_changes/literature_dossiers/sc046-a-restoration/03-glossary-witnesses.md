# SC046: three glossary witnesses and the limits of discrimination

## Readings checked, not normalized

Pheifer (1974; held 1998 reprint) is the edition used below. The manuscript
photographs were checked independently of its searchable text. Folio
numbers below identify the glossary leaves used by the photographic
sequence; the Latin lemma and column pair remove ambiguity with the
codex's other foliation.

| Witness | Exact edition locator and reading | Photograph and direct observation |
|---|---|---|
| *spadan* 'spades' | Pheifer p. 57, no. 1087; commentary p. 134. Épinal and Erfurt *uangas spadan*. Apparatus: Corpus 2079 *spadan*, Leiden 39/12 *spaedun*; Jun. 106/12 has weak nominative *spada*, beside strong feminine *spadu*. | Épinal glossary f. 14v, middle Latin/English pair, *uangas : spadan*, immediately below *uaricat : stridit*. Both root and ending contain a distinct *a*. |
| *-hebuc* 'hawk' | Pheifer p. 27, no. 497: Épinal *horodius uualhhebuc*, Erfurt *horodius uualhhaebuc*. Apparatus: the Épinal base writing is *uualhebuc* with another *h* above; initial *uu* is over an erasure. | Épinal glossary f. 6r, middle pair near its bottom, *horodius : uual(h)hebuc*. The root vowel is *e*, not silently substituted *æ/ae* from Erfurt. The correction does not change it to *a*. |
| *fristmearc* 'interval' | Pheifer p. 29, no. 547: Épinal *intercapido fristmearc*, Erfurt *intercapito fritmaerc*; apparatus Corpus 1108 *intercapido firstmaerc*. | Épinal glossary f. 6v, middle pair, immediately after *inter primores*. The second member reads *mearc*, with *ea*, not *merc*. |

These are early glossary witnesses conventionally discussed as Anglian/
Mercian evidence, not West Saxon endpoint spellings or three independent
samples of the same spoken variety. Seiler's direct source card
(`audits/seiler2019-direct-source-card.md`, especially the common
archetype and continental Erfurt transmission) is essential: independent
physical copies of *EE do not supply independent origins for the same
phonological spelling. Corpus is a related, enlarged recension. A
correction, copied archaism or revised spelling must be established
locally, not assumed merely to save a preferred reconstruction.

## Independent input evidence and predictions

The comparison is between the standard fronting--restoration account
(ST) and Versloot's restricted-fronting/late-breaking account (AF), in
the early Anglian setting for which he compares them. It is not a
comparison between CAPR's West Saxon transducer and an Anglian target.

| Witness | Independently supported stem/ending; remaining uncertainty | ST prediction | AF prediction | Discrimination |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *spadan* | Orel (2003), s.v. `*spađōn` (held PDF sheet 400), supplies the weak masculine stem; Pheifer p. 134 identifies the weak plural. Independently, Ringe (2017), pp. 312--313, reconstructs masculine n-stem nom.pl. `*-aniz`, acc.pl. `*-anunz`: applying these to this stem gives `*spadaniz` / `*spadanunz`, not an invented j-stem. But Ringe--Taylor (2014), pp. 153--154, 376 n.10, explicitly identifies remodelling in the comparable *guman* paradigm: OE acc.pl. *-an* is not the regular direct outcome of PGmc `*-anunz`. The Latin lemma is accusative plural, while the English spelling alone cannot distinguish syncretized nominative/accusative. Both ending histories supply a following back *a* at the relevant comparison. | Front stressed *a* then restore it: root *spad-*. | Block fronting in that environment: root *spad-*. | Both give the observed root. Independently reconstructing the suffixes does not prove which complete unlevelled prehistoric cell produced this glossary token. |
| *hebuc* | Kroonen (2013), p. 197, s.v. `*habuka-`, independently supplies a masculine a-stem with medial *u* and a-stem inflection, not an i-stem invented to yield *e*. The immediate historical back-vowel trigger is stem-internal *u*, independently of the final nominative *-az*. Kroonen flags its deeper origin as an uncertain Wanderwort; this does not establish a front-vowel suffix. | With restoration, root *hab-*; standard early fronting plus simple loss of restoration would leave *hæb-*, not by itself *heb-*. | Without fronting, root *hab-*. | Both simple accounts miss Épinal *e*. Erfurt *hae* must be preserved as distinct evidence. Later raising/second fronting or transmission is an additional hypothesis, not proof of either simple account. Pheifer §§46--47 discusses the complication; no reading is dismissed as an error here. |
| *fristmearc* | Kroonen (2013), p. 355, distinguishes `*markō-` f. 'boundary' from `*marka-` n. 'sign'. Ringe (2017), p. 312, independently reconstructs feminine ō-stem nom.sg. `*-ō`, acc.sg. `*-ǭ`; `*markō` is the nominative assumed by the published comparison, while the zero ending cannot itself exclude an accusative history. Neither supplies an i-trigger. The full prehistoric first member remains unestablished here. | In the categorical early-breaking plus Anglian-smoothing implementation under comparison, fronting and breaking followed by smoothing give root *mærk-*. | Restricted fronting plus later breaking gives *meark-*. | The observed *ea* distinguishes those packages, not SC046 alone. The target lies before *rk*, outside the ordinary restoration environments under either independently reconstructed ending. Delaying or restricting smoothing also changes ST's prediction without changing restoration. Erfurt/Corpus *maerc* make uniform manuscript or dialect predictions unsafe. |

The stem and suffix evidence is independent of the experiment's desired answer.
Where a unique complete selected cell is not independently recoverable, that gap
is explicit rather than filled from the observed output. In particular,
Ringe--Taylor's positive evidence for n-stem remodelling limits the
*spadan* inference; levelling is not merely invoked because an output
is inconvenient. None of these
three forms currently supplies an unambiguous exact-cell proof that
*dagum*-type *a* must have undergone restoration. *Hebuc* is useful
counterevidence to both simplified accounts, not an excuse to choose
one by accuracy.
