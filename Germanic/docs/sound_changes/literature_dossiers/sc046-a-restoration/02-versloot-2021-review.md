# Versloot 2021: the critical review, not the North Sea chapter

## Identity and direct reading

Arjen Versloot, 'The historical phonology of Old English: a critical
review', *Papers in Historical Phonology* 6 (2021), 68--106,
DOI 10.2218/pihph.6.2021.6690. Cite as `Versloot2021Review`.
The [intake record](../../../../../docs/references/versloot_2021_review/README.md)
records the publisher copy, hashes and pagination. Directly read for
SC046: pp. 85--90 (§2.1.1), 92--95 (§2.2.2), 95--97 (§2.2.3), and
97--99 (§2.3 and conclusions). These are not claims of checking every
runological or manuscript reference cited by Versloot.

## Adjudicative use

The source makes the standard *slēan* argument conditional on the
independently accepted derivation of the diphthong. It is legitimate to
retain the handbook account as the working West Saxon reconstruction
while recording that *slēan* does not alone logically force a fronted
antecedent for every *dagum*-type form. Where restoration and blocked
fronting predict the same attested vowel and endings, record that
equifinality rather than selecting the account by output score.

The 2026 article develops a distinct quantitative early-Anglian proposal;
its results and its explicit exclusion of West Saxon must be assessed
separately. CAPR does not adopt lexical diffusion or grammatical
conditioning from either author's broader theoretical vocabulary.
