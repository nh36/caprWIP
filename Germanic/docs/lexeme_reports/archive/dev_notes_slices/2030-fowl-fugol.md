## Development-note summary

`fugol` belongs to the handbook-level cluster of OE / NWGmc words that retain `u` where regular stressed `*u > o` before a following non-high vowel should have applied. The early DEV_NOTES survey keeps the literature split visible without pretending there is a rule to encode. Bülbring explicitly lists `fugol` among forms with OE `u` instead of expected `o`, but already concedes that the ordinary outcome is still `o` in most comparable environments; Luick rejects a labial-conditioned sound law because forms like `folc` and `folgian` show the same environment with regular lowering; Ringe–Taylor finally leave the set as unexplained exceptions rather than deriving them from a reliable analogical template [@Bulbring1902, §116, pp. 45-46; @Luick1914, §78 Anm. 3; @RingeTaylor2014, §2.3.1, pp. 32-33; @Campbell1959, §115]. For row 2030, that means the project may describe the exception and its scholarly framing, but should not promote the labial neighborhood into an FST rule.

A separate DEV_NOTES strand matters because it shows that not every odd-looking segment in `fugol` is problematic. Campbell's parasite-vowel discussion treats `fugol` as a normal West Saxon `-gol` outcome, and the project note accordingly treats `OEGLInsertion` as the regular source of the medial `o` after final `-gl` [@Campbell1959, §362]. The lexical problem is therefore narrower than the spelling might suggest: the irregularity lies in the root vowel `fu-`, not in the later `-gol` shape.

Project chronology then runs through one important false start. A late cluster note tried to rescue `fugol` by shifting the row to an oblique cell such as gen.sg. `*fúglis → fugles`, on the logic that a following high vowel would block u-lowering. A later rollback reverses that move after end-to-end FST checking: the same high vowel that blocks a-umlaut also triggers i-umlaut, so high-vowel cells produce fronted outputs like `**fygl-`, while low-vowel cells still give the regular lowered `fogol`. The lexeme therefore has no known PGmc/NWGmc paradigm cell that reaches attested `fugol` by regular sound change. Current row policy is the reverted one: keep `*fúglaz` as both `PROTO` and `PROTOFORM`, keep `fugol` as the counterpart, and document the item as a genuine exception rather than a hidden paradigm-cell success [@Stiles2012, §4.1.1.2; @Campbell1959, §115; @SieversBrunner1965, §230 Anm.].

Text or paraphrase:
The opening survey puts `fugol` inside the classic OE `u`-retention exception set. Bülbring's formulation is still worth carrying forward because it shows both the attraction and the limit of the labial-environment idea: OE has `u` where the main rule predicts `o`, especially "namentlich zwischen Labial und langem oder gedecktem l", yet "meist steht jedoch der Hauptregel gemäß o" in comparable words [@Bulbring1902, §116, pp. 45-46]. Luick answers that the environment cannot be made into a sound law because counterexamples such as `wolcen`, `folc`, `folgian`, and `bolt` also sit near labials or velars but show regular lowering [@Luick1914, §78 Anm. 3]. Ringe–Taylor accept the forms as genuine exceptions and state flatly, "We do not really know why *u failed to lower in these forms" [@RingeTaylor2014, §2.3.1, pp. 32-33]. For row 2030 this fragment establishes the baseline claim that regular development of `*fúglaz` points to `fogol`, while attested `fugol` must be carried as a documented exception rather than as a new rule [@Campbell1959, §115].

This is the authoritative lexeme-specific correction. After the late rescue plan was actually applied and probed through the compiled FST, DEV_NOTES records that the high-vowel rescue route fails for principled reasons: the following `*i` blocks a-umlaut only by simultaneously feeding i-umlaut. The revision states the trap in paradigm-wide form: cells with low vowels in the next syllable yield lowered outputs such as `**fogol`, while cells with high `*i/*ī/*j` yield fronted outputs such as `**fygl-` [@Stiles2012, §4.1.1.2]. It then rereads Campbell's exception list as evidence that the whole paradigm has been analogically reshaped to a surface `u` stem, not as a prompt to search for a hidden regular source [@Campbell1959, §115]. Brunner's remark that the attested instrumental `wulfe` has had i-umlaut levelled away confirms the same methodological point: invoking high-vowel oblique cells only multiplies analogies instead of removing them [@SieversBrunner1965, §230 Anm.; @SieversBrunner1965, §239]. For row 2030 the present consequence is explicit: there is no known lautgesetzlich PGmc/NWGmc cell behind attested `fugol`, so the row stays `*fúglaz → fugol` and remains `unexplained_unmodelled`.
