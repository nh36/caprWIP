## Development-note summary

Row 2169 does have a dedicated DEV_NOTES section, and that section is still the core authority for the row even though its implementation framing is partly stale. The section opens with the old transducer problem statement, “The FST produces `*sǣw` from `*saiwiz` instead of expected `sǣ`,” but immediately turns to the enduring linguistic claim: “Non-word-initial `*w` was regularly lost before fully unstressed `*i`,” citing Ringe & Taylor vol. 2 §6.7.1 [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:11844-11849]. DEV_NOTES then gives the exact row-specific chain that later work should preserve rather than re-derive from scratch: `PGmc *saiwiz 'sea' ... → *sāwi (after *ai > *ā monophthongization) → *sǣi (after i-umlaut from *-i) → sǣ (after *w loss before *i and final vowel apocope)` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:11851-11857]. Even where the section reflects an older bug state, it remains the project's most explicit row-level explanation of why OE `sǣ` requires the fuller i-stem input and a `w`-loss step.

The same section is also the main source for keeping `PROTO`, `PROTOFORM`, and OE target distinct. Kroonen is quoted under `*saiwi-` as an i-stem: `*saiwi- m. 'sea' ... OE sǣ m. 'id.'`, and the live TSV note compresses the same conclusion into row form [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:11881-11885; Germanic/data/germanic-aligned-final.tsv:927-927]. For replacement-note purposes, this means the row should not be flattened to a single undifferentiated proto label. The comparative headword currently kept in the TSV is `PROTO = *sái`, but the OE derivation being justified in DEV_NOTES is the fuller `PROTOFORM = *sáiwiz`, i.e. the form that still contains the medial `*w` and final `*-iz` needed for the cited chronology [Germanic/data/germanic-aligned-final.tsv:927-927; Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:11853-11857,11907-11908]. The OE target is then `sǣ`, not an oblique form, not a reconstructed spelling with retained glide, and not a special dialectal workaround [Germanic/data/germanic-aligned-final.tsv:927-927].

DEV_NOTES is citation-dense enough here that the replacement note should carry its source substance forward explicitly. Ringe & Taylor are quoted twice: first for the rule itself, “non-word-initial `*w` was regularly lost before fully unstressed `*i`,” and then with the row-specific trajectory, `PGmc *saiwiz 'sea' ... > *sāwi > *sǣi > OE sǣ` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:11861-11869]. Campbell is then quoted in directly row-relevant form: “OE forms often show loss of u [= w] before i, although yw is often analogically restored,” and specifically `sǣ sea < *sǣi < *saiui- (§406)` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:11870-11879]. Kroonen's entry adds the stem-class point that the source lexeme is an i-stem, not a bare monosyllabic headword [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:11881-11885]. Together these passages establish that the lexeme's difficulty in the project was not target selection but incomplete modeling of a known historical pathway.

The section also preserves the surrounding rule context that makes the row more than a one-off. DEV_NOTES lists other words for the same `*w`-loss environment, including `*aiwi` `'law'` > OE `ǣ`, and then states the FST implication in implementation terms: the grammar needs “a rule for loss of `*w` before unstressed `*i`,” applied after `*ai > *ā` monophthongization and before or together with the relevant umlaut/open-syllable processes [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:11889-11906]. For row 2169, the securely reusable takeaway is therefore concrete: once the row is fed with the fuller i-stem input `*sáiwiz`, the expected OE outcome is `sǣ` via the cited `*w`-loss pathway, while `*sǣw` belongs only to the older bug history [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:11846-11849,11895-11906].

What is no longer current is the section's closing implementation status. DEV_NOTES ends by saying `Changed PROTOFORM to *saiwiz` and `Currently marked as known issue pending *w-loss rule implementation` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:11907-11908].
