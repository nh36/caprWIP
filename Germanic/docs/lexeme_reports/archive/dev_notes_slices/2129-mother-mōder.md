## Development-note summary

Row 2129 currently keeps `PROTO` and `PROTOFORM` identical: both are the PGmc kinship noun `*mōdēr`, while the OE target represented by the row is `mōder` rather than an oblique form such as `mēder`. The packet, memo, and live TSV are consistent on that point, and the wider etymological references do not force a different PGmc headword: Orel gives `*mōdēr sb.f.` with OE `módor`, and Kroonen gives `*mōder- f. 'mother'` with OE `mōdor`; both preserve the same underlying PGmc lexeme even though the dictionary citation form in OE is normally `mōdor/modor` rather than exact row-target `mōder` (Germanic/data/germanic-aligned-final.tsv:772; Germanic/docs/lexeme_reports/packets/2129-mother-mōder.md:5-9, 17-42; Germanic/docs/lexeme_reports/research_memos/2129-mother-mōder.md:47-57; docs/references/orel_handbook_germanic_etymology.vision.txt:30850-30858; docs/references/kroonen_etymological_dictionary_pgmc.vision.txt:19541-19549).

The regular sound-law story inside the repo is also clear. The compact derivation trace and the `unstressed_e_o_before_r` analysis both give the same path: `*mōdēr -> *mōdǣr -> *mōdær -> mōder`, i.e. NWGmc lowering of unstressed long `*ē`, then OE shortening and merger to `e`. On purely derivational grounds the project's exact counterpart `mōder` is therefore coherent, and the memo is right to say that the problem here is representational rather than transducer failure (Germanic/docs/lexeme_reports/packets/2129-mother-mōder.md:17-42; Germanic/docs/analysis/unstressed_e_o_before_r.md:47-49; Germanic/docs/lexeme_reports/research_memos/2129-mother-mōder.md:68-90).

The philological complication is that the ordinary OE citation/headword tradition is not `mōder` but `mōdor/modor`, while oblique evidence preserves `mēder`. Campbell's r-stem paradigm gives nom./acc. `médor` and dat. `mēder`; Clark Hall has `mōdor (e²) f., ds. mēder`; Bright's Reader summarizes the paradigm as `modor, -ur, -er ... G. inodor (meder) ... D.I. ... meder`; Hogg likewise cites `modor 'mother'` among the OE r-declension kinship terms. Ringe-Taylor make the analogical point most explicitly: in kinship lexemes with back vowels in the first syllable, "the vowel of the suffixal syllable has generally been replaced by `u` or its later reflex," yielding early Mercian `modur` and early WS `modor ~ -ur`, whereas the regular oblique evidence with `mēder` still survives (docs/references/campbell_old_english_grammar.txt:16650-16670; docs/references/clark_hall_concise_anglo_saxon_dictionary.vision.txt:27738, 28662; docs/references/bright_anglo_saxon_reader.txt:1430-1435, 23218-23222; docs/references/hogg_vol1.txt:6615-6618; docs/references/ringe_taylor_linguistic_history_vol2.txt:21609-21618, 21640-21645).

The safest row-specific conclusion is therefore conservative and must stay explicit. No securely attachable, current, dedicated DEV_NOTES section survives for row 2129 alone; what survives is shared kinship-term DEV_NOTES context plus the later analysis note and the memo's audit of the row. Those materials support three distinct labels that should not be collapsed: (1) PGmc cognate-set headword `*mōdēr/*mōder-`; (2) project-selected regularized OE target `mōder`; and (3) ordinary attested/headword tradition `mōdor/modor`, with oblique `mēder` as the strongest inherited `-e-` evidence. If `mōder` is retained, it should be described as a normalized regular OE target, not as an unqualified ordinary manuscript citation form; late West Saxon `*mēddor` belongs to a different, later analogical/gemination layer and is diagnostic only (Germanic/docs/lexeme_reports/research_memos/2129-mother-mōder.md:11-18, 41-45, 59-67, 94-100; Germanic/docs/analysis/arestoration_r_l_research.md:93).

## Superseded or diagnostic material

The main diagnostic hazard is not a broken sound law but a collapsed philological distinction. DEV_NOTES and the handbook extracts repeatedly use citation-form `modor/mōdor` when discussing the OE r-declension, and those citations are real evidence for the transmitted lexical tradition (Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:33404-33410; docs/references/hogg_vol1.txt:6615-6618; docs/references/bright_anglo_saxon_reader.txt:1430-1435, 23218-23222). They are not, by themselves, evidence that the live row's exact counterpart `mōder` is the ordinary directly attested lemma.

The late-West-Saxon `*mēddor` material also has to stay fenced off. The repo-local `arestoration_r_l_research` file cites Campbell on late forms such as `*mēddor* mother`, explicitly as part of a later analogical/gemination phenomenon after inflected forms (Germanic/docs/analysis/arestoration_r_l_research.md:93). That is useful project history and a reminder that the paradigm kept changing, but it is not current row authority for 2129 and should not be allowed to blur the distinction between inherited regularized `mōder`, ordinary citation `mōdor/modor`, and later analogical `mēddor`.
