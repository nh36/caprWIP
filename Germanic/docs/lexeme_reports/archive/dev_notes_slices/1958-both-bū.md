## Development-note summary

The live row deliberately models the attested Old English neuter dual `bū`, not the whole paradigm and not the later Modern English continuation. The attestation table carried into DEV_NOTES gives masculine `bēġen`, feminine `bā`, and neuter `bā, bū`, with genitive `bēġra, bēġ(e)a`, dative `bǣm`, and compounds such as `bū tū`; that is the philological basis for choosing `bū` as the row target rather than treating it as a mere reconstruction [@SieversBrunner1965, §324 Anm. 2; @Campbell1959, §683; @Fulk2018, §10.1]. The decisive comparator split comes from Kroonen's account of the inherited paradigm: bare PGmc `*bai, *bōz, *bō` underlies the neuter form, while OE `bēġen` is at least partly analogical, with `-en` remodelled after `twēġen` [@Kroonen2013, p. 47]. DEV_NOTES preserves Fulk's caution that the umlaut in `bēġen` and `twēġen` "can only be the umlaut of ō" [@Fulk2018, §10.1]; that remains useful background for older `*bō-jen-` analyses, but it is not the live row policy.

For the current row, the regular comparator and the attested row target now coincide: `*bō → bū`. DEV_NOTES first recorded this as a real phonological gap, because the cascade was shortening the only vowel of a stressed monosyllable and producing short `bu` (DEV_NOTES:line-37300-37320). The handbook statement preserved there still matters and should be kept in quoted form: Campbell writes, `"A change of final accented ō to ū appears to have taken place ... OE cū, hū, tū, bū"` [@Campbell1959, §122]. What changed is project status, not the comparator: the later closure note says row 1958 now passes (DEV_NOTES:line-37749-37753), so the old `bu` mismatch is superseded project chronology rather than current analysis.

The slice also has to keep a second contrast explicit: Modern English `both` is not the direct continuation of this OE row. DEV_NOTES treats the OE target as the unextended neuter `*bō > bū`, but the English, Dutch, and German `both / beide` type belongs with the extended `*bai-þ-` series and Norse-reinforced Middle English history [@Kroonen2013, p. 47]. The superseded project detours are therefore twofold and must stay labelled as such: the discarded target `bā]] [[þā` was just Wiktionary extraction garbage (DEV_NOTES:line-37321-37358), and the earlier short `bu` output was a temporary FST bug (DEV_NOTES:line-37300-37320). Neither detour replaces the current row decision to keep `PROTO = PROTOFORM = *bō`, target attested neuter `bū`, and treat `bēġen` plus Modern English `both` as comparators rather than as the row's own outcome.

## Superseded or diagnostic material

Two abandoned strands need to stay separated. The older target `bā]] [[þā` is superseded because it was extraction noise, not because it lost a philological argument. The short-output discussion `*bō → bu` is superseded for the opposite reason: the philology behind `*bō → bū` was sound all along, but the FST temporarily failed to implement it. Older `*bōjenō` discussion belongs to a third bucket again: useful reconstruction background for attested `bēġen`, but not the row's chosen derivational path.
