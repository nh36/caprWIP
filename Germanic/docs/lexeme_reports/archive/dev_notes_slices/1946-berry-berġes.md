## Development-note summary

The live row is a deliberate paradigm-cell split, not a silent respelling of the lexeme. `PROTO` `*bázją` remains the cognate-set headword, but the row-level derivational input is gen.sg. `*bázjas`, and the selected OE target is likewise gen.sg. `berġes`. That distinction has to stay explicit because the citation/headword side is different: Campbell lists `berige` ‘berry’ among feminine nouns where West Germanic consonant doubling applies "except in the case of r" and where `-j-` is retained after `r` [@Campbell1959, §619; cf. §407]. For this slice the regular comparator is therefore `*bázjas -> berġes`; the attested/citation-form comparator in the handbook tradition is `berige/berġe`; and the older project detour was the attempt to make nom.sg. `*bázją` itself land on citation-form OE `berġe` or `bere`.

The current DEV_NOTES fragment worth preserving is the light-syllable ja-stem rule witness. It carries over the Ringe-Taylor formulation, `"Upon the loss of unstressed *a and *ą, preceding postconsonantal *j and *w became syllabic *i and *u respectively"`, and then cites `*bazją -> *bazi -> berġes` beside `*harjaz -> *hari -> here` and `*natją -> *nati -> net` [@RingeTaylor2014, §3.1.2, p. 46]. That fragment is still current for chronology, but it should not be over-read as if the row still solved berry through nominative `*bázją` alone. The live row instead depends on the oblique cell `*bázjas`, and the more exact comparator for the row note is Ringe-Taylor's `*harjaz`, gen.sg. `*harjas` > OE `here, herges` [@RingeTaylor2014, §6.8.2]. The reason the row note cites that section is methodological as well as phonological: OE oblique forms can preserve `-rj- > -rg-` without any need to invent hidden gemination after `r`.

That no-gemination point should remain explicit in later report work. Campbell's noun-class discussion says that consonant doubling appears after short syllables "except in the case of r" and gives `berige berry` as the example with `-j-` retained after `r` [@Campbell1959, §§407, 619]. Ringe-Taylor's surviving `here, herges` paradigm supplies the parallel oblique outcome inside Old English [@RingeTaylor2014, §6.8.2]. So the current project decision is narrower than the older diagnostics: keep `*bázją` as the lexeme-level proto, keep `berige/berġe` in mind as the citation-form side of the OE evidence, but use gen.sg. `*bázjas -> berġes` as the row's regular comparator because that is the cell the cascade matches without contradicting the non-geminating `*rj` evidence.

Older DEV_NOTES material is still useful, but only when labelled as project history. The February 2026 berry note treated the problem as a citation-form chronology bug: `*bázją -> PWGmc *bazi -> OE berġe`, with the claimed fix being to let OE weak-tail lowering happen before apocope. That passage remains worth preserving because it records the exact abandoned question and the now-missing session memo it relied on, but it is superseded once the row is understood as a gen.sg. solution rather than a nominative repair. The December 2025 rhotacism-debug note is even further from current policy: it traced `bazją` among forms still surfacing with `{*z}` and argued that the `EnglishZRhotacism` context was historically too narrow. That fragment still explains why berry once sat in the rhotacism bucket, but it is diagnostic history only, not the row's present explanation.

## Superseded or diagnostic material

The superseded material here is not random noise; it is the project's earlier answer to a different question. The abandoned question was, "How can citation-form `*bázją` be made to reach citation-form OE `berġe/bere`?" The answer now recorded in the TSV is gen.sg. `*bázjas -> berġes`.
