## Significant human-review issues

1. `1992 door / dor` — attested `dor` must remain distinct from parallel
   attested `duru`, which belongs to a separate Old English stem history.
2. `2003 fare / faran` — infinitive `faran` should remain distinct from
   present-tense `fære, færst, færð` and from weak `færan` 'to frighten'.
3. `2007 fell / fell` — the noun `fell` should remain distinct from verb
   `fellan`, and packet noise around `feoll` should stay out of final prose.
4. `2008 fern / fearn` — comparative `*farna- / *farnan` should stay distinct
   from the selected input `*fárnaz`, and `fearn` should remain primary unless
   later manuscript-specific work strengthens `fern`.
5. `2009 field / feld` — the historical `*felþu- ~ *feldu-` ambiguity should
   remain explanatory background rather than displacing the stable attested
   target `feld`.
6. `2022 fly / flēogan` — attested infinitive `flēogan` should remain distinct
   from related Anglian `flégan` and noun-like `fléoge / flége`.
7. `2028 forlorn / lēosan` — the simplex comparison form `lēosan` should stay
   clearly distinct from the prefixed attested family `forlēosan / forloren`,
   and later editorial review may still prefer the prefixed citation form.
8. `2038 gang / gang` — the noun `gang` should remain distinct from the separate
   verb `gangan`, and Bright's `gong (gang)` variation should remain subordinate
   to the noun entry.
9. `2041 give / ġiefan` — handbook `giefan` should remain distinct from
   normalized `ġiefan`, and the West Saxon target should stay separate from
   Mercian `for-geofan` and Northumbrian `geafa`.
10. `2043 gold / gold` — diagnostic `*gúldą` should stay subordinate to the
    selected input `*gúlθą`, and the ambiguity between inherited alternation and
    regular `*lþ > ld` should remain explicit.
