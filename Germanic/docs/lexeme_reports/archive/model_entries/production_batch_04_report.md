## Significant human-review issues

1. `2169 sea / sǣ` — the entry depends on keeping abbreviated comparative
   `*sái` distinct from the fuller i-stem input `*sáiwiz`, and the present
   `early_analogy` label may be broader than the actual derivational issue.
2. `2184 shove / sċēaf` — the selected singular preterite competes with an
   equally clean participial line, and the paradigm comparison remains manual.
3. `2189 sieve / sife` — the comparative headword `*síbaz` remains weaker than
   the i-/s-stem analysis used for the Old English noun, and the entry must stay
   clearly separate from the kinship noun `sibb`.
4. `2205 spare / sparian` — the entry depends on a careful separation between
   inherited class-III `*sparēną` and the selected class-II formation
   `*spárōjaną`, while mixed relic forms remain review-sensitive.
5. `2212 staff / stæf` — the comparative stem class remains genuinely unsettled,
   and later editing must preserve the distinction between singular `stæf` and
   plural `stafas`.
6. `2216 stem / stefn` — the comparative label `*stámnaz` and the OE-facing
   transponent `*stébnō` belong to different source traditions, and the entry
   may sit more securely with `stefn` 'voice, sound' than with the English
   headword `stem`.
7. `2227 strew / strīeġan` — the selected comparator is reconstructed West
   Saxon `strīeġan`, while the better-attested evidence is Anglian `strēgan`
   plus West Saxon class-II forms; the inherited/remodelled distinction must
   stay explicit.
8. `2235 swan / swanes` — the selected form is a genuine attested genitive
   singular, but the ordinary lexical headword `swan` is also regular and well
   supported.
9. `2242 ten / tēon` — the strongest direct simplex attestations are
   `tien/tīen`, `tēn`, `tēo`, and `tēa`, so exact `tēon` must remain labelled as
   a normalized comparison form; the `attested_variant` label may still need
   review.
10. `2252 thousand / þūsend` — the secure conclusion is chronological rather
    than fully etymological, since preserved `ū` excludes a simple surviving-`ī`
    umlaut path while the exact explanation of medial `e` remains less certain.
