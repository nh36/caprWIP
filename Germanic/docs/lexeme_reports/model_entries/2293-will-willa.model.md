### Reconstruction and comparative evidence

Kroonen separates noun `*weljan- 2` 'will, wish' from verb `*weljan- 1` 'to want', while Orel and Kluge represent the noun as `*weljōn/*weljOn` [@Kroonen2013; @Orel2003; @Kluge2002]. The selected derivational form `*wéljô` is the noun-side input used for this row.

### Old English evidence

Clark Hall lemmatizes noun `willa m.` separately from verb `willan` [@ClarkHall1960]. The row therefore targets the noun citation form, not the related verb.

### Development to Old English

From `*wéljô`, j-gemination yields a heavy stem, i-umlaut gives `will-`, and later shortening plus j-loss produce `willa`. The noun is therefore a regular weak masculine outcome.

### Lexical note

The target here is the noun `willa` 'will, wish'. Related verb `willan` belongs to a separate lexeme and should not be substituted for the noun row [@Kroonen2013; @ClarkHall1960].
