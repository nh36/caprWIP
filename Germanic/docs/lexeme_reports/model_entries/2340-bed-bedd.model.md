# bed — OE bedd

PROTO: *bádją
PROTOFORM: *bádjai
COUNTERPART: bedde
DERIVATION_CLASS: regular

### Reconstruction and comparative evidence

Kroonen reconstructs the light neuter stem [badja-]{.iv .recon lang=pgmc sort=badja role=comparison_form} ‘bed’, supported by Gothic [badi]{.iv lang=goth sort=badi role=comparison_form} ‘bed’ and Old High German [betti]{.iv lang=ohg sort=betti role=comparison_form} ‘bed’. Old Norse *beðr* is masculine and does not establish the inherited English gender [@Kroonen2013, p. 84].

The Munich gloss Clm 18550a, fol. 154v, l. 22, gives *p&i* (edited [peti]{.iv lang=ohg sort=peti role=comparison_form} ‘bed’) by Latin *stratum* (Steinmeyer and Sievers 1882, p. 221, l. 60). Frankish [bedi]{.iv lang=ohg sort=bedi role=comparison_form} ‘mattress’ glosses *culcitram* in the Paulus tradition (1878 edition, p. 144, l. 4, apparatus p). The Latin establishes the glossing context, not necessarily the vernacular case. The two witnesses support a regional singleton with a following vowel; they do not date nasal loss in the varieties ancestral to English.

### Old English evidence

The Paris Psalter has *tō his bedde* ‘to his bed’ and *eall his* [bedd]{.iv lang=oe sort=bedd role=comparison_form} ‘all his bed’ in Psalm 40.3. The former is dative and the latter is the object of *onwent*. *Beowulf* 1791 attests [beddes]{.iv lang=oe sort=beddes role=comparison_form} ‘of a bed’ with *nēosan*, and 1240 has [beddum]{.iv lang=oe sort=beddum role=comparison_form} ‘beds’ beside *bolstrum*. The isolated Épinal–Erfurt gloss *bedd* does not establish vernacular case [@Thorpe1835, p. 99; @Pheifer1974, no. 243].

### Development to Old English

Both histories select the dative [bádjai]{.recon} ‘to a bed’. Unstressed *ai* monophthongizes, the retained vowel protects *j* through gemination, and the double survives umlaut, shortening, and glide loss: \emph{*bádjai → bádjē → báddjē → bedde}. The inherited direct [bádją]{.recon} ‘bed’ gives A \emph{*bede} and B2 *bedd*. In A the attested direct double can come from protected forms or the direct plural; the availability of those donors does not establish transfer [@RingeTaylor2014, pp. 44–47; @HoggFulk2011, §2.23].
