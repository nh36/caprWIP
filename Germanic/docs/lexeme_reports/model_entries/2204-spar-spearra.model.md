### Reconstruction and comparative evidence

Kroonen and Orel place this noun in the beam or rafter set `*spar(r)an-`, with cognates such as Old Saxon and Old High German `sparro` [@Kroonen2013; @Orel2003]. The selected input `*spárrô` is the OE-facing nominal form used here for that same lexeme.

### Old English evidence

The noun is `spearra`. English gloss overlap also reaches the unrelated verb `sperran` 'to bar', which does not belong to this row.

### Development to Old English

From `*spárrô`, Anglo-Frisian brightening gives `*spærrô`, and OE breaking before geminate `rr` yields `*spearrô`, later `spearra`. The development is therefore regular for a breaking-conditioned noun of this type [@Luick1914].

### Form note

This entry concerns the noun `spearra` only. It should be kept separate from verb `sperran`, even though the Modern English glosses overlap [@Kroonen2013; @Orel2003].
