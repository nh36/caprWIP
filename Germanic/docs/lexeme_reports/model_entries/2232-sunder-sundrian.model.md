### Reconstruction and comparative evidence

Orel distinguishes three related formations: adverbial [sunþraz]{.recon} 'asunder' > [`sundor`]{.iv lang=oe sort=sundor role=comparison_form} 'apart, asunder', Class I verbal [sunþrjanan]{.recon} 'sunder' > [`syndrian`]{.iv lang=oe sort=syndrian role=comparison_form} 'separate, sunder', and Class II verbal [sunþrōjanan]{.recon} 'sunder' > `sundrian` 'sunder' [@Orel2003].

Kluge-Seebold aligns the cognate set with German [`sondern`]{.iv lang=german sort=sondern role=comparison_form} 'separate' and OE [`gesundrian`]{.iv lang=oe sort=gesundrian role=comparison_form} 'separate', so this entry belongs with the Class II verb, not the adverb [@KlugeSeebold2011].

### Old English evidence

Clark Hall and Bosworth-Toller list `sundrian` 'sunder' and `syndrian` 'sunder' separately from adverbial `sundor` 'asunder'; both also record the prefixed verbal family `ā-sundrian` 'sunder apart' [@ClarkHall1960, 296; @BosworthToller1898]. The target is therefore the weak verb `sundrian` 'sunder'.

### Development to Old English

From [súndrōjaną]{.recon} 'sunder', the Class II weak-verb suffix yields regular OE `-ian`, producing `sundrian` 'sunder'. Because this is the `*-ōjan-` verb and not the Class I `*-jan-` formation, the form represented here does not belong to the umlauted [`syndrian`]{.iv lang=oe sort=syndrian role=comparison_form} 'separate' branch.

### Form note

The earlier confusion was lexical, not phonological: [`sundor`]{.iv lang=oe sort=sundor role=comparison_form} 'apart' is the separate adverb, and [`syndrian`]{.iv lang=oe sort=syndrian role=comparison_form} 'separate' is a related but different verb. The verb treated here is the Class II verb `sundrian` [@Orel2003; @ClarkHall1960, 296].
