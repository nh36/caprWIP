The selected target is an attested inflected adjective, not the dictionary
headword *eorþcund* 'earthly'. Its documentary classification does not
assert an irregular sound change, a dialectal exception or analogical
levelling. The citation nominative and the selected weak neuter accusative

### Comparative formation and the limits of the citation

Orel gives the whole formation \emph{*erþa-kunđaz}, with Old English
*eorþ-cund* and Gothic *airþa-kunds* 'earthly'
[@Orel2003, p. 85]. This is comparative evidence for the compound, not
a printed reconstruction of its accented weak-neuter cell. CAPR's
\emph{θ} and \emph{ð} serialize the source's dental signs; its acute,
grave and selected ending are analytical additions, not diplomatic
transcription. The Gothic comparison supports a formation extending
beyond Old English, without independently proving the date or uninterrupted
inheritance of every Old English inflection.

Kroonen independently gives \emph{*kunda-} 'born', with Gothic
*-kunds*, Norse *-kundr* and Old English *-cund*, as the old
participle of the root 'be born'. His \emph{*erþō-} 'earth' supplies
the lexical first constituent, not the compound's linking vowel.
The separate \emph{*kunþa-} 'known', associated with 'know', is
not the same formation. The bounded consultation recovered component
evidence, not an authored whole earthly compound or a weak-neuter
ending [@Kroonen2013, pp. 118--119, 310--312].

The second constituent's subsequent suffixal behavior matters. Hogg
includes *-cund* among elements historically derived from compound
constituents, distinguishing older compound stress from later subordinate
stress in disyllabic formations or inflected forms
[@HoggGrammar2011, pp. 49--50, §2.89]. Neither a dictionary hyphen nor
the spelling *-cund* independently establishes prehistoric prominence.
No unsourced derivation from the verb 'know' is needed here.

### Three located passages, not a complete six-token concordance

Ciszek-Kiliszewska's published DOEC survey reports six occurrences of
*eorþcund*, against 761 of *godcund* 'divine', and includes inflections
in its wildcard searches. The lexeme-level table is not a six-item
concordance [@CiszekKiliszewska2015, pp. 489--490].
The bounded documentary record locates three distinct passages: Genesis
1627, An. Ox. 691 and R. Ben. 139, 27–28. It does not identify the
remaining occurrences or explain the discrepancy by counting unrelated
forms. Two dictionary quotations of the Benedict sentence represent one
occurrence, not two.

### The Genesis object and its weak neuter agreement

Bosworth–Toller quotes “Se rinc ageaf eorþ-cunde eád”, with locators
Cd. 79, Th. 98, 8 and Gen. 1627. Its separate *eád* 'wealth' entry
explicitly marks the noun as neuter. In context *eád* is the object of
*ageaf* 'gave up'; *eorþcunde* agrees with it as a weak neuter
accusative singular [@BosworthToller1898, s.vv. eorþ-cund, eád].

The passage has also been located in the OCR of Thorpe's 1832 edition
on the page numbered 98. That edition-OCR consultation supports the
sentence context and absence of a determiner. It is not a collation of
Junius 11, Thorpe's page image or Krapp's critical edition. Apparent
eth/thorn differences and hyphenation in OCR must not be described as
verified printed or manuscript spellings. The target *eorþcunde* is a
normalized scholarly representation of the dictionary-located form.
No exact manuscript date or narrower dialect is inferred from it.

### Weak inflection without a determiner

The absence of a demonstrative or possessive does not make the weak ending
an anomaly requiring repair. Campbell describes the freer use of weak
adjectives in poetry, and Fulk independently recognizes the poetic use
without the usual specifier. Their weak-adjective paradigms give neuter
nominative–accusative singular *-e*, including the ordinary comparison
*blinde* 'blind' [@Campbell1959, pp. 261, 272, §§638, 656;
@Fulk2018, pp. 216--217, §§9.7--9.8].

Applying those general grammatical statements to this sentence is the
report's syntactic inference. The adjective need not be reinterpreted as
a masculine accusative with an inexplicably lost *n*. Nor is weak
inflection here evidence for an otherwise unrecorded levelling event.

### The inherited weak-neuter ending

Fulk explicitly treats weak adjectives as n-stems. His n-stem discussion
and paradigms supply the relevant neuter singular ending, \emph{*-ōⁿ},
for the West Germanic and Norse comparison
[@Fulk2018, p. 168 n. 1, pp. 169--170, §§7.29--7.31].
The macron denotes a bimoric long vowel; superscript \emph{ⁿ} marks
nasalization, not a consonant to be added to the string.
CAPR therefore represents this ending as \emph{*-ǭ}, not trimoric
\emph{*-ôⁿ} or literal consonantal \emph{*-ōn}.

The ending belongs on the unaugmented compound stem. Fulk's neuter
*augō* 'eye' paradigm contrasts singular forms without the oblique
\emph{n} formative with plural forms containing it. The class name
“n-stem” is not permission to insert \emph{*-an-} before the selected
singular ending. CAPR consequently assembles
\emph{*erþa-kunđ-ōⁿ}, serialized \emph{*érθa-kùnðǭ}.
This whole weak adjective is a comparative inference from Fulk's
class statement and noun paradigm, not a whole word printed by him.
Campbell independently supports the Old English ending and syntax;
he has not been used as an independent check of that entire
Proto-Germanic assembly.

### Inheritance and the reported origin alternative

Fulk connects the neuter ending with inherited PIE
\emph{*-ōn < *-on-h₂}, citing Jasanoff. He also reports Boutkan's
alternative: analogical extension from the neuter plural
[@Fulk2018, p. 170, citing Jasanoff 2002, p. 33,
and Boutkan 1995b, pp. 285--286].
These are alternative accounts of the ending's prehistory, not different
readings of the OE-relevant macron. Neither cited work has been
independently consulted for this entry.

The report prefers Fulk's regular inherited account, without claiming that
successful execution decides between it and the reported analogy.
Fulk's separate discussion of a perplexing masculine trimoric origin
must not be transferred to this bimoric neuter cell. No
trimoric-to-bimoric repair or additional ending law is adopted.

### Medial prominence after an initial heavy foot

The adopted protection concerns phonological weight and domain, not
membership in *-cund* or the weak-adjective paradigm. In
*eorþ.cun.de*, the initial syllable is heavy and the following
nonfinal closed syllable can head a medial foot. Goering distinguishes
this configuration from an initial light syllable accommodated together
with a following heavy syllable in the initial foot
[@Goering2023, pp. 79--84, §§4.5.1.1--4.5.2].

McCully and Hogg's revised quantity-sensitive conditions likewise make
initial-foot accommodation essential, rather than stressing every medial
heavy-looking string [@McCullyHogg1990, pp. 323--333,
especially pp. 330--333]. These analyses motivate CAPR's bounded
projection; neither author prints a full accented derivation of earthly.
The exact syllabification and prosodic domain remain explicit modelling
assumptions, not a complete Old English cyclic stress grammar.

### World, rainbow and noncircular controls

*Weorold* 'world' has the contrasting initial light-plus-heavy
configuration. At the late-lowering checkpoint the inflected diagnostic
\emph{*weo.rul.des} contains one short diphthongal \emph{eo}
nucleus in its initial light syllable. The analytical accented
\emph{*éo} is neither two separately represented vowels nor a long
vowel. Initial L+H is accommodated together in the initial foot rather
than giving the second nucleus an independent medial head. By contrast,
*eorþ.cun.de* has initial H followed by nonfinal H, permitting a medial
head.
Its selected weak-vowel history therefore remains compatible with late
lowering [@Goering2023, pp. 79--82, §4.5.1.2].
*Reġnboga* 'rainbow' already has second-root \emph{o} from
[SC017 PNWGmcULowering](#rule-PNWGmcULowering); it cannot establish
that subordinate prominence disappeared before late lowering.
Neither adopted history is reopened.

Hogg warns that “tertiary stress” can merely rename a desired
retention. Ordinary *-un > -on* also prevents a blanket dental-nasal
exemption; labial-nasal preservation is a different environment
[@Hogg1996, pp. 8--11, especially p. 11 n. 7;
@HoggGrammar2011, pp. 239--241, §§6.55--6.58].
Dictionary-located *godcundne* 'divine' and *godcundre* 'divine'
are real retention comparisons, but do not by themselves establish
every ending's history. The earlier record's *godcundum* in
*in godcundum mægne* is dative singular in context; its syncretic
*-um* must not be counted as an independently demonstrated plural.
Final uninflected *godcund* remains a qualification of an ordinary
nonfinal-foot account [@Toller1921, s.v. god-cund].
Metrical prominence is not automatically attested speech stress
[@MinkovaZhou2023, pp. 86--90].

### The selected regular span and its executable representation

The supported chain is
\emph{*érθa-kùnðǭ > *érθa-kùndǭ > *érθkùndǭ >
*érθkundǭ > *éorθkundǭ > *éorθkundæ > *éorθkunde},
followed by *eorþcunde*. Dental hardening, linking-vowel deletion,
breaking and existing final shortening and merger account for the
changes other than medial protection. The selected bimoric nasal
ending is already within the input notation.

[SC037 OECompoundLinkingSyncope](#rule-OECompoundLinkingSyncope)
reads the earlier compound accent before
[SC038 OEStripSecondaryStress](#rule-OEStripSecondaryStress)
removes it at its inherited position. At
[SC040 OEMedUnstressedULowering](#rule-OEMedUnstressedULowering),
the independently computed medial prominence excludes this
\emph{u} from unstressed lowering; without that protection the
experimental comparison gives \emph{*eorþconde}.

The adopted calculation retains temporary grave marking for all five
short vowels \emph{a e i o u} in the computed heads and immediately
decodes the marks after lowering. It accommodates an existing pretonic
prefix in the sentence domain and permits consonants preceding the first
syllable boundary. Such consonants do not add a nucleus or change its
weight. No new initial boundary is inserted, and the computation is not
wrapped in the separate pretonic-first-syllable context used by the
lowering operation itself.

Lowering retains its canonical consonant context. Its only additional
preceding-vowel exclusion is temporarily headed \emph{ù}: a headed
\emph{u} must not license lowering of a subsequent \emph{u} merely
because its technical annotation changes. This restores the existing
\emph{u}-exclusion after marking rather than introducing a new historical
condition. Complete-relation checks establish a single-valued
calculation, while complete-corpus controls preserve the existing
derivations. Both the full weak-neuter and genitive probes succeed.
Those results support the selected regular span without establishing
an entire adjective paradigm or removing its documentary qualifications.

The temporary marks are not survival of the original grave, a second
historical deaccentuation, or a new chronology edge. The projection
addresses the short-\emph{u} lowering question; marking five short-vowel
classes does not supply a full stress representation for all nuclei,
boundaries, final-overheavy syllables or transparent compound domains.

### Other cells and the limits of adoption

The Oxford gloss has *eorþcundre* 'earthly', feminine genitive singular,
in *ealre eorþcundre gesceafte*, translating Latin
*totius terrestris (i. terrene) creaturae*
[@Toller1921, s.v. eorþ-cund, An. Ox. 691].
The exact edition, compiler and manuscript behind that locator have not
been independently established. Fulk's strong paradigm allows
\emph{*-(a)izōz}; the experimental
\emph{*eorþcundere > *eorþcundre} requires additional pre-\emph{r}
syncope research, not an adopted ending repair
[@Fulk2018, pp. 209--211; @Campbell1959, §§388--393;
@RingeTaylor2014, pp. 264--266].

The Benedict sentence has *eorþcundum* 'earthly' agreeing with
masculine dative plural *lustum* 'desires', governed by *filigaþ*
'follow'. The dictionary cites it at 139, 27 under *grædiglice* and
139, 28 under *sceamleáslice*. The underlying edition and manuscript
have not been independently checked
[@Toller1921, s.vv. grædiglice, sceamleáslice].
The conventional strong dative plural \emph{*-aimiz} currently gives
experimental \emph{*-em}, not the attested *-um*. Secure synchronic
inflection does not resolve that whole inherited derivation
[@Fulk2018, pp. 209--211].

The strong genitive \emph{*eorþcundes}, from the supported
\emph{*-as(a)} ending, is an experimentally regular but unattested
fallback, not a documentary substitute for the Genesis cell. Bounded
negative searches are not proof that it never existed.
*Eardcundes* 'native' has a different first constituent; *eorþcundlic*
'earthly' is a separately suffixed lexeme. Their dictionary grouping
does not make either an occurrence of the selected adjective.
The chosen weak-neuter span needs neither Oxford r-syncope, a dative
ending repair, final-overheavy protection, nor an additional analogy.
