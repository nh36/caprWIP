### Reconstruction and comparative evidence

Kroonen reconstructs a root noun with the inherited alternation
`*kō- ~ *ku-`, explicitly `nom. [kōz]{.recon} 'cow', obl. *kū-` [@Kroonen2013, 299].
The citation form therefore belongs to the nominative singular, whereas the
selected input [kūi]{.recon} ‘cow’ belongs to the oblique stem.

Ringe and Taylor also posit a later PNWGmc nominative [kūaz]{.recon} 'cow' > [kūz]{.recon} 'cow' behind Old
English [`cū`]{.iv lang=oe sort=cu role=comparison_form} 'cow' [@RingeTaylor2014, §3.1.3]. That nominative history accounts for
the headword, whereas the form cȳ 'cow' depends on the oblique `*kū-` stem and a
following `*i`.

### Old English evidence

Clark Hall lemmatizes the noun under [`cū`]{.iv lang=oe sort=cu role=comparison_form} 'cow' and records gen.sg. [`cū`]{.iv lang=oe sort=cu role=comparison_form} 'cow' (or [`cū(e)`]{.iv lang=oe sort=cue role=comparison_form}), [`cȳ`]{.iv lang=oe sort=cy role=comparison_form} 'cow', [`cūs`]{.iv lang=oe sort=cus role=comparison_form} 'cow (gen.)',
dat.sg. [`cȳ`]{.iv lang=oe sort=cy role=comparison_form} 'cow', nom.-acc.pl. [`cȳ`]{.iv lang=oe sort=cy role=comparison_form} 'cow', and dat.pl. `cūm` 'cow' [@ClarkHall1960, 67]. Ringe
and Taylor likewise state that the root-noun [`cū`]{.iv lang=oe sort=cu role=comparison_form} 'cow' exhibits dat.sg., nom.-acc.pl.
[`cȳ`]{.iv lang=oe sort=cy role=comparison_form} 'cow' < [cūi]{.recon} 'cow', dat.pl. `cūm < *cūm`, and apparently gen.sg. [`cā`]{.iv lang=oe sort=ca role=comparison_form} 'cow (gen.)' < [cūiz]{.recon} 'cow (gen.)'
[@RingeTaylor2014, §6.6.1].

The dictionary headword is therefore [`cū`]{.iv lang=oe sort=cu role=comparison_form} 'cow', but [`cȳ`]{.iv lang=oe sort=cy role=comparison_form} 'cow' is an established Old
English paradigm form rather than a convenient reconstruction. It serves as
dative singular and also as nominative-accusative plural. The genitive
singular is less stable, with [`cā`]{.iv lang=oe sort=ca role=comparison_form} 'cow (gen.sg.)', [`cȳ`]{.iv lang=oe sort=cy role=comparison_form} 'cow', `cū(e)`, and [`cūs`]{.iv lang=oe sort=cus role=comparison_form} 'cow (gen.sg.)' all appearing in
the local source record.

### Development to Old English

[kūi]{.recon} 'cow' is the dative singular of the oblique `*kū-` stem. The following `*i`
triggers i-umlaut, so `ū` becomes `ȳ`, and loss of the final high vowel leaves
[`cȳ`]{.iv lang=oe sort=cy role=comparison_form} 'cow'. The development is [kūi]{.recon} 'cow' > [kȳi]{.recon} 'cow' > [kȳ]{.recon} 'cow' > cȳ.

This is the regular oblique-cell path recognized by Ringe and Taylor's paradigm
statement [@RingeTaylor2014, §6.6.1]. It also explains why [`cȳ`]{.iv lang=oe sort=cy role=comparison_form} 'cow' is the cleanest
comparison form for the present entry, even though the ordinary headword is
[`cū`]{.iv lang=oe sort=cu role=comparison_form} 'cow'.
