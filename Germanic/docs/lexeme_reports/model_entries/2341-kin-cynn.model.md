# kin — OE cynn

PROTO: *kúnją
PROTOFORM: *kúnjas
COUNTERPART: cynnes
DERIVATION_CLASS: regular

### Reconstruction and comparative evidence

Kroonen reconstructs neuter [kunja-]{.iv .recon lang=pgmc sort=kunja role=comparison_form} ‘clan’, distinct from the homonym ‘omen’, from Gothic [kuni]{.iv lang=goth sort=kuni role=comparison_form} ‘family’, Old Norse [kyn]{.iv lang=on sort=kyn role=comparison_form} ‘kin’, Old Saxon [kunni]{.iv lang=os sort=kunni role=comparison_form} ‘kin’ and other cognates [@Kroonen2013, p. 351].

### Old English evidence

The selected genitive *cynnes* ‘of stock’ stands with the agreeing adjective in *Sirofenisces cynnes*, West Saxon Mark 7:26 (Bosworth–Toller, s.v. *hǣþen*); compare *Exodus* 228 and 436. *Exodus* 352 has dative [cynne]{.iv lang=oe sort=cynne role=comparison_form} ‘generation’ in *cynn æfter cynne*. Psalm 23.6 prints nominative [cyn]{.iv lang=oe sort=cyn role=comparison_form} ‘generation’; the Latin and singular demonstrative confirm the construction. That single-*n* spelling and the separate [cynn]{.iv lang=oe sort=cynn role=comparison_form} ‘kin’ spelling are not interchangeable evidence for historical quantity. The compound *moncynnæs* in the Moore Bede is corroboration, not a simplex token [@BosworthToller1898; @Thorpe1835, p. 51].

### Development to Old English

Both models select genitive [kúnjas]{.recon} ‘of kin’. The unchanged ending protects the glide while *n* doubles. Unstressed brightening, umlaut, the unstressed vowel merger and glide loss give \emph{*kúnjas → kúnnjas → kynnjæs → kynnes}, written *cynnes*. Direct [kúnją]{.recon} ‘kin’ instead gives A \emph{*cyne}. B2 gives \emph{*kunnją → kynni → kynn → kyn}: high-vowel loss precedes final simplification. A possible source for remodelling in A is the protected double; neither the compound *cyne-* nor the final written quantity dates the transfer [@RingeTaylor2014, pp. 44–47; @HoggFulk2011, §2.23].
