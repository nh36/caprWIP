# Source extraction ledger — hay / hīeġ

Hecht's edition of the Old English *Dialogues*, p. 36, ll. 2 and 13,
provides the direct mowing context; *heig/hig* and apparatus *heg*
are not the normalized spelling *hīeġ*. Bosworth–Toller Supplement,
s.v. *hīg*, also records *hig/heg* in Psalm 36.2 and *mid hig* at
*Colloquy* 20.1. The preposition identifies dative use; the spelling
does not witness an overt inherited dative ending. The variants do
not establish the quantity or the dialect of an otherwise unattested
donor form.

Ringe and Taylor (2014: 53, 173, 246) contrast the inherited direct
*hawi* and the protected geminated stem; p. 173 identifies the
source of Old High German *houwi* as the oblique stem, not one named
case in English. Campbell (1959: 46 §120.2; 231 §579(4)) gives the
labial development and invokes the other cases collectively. The
English evidence confirms the surviving stem, not an inherited
genitive or plural capable of replacing the selected direct cell.
