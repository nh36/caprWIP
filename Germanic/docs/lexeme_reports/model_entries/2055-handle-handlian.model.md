### The verb and its nominal relatives

The selected Old English infinitive [`handlian`]{.iv lang=oe sort=handlian role=target_form} 'handle, touch' glosses Latin *contrectare* in the glosses printed at *Anecdota Oxoniensia* 17.44. The dictionary also records finite *handlað* ‘handles’ and preterite plural *handledon* ‘handled’. The noun [`handle`]{.iv lang=oe sort=handle role=comparison_form} 'handle' is a different formation; it is not an alternate spelling or grammatical cell of the verb [@BosworthToller1898, s.vv. *handle*, *handlian*].

Orel reconstructs [xandlōjanan]{.recon} ‘handle’ and derives it from a nominal derivative of [xanduz]{.recon} ‘hand’. His comparisons include Old Norse [`hǫndla`]{.iv lang=on sort=hondla role=comparison_form} ‘handle’, Old Frisian *handelia* ‘handle’, and Old High German *hantalōn* ‘handle’. Ringe and Taylor instead reconstruct an internal *u* in the older verb and retain it at the West Germanic stage. Their West Germanic *handulōn* and later English *handulōjan* distinguish inheritance from English class-II remodelling. The Old Norse and Old High German comparisons support the family; the Old English infinitive alone cannot settle when the internal vowel disappeared or when the class-II extension arose [@Orel2003, p. 159; @RingeTaylor2014, pp. 131, 269].

### Selected derivation

CAPR uses \emph{*xándlōjaną} > *handlian* in compatibility and in both selectable models. This input follows Orel's compact verbal stem and represents the class-II ending from the start. In the models the weak suffix passes through an *e.i* sequence before SC083 deletes the first vowel, leaving *i* before the infinitival continuation. This is a representation of weak-suffix development, not evidence that the earlier West Germanic stem lacked *u*. The derivation is regular from the selected input; the earlier comparative reconstruction and possible morphological remodelling remain separate questions [@RingeTaylor2014, pp. 228, 269].
