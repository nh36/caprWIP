### Reconstruction and comparative evidence

Orel reconstructs the verb as [bakanan]{.iv .recon lang=pgmc sort=bakanan} 'bake' and cites Old English [`bacan`]{.iv lang=oe sort=bacan} 'bake' beside Old High German `backan, bahhan` [@Orel2003]. Campbell gives [`bacan`]{.iv lang=oe sort=bacan} as one of the standard examples of Old English A-restoration before a single consonant, and Ringe and Taylor state the same development from [bakan]{.iv .recon lang=pwgmc sort=bakan role=comparison_form} ‘bake’ to Old English [`bacan`]{.iv lang=oe sort=bacan} [@Campbell1959, 61; @RingeTaylor2014].

### Singleton and geminate present alternatives

The selected infinitive keeps singleton *k*. Kroonen explicitly
distinguishes \emph{*bakan-} beside \emph{*bakkan-}, considering
contamination with, or back-formation from, the iterative
\emph{*bak(k)ōn-}; a present *n*-suffix explanation is less likely
[@Kroonen2013, p. 49]. Orel prints singleton \emph{*bakanan}
and separately the long-*ō* preterite
[@Orel2003, p. 33]. The inspected passages do not establish
Orel's reason for excluding the geminate from this citation.
This is a qualified present-formation question, not proof of a
mutually exclusive reconstruction debate.

Later finite-cell front vowels and the inherited preterite grade
are not rival infinitive vowels. Ringe and Taylor distinguish
West Saxon infinitive *bacan* 'bake' from second singular
*becst* 'you bake', preserving the umlauted vowel in syncopated
forms while *a* spread elsewhere
[@RingeTaylor2014, pp. 232--233]. Regular infinitive production
does not resolve the earlier singleton/geminate distribution.

### Old English evidence

Bosworth-Toller and Clark Hall both record [`bacan`]{.iv lang=oe sort=bacan} 'bake' as the ordinary Old English verb [@BosworthToller1898, 72; @ClarkHall1960]. The target in this entry is therefore the attested infinitive headword itself, not a selected oblique or finite paradigm cell.

### Development to Old English

From [bákaną]{.iv .recon lang=pgmc sort=bakana} 'bake', Anglo-Frisian brightening first gives [bækaną]{.recon} 'bake'. A-restoration then returns the stem vowel to `a` before single `k` plus the back-vocalic infinitive suffix, and later apocope and weak-tail reduction yield [`bacan`]{.iv lang=oe sort=bacan} 'bake' [@Campbell1959, 61; @RingeTaylor2014]. The development is therefore straightforward: [bákaną]{.iv .recon lang=pgmc sort=bakana} > [`bacan`]{.iv lang=oe sort=bacan}.
