## Significant human-review issues

1. `1942 beech / bōc` — nominative `bōc` should remain clearly separated from
   related oblique `bēċe`, and from the homographic noun `bōc` 'book'.
2. `1949 bier / bǣr` — normalized `bǣr` should stay distinct from dictionary
   spellings `bær` and `bar`.
3. `1951 birth / byrd` — simplex `byrd` should remain distinct from prefixed
   `gebyrd/ġebyrd`, and older debugging forms such as `byrde` should stay out
   of final prose.
4. `1954 bone / bān` — comparative headwords `*baina-` / `*bainan` should stay
   distinct from the selected singular input `*báiną`, and oblique `bāne`
   should remain supporting paradigm evidence only.
5. `1961 bow / bīeġan` — the weak causative verb should stay distinct from the
   strong verb and noun elsewhere in the bow family.
6. `1969 breeches / brēċ` — the plural target `brēċ` should remain distinct from
   singular `brōc`.
7. `1975 calf / ċealf` — the normalized spelling `ċealf` should stay explicitly
   tied to attested dictionary `cealf`.
8. `1979 corn / corn` — lemma-style comparative forms such as `*kurna-` and
   `*kurnan` should remain distinct from the selected singular input `*kúrną`.
9. `1987 deed / dǣd` — the West Saxon target `dǣd` should stay distinct from
   Anglian `dēd`, and the stress-marked input `*dḗdiz` should remain visibly
   subordinate to the ordinary comparative citation form `*dēdiz`.
