### Reconstruction and comparative evidence

Kroonen cites the verb as `*wahs(j)an-`, Orel as `*waxsanan`, and Ringe and Taylor discuss the prehistory of Old English `weaxan` within the same verbal family [@Kroonen2013; @Orel2003; @RingeTaylor2014].

### Old English evidence

Clark Hall gives `weaxan` as the verb headword and separately records bare `wax` as a preterite form; Bright likewise treats `weaxan` as the infinitive [@ClarkHall1960; @BrightCassidyRingler1971].

### Development to Old English

From `*wáxsaną`, brightening and breaking yield `weax-`, and the infinitive develops regularly to `weaxan`. The cluster is preserved here, since `xs > s` belongs to forms where another consonant follows, such as `wæstm`, not to the infinitive itself [@Campbell1959; @SieversBrunner1965].

### Lexical note

The target here is the infinitive `weaxan`. Noun `weax` and preterite `wax/wēox` belong to different lexical or paradigm slots.
