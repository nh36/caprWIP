### Reconstruction and comparative evidence

Orel reconstructs the verb as [skawōjanan]{.iv .recon lang=pgmc sort=skawojanan role=source_protoform} ‘show’ and cites Old English [`sceáwian`]{.iv lang=oe sort=sceawian role=comparison_form} 'show' beside Old Frisian [`skawia`]{.iv lang=ofris sort=skawia role=comparison_form} 'show', Old Saxon [`skawōn`]{.iv lang=os sort=skawon role=comparison_form} 'show', and Old High German [`scouwōn`]{.iv lang=ohg sort=scouwon role=comparison_form} 'show' [@Orel2003, p. 337]. This is infinitive-family evidence. CAPR assembles the present third singular [skáwōθi]{.iv .recon lang=pgmc sort=skawothi role=selected_input} ‘show (3sg)’, a finite class-II cell with stable `a` in the 3sg ending [@RingeTaylor2014, p. 80].

Campbell states the class-II present endings as `lufas, -aþ (< -ōsi, -ōþi)` in his late-shortening discussion [@Campbell1959, p. 148]. Ringe and Taylor likewise note that class-II weak present 2sg. `-as(t)` and 3sg. `-aþ` have stable `a` [@RingeTaylor2014, p. 80]. The relevant comparison is therefore the 3sg cell itself, not an i-umlauted alternative.

### Old English evidence

The held fourth edition of Bright's reader contains the simplex infinitive [`scēawian`]{.iv lang=oe sort=sceawian role=comparison_form} 'show' and imperative [`scēawa`]{.iv lang=oe sort=sceawa role=comparison_form} 'show' in the Blickling homily [@Bright1917, p. 71]. The Wanderer contains prefixed `geondscēawað` 'surveys' [@Bright1917, p. 161]. This supports the finite ending pattern, not a separately quoted unprefixed third singular or a passage in the separate Cassidy/Ringler edition.

The form written here as [`sċēawaþ`]{.iv lang=oe sort=sceawath role=target_form} 'shows' is the normalized simplex comparison form for that weak class-II pattern. It is therefore not a dictionary headword but a finite comparison form aligned with the attested `scēaw-` evidence and the directly cited prefixed `geondscēawað` 'surveys' ending pattern.

### Development to Old English

Campbell includes [`scéawian`]{.iv lang=oe sort=sceawian role=comparison_form} 'show' among inherited-geminate developments through \emph{*auw}; this is a sound sequence, not a quoted reconstructed 3sg word [@Campbell1959, p. 45]. The dictionary singleton root and that handbook analysis remain distinct premises [@Orel2003, p. 337; @Kroonen2013, p. 442; @SieversBrunner1965, p. 49]. The transducer retains its long-diphthong proxy without claiming that output fit settles this root history.

The complete trace of [skáwōθi]{.iv .recon lang=pgmc sort=skawothi role=selected_input} ‘show (3sg)’ has already lost final `i` before that proxy: the actual checkpoint is `*s*k*á*w*ō*θ`. Thus absence of `j` alone is not the explanation of the compiled lack of mutation. Campbell's late-shortening account and Ringe and Taylor's stable-`a` discussion independently support `-aþ`, not `-eþ` [@Campbell1959, p. 148; @RingeTaylor2014, p. 80].
