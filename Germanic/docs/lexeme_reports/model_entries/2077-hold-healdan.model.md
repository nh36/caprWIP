### Reconstruction and comparative evidence

Campbell and Ringe-Taylor treat the verb as a regular [a + lC]{.recon} ‘hold’ breaking case, with West Saxon `healdan` 'hold' opposed to Anglian and Mercian `haldan` 'hold' [@Campbell1959; @RingeTaylor2014].

### Old English evidence

Bright gives the ordinary strong-verb citation form and principal parts `healdan, heold, heoldon, healden` [@BrightCassidyRingler1971]. The target is therefore the attested infinitive headword itself.

### Development to Old English

From [xáldaną]{.recon} ‘hold’, Anglo-Frisian brightening first yields a fronted vowel, and West Saxon breaking then produces `ea` before `ld`. Later reduction of the infinitive ending gives `healdan` 'hold' [@Campbell1959; @RingeTaylor2014].

### Dialect note

West Saxon `healdan` 'hold' is the selected target here. Anglian and Mercian `haldan` 'hold' are genuine non-West-Saxon doublets rather than corrections to that choice [@Campbell1959; @RingeTaylor2014].
