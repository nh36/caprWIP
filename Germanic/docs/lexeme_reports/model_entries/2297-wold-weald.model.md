### Reconstruction and comparative evidence

Kroonen reconstructs the noun as `*walþu-` and gives OE `weald` 'wold' beside other West Germanic `wald` 'wold' forms [@Kroonen2013]. The selected input [wálθuz]{.recon} ‘wold’ is the nominative singular used for the derivation.

### Old English evidence

Clark Hall makes `weald` 'wold' the main noun headword and cross-refers `wald` 'wold' and `wold` 'wold' to it [@ClarkHall1960]. The Anglian-looking `wald` therefore remains variant background rather than the main target.

### Development to Old English

`*lþ` voices to `ld`, Anglo-Frisian brightening yields `wæld-`, and breaking before the cluster gives `weald-`; apocope then yields `weald` 'wold'. The noun is therefore a regular breaking outcome.

### Dialect note

`wald` 'wold' survives as an Anglian-type variant in the same family. The selected target is normalized `weald` 'wold', not the variant form [@ClarkHall1960; @RingeTaylor2014].
