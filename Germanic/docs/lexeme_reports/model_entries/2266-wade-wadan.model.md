### Reconstruction and comparative evidence

Campbell and Ringe and Taylor describe A-restoration before a following back vowel, and Luick explicitly includes [`wadan`]{.iv lang=oe sort=wadan role=comparison_form} 'wade, go through' among the standard open-syllable examples [@Campbell1959; @RingeTaylor2014; @Luick1914, 239].

### Old English evidence

Clark Hall gives [`wadan`]{.iv lang=oe sort=wadan role=comparison_form} 'wade, go through' as the verb 'to go, move, stride, advance', and Bright lists the same infinitive in the strong-verb paradigm [@ClarkHall1960; @BrightCassidyRingler1971].

### Development to Old English

From [wádaną]{.recon} ‘wade’, Anglo-Frisian brightening first gives [wædaną]{.recon} ‘wade’. A-restoration before the back-vocalic infinitive ending then returns `a`, and later reduction yields [`wadan`]{.iv lang=oe sort=wadan role=comparison_form} 'wade, go through' [@Campbell1959; @RingeTaylor2014].

### Development note

The infinitive belongs to the A-restoration class. The citation form is therefore `wadan` 'wade', not a fronted `wæden` 'waded'-type output.
