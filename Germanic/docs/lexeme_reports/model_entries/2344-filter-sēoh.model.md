# filter — OE sēoh

PROTO: *sḯxwaną
PROTOFORM: *sḯx
COUNTERPART: sēoh
DERIVATION_CLASS: known_unmodelled

### The verb and its grades

This class I strong verb is distinct from the homophonous Old English infinitive *sēon* ‘see’. Seebold's comparative stem *seihw-a-* links Old High German *sīhan* and Old Frisian *sīa* with the English verb ‘strain, filter’, also used intransitively of oozing. Ringe and Taylor reconstruct a long root vowel and a voiceless labiovelar for the inherited infinitive \emph{*sīhʷaną}, in agreement with Orel's long vowel; Kroonen prints an unmarked *i* in his stem. CAPR follows the explicitly long account, \emph{*sḯxwaną}. The contrasting participial grade is historically voiced, as shown by *āsiwen* and *āseowen* ‘strained’. It does not have a voiceless dorsal followed by a labial and is not an exception to medial delabialization [@Seebold1970, pp. 389–390; @RingeTaylor2014, pp. 178–179, 344–345; @Orel2003, pp. 329–330; @Kroonen2013, pp. 435–436].

### The selected command

The singular command [`sēoh`]{.iv lang=oe sort=seoh role=target_form} 'strain!' occurs in *Sēoh þurh clāþ* ‘strain through cloth’ (*Leechdoms* II.24.1 and III.14.18, as cited by Bosworth and Toller). CAPR selects the endingless West Germanic imperative with long root vowel and final dorsal, \emph{*sḯx}. The earlier strong singular imperative and its ending history are not separately scored here. The selected input is after the disappearance of final labialization; it is not the Proto-Germanic infinitive with its suffix removed by the medial law. A final dorsal survives, as it does in the independently attested *þurhsēah* ‘strained through’ of *Bede* V.12, a past singular instead of an imperative. All three current orders produce *sīoh*. The retained dorsal is right; the remaining difference is the long broken vowel [@BosworthToller1898, s.v. *seón* ‘strain’; @RingeTaylor2014, pp. 48–49, 344; @Campbell1959, pp. 186–187 §464].

### The prefixed evidence

In *Christ* C 1086 (1088 in the numbering used by Bosworth and Toller), *biseon mid swate* ‘sprinkled with blood’ describes the cross in parallel with *blode bistemed*. Despite looking like an infinitive, it is used as a participle; the prefixed infinitive \emph{*bisīhwan} is a comparative projection, not the earlier cell of that token. The short prefix is independently supported by Germanic *bi* and Seebold's *bi-* comparisons. Cockayne's *āsiwen* and the genitive *āseownes* ‘of strained [honey]’ show an overt labial in another prefix and a distinct grade. Do not select an unprefixed stem to match a prefixed attestation or resolve the unusual *biseon* by assigning it to the ordinary voiced grade without further evidence [@BosworthToller1898, s.vv. *be-seón*, *seón*; @Seebold1970, pp. 389–390; @RingeTaylor2014, pp. 65, 344–345].
