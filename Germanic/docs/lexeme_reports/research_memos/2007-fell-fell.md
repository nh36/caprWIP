## Reconstruction and early-stage forms

Three different levels need to stay separate:

1. **Cognate-set proto / etymological headword:** Kroonen's dictionary lemma is `*fella-` 'skin, hide'. This is a comparative headword convention, not the exact row input.
2. **Project input form:** TSV `PROTO` and `PROTOFORM` are both `*féllą`, i.e. the specific accented form fed into the derivation pipeline.
3. **OE target form:** the row represents OE **fell**.

These are compatible, not competing, reconstructions. The project is not disagreeing with Kroonen; it is converting the comparative lemma-style headword `*fella-` into a derivable project input `*féllą`. For this row the sound history is straightforward: no special PWGmc/NWGmc reshaping is needed in the live trace, and OE heavy-syllable nasal apocope yields `*féll`, surfacing as **fell**.

## Old English philology

OE **fell** 'skin, hide' is attested in the repo's lexicographic sources; this is not a reconstructed-OE case. Clark Hall gives `fell I. n. 'fell, skin, hide'`, and Bright's glossary confirms both the headword and several inflected cells.

The philological distinction that matters is **citation form vs. inflected form**:
- the row's **COUNTERPART** `fell` is the lexeme/citation form represented by the dictionaries;
- Bright's forms `fel`, `fellum`, and `fell` show that ordinary inflection exists around that headword.

There is no repo-local evidence here for a dialect-only or manuscript-only restriction that would force a different OE target. Just as importantly, packet noise around **feoll** or **fellan** should not be allowed to blur three separate lexemes: noun **fell** 'skin, hide', verb **fellan** 'to fell', and preterite **feoll** 'fell'.

## Project problem and solution

The project problem is mainly one of **evidence hygiene and category distinction**, not phonological repair. The row itself is already regular: the live input `*féllą` derives the target **fell** without difficulty.

What needs protecting in the eventual report is the distinction between:
- Kroonen's cognate-set headword `*fella-`;
- the project's row input `*féllą`; and
- the attested OE noun **fell**.

The right project solution is therefore to keep the row as a regular derivation, treat the packet as a starting dossier only, and explicitly discard the packet's homograph false positives (`fell together`, **feoll**, **fellan**) as non-evidence for this noun.
