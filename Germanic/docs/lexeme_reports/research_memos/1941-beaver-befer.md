# beaver — OE befer

PROTO: *bébruz
PROTOFORM: *bébruz
COUNTERPART: befer
DERIVATION_CLASS: regular

### Reconstruction and competing interpretations

CAPR selects \emph{*bébruz}, in both its Proto-Germanic citation
and input fields, for Old English *befer* 'beaver'. Its accented
complete input is project notation, not a diplomatic quotation of
every compared author. The model reaches the selected target, but
that result does not settle the animal name's formation.

Orel's \emph{*bebruz} is originally a colour adjective, compared
with Sanskrit *babhrú-* 'reddish-brown' and the inherited animal
names [@Orel2003, pp. 40--41]. Kroonen explicitly reverses
the usual direction: productive Indo-European colour *u*-stem
adjectives can instead derive from the animal name. He proposes an
ablauting nominative \emph{*bhé-bhr}, genitive \emph{*bhi-bhr-ós}
paradigm, with Germanic \emph{*bebur-} continuing the nominative
through *r*-vocalization; Baltic *u*-stems are probably secondary
[@Kroonen2013, pp. 56--57].

These are opposed derivational directions for the same animal
formation, not a stem-dash or ending difference. Connecting
Kroonen's reversal to Orel's account is our comparison, not a
named author-to-author rebuttal. Orel's independent directional
grounds, comparative paradigm weighting and the relation between
\emph{*bebr-} and \emph{*bebur-} remain unresolved.
The selected regular result cannot decide them.

### The selected result and source endpoints

Fulk explicitly gives PGmc \emph{*bebruz} to OE *beofor* 'beaver'
as an example of back mutation [@Fulk2018, p. 69].
His quoted *beofor* is not silently respelt to CAPR's *befer*.
That later vowel process neither explains the dictionary formation
difference nor establishes an inherited *e*-to-*i* argument.
The complete selected derivation is retained alongside this
bounded formation discussion; no input, target or sound law is changed.
