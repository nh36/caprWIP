the paradigm offered no high-vowel escape cell and its suggested class

## Source-supported cells

Ringe (2017, pp. 242–243) explicitly reconstructs nominative `*wullō` and
accusative `*wullǭ`; pp. 171–173 and 241–244 explain their quantities and
inherited morphology. Orel (2003, p. 474) and Kroonen (2013, pp. 598–599)
are formation/comparative authorities, not attestations of an early English
`*woll-` accusative. CAPR's acute marks are its input-stress convention.

These are complete-cascade results, not conjectural strings passed off as
attested cells. The second column is the implemented working history.
The third establishes a genuine high/low alternation: Ringe and Taylor's
proposed nominative-u generalization (2014, p. 32) is plausible but
unproved, not contradicted by the feminine inflection. The generic
credibility of levelling has independent support (Ringe and Taylor 2014,
pp. 33–34; Stiles 2012, pp. 47–48); its occurrence and direction in this
particular early paradigm remain unverified.

## Decision and explanatory economy

Retain SC017 → SC018 → SC019, provisionally following Fulk (2018, p. 56).
The current sequence regularly accounts for the selected low roots in
nose2143, shovel2185 and sorrow2200 and for Fulk's `scolu`, without
additional oblique-o generalizations. This is a qualitative economy
assessment of source-backed paradigms, not a conclusion from 379 versus
377 matches. Nose's earlier stem remodelling is already part of its
selected history (Kroonen 2013, p. 383). Luick's inferred shovel doublet
supports possible alternation, not demonstrated later o-generalization
(1921, pp. 105–106, §78). The root of Fulk's `scolu` must be unremodeled
for that example to exclude earlier raising. The opposite order remains
expressly represented by Stiles (2012, p. 45).

Full comparative reasoning belongs in the SC019 reader and
`audits/sc017-sc019-adoption.md`; the lexical model cross-refers there
rather than reproducing the entire experimental discussion.
