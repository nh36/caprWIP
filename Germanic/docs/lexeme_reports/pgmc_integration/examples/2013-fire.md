# Fire: comparative ancestry, selected input and restored inflection

CAPR selects \emph{*fūri}, in both citation and input fields, for Old English
*fȳre* 'to a fire'. The model instead produces *fȳr*. The entry is classified
as a known but unmodelled development because its selected target contains
an inflectional *-e* not supplied by that path. This familiar pilot case
also exposes a more fundamental distinction: a workable input is not
necessarily an author's reconstructed ancestral cell.

Kroonen reconstructs the inherited heteroclitic opposition
\emph{*fōr} versus \emph{*fun-}, with a dative \emph{*fu(w)eni}.
He connects front-mutated reflexes to an *r*-remodelled dative
\emph{*fu(w)eri}, and distinguishes a metathesized \emph{*feuri}.
His preferred pre-Germanic nominative is \emph{*péh₂-ur}; he rejects
the \emph{*ph₂-uōr} proposal he reports from Simms because it would
produce a different Germanic result [@Kroonen2013, p. 151].
The reported and rejected proposal is not Kroonen's preferred reconstruction,
and the original Simms work has not been independently checked here.

Ringe instead gives a reaccented zero-grade collective preform, printed
with a final accent, \emph{*ph2uōŕ}, and separately discusses the
possibility of post-Proto-Germanic dissimilation in the oblique stem and
replacement of its *n* by *r* [@Ringe2017, pp. 147, 309].
The preforms therefore differ consequentially in formation and accent.
That is a real issue for publication, not a contest decided by which
preform can be adapted most conveniently to the current transducer.

Ringe and Taylor reconstruct an apparently disyllabic West Germanic
\emph{*fuir}, with nominative-accusative *r* levelled into the oblique
stem. They expressly doubt whether the cited endingless dative evidence
establishes an inherited \emph{*fuiri} [@RingeTaylor2014, p. 119].
For English *fȳr* they regard a development through a front-rounded
intermediate as plausible, while stating that the intervening stages cannot
be reconstructed with certainty [@RingeTaylor2014, p. 225].
This is not an unqualified endorsement of CAPR's long-*ū*,
single-vowel-plus-ending input \emph{*fūri}.

CAPR's selected form should accordingly be presented as its inherited-input
proxy, with its exact quantity, cell and historical legitimacy still requiring
independent assessment. The current metadata treats equal citation and input
as Proto-Germanic; that code convention is not new historical evidence.
The sources support the heteroclitic problem and front-vowel ancestry,
but none of the passages just cited certifies the selected complete word
in exactly that form.

The existing target distinction remains intelligible. Toller's supplement
attests both the noun and *fyre* obliques [@Toller1921, p. 276].
CAPR's current report interprets the target's *-e* as restored inflection
after the endingless outcome. That interpretation is kept distinct from
the comparative debate about the vowel and from proof of the selected
ancestral dative. No analogical sound law is silently added to produce it.

The model demonstrates its selected input's path to *fȳr*, not a derivation
of every attested inflection or a resolution of the inherited formation.
An extended report must therefore explain both the known target mismatch
and the source-backed ancestry alternatives. A separate scientific review,
not this provisional report, would be required before revising the input,
its stage or its computational history.
