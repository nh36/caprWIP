# Hue: a source-led input choice and a distinct glide history

For Old English *hīew* 'hue, appearance', CAPR now uses
\emph{*xíwją} as both its Proto-Germanic citation reconstruction and
its selected input. This is the previously adopted source-led correction,
not a new reconstruction proposed by the present editorial assessment.
Its importance is that the inherited vowel and subsequent glide history
must be justified independently of the final English spelling.

Orel gives \emph{*xewjan} and considers derivation from a related verb
probable [@Orel2003, pp. 171--172]. Kroonen gives \emph{*heuja-},
probably developed from \emph{*kēu-ió-} by Dybo's law, and relates the
formation to a 'bluish' adjective and the semantic field of a visible layer
[@Kroonen2013, p. 224]. These are genuine published formation accounts
with *e*, not errors to be silently normalized away. Their suffixes,
derivational premises and reconstructed endpoints also require comparison;
the visible vowel alone does not explain the difference.

Ringe and Taylor explicitly begin the hue history with Proto-Germanic
\emph{*hiwją}. They distinguish nongeminated and palatalized-geminate
stems and propose reanalysis through an offglide-bearing sequence whose
remaining fronted semivowel can still induce mutation. They describe the
development as surprising and leave its phonemic analysis unclear
[@RingeTaylor2014, p. 250]. That explicit word is different evidence
from Ringe's general argument for early raising before *i* or *j*, whose
Proto-Germanic date he regards as probable rather than certain
[@Ringe2017, pp. 151--153].

CAPR's adopted preference joins the explicit inherited-*i* reconstruction
to that independently argued raising account. It does not attribute a
complete hue word to Ringe merely because his general law can support
the vowel. Fulk's discussion of raising and his objections to traditional
geminate dismantling likewise supply arguments, not another complete
quoted hue reconstruction [@Fulk2018, pp. 57--59, 71--72, 126].

The model distinguishes the *j*-created reanalysis from earlier inherited
geminate-*w* development. It then realizes a long *īo* product before
mutation to *īe*, rather than borrowing the ordinary long-*ēo* realization
as a proxy for the entire history. Ringe and Taylor's retained palatalizing
semivowel is essential to that distinction [@RingeTaylor2014, p. 250].
Campbell independently separates the relevant *awj* and *iwj* developments
and their English outcomes [@Campbell1959, p. 46].

The result demonstrates the adopted input's complete modelled path to
*hīew*. Publication should preserve the substantive vowel and formation
alternatives, the qualified dating warrant and the unresolved phonemic
detail. None requires silently reversing the adopted correction; none
is resolved simply because the new and old inputs once produced the same
English spelling.
