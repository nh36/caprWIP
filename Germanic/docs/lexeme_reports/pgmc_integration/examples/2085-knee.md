# Knee: the selected cell is not the citation form

For Old English *cneowe* 'to a knee', CAPR retains the Proto-Germanic
citation \emph{*knéwą} but selects the dative input \emph{*knéwai}.
The distinction is essential: the regular history of an overtly inflected
stem is not the history of the endingless nominative, and a citation-to-input
difference need not be a disagreement between scholars.

Ringe and Taylor contrast endingless *cnēo* with short *cneow-* before
a syllabic ending, subsequently allowing levelling of *w* into endingless
forms and of long quantity into the inflected stem. They caution that
metrical attestations establishing the latter development are elusive
[@RingeTaylor2014, pp. 174, 187--188, 387]. Hogg explicitly distinguishes
short *cneowe*, the noun's dative, from long *cnēowe*, a form of the verb
'know'; confusing those homographs would manufacture a quantity problem
[@HoggGrammar2011, pp. 21--22, 86]. Campbell likewise distinguishes
short obliques from long endingless forms and secondary long obliques
[@Campbell1959, pp. 232--233].

The inherited stem and an independently argued dative ending supply the
components of CAPR's input. Fulk prefers a West Germanic dative descending
from Proto-Germanic \emph{*-ai}, but discusses competing ending analyses
and persistent uncertainties [@Fulk2018, p. 147]. The complete
\emph{*knéwai} is CAPR's paradigm construction from those components,
not a verbatim reconstructed word attributed to Fulk. His treatment of
knee as a neuter *wa*-stem supplies relevant paradigm evidence, not a
quotation of this complete dative [@Fulk2018, pp. 153--154].

The selected development uses short-vowel diphthongization before *w*
and retains short quantity in the inflected stem. Ringe and Taylor exclude
that diphthongization when the following glide is followed by a high front
vocalic; Luick's discussion likewise distinguishes the relevant
pre-ending environments [@RingeTaylor2014, pp. 187--188;
@Luick1914, p. 139]. These are phonological conditions, not a special
sound law for the noun 'knee'.

The entry belongs under late analogy and paradigm-cell selection because
the larger paradigm contains secondary levelling, not because an analogical
rewrite is needed to derive the selected short dative. CAPR demonstrates
the regular oblique path. The preferred ending remains a reasoned
reconstruction rather than a uniquely attested ancestral cell; the precise
extent of subsequent quantity levelling remains a separate evidential
question. An extended report is warranted by that paradigm argument,
not by falsely comparing the nominative citation and dative input as rivals.
