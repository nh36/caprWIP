# Strainer — seohhe

This weak feminine is derived from the ‘filter’ root, not from the source of *sife* ‘sieve’. Seebold registers a zero-grade feminine \emph{*sihw-ōn-} on the Old English evidence, separate from the full-grade Old Norse and Old High German noun comparisons. Ringe and Taylor reconstruct a stage with dorsal gemination by the following glide, West Germanic \emph{*sihhwā}; the root vowel remains short. Their proposed history passes through a broken-vowel stage with *hh*, followed by *seohhe*. The consonantal quantity, the labial, and the eventual quality of the diphthong are three separate problems [@Seebold1970, pp. 389–390; @RingeTaylor2014, pp. 48–50, 178].

The vocabulary gloss *Seohhe* for *colatorium* supplies a direct citation form, not a syntactic test of the case. In the remedy reported by Bosworth and Toller from *Anglia* IX, 264.18, *seohhan* is the object of *habban*, therefore accusative singular. It retains the same double dorsal with a different weak ending. There is no reason to infer a second independent etymological witness from that inflected form, or to count the derivative and its source verb as different roots [@WrightWuelcker1884, p. 24, l. 52; @BosworthToller1898, s.v. *seohhe*].

The West Germanic direct-cell input \emph{*síxxwā} is rejected by the current input gate. The early stem-based submissions \emph{*síxwōn} and \emph{*síxwǭ} yield *siohwe*, without the historically reconstructed dorsal gemination; accepting either one computationally does not decide the earliest full nominative or recover the missing geminate. A stage-local test from \emph{*síxxwā} verifies that removing the labial need not remove either dorsal position. It does not settle the age of this reduction, the exact equivalence with the singleton development, or the \emph{*io} to *eo* realization. Retain the derived noun as a provisional lexical object until its selected entry point can be supported and evaluated as a full word.
