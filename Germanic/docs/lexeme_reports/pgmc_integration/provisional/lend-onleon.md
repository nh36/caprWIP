# Lend — the simplex comparison and the prefixed Old English paradigm

Kroonen groups Gothic *leiƕan*, Old Saxon and Old High German *līhan* and the Old English loan verb under *lihwan-*. Comparative vowel quantity and the Gothic consonant support Proto-Germanic \emph{*līhʷaną}; \emph{*lḯxwaną} is CAPR's notation for the full simplex infinitive. Ringe and Taylor reconstruct a West Germanic sequence \emph{*līhwan} and then a broken form with a singleton dorsal, \emph{*līohan}. Dorsal loss and contraction are logically and historically distinct from delabialization [@Kroonen2013, s.v. *lihwan-*; @Seebold1970, pp. 327–328; @RingeTaylor2014, pp. 178–179, 344–345].

The ordinary Old English sources attest *on-* with this sense. In *Judith* 124, the lord of heaven *onleah* ‘granted’ her victory. This is the strong singular preterite, normalized *onlēah*, not an infinitive, and the final dorsal is retained. The length and broken vowel of the West Saxon preterite are part of the partial shift into the class II pattern, not the vowel history of the infinitive. The prefixed select present *onlīhþ* ‘lends’ in the Exeter *Maxims* retains the preconsonantal dorsal, and *onliġenre* in the glossaries is an inflected participle, not evidence that the present retained a voiced velar. Seebold's Old High German *giliwan* and Old Saxon *-liwan* support the historical voiced grade; the Old English participle has instead generalized the velar according to Ringe and Taylor. It is not a regular outcome of feeding the voiced grade into the proposed singleton rule [@BosworthToller1898, s.v. *on-león*; @WrightWuelcker1884II, p. 111, l. 31; @Seebold1970, pp. 327–328; @RingeTaylor2014, pp. 344–345].

The inherited source of *on* is *ana*; Seebold also cites High German prefixed formations. This supports a real comparative prefix, not license to delete it from an Old English witness. The exact prefix stage for a selected input still needs to be established. The three orders accepted the reconstructed simplex with *līohwan* before SC053 Postvelar labial loss was extended; with the extension they remove the labial and later the dorsal, yielding *līoan*, not the contracted *lēon*. Submissions with *an-* or *ana-* preserving the historical prefixed input are not accepted by the current gate. The source normalization *lēon* is useful for discussing the simplex development but does not establish a matching independent simplex token. Select a prefixed cell and verify its prefix, ablaut, and ending before treating a whole-word derivation or a negative firing as evidence. The observations above are not a scored admission [@RingeTaylor2014, pp. 205, 344–345].
