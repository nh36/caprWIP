## Development-note summary

The door note is about stem history rather than about a phonological exception. It starts from the mismatch between the earlier row target `duru` and the actual FST output `dor`, then argues that the discrepancy comes from conflating different reflex histories. Kroonen is used to separate them: `*dura-` is the a-stem neuter "gate, (single) door," while a different line leads to the feminine door-word, and DEV_NOTES copies the crucial wording that "OE `duru`, OFri. `dore`, OHG `tura` f. 'door', on the other hand, goes back to **`*durō-`**" [@Kroonen2013, p. 110]. On that basis `duru` becomes a later morphological development rather than the direct test target of the row.

Ringe-Taylor and Clark Hall make the later stage concrete. DEV_NOTES carries over Ringe-Taylor's statement that `duru` is one of the few surviving OE u-stems and that it was "originally a root-noun that had shifted into the u-stems" [@RingeTaylor2014, p. 385]. It also copies Clark Hall's dictionary evidence: "`duru` (dure) f. gs. `dure`, ds. and nap. `dura`, ... door, gate, wicket" [@ClarkHall1960, s.v. duru]. That material establishes that `duru` is real and well attested, but also already morphologically remodeled.

The decision in DEV_NOTES is therefore methodological. The note works through three policies: keep the etymological proto and retarget to `dor`; rewrite the row around a u-stem form that would yield `duru`; or split the row. It chooses `dor` because `*dúrą → dor` is the direct sound-law development, whereas using a u-stem input would privilege an analogically remodeled reflex over the etymological one. This row is therefore a model case for the difference between cognate-set headword, row-specific FST input, and later analogical OE citation form.
