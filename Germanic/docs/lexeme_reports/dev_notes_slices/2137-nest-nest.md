## Development-note summary

Row 2137 is stable in the live dataset, but the philological explanation behind that stability is unusually narrow. The current row models OE `nest` from repo-normalized `*nístą`, and the row is marked `regular`; however, both DEV_NOTES and the reference grammars treat the relevant front-vowel lowering as a lexically tiny phenomenon centered on `nest` and `wer`, not as a generally productive OE change [Germanic/data/germanic-aligned-final.tsv:804; Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:5164-5176; Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:6624-6630; docs/references/campbell_old_english_grammar.txt:3762-3765].

The strongest current authority is the dedicated nest note plus the later methodological restatement. DEV_NOTES surveys the main reconstructions and shows that the literature is split: Hogg writes Germanic `*nesta-` and treats it as the regular continuation of IE `*ni-sd-o-`; Kroonen, Orel, and Kluge-Seebold instead cite forms of `*nista-`/`*nistan`; Ringe-Taylor leave the vocalism openly uncertain as `*nistaz (*nestaz??)` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:5085-5176; docs/references/hogg_vol1.txt:2504-2508,2550-2553; docs/references/kroonen_etymological_dictionary_pgmc.vision.txt:20494-20500; docs/references/orel_handbook_germanic_etymology.vision.txt:32311-32317; docs/references/ringe_taylor_linguistic_history_vol2.txt:2724-2730; docs/references/kluge_seebold_etymologisches_woerterbuch.txt:65711-65715]. The slice therefore needs to preserve both facts at once: the live row-level modelling input is `*nístą`, but the comparative literature does **not** speak with one voice about whether the pre-OE vowel should be written with `i` or `e`.

The OE target itself is not in doubt. DEV_NOTES says flatly that `The OE form is unambiguous: nest (with e)` and that there is no attested OE `*nist`; the later summary reiterates that the early stressed/root-initial change is shown `only` by `nest` and `wer`; Campbell says the same; Clark Hall simply lists `nest I. n. 'nest'` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:5177-5185; Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:6622-6632; docs/references/campbell_old_english_grammar.txt:3762-3765; docs/references/clark_hall_concise_anglo_saxon_dictionary.vision.txt:29677-29679]. For row work, that is the secure target-side anchor.

What remains less secure is the explanatory mechanism. The main DEV_NOTES survey entertains several positions: an already-lowered Proto-Germanic/WGmc form, a limited `*i > *e` lowering before non-high vowels, or a more analogical account in which `nest` is one of the lexically preserved `e` outcomes. Fulk explicitly calls `nest` an "undeniable" example of lowering but also stresses that the results are "much less systematic" than `u > o`; the early expert summary in DEV_NOTES likewise says `*nest* and *wer* 'man' are the usual suspects` and that there are not many more candidates [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:5143-5160; Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:5237-5262; Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:165-169; docs/references/fulk_comparative_grammar_early_germanic.vision.txt:3741-3745,3792-3796]. For the slice, the safest formulation is therefore conservative: OE `nest` is secure; the presence of `e` is secure; the exact chronological/proto-level placement of that `e` is still discussed differently by the authorities.

Later DEV_NOTES material moves from source audit into model engineering. `nest` becomes one of the positive test items in the coronal-coda and later onset-velar-blocking implementations, and the results table explicitly records `*nistą ... nest ... ✓ Fixed`; but those blocks are shared rule-development history, not standalone proof that the literature endorses the final project-specific conditioning story [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:5363-5388; Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:5740-5782]. For row 2137, they are useful because they explain why the live cascade now accepts `*nístą > nest`; they should not be mistaken for the strongest philological authority, which remains the smaller set of grammar and dictionary citations above.
