# DEV_NOTES material — 2216 stem / stefn

## Current row state

- The live OE row is `2216`, `CONCEPT stem`, `COUNTERPART stefn`, `PROTOFORM *stébnō`, `PROTO *stámnaz`, `DERIVATION_CLASS early_analogy` [Germanic/data/germanic-aligned-final.tsv:1110-1110].
- The live TSV note already encodes the narrow current policy: `PROTOFORM *stébnō` is a **local pre-OE transponent** chosen because `*stebn- > stefn` works in the OE pipeline, while `stemn` is treated as a later West Saxon variant created by `fn > mn`; the note also says directly that the deeper Proto-Germanic reconstruction is still disputed [Germanic/data/germanic-aligned-final.tsv:1110-1110].
- The row therefore has to be read with the three levels kept separate. `PROTO *stámnaz` is the row's comparative headword label; `PROTOFORM *stébnō` is the OE-facing modelling input; `COUNTERPART stefn` is the conservative OE target. DEV_NOTES securely defends the `PROTOFORM > COUNTERPART` step, but does **not** claim that the same dossier settles the deeper comparative headword for all branches [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:3258-3280].
- The linked packet matches that restricted claim exactly: its compact trace runs `*stébnō > stefn`, not `*stámnaz > stefn`, and its note repeats that the transponent is local to the OE stage and that cross-branch reconstruction is deferred [Germanic/docs/lexeme_reports/packets/2216-stem-stefn.md:17-42].

## Detailed development-note summary

The surviving DEV_NOTES material is strong on one point and deliberately cautious on another. The strong point is OE-local: the project should feed the OE cascade with pre-OE `*stebn-` / citation-form `*stébnō`, because that input yields conservative OE `stefn` without adding new machinery. DEV_NOTES states the decision plainly: “The TSV uses pre-OE transponent `*stebnō` … Pipeline output: `stefn`. The OE target is `stefn`, not `stemn`” [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:3264-3264]. The same note immediately narrows the claim: `*stébnō` is “explicitly a transponent for the OE stage, not a claim to have solved Proto-Germanic” [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:3280-3280; @RingeTaylor2014, p. 330]. For row `2216`, that is the first fact a replacement working note has to preserve.

The row also needs the `PROTO` / `PROTOFORM` / `COUNTERPART` distinction stated explicitly rather than inferred. DEV_NOTES justifies `PROTOFORM *stébnō > COUNTERPART stefn`; it does not show that live `PROTO *stámnaz` has been comparatively reconciled with that OE-local modelling choice. The dossier itself says that “multiple PGmc starting points could yield the same pre-OE `*stebn-`” and that deciding among them “requires evidence from other daughter languages that the OE pipeline cannot provide” [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:3280-3280]. In other words, the row's current OE-side repair is intentional, but the comparative headword side remains deferred rather than closed [Germanic/data/germanic-aligned-final.tsv:1110-1110; Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:3258-3280].

The OE-internal chronology in DEV_NOTES is detailed enough to serve as the durable rationale for choosing `stefn` over `stemn`. The note preserves the attested chain `stebn > stefn > stemn`: earliest `stebn` in the Corpus Glossary, then early and dialectally widespread `stefn`, then specifically late West Saxon `stemn` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:3286-3294; @RingeTaylor2014, p. 330]. DEV_NOTES retains Bülbring's compact chronological formulation “stebn stefn stemn ‘Stimme’” and his West-Saxon-only rule statement “Im Ws. geht f vor n + Vokal in m über,” then carries over the dating note “erst in Alfreds Zeit” [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:3290-3292; @Bulbring1902, §§445, 485]. Luick's corroborating line “e vor jüngerem mn aus fn: emn ‘eben’, stemn ‘Stimme’” is kept for the same reason: it confirms that `stemn` is secondary and late, not the conservative row target [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:3294-3294; @Luick1914, §75 Anm. 1].

DEV_NOTES also preserves the best internal analogy for the consonant chronology: `efn / emn`. The note quotes Ringe-Taylor's `*ebnaz > efen ~ efn > emn` sequence and says explicitly that the chain `ebn > efn > emn` is structurally identical to `stebn > stefn > stemn` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:3296-3306; @RingeTaylor2014, p. 330]. That comparison matters for row `2216` because it explains why the project can accept `*stebn- > stefn` as a normal OE path while still treating `stemn` as secondary West-Saxon assimilation, not as the target form that the row should privilege.

The Middle English continuation is the other part of the OE-side argument that should not be lost. DEV_NOTES quotes Luick's “ME **stevne** ‘Stimme’ … beside ME **stem** ‘Stimme’,” then argues that `stevne` with `-v- < OE -f-` proves continuation of the `fn`-type rather than primary `mn` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:3310-3316; @Luick1914, §347]. For row `2216`, the practical consequence is simple: `stefn` is the conservative form that actually continues forward in the historical record, while `stemn` remains a later dialectal reshaping.

Where DEV_NOTES becomes cautious is the deeper comparative reconstruction. It explicitly sets out a three-way comparative split: Gothic `stibna` and OE `stebn/stefn` support a `*-bn-` type, Old Saxon `stemna` and some OE material support a `*-mn-` type, and OHG/OFri. can point to `*-mm-` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:3326-3345; @Kroonen2013, p. 480]. The literature survey is not presented as a solved consensus. Ringe-Taylor reconstruct `*stebnō` [@RingeTaylor2014, p. 330], Kroonen heads the entry under `*stimnō-` while discussing several ablaut/thematization possibilities [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:3353-3353; @Kroonen2013, pp. 480, 488], Orel gives dual `*stebnò ~ *stemnò` and concludes “Of unknown origin” [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:3355-3355; @Orel2003, p. 374], Fulk calls the etymologies of `stefn, stemn` “rather insecure” [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:3357-3357; @Fulk2018, §6.11 n.6], Kluge-Seebold prefer `*stemnō` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:3359-3359; @KlugeSeebold2011, s.v. Stimme], and Polomé explicitly reverses the directionality by treating `*-mn-` as older than `*-bn-` here [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:3361-3361; @Polome1967, p. 819]. The replacement note therefore has to preserve uncertainty rather than silently presenting `*stébnō` as the universally accepted comparative reconstruction.

For row `2216`, the working conclusion is accordingly narrow and conservative. The OE-facing part of the row is well supported: use local `PROTOFORM *stébnō`, derive `COUNTERPART stefn`, and treat `stemn` as a late West Saxon secondary form [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:3264-3322]. The comparative part is not equally secure: DEV_NOTES itself says the deeper Proto-Germanic question is deferred and should be revisited only when cross-branch transducers can test Gothic, OHG, and OS reflexes together [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:3401-3430]. Because the live row still carries `CONCEPT stem` and `PROTO *stámnaz`, later report writing should not pretend that the OE-local `stefn/stemn` dossier automatically resolves the row's full comparative identity. At present the dossier is best treated as decisive for the **local transponent and OE target**, but not yet as a clean final authority for indexing the row as a fully stabilized lexeme note.
