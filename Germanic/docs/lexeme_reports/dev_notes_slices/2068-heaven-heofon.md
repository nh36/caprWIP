## Development-note summary

The heaven note starts from a concrete failure: `*xemenăz` produced `hemen`, with neither back umlaut nor the final `-on` that the row needs. DEV_NOTES then assembles a source dossier that still governs the row. Kroonen is quoted for "`*hemina-` ~ `*hemna-` m. 'heaven'" [@Kroonen2013, p. 220]; Kluge-Seebold says that in OE and OS "das m dissimilatorisch zu v ... weiterentwickelt haben" [@KlugeSeebold2011, s.v. Himmel]; Campbell says "So heofon is for older hefzen (CH)" [@Campbell1959, §381] and also "In W-S u-umlaut is general before labials and liquids, e.g. heofon heaven, eofor boar" [@Campbell1959, §210.1]; and Ringe-Taylor give "northern WGmc `*hebun` 'sky, heaven' (OS heban) > WS, North. OE heofon, Merc. heofen" [@RingeTaylor2014, p. 324]. That combination already shows that the row is a problem of stem choice and chronology, not just a missing OE rule.

The middle stage of the DEV_NOTES analysis improves the row in steps. First it abandons `*xemenăz`, because a front-vowel `-en-` suffix cannot trigger the `eo` outcome. Then it tries `*xemunăz`, treating the form as a "virtual proto-form" with `-un-` because Campbell's `heofon` versus `hefen` contrast implies that the back-vowel oblique stem has been generalized [@Campbell1959, §210.1]. That is a real stage in the project's reasoning, but DEV_NOTES itself later treats it as incomplete because it writes `-un-` into the row without yet identifying what actual PGmc case form supplied it.

The later heaven work makes that paradigm-cell argument explicit and is the part that now matches the row. Kroonen's mn-stem analysis is quoted as "nom. `*hemô`, gen. `*hemnaz`, dat. `*hemeni`" [@Kroonen2013, p. 220], and Fulk is quoted on `*-on-` yielding `*-un-` before following `*u` [@Fulk2018, §7.31]. Ringe-Taylor's `*hebun` is then read as the generalized oblique stem [@RingeTaylor2014, p. 272]. DEV_NOTES accordingly moves from `*xemunăz` and bare `*xemon` to `*xemonų`, treating the form as the PGmc accusative singular or generalized oblique input. The same note also corrects the chronology of dissimilation and apocope: `m > β/f` is moved into NWGmc on Fulk's and Campbell's evidence [@Fulk2018, §6.14; @Campbell1959, §381], and Campbell's rule for final high-vowel loss after a long accented syllable is used to justify the trisyllabic apocope that finally yields `heofon` [@Campbell1959, §345]. The live row's `PROTOFORM = *xémonų` is the end product of that whole chain.

The first full heaven note carries over the essential philology in direct quotations. Kroonen gives "`*hemina-` ~ `*hemna-` m. 'heaven'" and DEV_NOTES glosses this as evidence for two stems [@Kroonen2013, p. 220]. Kluge-Seebold adds that OE and OS have developed the nasal by dissimilation, "das m dissimilatorisch zu v ... weiterentwickelt haben" [@KlugeSeebold2011, s.v. Himmel]. Campbell contributes two separate pieces: "So heofon is for older hefzen (CH)" [@Campbell1959, §381] and "In W-S u-umlaut is general before labials and liquids, e.g. heofon heaven, eofor boar" [@Campbell1959, §210.1]. Ringe-Taylor then give the strongest direct stem cue: "northern WGmc `*hebun` 'sky, heaven' (OS heban) > WS, North. OE heofon, Merc. heofen" [@RingeTaylor2014, p. 324]. On the FST side, this fragment records exactly why `*xemenăz` fails: the back-umlaut rule needs labial + `u`, but `*e*m*e*n` has no back vowel in the following syllable. That is why DEV_NOTES first turns toward an oblique-stem input with `-un-`.

Text or paraphrase:
This later heaven fragment is where the row becomes methodologically exact. Kroonen's mn-stem analysis is quoted as "it is probably more attractive to think that both stems split off from the oblique cases of a paradigm **nom. `*hemô`, gen. `*hemnaz`, dat. `*hemeni`**" [@Kroonen2013, p. 220]. Fulk is then quoted on the crucial vowel development: "`*-on-` should have yielded, in (N)WGmc., `*-un-` before `*u` in the next syllable" [@Fulk2018, §7.31]. Ringe-Taylor's "`*hebun` 'sky, heaven', gen. `*hebunas`" then becomes interpretable as a generalized oblique stem [@RingeTaylor2014, p. 272]. DEV_NOTES therefore replaces the merely virtual `*xemunăz` with `*xemonų`. The same fragment also tightens the chronology of dissimilation: Fulk says that in `mn` clusters "the first consonant tends to lose its nasality by dissimilation" [@Fulk2018, §6.14], Kluge-Seebold confirms `m > v` [@KlugeSeebold2011, s.v. Himmel], and Campbell's "older hefzen" shows that the change predates OE [@Campbell1959, §381]. Finally, Campbell's rule for high-vowel apocope is quoted directly: "`u and i... were lost in Prim. OE, in final unaccented syllables after a long accented syllable, or a short accented syllable and another syllable`" [@Campbell1959, §345]. DEV_NOTES applies that to the traced stage `*ç*e*o*β*u*n*ų` and records the successful derivation `xemonų → heofon`. This fragment is the closest direct match to the live row.

## Superseded or diagnostic material

The heaven note preserves three real intermediate row states: `*xemenăz`, then `*xemunăz`, then bare `*xemon`. Each solved part of the problem, and each should remain visible as project history. None of them, however, should now be read as the final row policy, because DEV_NOTES eventually makes the relevant oblique/mn-stem cell explicit as `*xémonų`.
