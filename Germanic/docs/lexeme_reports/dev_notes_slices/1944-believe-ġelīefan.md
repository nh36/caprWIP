## Development-note summary

The useful DEV_NOTES support for row 1944 is real, but it is **distributed across shared sound-change notes and a few exact heavy-verb inventory lines**, not concentrated in one long lexeme dossier. The replacement note therefore has to preserve several separate claims together: (i) the row belongs with heavy Class I weak verbs that should now be written with `*-ijăną` at the PGmc input level, (ii) the inherited `ga-` prefix must still pass through ordinary unstressed-prefix reduction to yield OE `ġe-`, and (iii) the root vowel pathway is the ordinary West Saxon `PGmc *au` + i/j-triggered umlaut development that gives `īe/ie` in verbs of the `biegan / hieran / liefan` type [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:8767-8884; Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:8915-8922; Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:8972-9004; Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:20663-20668; Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:35039-35048; Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:35481-35490].

The most row-specific current DEV_NOTES material is the March 2026 Sievers'-Law revision. Earlier project notes had surveyed heavy Class I weak verbs under post-leveling `-jăną` notation and explicitly listed `*galaubjăną ... ġelīefan`; that analysis was then superseded when the project decided to adopt **PGmc input notation** instead of normalized PWGmc notation. Under the updated policy, heavy stems keep `*-ijăną`, and row 1944 appears directly in the updated-form and source-attestation tables as `*galaubijăną / *galaubijana → ġelīefan` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:8719-8759; Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:8767-8799; Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:8917-8922; Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:8974-9004]. For the live row, that means the current `PROTOFORM = *galáubijaną` is not just compatible with DEV_NOTES; it is the direct outcome of the later row-family decision.

That same DEV_NOTES cluster matters because it explains **why** `*-ij-` is retained in the proto input even though later West Germanic / Old English reflexes no longer visibly show it. The note separates two phenomena: analogical leveling in indicative stem alternation versus the regular PWGmc syncope `*-CijV- > *-CjV-`. It states that heavy-stem infinitives keep `*-ija-` in PGmc notation, that the blocking `*i` prevents early gemination, and that a later syncope then removes the `i` before later palatalizing developments apply [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:8776-8884]. For row 1944 this is important project substance, not background trivia: `*galáubijaną` is the historically appropriate input precisely because the project now wants the grammar to carry the heavy-verb `*-ij-` stage explicitly.

The prefix side of the derivation is also explicitly preserved in DEV_NOTES. In a later audit of plain versus accented `{*a}` clauses, DEV_NOTES warns that the plain `{*a}` clauses are “load-bearing for non-root `{*a}` that must undergo the change — most importantly **AFB on prefix `ga-`**, which converts unstressed `{*a}` to `{*æ}` → `{*e}` to give OE `ġe-` in `ġelīefan`, `ġeloven`, etc.” [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:20663-20668]. For row 1944 this is the closest thing to a direct lexeme-specific rule memo: the prefixed shape is not decorative, and `ġe-` must be derived by the ordinary unstressed-prefix pathway rather than imported ad hoc.

The vowel-quality side of the derivation is supported by later literature-focused DEV_NOTES material on `PGmc *au` under i-umlaut. Campbell is quoted directly: “**éa the OE development of Prim. Gmc. au is subject to i-umlaut in all dialects**,” with West Saxon examples including `biegan`, `hieran`, and `liefan` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:35039-35048]. A later recap restates the same point more lexically, listing `liefan 'to allow' < *laubijan (with i-umlaut)` among the canonical West Saxon `ie` examples [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:35481-35490]. Row 1944 is not the exact unprefixed `liefan` example, but it belongs to that same inherited sound-change class; the only extra row-local issue is the prefix, and DEV_NOTES handles that separately via the `ga- > ġe-` note.

One smaller but still useful current fragment is the stem-shape clarification in the `*baug-` / `*straw-` discussion. DEV_NOTES explicitly treats `*galaubjăną` as a case where `au` is a **nucleus diphthong** followed by `bj` consonants, not as `a + wj` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:9116-9132]. Even though that exact spelling belongs to the superseded `-jăną` stage, the structural point remains relevant to row 1944: the project understands the verb as a heavy `galaub-` stem with inherited `au`, and that heavy-stem diagnosis is part of why the row was later rewritten to `*galaubijăną`.

Overall, DEV_NOTES support for row 1944 is therefore **substantial but shared**. It is enough to document a coherent regular derivation policy for `*galáubijaną → ġelīefan`, but it is not the kind of single-row controversy note that would by itself justify portraying the lexeme as a special exception case. The row should be written up as a regular prefixed West Saxon reflex whose replacement note preserves three pieces of project history: the superseded `-jăną` notation, the current `-ijăną` policy, and the explicit requirement that prefix `ga-` reduce to OE `ġe-` in this verb family [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:8719-8759; Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:8767-9044; Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:20663-20668].

## Relevant DEV_NOTES fragments

### Heavy Class I weak-verb notation: row 1944 moved from superseded `-jăną` to current `-ijăną`

- Source heading / locus: Sievers'-Law notes and March 2026 decision update
- Status: current, with an explicitly superseded prehistory
- Recommended use: cite when justifying why the live row keeps `*galáubijaną` rather than older `*galaubjăną`

DEV_NOTES first preserved row 1944 in the now-superseded survey of heavy Class I weak verbs under post-leveling notation: `| *galaubjăną | CVCC (heavy) | -jăną | ġelīefan |` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:8719-8725]. That note is immediately overtaken by the decision update: “After further consultation, we are adopting **PGmc** (Proto-Germanic) input notation, NOT PWGmc. This means: **Heavy-stem Class I weak verbs need `*-ijăną`, not `*-jăną`**” [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:8767-8773]. The updated-form table then names the row directly — `*galaubjăną` → `*galaubijăną` — and the source-attestation table follows with `*galaubijăną | *galaubijana | ġelīefan | p.245: "PGmc *galaubijana 'to believe'"` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:8917-8922; Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:8974-9004]. For replacement-note purposes, this is the clearest surviving row-local DEV_NOTES substance.

### Why `*-ij-` belongs in the proto input even though it does not survive transparently

- Source heading / locus: technical research and implementation note inside the same decision update
- Status: current
- Recommended use: cite when explaining the project's phonological chronology rather than merely its spelling convention

The same decision-update note explains that the crucial sound change is not analogical erasure of the heavy-verb infinitive suffix, but later syncope: “the sequence `*-CijV-` was syncopated to `*-CjV-`” [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:8795-8805]. DEV_NOTES then lays the chronology out explicitly: PGmc heavy stems begin with `*-ij-`; early j-gemination is blocked because the `i` intervenes; later PWGmc syncope yields `*-CjV-`; only after that do later palatalizing developments apply [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:8807-8884]. This shared note bears directly on row 1944 because `*galáubijaną` is exactly one of the heavy verbs whose current row spelling presupposes that chronology.

### Prefix derivation: ordinary `ga- > ġe-` behavior is explicitly tied to `ġelīefan`

- Source heading / locus: audit of plain and accented vowel clauses
- Status: current
- Recommended use: cite when explaining why the row is specifically `ġelīefan`, not merely `liefan`

This is the most directly row-specific sentence in the live DEV_NOTES file. The audit warns that plain `{*a}` clauses cannot simply be removed because they are “load-bearing for non-root `{*a}` that must undergo the change — most importantly **AFB on prefix `ga-`**, which converts unstressed `{*a}` to `{*æ}` → `{*e}` to give OE `ġe-` in `ġelīefan`, `ġeloven`, etc.” [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:20663-20668]. That statement should be carried forward almost verbatim in any future report because it preserves the project's explicit claim about how the prefixed verb is meant to derive.

### Shared `PGmc *au` + i-umlaut material: `liefan` and related verbs as the comparandum

- Source heading / locus: literature review on OE `ie` outcomes from `au` under i-umlaut
- Status: current
- Recommended use: cite when explaining the `īe/ie` vocalism of the root

Campbell is quoted directly in DEV_NOTES: “**éa the OE development of Prim. Gmc. au is subject to i-umlaut in all dialects**, e.g. W-S **biécnan** ... **hieran** hear, **liefan** allow ...” [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:35039-35048]. A later recap turns the claim into a lexeme list: `liefan 'to allow' < *laubijan (with i-umlaut)` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:35481-35490]. Row 1944 is not identical to that unprefixed comparator, but the root behavior is the same inherited pattern: `laub- / galaub-` belongs with the regular West Saxon `ie/īe` outcomes of `PGmc *au` in i/j-triggered environments.

### Stem-shape clarification: `galaub-` is treated as an `au` diphthong stem, not `a + w`

- Source heading / locus: `*baug-` vs `*straw-` stem-structure note
- Status: current in substance, though the exact row spelling there is the earlier `-jăną` form
- Recommended use: optional supporting citation where stem weight or diphthong structure matters

DEV_NOTES distinguishes `au` diphthongs from `aw` sequences and verifies that the TSV uses `au` in the relevant class: `*baugjăną`, `*galaubjăną`, `*xlaupaną`; the verification line reads `*galaubjăną ✓ (au diphthong + bj consonants)` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:9116-9132]. This is not the main authority for the current row spelling, but it is useful supporting evidence that the project has consistently understood the root as heavy `galaub-`, which underlies both the `*-ijăną` decision and the later OE vocalism.
