## Development-note summary

This row has securely attachable current DEV_NOTES authority, but that authority is **not** the first `habban` note that appears in `DEV_NOTES.md`. The earlier March 2026 note diagnosed `*xabēną -> hafon` versus expected `habban` as a TSV proto problem and argued that the OE-facing infinitive should instead use `*xabjăną`, because geminate `bb` requires a `*-j-` stem and the regular FST outcome there is `hebban`, not `habban` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:11546-11800]. That reasoning remains important as project history and as the basis for calling the infinitive analogical, but it was later superseded at row level by the explicit decision `Use 3sg present indicative *xabēθi -> hæfeþ instead of infinitive` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:11803-11842]. The live TSV row and the memo follow that later decision exactly: `PROTO` remains the comparative headword `*xabēną`, `PROTOFORM` becomes the selected 3sg cell `*xábēθi`, and the OE target is the normalized unsyncopated 3sg `hæfeþ`, not the citation infinitive `habban` [Germanic/data/germanic-aligned-final.tsv:497; Germanic/docs/lexeme_reports/research_memos/2058-have-hæfeþ.md:58-66,92-99].

The class-III weak-verb paradigm split is the main philological reason the row works this way. DEV_NOTES preserves Ringe-Taylor's table showing two different present stems for `have`, `say`, and `live`: the infinitive and plural belong to the `*-ja-` stem (`habban`, `habbaþ`, with gemination), while the 2sg/3sg indicative belong to the `*-ai- (> *-ē-)` stem (`hæfeþ`, `segeþ`, `lifed`) with no gemination [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:4091-4105]. The memo keeps the same three-way distinction explicit: `*xabēną` is the cognate-set proto / lexeme headword, `*xábēθi` is the row's derivational input, and `hæfeþ` is the OE target form represented by the row [Germanic/docs/lexeme_reports/research_memos/2058-have-hæfeþ.md:58-66]. Later work therefore needs to keep PROTO vs. PROTOFORM vs. OE target separate: the row is not claiming that the dictionary lemma `habban` disappeared, only that the selected paradigm cell can be modeled lautgesetzlich while the ordinary infinitive cannot [Germanic/docs/lexeme_reports/research_memos/2058-have-hæfeþ.md:68-99].

The current row-specific DEV_NOTES decision relies on two kinds of evidence that should stay visible together. First, DEV_NOTES tests the 3sg pathway directly and records successful probes `hafēþi → hæfeþ` and `habēþi → hæfeþ`, then concludes that `The 3sg present pathway is lautgesetzlich` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:4326-4352]. Second, the later `habban → hæfeþ (3sg)` note carries over Campbell's direct attestation background: `As in other types, uncontracted forms of the 2nd and 3rd sg. pres. indic. sometimes occur in W-S, e.g. segep, hygep`, and `In W-S prose 2nd and 3rd sg. pres. indic. hafast, hafað occur occasionally` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:11807-11821]. DEV_NOTES then uses those quotations to justify the normalized unsyncopated class-III target `hæfeþ` beside more familiar syncopated `hæfþ`, and the memo restates the same point: `hæfeþ` should be treated as a normalized OE 3sg present form backed by class-III evidence, not as the lexeme's citation headword [Germanic/docs/lexeme_reports/research_memos/2058-have-hæfeþ.md:70-83].

The superseded infinitive note still matters, but only in a controlled way. It preserves the key Fulk quotation that `umlaut has been... leveled out of that of habban, in the latter case perhaps to differentiate the present paradigm from that of hebban 'raise'`, and it records the regular comparator `*xabjăną -> hebban` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:11652-11784]. That remains the best compact explanation for why `habban` and `habbaþ` belong on the analogically leveled `*-j-` side of the paradigm, while the current row intentionally avoids that side by targeting the non-geminating 3sg cell. What must not be lost is the chronology: the earlier note was not simply wrong about infinitival phonology; it became superseded only because the project later decided to represent the lexeme with a different, regular paradigm cell [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:11780-11842; Germanic/docs/lexeme_reports/research_memos/2058-have-hæfeþ.md:85-99,113-126].
