## Development-note summary

The live row deliberately separates cognate-set headword from row-specific derivation. Kroonen reconstructs a root noun with nominative `*kōz` but oblique `*kū-`, continuing an inherited ablaut paradigm [@Kroonen2013, p. 299]. The row therefore keeps `PROTO` `*kōz` as the etymological headword while using dative-singular `PROTOFORM` `*kūi` for the actual OE test cell. Ringe-Taylor's paradigm statement is the decisive comparator: `cū` "likewise exhibits dat. sg., nom.-acc. pl. cȳ < *cūi" [@RingeTaylor2014, §6.6.1]. For this slice the regular comparator is therefore `*kūi → cȳ`; the better-known OE headword `cū` is an attested nominative outcome shaped by later leveling of the zero-grade stem, not the row's live Lautgesetz target.

DEV_NOTES preserves cow mainly as a methodological precedent rather than as a long stand-alone case note. In the ræst repair it is named explicitly as `cow (*kūi → cȳ, dat.sg.)`, alongside fire, night, and hammer, to justify encoding an oblique paradigm cell when that cell is the one that reaches the attested OE form by regular sound change. The later correction sharpens the point: the TSV may record an oblique cell "explaining the attested OE root vowel through regular sound change rather than analogical leveling." That is the current project decision for row 1980 as well. The row's `late_analogy` label belongs to the nominative/headword history behind `cū`, whereas the dative target `cȳ` is treated as the regular comparator [@RingeTaylor2014, §6.6.1; @Kroonen2013, p. 299].

A separate DEV_NOTES detour is still worth preserving as superseded chronology. Before the row was rewritten to the dative, the project explored a nominative-style comparator `*kō → cū` and treated cow as another example of the stressed-monosyllable `ō > ū` problem. That discussion remains philologically useful because DEV_NOTES carries direct handbook quotations that still matter for the history of nominative `cū`: Campbell writes, `"§ 122. A change of final accented ō to ū appears to have taken place ... OE cū 'cow'"` [@Campbell1959, §122], and Fulk adds that `"final stressed *-wō yields -ū"` and that `"The same change probably results in OE cū"` [@Fulk2018, §4.11]. But that material must stay subordinate in the slice. It explains the superseded nominative comparator `*kōz/*kwō- → cū`; it does not replace the live row policy `*kūi → cȳ`.

## Superseded or diagnostic material

What must not be blurred here is the difference between a true but secondary nominative history and the live row comparator. The older `*kō → cū` / Campbell-§122 discussion is not nonsense; it is simply no longer the chosen row policy. The slice should therefore preserve it as superseded project history while keeping the current row centered on dative `*kūi → cȳ`.
