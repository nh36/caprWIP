The row-level comparator is plural `*brōkiz`, not the verbal ancestor `*brekăną`, and the row target is plural `brēċ`. Ringe and Taylor give this plural pathway directly: `PNWGmce *brokiz 'leggings' ... > *breeci > OE bréc`, which matches the live row's `regular` classification and the current derivational trace [@RingeTaylor2014, pp. 208, 227]. For this slice, the regular comparator is therefore explicit: `*brōkiz -> brēċ`.

Later report prose should therefore keep the contrast visible: attested row-target plural `brēċ`, with singular `brōc` as lexicographic background rather than as the row's actual counterpart.

Kroonen's etymology does connect the noun family with the break/bruising verb, but that is deeper background, not the row's modelling input [@Kroonen2013, s.v. "*brōk-"]. In the live system, `*brekăną` is the input that yields OE `brecan`, whereas `*brōkiz` yields `brēċ`; conflating those two levels would erase the distinction between comparative etymology and current row policy. The present row therefore needs no special repair rule and no paradigm-cell rescue: regular comparator `*brōkiz -> brēċ`, attested outcome `brēċ`, superseded over-reading `*brekăną` as direct `PROTOFORM`.
