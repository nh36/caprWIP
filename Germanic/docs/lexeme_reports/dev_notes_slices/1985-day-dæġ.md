## Development-note summary

No single row-dedicated DEV_NOTES dossier survives for `1985 day / dæġ`. The row instead sits at the intersection of two shared OE repair stories that DEV_NOTES discusses repeatedly and explicitly: (1) A-restoration must **not** treat weak-tail `*-ăz` as a back-vowel trigger for nominative singular a-stems like `day`; and (2) once the weak tail is gone, inherited `*g` after a front vowel must still palatalise word-finally to give `ġ` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:1668-1674,2422-2429,3169-3178,43176-43387].

The A-restoration side of the dossier is substantive, not incidental. DEV_NOTES first records the general modelling problem as “**paradigmatic alternation problem** — dataset uses single proto-forms (e.g., `*brandăz`, `*dagăz`) to represent lexemes that historically had alternations (nom.sg. `dæġ` vs dat.pl. `dagum`). FST derives one form per proto-form” [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:1668-1674]. Later, the dedicated A-restoration discussion makes `day` the textbook example: “The critical insight comes from R/T’s paradigm of `dæg`,” then walks through `*dagas → dæges`, `*dagos → dagas`, `*dagum → dagum`, and states the principle in exactly the form later notes need: “**original PGmc *a in suffixes is fronted by AFB and does NOT trigger restoration. Original PGmc *o, *u in suffixes stay back and DO trigger restoration**” [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:3173-3178]. In other words, the live row is not being treated as an exception; it is one of the key reasons the project rejected a broader weak-tail trigger analysis.

DEV_NOTES also preserves the repair chronology that turned this from a regression case into a regular match. A December 2025 OE TODO/status note says plainly: “**Early final‑ă apocope (post‑z deletion) now in place; `*dagăz` yields `dæġ`**” [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:2422-2429]. That line is the clearest direct statement that the row’s nominative-singular shape depends on deleting the reduced final vowel early enough that no back-vowel trigger remains for A-restoration. The older spelling `*dagăz` there is diagnostic notation for the weak-tail stage, not a competing live TSV protoform; current row metadata stores `*dágaz` [Germanic/data/germanic-aligned-final.tsv:211-211; Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:2422-2429].

The regression history matters because DEV_NOTES uses `day` as a hard falsification test. When `{*ă}` was added to the restoration trigger set, DEV_NOTES records `dagăz → dag (should be dæġ) - REGRESSED` and lists `day` among the nine items worsened by the overgeneralization [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:9518-9545]. A later follow-on note repeats the same point in the newer notation layer: after the split-AFB work, remaining regressions included “`*dágaz → dag (expected dæġ)`,” and stepwise tracing showed that `OEARestoration` was firing wrongly when the weak tail was bare `{*a}` or `{*a}{*z}` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:22239-22252]. For row 1985, those passages are not obsolete noise: they preserve the reason the current row should stay regular **without** any analogical or exception label.

The second half of the row’s surviving DEV_NOTES support concerns palatalisation of inherited `*g`. DEV_NOTES first frames the bug by noting that `*weġ*` and `*dæġ*` are the correctly palatalised word-final cases, while `nigon` is the case where front-vowel-left/back-vowel-right should remain velar [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:43176-43207]. The later source canvass then quotes the primary tradition directly. Campbell is summarized as saying that `g` is palatal “**word-finally after any front V (`*weġ, dæġ, mǣġ*`)**” and intervocalically before a front vowel (`*sige, dæġes, weġes*`), but remains velar before a back vowel (`*wegas, nigon, þinga, daga*`) [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:43229-43243,43275-43287]. Ringe–Taylor are quoted in the same section: “**preconsonantal and word-final *g were palatalized by any preceding front vowel**,” while intervocalic `*g` palatalises “**between any two front vowels**” [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:43245-43252]. DEV_NOTES then warns against an implementation shortcut that would accidentally lose the row’s word-final palatalisation: “**Under this rule, `*weġ*` and `*dæġ*` would silently fail to palatalise**” [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:43374-43387]. For this row, that is the core palatalisation dossier in one place.

## Relevant DEV_NOTES fragments

### Weak-tail alternation baseline

- DEV_NOTES records the underlying modelling problem as a paradigmatic one, not as an isolated `day` oddity: “dataset uses single proto-forms (e.g., `*brandăz`, `*dagăz`) to represent lexemes that historically had alternations (nom.sg. `dæġ` vs dat.pl. `dagum`)” [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:1668-1674].
- This matters directly for row 1985 because it explains why a single stored nominative-style protoform can coexist with oblique forms showing `dag-` without making the row itself `late_analogy` or `known_unmodelled` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:1668-1674].

### A-restoration discussion built around `dæg`

- The clearest row-relevant DEV_NOTES wording is the explicit appeal to Ringe–Taylor’s paradigm: “**The critical insight comes from R/T’s paradigm of `dæg` (day, §6.3.2 p.193)**” [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:3173-3173].
- DEV_NOTES then spells out the contrastive forms that drive the rule decision:
  - “Gen.sg. `*dagas → dæges`: suffix `*-as` has `*a`, which after AFB becomes `*-æs` (front) → no restoration → `dæg-`”
  - “Nom.pl. `*dagos → dagas`: suffix `*-os` has `*o` (back) → restoration fires → `dag-`”
  - “Dat.pl. `*dagum → dagum`: suffix `*-um` has `*u` (back) → restoration fires → `dag-`” [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:3173-3177].
- DEV_NOTES immediately turns that paradigm into project policy: “**original PGmc `*a` in suffixes is fronted by AFB and does NOT trigger restoration. Original PGmc `*o, *u` in suffixes stay back and DO trigger restoration**” [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:3178-3178].

### Current-status note on the successful repair

- The one-line repair note is unusually direct and should be quoted almost verbatim in any later report: “**Early final‑ă apocope (post‑z deletion) now in place; `*dagăz` yields `dæġ`**” [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:2422-2429].
- This is thin as a standalone dossier, but it is the surviving DEV_NOTES sentence that most clearly states the current outcome for the row itself [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:2422-2429].

### Regression notes showing what had to be blocked

- DEV_NOTES preserves a failed experiment in which `{*ă}` was added to `OEARestorationTriggerVowel`. The resulting regression list includes:

  > `dagăz → dag (should be dæġ) - REGRESSED` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:9524-9529]

  and then explicitly counts `day` among the “**Regressed: 9**” items [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:9536-9539].

- A later follow-on diagnostic restates the same failure after another refactor: remaining regressions included “`*dágaz → dag (expected dæġ)`,” because `OEARestoration` was still firing when the weak tail was bare `{*a}` or `{*a}{*z}` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:22239-22252].
- These fragments should be kept because they preserve the negative evidence: any analysis that lets a bare reduced `a` weak tail count as the restoration-triggering back vowel will break `dæġ` [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:9518-9545,22239-22252].

### Shared palatalisation discussion that directly bears on `dæġ`

- DEV_NOTES first identifies `dæġ` as one of the forms that are already correct under word-final palatalisation after front-vowel-plus-apocope: “`*wégaz → weġ` — but here `*-g` is word-final after weak-tail apocope, so palatalisation is **correct** (**Campbell: dæġ, weġ**)” [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:43179-43187].
- In the source-canvass section, Campbell’s rule is preserved in row-relevant form: `g` is palatal “**word-finally after any front V (`*weġ, dæġ, mǣġ*`)**” and remains velar when a back vowel follows (`*wegas, nigon, þinga, daga*`) [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:43229-43243,43275-43287].
- The same section quotes Ringe–Taylor’s generalization: “**preconsonantal and word-final *g were palatalized by any preceding front vowel**,” with intervocalic palatalisation “**between any two front vowels**” [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:43245-43252].
- DEV_NOTES also preserves a practical implementation warning that matters specifically for this lexeme: “**Under this rule, `*weġ*` and `*dæġ*` would silently fail to palatalise**” if the negation shorthand were used without separately matching word boundary [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:43374-43387].

## Superseded or diagnostic material

For `dæġ`, however, the retained takeaway is stable: word-final front-vowel environments must continue to palatalise [Germanic/docs/DEV_NOTES.md:43229-43252,43374-43387].
