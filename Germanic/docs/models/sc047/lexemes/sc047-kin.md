**Reconstruction.** Kroonen [-@Kroonen2013, p. 351, s.v. *kunja-* 1]
reconstructs neuter *kunja-* ‘clan’ from Gothic *kuni*, Old
Norse *kyn*, Old Saxon *kunni*, Old Dutch *cunni* and their
cognates. This is not the homonymous formation ‘omen’.
The inherited nominative and accusative singular share
\emph{*kúnją}; protected forms include dative \emph{*kúnjai}
and genitive \emph{*kúnjas}.

**Selection.** The Paris Psalter prints nominative *cyn*
‘kind, generation’ in Psalm 23.6 [@Thorpe1835, p. 51]: the singular
demonstrative and relative clause identify the noun whose
kind is described, and Latin *haec est generatio* confirms the
construction. Psalm 13.12 independently has singular subject
*Iacobes cyn*. B2 selects the actual single-*n* edition form
from 23.6, not a silently normalized *cynn*. A selects
genitive *cynnes* ‘of stock’, with an agreeing genitive adjective
in West Saxon Mark 7:26, *Sirofenisces cynnes*, as transcribed in
[Bosworth–Toller, s.v. *hǣþen*](https://bosworthtoller.com/51580).
Dative *cynne* remains a control; the genitive has the firmer
edition locator for a simplex protected cell.
Thorpe's Psalm 17.48 prints *his cynne* but his apparatus
reports manuscript *cyn* there: it is not an independent
manuscript witness for *cynne*. Moore Bede
*moncynnæs* in *Cædmon's Hymn* is an early compound
corroboration, not a simplex token.

**Quantity and spelling.** A's direct \emph{*kúnją} loses its
ending and vocalizes *j* before gemination, giving \emph{*cyne}.
B2 first develops \emph{*kunnją}, then loses *ą*, vocalizes
the glide, loses the high vowel and simplifies final
\emph{*kynn} to *cyn*. The preserved geminate in *cynne*
and *cynnes* is independently supported. Conversely,
spellings *cynn* (e.g. *ðis cynn*, cited in Bosworth–Toller)
do not establish a phonetic final double, and the single-*n*
token does not establish that it was never doubled. The
selection compares the exact single-*n* spelling with the
predicted simplified output; reconstructing the quantity
before simplification is a separate matter. Neither the
bound form *cyne-* (cf. *cynedōm*) nor the unsegmented
Weser bone *kunni* supplies a secure inherited free English
direct cell. Under A the oblique stem could have supplied
the remodelled direct stem, but the direction and date of
transfer are unproved.
