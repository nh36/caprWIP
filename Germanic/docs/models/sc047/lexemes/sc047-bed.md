**Reconstruction.** Kroonen [-@Kroonen2013, p. 84, s.v. *badja-*] reconstructs
a neuter light *ja*-stem, supported by Gothic *badi*, Old Dutch
*beddi* and Old High German *betti*. The Old Norse cognate *beðr*
is masculine and does not determine the inherited English gender.
The inherited neuter nominative and accusative singular share
\emph{*bádją}; the dative and genitive are reconstructed as
\emph{*bádjai} and \emph{*bádjas}.

**Old English witnesses.** The Paris Psalter (BnF lat. 8824), Psalm
40.3, has *tō his bedde* ‘to his bed’ and *eall his bedd*
‘all his bed’ in the same verse [@Thorpe1835, p. 99]. The former is
dative after *tō*; the latter is the object of *onwent* and matches
Latin *universum stratum*. *Beowulf* 1791 has *beddes* ‘of a bed’
with *nēosan*; 1240 has *beddum* ‘with beds’ beside *bolstrum*.
The isolated Épinal–Erfurt gloss *bedd* (Pheifer 1974, no. 243)
does not establish vernacular nominative versus accusative. The
Old English *Bede* IV.25 (*bedd*, variant *bed*, Schipper p. 500,
l. 12) supplies a direct plural in coordinated context.

**Regular and remodelled forms.** A's inherited nominative and
accusative lose *ą* before gemination: \emph{*badj} becomes
\emph{*badi}, and the light stem retains its vowel, giving
\emph{*bede}. In B2 the glide is still prevocalic at gemination;
\emph{*baddją} ultimately gives *bedd* after heavy-stem apocope.
Both models give protected *bedde*, *beddes* and direct plural
*bedd*. A selects the attested dative, not a direct form.
Its possible sources for the surviving direct stem include the
protected obliques and the endingless plural. The attested
alternants establish the available stem material, not the
direction or date of its spread.

**Continental comparison.** Munich, BSB Clm 18550a, fol. 154v,
l. 22, has *p&i* (edited *peti*) beside Latin accusative *stratum*
(Steinmeyer and Sievers 1882, p. 221, l. 60). The ninth-century
gloss hand supplies a singleton-plus-vowel independent of the
English paradigm. Frankish *bedi* beside accusative *culcitram*
(Paulus Diaconus V.2, Paulus 1878, p. 144, l. 4, apparatus p)
corroborates the shape in a second witness, not a second lexeme.
Latin case identifies the glossing context, not necessarily a
separately inflected vernacular token. Doubled *betti/bette* in
the Tatian tradition shows the competing shape. The singletons
may preserve ungeminated direct stems, but regional innovation,
later remodelling and the limited witness distribution prevent
them from dating English nasal loss. See the full
[Continental collation](../../../sound_changes/literature_dossiers/sc047_paired_chronologies/comparative_followup/morphology/peti.md).
