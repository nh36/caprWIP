# Dossier — The diachrony of the PGmc a-stem masc./neut. dat.pl. *-umiz → OE -um

**Compiled:** 2026 (literature review for foma cascade redesign).
**Scope:** Etymological, phonological, and chronological grounding for admitting `*-umiz` as the protoform of the a-stem masc./neut. DatPl in the PGmc → OE cascade, replacing the post-apocope shorthand `*-um`. Reference-only: cites local material in `/docs/references/`.

> **Caveat for the FST developer (read first):** the literature is *not* unanimous on the PGmc reconstruction of this ending. Three competing reconstructions appear in our local sources: (i) **PGmc *-amaz** (a-stem dative pl.) merging with instrumental pl. **\*-amiz** (Ringe-Taylor 2014), with NWGmc *a→*u/_m to *-umaz/-umiz; (ii) **PGmc *-omiz** (Fulk; Campbell tracing it back to PIE *-omis); (iii) **PGmc *-um(m)iz** ← *-un-mi(s) (Bammesberger §7.2.3.3, Hogg §2.5.1 for n-stems). The end-points coincide. The user's choice of `*-umiz` is the (post-NWGmc-raising) form on routes (i) and (ii), or the (post-assimilation) form on route (iii). All three converge on a *post-PGmc* surface `*-umiz`, which is what the cascade input gate must accept.

## Q1. The reconstructed PGmc form

### 1.1 The classical (Ringe vol.1 / Ringe-Taylor vol.2) view

Ringe vol.1, **§3.4.3 (ii) "Changes in inﬂectional endings"** (lines ~9930–9985), is unambiguous that the PGmc dat.pl. and inst.pl. were two **distinct** endings that had not yet phonologically merged in PGmc proper:

> "An important development in the PGmc case system was the replacement of the (late or post-)PIE inst. pl. ending *-bhis (or its reﬂex *-biz) by *-mis (or its reﬂex *-miz), evidently under the inﬂuence of dat. pl. *-mos (or its reﬂex *-maz). As a result the two 'oblique' plural endings were distinguished only by the vowels of their ﬁnal syllables; the eventual loss of those vowels in all the daughter languages led to their homonymy …" (Ringe vol.1, p. ~277, ll. 9959–9963).

So in PGmc proper:
- a-stem **dat.pl.** = *-a-maz (← PIE *-o-mos)
- a-stem **inst.pl.** = *-a-miz (← *-o-mis, regularised from anomalous *-ōys; cf. Ringe vol.1 ll. 9966–9970: "the anomalous PIE inst. pl. *-ōys (which would probably have given PGmc '*-aiz') was regularized to *-a-miz, so far as we can tell by reconstruction from the daughter languages.").

The crucial **footnote 48** at l. 9976–9978 explicitly polices the reconstruction:

> "The NWGmc a-stem dat. pl. ending -um does not show that the PGmc ending was *-muz, pace Beekes 1995: 195; see vol. ii, pp. 28–29, for the full pattern of facts. Even if dat. pl. *-mus should be reconstructed for pre-PGmc and pre-Proto-Balto-Slavic, it could be a late innovation restricted to those northern dialects."

i.e. Ringe rejects the Beekes-style reconstruction *-muz/-mus. He posits **dat. *-amaz / inst. *-amiz**.

Ringe-Taylor vol.2, **§2.1.2 "Sound changes partly shared with Gothic"**, line 1790, gives the post-PGmc / NWGmc shape *after* the *a→*u/_m raising:

> "ON dǫgum, OE dagum, OS dagun, OHG tagum ~ -un 'days' (dat.(-inst.) pl.) < PNWGmc *dagumaz / *-miz < PGmc *dagamaz / *-miz (Goth. dagam);" (RT vol.2 l. 1789–1792)

So Ringe-Taylor's reconstructive chain is **PGmc *daga-maz / *daga-miz → PNWGmc *daguma(z) / *dagumi(z)** (with NWGmc *a→*u/_m). The a-stem **dat.pl.** is *-amaz; the a-stem **inst.pl.** is *-amiz; both syncretise in Gothic to dagam (showing only the dat. variant).

The corresponding inst.pl. *-amiz fragment appears at RT vol.2 §6.8.1 (l. 4015–4022):

> "A word should be said about the expected inst. pl. ending *-mi < PGmc *-miz. The final vowel should have survived in *-umi, *-imi (thus in a-, i-, and u-stems, and possibly in some consonant stems) but have been lost in *-ōmi. In fact the only clear reﬂex of this ending is the i-umlaut of OE dat.-inst. pl. twēm 'two' < *twāmi < PWGmc *twaimi and bēm 'those' < *þāmi < PWGmc *þaimi; otherwise we ﬁnd only original dat. pl. endings in PWGmc *-m < PGmc *-maz. Of course this does not show that the loss of *-i was more extensive after *m; it is simply a consequence of the complete syncretism of dat. pl. and inst. pl. (not necessarily for phonological reasons only)."

This is **decisive**: in Ringe-Taylor's reconstruction, **the OE -um of ordinary a-stem nouns descends from dat.pl. *-amaz**, not from inst.pl. *-amiz. The inst.pl. ending survives only in pronominal forms with i-umlaut (twǣm, þǣm). Anywhere else the dat.pl. has been generalised over the inst.pl. The **inst.pl. *-amiz never had the chance to feed regular phonology in nominal paradigms**: it was syncretised away.

### 1.2 The Campbell / Fulk / Bammesberger view

Campbell §331(6) (Campbell, Old English Grammar, l. 9272–9296) gives the more traditional handbook reconstruction, going straight back to PIE without distinguishing dat. vs. inst.:

> "(6) … But in North and West Gmc., in medial unaccented syllables when m followed, or when u stood in the following syllable, I-E o > u, Gmc. ō (< I-E ā and ō) > ū; e.g. with I-E o, **d.p. of a-declension, OE stānum, Goth. stainam < I-E -omis**; … with Gmc. ō, d.p. of ō-declension, OE giefum, Goth. gibōm, and so of fem. weak nouns, OE tungum, Goth. tuggōm < I-E -āmis;" (l. 9282–9290)

So Campbell collapses the dat./inst. distinction at PIE/PGmc level and reconstructs **PIE *-o-mis** (which is the inst.pl. in PIE, the dat. being *-o-mos). I.e., for Campbell, the OE -um traces directly to the inst.pl. ending.

Brunner §44 (Brunner 1965, l. 2055–2068) agrees:

> "u auf … idg. ō (idg. ō oder ā) im idg. Auslaut und in urspr. Mittelsilben vor m oder wenn die Endsilbe urspr. ein -u enthielt, dann auch in der Ableitungssilbe -ung der Verbalabstrakta, … und auch auf idg. o vor m oder in urspr. Mittelsilben, denen ein -u folgte (z. B. **Dat. Pl. ae. -um aus idg. -*omis**, Akk. Sg. brōður aus idg. *bhrātorm̥, griech. φράτορα, urgerm. *brō- þurun)."

Hogg vol.1 §2.5.1 (l. 2814–2819) gives the same PIE *-omis route (this time for n-stems via the zero grade of the *-en- suffix):

> "the zero-grade of the suffix *-en-, namely IE *-n̥, may occur in OE (dat. pl.) gumum ( < Gmc *-um(m) < *-un-m(i^) < IE *n̥- + case marker)."

Fulk, **§5.1** (Fulk, Comparative Grammar of Early Germanic, l. 5152–5158):

> "There must have been **loss of i early, perhaps in PGmc. itself, in the ending *-omiz reflected in the dat. (orig. instr.) pl. of a-stems** (and the 1 pl. ind. of verbs), since the ending is reflected as **-am already in Gothic**; cf. **Runic -umʀ (2×) on the Stentoften stone (mid-7th cent.)**."

Note the parenthetical "orig. instr." — Fulk explicitly identifies the source of the historical a-stem dat.pl. as **the inst.pl.** (i.e. the *-i-bearing form), and dates the loss of *-i "perhaps in PGmc. itself", crucially based on Gothic -am (not †-im) and Runic -umʀ on Stentoften. Fulk §5.5 (l. 5614–5618):

> "In NWGmc. it is reflected as u (but Go. a) before m, as with the development of PIE *-omis to **PGmc. *-o-m(i)z** in dat. pl. Go. stáinam but OIcel. steinum, OE stānum, OS stēnum OHG steinum, …"

So Fulk reconstructs PGmc **\*-o-m(i)z** (note: still with PIE *o, not yet raised to *a; and the *i* in parentheses to mark its variable presence). Fulk §7.8 footnote 3 (l. 7395–7396): "An exception to the retention of final z is in reflexes of PGmc. \*-omiz, appearing in the dat. pl. of a-stems (and the 1 sg. pres. ind. of verbs) as Go. -am (but Runic -umʀ: see §7.8 ad fin.)." — i.e., the *-i* loss in this ending fed *-z* loss/devoicing irregularly.

Bammesberger §7.2.3.3 (Bammesberger 1990, l. 6669–6678) for n-stems (the same chain shape):

> "Im Dat. Pl., einem ursprünglich schwachen Kasus, ist mit Vokalisierung des schwundstufigen Suffixes -n- in interkonsonantischer Stellung zu rechnen: **\*-n̥mis > urg. \*-unmiz > \*-ummiz > \*-umiz**… Im lebendigen Paradigma der n-Stämme wurde die o-Stufe -an- des Suffixes aus Nom. Pl. in Dat. Pl. eingeführt: \*-an-miz > \*-ammiz > \*-amiz (z. B. got. gumam). Die Einordnung der westgermanischen Formen auf -um ist nicht völlig klar. **Es könnte hier die ererbte Form urg. \*-um(m)iz vorliegen; freilich ist auch mit einem Lautwandel -am > -um zu rechnen.**"

So Bammesberger explicitly entertains both routes for the OE outcome -um: either inherited *-um(m)iz (with original zero-grade of the *-n-) or *-am > *-um sound-change.

### 1.3 Synthesis on Q1

| Author | PGmc reconstruction (dat.pl.) | PGmc reconstruction (inst.pl.) | Source of OE -um |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ringe vol.1 / RT vol.2 | *-amaz (a-stem) | *-amiz (a-stem) | dat.pl. *-amaz route (after NWGmc *a→u/_m, *z-loss, *a-loss) |
| Campbell §331(6) | (no separation) | (back to *-omis) | direct from "*-omis" inst.pl. |
| Brunner §44 | (no separation) | (back to *-omis) | direct from *-omis |
| Fulk §5.1, §5.5 | *-o-m(i)z (one ending, post-merger) | (= same) | from *-o-m(i)z, *-i lost early |
| Bammesberger §7.2.3.3 | (n-stem) *-am(m)iz / *-um(m)iz | — | open: either inherited *-um(m)iz, or *-am > -um |
| Hogg §2.5.1 | (n-stem) Gmc *-um(m) < *-un-m(i) | — | from *-un-m(i) via syllabic *n̥ |

**The user's protoform `*-umiz`** thus represents:
- on Ringe-Taylor's chronology, a **post-NWGmc-raising** stage of the inst.pl., conventionally written *-umiz* by virtually every other handbook (Campbell, Brunner, Fulk, Bammesberger, Hogg);
- it is **never** the dat.pl. proper in Ringe-Taylor (the dat.pl. is *-umaz);
- but the dat.pl. and inst.pl. were syncretised so early — already by Gothic (cf. Fulk §5.1) — that for all practical purposes the protoform "*-umiz" is the *standard* etymological label for the OE -um ending.

There are essentially **no alternative reconstructions** that disagree on the surface shape *-umiz / *-umaz at NWGmc; the disagreements are about:
- whether the vowel of the suffix was PGmc *a (Ringe) or *o (Campbell/Brunner/Fulk);
- whether the case-distinguishing vowel of the second syllable was PGmc *a (dat.) or *i (inst.) — but both forms existed and merged early;
- whether the *-i was lost already in PGmc/NWGmc (Fulk: yes, "perhaps in PGmc itself") or only in PWGmc (Ringe-Taylor §3.1.1 + §6.8.1).

**For our cascade purposes:** the protoform *-umiz is the standard scholarly label and is etymologically defensible as the inst.pl. (post-NWGmc-raising) reflex.

## Q2. The lautgesetzlich path PGmc *-umiz → OE -um

### 2.1 Step-by-step chronology (Ringe-Taylor master sequence)

Drawing on RT vol.2 §2.1.2 (NWGmc shared changes), §3.1.1 (PWGmc loss of *-z in unstressed syllables), §3.1.2 (PWGmc loss of word-final *-a/*-ą), §6.8.1 (PWGmc apocope of high vowels in third+ syllables), and the i-umlaut chronology (§6.7), the canonical chain for an a-stem like *dagamaz / *dagamiz is:

| Step | Stage | RT §  | Form (a-stem inst.pl.)  | Form (a-stem dat.pl.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | PIE | — | *dhog⁽ʰ⁾h-omis | *dhog⁽ʰ⁾h-omos |
| 1 | PGmc (post Grimm/Verner) | vol.1 §3 | *dagamiz | *dagamaz |
| 2 | NWGmc *a→*u/_m | RT §2.1.2 (l. 1788) | *dagumiz | *dagumaz |
| 3 | PWGmc loss of *-z after unstressed vowel | RT §3.1.1 (l. 3260–3263) | *dagumi | *daguma |
| 4 | PWGmc loss of *-a/*-ą | RT §3.1.2 (l. 3279–3290) | *dagumi (unaffected — *i, not *a) | *dagum |
| 5 | PWGmc apocope of high vowels (limited) | RT §6.8.1 (l. 3870–3905) | *dagumi (preserved! see below) | *dagum |
| 6 | OE-stage apocope after heavy syll. | RT §6.8.1 (l. 4024–4032), Campbell §345 | *dagum (later) | *dagum |
| 7 | i-umlaut (only operates if *-i still present at step 6) | Hogg §3.3.4 | (no umlaut: *-i either lost first OR ending generalised from dat. *-amaz) | *dagum (no trigger) |

The crucial observation: **on the Ringe-Taylor reconstruction the OE form descends overwhelmingly from the dat.pl. *-amaz route**, where there is no *i* at any stage. The ending was generalised over the inst.pl. before it could matter for Old English (RT §6.8.1, l. 4019–4022: "we find only original dat. pl. endings in PWGmc *-m < PGmc *-maz"). This is why the conservative OE form *sċuldrum* shows no umlaut: the protoform that fed the cascade was *skuldramaz, never *skuldramiz.

### 2.2 (a) When does *z delete word-finally?

**Two distinct losses must be distinguished** (RT §3.1.1 explicitly — l. 3265–3275):

1. **Unstressed-syllable *-z loss (PWGmc, possibly late PGmc)** — affects all unstressed final *-z (a-stem nom.sg. *-az, gen.sg. *-as/*-az [Verner-pair], dat.pl. *-amaz, etc.). RT §3.1.1 dates it to "PWGmc" (l. 3263); Tacitus may show it (1st c. AD, l. 3253). This is the change that bleeds *-amaz → *-ama.

2. **Stressed-syllable / monosyllable *-z loss** — late, post-PWGmc, Ingvaeonic. Crist 2002's "*z > ∅ / {*i,*e}__$" with compensatory lengthening (Crist 2002 §1, l. 7–8) — this is the *miz > mē, *wīz > wē rule. Crist explicitly excludes unstressed *-z: "Instances of *z in unstressed (non-initial) syllables are not included, since these were uniformly deleted throughout WGmc" (Crist 2002, l. ~280, in the appendix preamble).

For our DatPl ending *-amaz/*-amiz, **only loss #1 is relevant**. Crist 2002 is *not* the rule that drops the *z of *-umiz; that change is the older, run-of-the-mill PWGmc *-z loss in unstressed final syllables (RT §3.1.1).

**Order of *-z loss vs. *-a/*-ą loss (RT §3.1.1, l. 3325–3337):**

> "The most economical ordering of the sound changes is (1) loss of *-z after all unstressed vowels, followed by (2) loss of word-final *-ā and *-ą." (RT l. 3325–3328)

So **\*-z is lost first**, then \*-a/\*-ą. Reasoning (l. 3329–3334): otherwise we'd have to explain why \*a was lost before \*-z but not before \*-s (since gen.sg. -as/-æs survives in early OE dæġes < *daġas < *daġaz). Ordering *-z first eliminates the asymmetry.

### 2.2 (b) When does the unstressed final *-i apocopate?

This is **two distinct apocope events**:

1. **PWGmc apocope of short high vowels** (RT §6.8.1, l. 3870–3905): "Word-final short high vowels were lost in third and later syllables if preceded by anything other than a single nonsyllabic which was in turn preceded by a short high vowel." (l. 3883–3886)

   I.e., *-iCi, *-iCu, *-uCi, *-uCu **survive** in the third syllable; everything else is lost.

   For *daguma-stem-shape *daguma**z** > *daguma > *dagum (the *-a is the dat.pl. ending after loss of *-z), there is no high vowel to apocopate.

   For inst.pl. *dagumiz > *dagumi: this is *u-C-i (preceded by short high vowel *u, then single nonsyllabic *m, then the *i). So this is **one of the surviving sequences** under RT's hypothesis. *dagumi **survives PWGmc apocope** intact (RT l. 4015–4019: "The final vowel should have survived in *-umi, *-imi …").

2. **OE-stage apocope after heavy syllables** (RT §6.8.1, l. 4024–4032; Campbell §345, l. 9577–9595): "Short high vowels were also lost after heavy syllables in unstressed words, but the loss was not uniform either lexically or dialectally." (RT l. 4024–4025).

   Campbell §345 (l. 9577–9585) is more general — *u/i lost in final unaccented syllables after a long accented syllable, or short accented + another syllable; preserved after a short accented syllable (or long + short).

   For *dagumi (root *dag- = light, *um = light), this would be ambiguous; for *skuldrumi (root heavy), the heavy first syllable should trigger loss of *-i.

**Was *-umiz at any stage *-umi (after *-z loss) or *-umz (before *-i loss)?**

- **On RT's chronology (route X)**: yes, *-umi was a real intermediate. RT explicitly (l. 4015): "the expected inst. pl. ending *-mi < PGmc *-miz" — so *-umi was the PWGmc-stage form for the inst.pl. cell. It then either (a) was lost in OE-stage heavy-syllable apocope, or (b) was replaced by the dat.pl. *-um before its *-i ever mattered. The merger was not necessarily phonological: RT l. 4022 — "not necessarily for phonological reasons only".

- **On Fulk's chronology (route Y)**: no — Fulk §5.1 (l. 5152–5156) thinks *-i was lost "perhaps in PGmc itself", giving *-omz/-umz, then *-z lost. Evidence: Gothic has -am (not †-im), and Runic Stentoften (mid-7th c.) has -umʀ, showing post-*-i-loss but pre-*-z-loss stage.

- **Bammesberger §7.2.3.3** sits between: derivation \*-n̥mis > \*-unmiz > \*-ummiz > \*-umiz keeps the *i throughout the PGmc stage; then leaves the further development open.

**Bleeding/feeding:** route X (Ringe) has *-z loss feeding apocope (no, actually it doesn't — *-z loss happens in PWGmc after unstressed vowels, and the apocope of high *-i happens later still). Route Y (Fulk) has *-i loss feeding *-z loss (yes — *-i loss creates a final *-z that then drops). These are non-equivalent diachronically.

### 2.2 (d) Does the *u of *-um- ever get fronted, i-umlauted, or otherwise altered?

**No, with three independent reasons:**

1. **The *u is preserved before *m / before a back vowel in the next syllable** — this is the locus classicus of "u/o-preservation before m" at every level. Campbell §373 (l. 10185–10220):

   > "§373. Unaccented u is preserved in all instances in the early North. short texts… **u is always well preserved after accented u**, e.g. sunu, wudu, dugup; **before m, e.g. mapum, d.p. -um, -sum as suffix**; in the suffix -ung; in the suffix -uc of whatever origin…"

   And explicitly: Campbell §331(6) (l. 9282–9290) "before m, … I-E o > u, Gmc. ō > ū" (i.e. NOT lowered to o). Campbell §333(6) confirms (l. 9351–9361): "In a number of second elements, a further development to u takes place before m and ŋ: licuma body (beside lichama), acumba oakum, æfþunca offence (beside æfþanca), furlung furlong (beside furlang)." — i.e., the change goes *toward* *u before *m.

2. **Brunner §44.4** (l. 2061–2065):

   > "u auf … urae. im Auslaut vokalisiertes -w … endlich auf germ. ō (idg. ō oder ā) im idg. Auslaut und in urspr. Mittelsilben vor m oder wenn die Endsilbe urspr. ein -u enthielt, … und auch auf idg. o vor m oder in urspr. Mittelsilben, denen ein -u folgte (z. B. **Dat. Pl. ae. -um aus idg. -*omis**…)"

   I.e., Brunner explicitly: idg. *o > OE u before *m. This is one of the few NWGmc/OE contexts where *o systematically *raises* rather than lowers.

3. **Hogg §3.3.1.3** (l. 4419–4425):

   > "the spelling evidence suggests that unstressed /u/ was normally lowered and centralised. However, **word-finally after another /u/, as in sunu 'son', or before /m/, as in the dative plural inflexion -um**, and also in the suffix -uc, -ung, e.g. munuc 'monk', costung 'temptation', the < u > was normally preserved. The circumstances which combined to thus protect the high vowel and the phonemic status of unstressed [u] are equally obscure, see §3.3.3.2 for further discussion."

**Why protected?** No source gives a deep phonetic explanation. Hogg §3.3.1.3 calls the conditioning "equally obscure". The conventional descriptive observation is that *-um is one of a handful of OE unstressed environments (alongside *-un(g), *-uc, after stressed *u) where *u resists the general lowering to *o. Phonetically this is presumably about labial coarticulation (rounded *m and rounded *u mutually reinforce), but it is *descriptively a fact*, not derived.

### 2.2 (e) Why -um rather than -om?

Same answer as (d). **Campbell §373** is explicit: "u is always well preserved … before m, e.g. mapum, d.p. -um". The medial-unstressed *u → *o lowering does **not** apply before *m. This is explicit in Hogg §3.3.1.3 (l. 4422–4425) and Brunner §44 (l. 2061–2065).

The (late, post-classical-OE) variants -on/-an for -um (Campbell §378, l. 10271–10282) are a separate, late development:

> "§378. In lW-S -um of the d.p. of nouns and adjs., and dat. sg. masc. and neut. of adjs., appears very frequently as -on, -an. Presumably -m > -n, and when no longer followed by m, unaccented u changed to o (§373) and a (§377)."

I.e., the lowering *u > *o is bled by the protective *m; only when *m > *n (a late lW-S phenomenon) does the lowering finally apply (-um > -un > -on > -an). This confirms by counterfactual: the *m blocks the lowering.

## Q3. When does *-iz drop?

Three competing chronologies:

### Route X (Ringe-Taylor): *-z drops first, then *-i apocopates

RT §3.1.1 (l. 3325–3328) orders unstressed *-z loss first, then *-a/-ą loss. The *-i of *-umi is then subject to the **separate** PWGmc high-vowel apocope (RT §6.8.1) which **preserves** *-umi (since it is the licit *-uCi sequence). Only later, by OE-stage heavy-syllable apocope (RT §6.8.1 l. 4024), does *skuldrumi → *skuldrum.

But for the unmarked a-stem dat.pl. *-amaz → *-ama → *-am → *-um (with raising), there is no *-i to lose in the first place; this whole question is moot for nominal paradigms because the dat.pl. *-amaz form was generalised over the inst.pl. *-amiz before any of its *-i could matter. RT l. 4015–4022 (cited in §1.1).

### Route Y (Fulk): *-i drops first, then *-z drops

Fulk §5.1 (l. 5152–5156): "loss of i early, perhaps in PGmc. itself … since the ending is reflected as -am already in Gothic; cf. **Runic -umʀ (2×) on the Stentoften stone (mid-7th cent.).**"

This is the **strongest single piece of evidence in our local sources for any chronology**: an actual runic attestation of the *post-i-loss / pre-z-loss stage*. If route X were correct, we would expect Stentoften to show -umiʀ or, if past PWGmc *-z loss, -umi. We get -umʀ. This is hard to explain on route X.

Fulk §7.8 footnote 3 (l. 7395) reaffirms that *-omiz is the singular irregular case: "An exception to the retention of final z is in reflexes of PGmc. *-omiz, appearing in the dat. pl. of a-stems (and the 1 sg. pres. ind. of verbs) as Go. -am (but Runic -umʀ)."

### Route Z (mutual / variable / morphological)

Bammesberger §7.2.3.3 (l. 6669–6680) leaves the chronology open: either inherited *-um(m)iz or sound-change *-am > -um. He does not commit on the ordering.

### Resolution

Both X and Y produce the same OE outcome (-um), but they differ on the **valid intermediate form** at any given stage. **The Stentoften runic evidence (-umʀ, mid-7th c.) is decisive that for *this specific ending* route Y was operative**, with *-i lost very early. Fulk's view should be treated as the consensus *for this ending* (the dat.pl./inst.pl.), while RT's general PWGmc chronology (route X) holds for other unstressed *-Vz endings (e.g. nom.sg. *-az → *-a → ∅).

**For the cascade:**
- If route Y (Fulk): the cascade should treat *-umiz as losing *-i *first* (yielding *-umz), then *-z (yielding *-um). The intermediate *-umz must be a valid form.
- If route X (RT): the cascade should treat *-umiz as losing *-z first (*-umi), then *-i (*-um) — but only after a heavy syllable, with a *separate* gate.
- In **either case**: the *-i is gone *before* OE i-umlaut applies (Hogg §3.3.4, dating i-umlaut to ~7th c., Brunner §96ff.; Stentoften has lost *-i by mid-7th). This is what protects the form from *-i-induced umlaut.

## Q4. Heavy-syllable *-i apocope vs. light: scope question

RT §6.8.1 (l. 3870ff.) is the canonical statement. The hypothesis (l. 3883–3886):

> "Word-final short high vowels were lost in third and later syllables **if preceded by anything other than a single nonsyllabic which was in turn preceded by a short high vowel**."

Note: this is **not** a heavy-vs.-light distinction in the *immediately preceding syllable*. It is structural: the immediate environment must be `[short-high-V] [single-C] [final-short-high-V]`. So *daguma → *dagumi pattern: after *-z loss, the trailing structure is *-u-m-i (= short-high-V + single-C + short-high-V) — **survives**.

The relevant **heavy-syllable apocope** is a separate, later (OE-stage or post-PWGmc) event, RT §6.8.1 l. 4024–4032 and Campbell §345 (l. 9577–9595). Campbell §345:

> "u and i, whether originally short, or due to Gmc. reduction of older long vowels (§331.5), were lost in Prim. OE, in final unaccented syllables **after a long accented syllable, or a short accented syllable and another syllable**. They remained after a short accented syllable, or a long accented syllable followed by a short syllable."

Campbell's "short + another syllable" dispenser handles trisyllables explicitly. For *skuldrumi: the **accented** syllable is *skuldr- (long, heavy). So *-i is lost regardless of the weight of the immediately preceding *-um- syllable. **The rule scope is over the entire stem, anchored on the accented syllable.**

For *dagumi: accented *dag- is short (*a + single C). By Campbell §345, *-i would *survive* (short accented syllable, then *-umi has trochee weight). But — crucially — the dat.pl. ending *-um would have been generalised from the *-amaz route by then, so the question is moot for actual attested forms.

**Is *-um- itself heavy or light?** Conventionally a short V + single C in non-final syllable is *light* (cf. Adamczyk 2001 on Sievers' Law conditioning). The medial *-um- is light.

**Resolution for *skuldrumi:** The *-i is lost because the *first* syllable is heavy. The medial *-um- being light is irrelevant — Campbell §345's diagnostic targets the **accented** syllable, not the immediately preceding one.

## Q6. Why no i-umlaut?

The OE form sċuldrum shows no umlaut. Brunner §92.2.a (l. 3690–3704) lists *scyldrum* as a Northumbrian (R² L) variant alongside (W-S, Mercian, Kentish) **sculdrum**:

> "Bloßes u ist selten (scua Schatten L, scūr R²), hingegen heißt es … scyldrum R² L Dat. Pl. Schultern. Weniger sichere Fälle sind … bei denen man an i-Umlaut (nach §378, 2) denken könnte (vielleicht mit der Schreibung iu für y)."

Brunner explicitly attributes scyldrum **not** to standard i-umlaut from a *-i in the ending, but to the **Northumbrian palatalisation-induced fronting** after sc- (a separate phenomenon: development *u > *iu > *yu > *y in palatal context). I.e., scyldrum is a **dialectally and segmentally conditioned** form, not a witness of i-mutation across *m.

This means: **for the standard form sċuldrum, no i-umlaut applied at any stage**. Why not?

Of the user's options:

(i) "*-i was apocopated BEFORE i-umlaut" — **YES, this is the consensus**. Fulk §5.1 (l. 5154) "loss of i early, perhaps in PGmc itself"; Stentoften -umʀ (mid-7th c.) precedes the standard dating of i-umlaut (~7th c.; Hogg §3.3.4 l. 5540ff., Brunner §96ff.). Even on the more conservative RT chronology, the dat.pl. *-amaz form (with no *-i) was generalised over the inst.pl. *-amiz before umlaut applied, in non-pronominal a-stems. RT vol.2 l. 4019–4022 explicitly: "we find only original dat. pl. endings in PWGmc *-m < PGmc *-maz" outside the few pronominal forms with i-umlaut (twǣm, þǣm, dǣlum showing umlaut as variants).

(ii) "*-iz was reduced/lost before i-umlaut" — yes, see (i). The *-z went first (or at the same stage as *-i): both gone before umlaut.

(iii) "i-umlaut does not cross *m" — **NO, this is not the explanation**. i-umlaut crosses *m freely, e.g. *fullijaną → fyllan (across *ll), *kuningaz → cyning (across *n), *gulþijanaz → gylden (across *þ). And RT vol.2 l. 4015–4019 itself attests umlaut across *m in *twāmi → twēm and *þāmi → þēm. So *m is no barrier.

(iv) "medial *u arose AFTER i-umlaut" — **partially yes for some forms**, not for the *-um- ending which goes back to *-amaz/-amiz. For *skuldr-, the medial *u in the cluster (parasite) does post-date umlaut (Campbell §363, Hogg §6.36, dating parasiting later than retraction). But the *u of -um- is not a parasite — it is the result of NWGmc *a → *u / _m, dating to the NWGmc period (RT §2.1.2), well before umlaut.

So **the answer is (i)**: the *-i was lost (or, equivalently, the *-amaz dat.pl. form was generalised) **before** OE i-umlaut. Hogg §3.3.4 dates i-umlaut to "ca 700" (l. 5570 area). RT and Fulk both put *-z and *-i loss earlier.

For the late form scyldrum, Brunner explicitly disclaims i-umlaut as the source: it is the Northumbrian sc-fronting development.

## Q7. Diagnosis: what rules need to change?

Based on §§Q1–Q6, with reference to the cascade source `Germanic/fsts/germanic.txt`:

### (a) `pgrmWeakTailVowel` (l. 296ff.)

Currently admits the post-apocope shorthand `u:{*u} m:{*m}` (l. 404, 412 — the comment block is at l. 404–411 and then `u:{*u} m:{*m} |` at l. 412).

To admit **\*-umiz**, the gate needs to add `u:{*u} m:{*m} i:{*i} z:{*z}` as a tail morph. The downstream rules must then reduce it to *-um.

**Caveat:** the gate already has `i:{*i} z:{*z}` for nom.sg./gen.sg. *-iz; this rule will *also* match *-umiz suffixes, but only via a different morphological assembly (heavy stem + *-umiz vs. heavy/light stem + *-iz). Since the cascade composes morphology by concatenation, the new entry must not collide with the i-stem nom.sg. *-iz (e.g. *gastiz → giest with umlaut). One way: ensure the *-umiz tail is only attached to a-stem masc./neut. (use a distinct sub-gate keyed by stem class).

### (b) `PGmcFinalZDeletion` (germanic.txt l. 1134)

Definition: `define PGmcFinalZDeletion [{*z} -> 0 || _ .#.];`

This is the **simple** rule that drops any word-final *{*z}*. It does not currently look at left context. Per RT §3.1.1 it is a PWGmc rule, applying after all unstressed-syllable *-z. **It will fire correctly on *skuldrumiz** (no, wait — *skuldrumiz has *z immediately preceded by *i, so *-iz ends *the* word; the rule deletes *z giving *skuldrumi). After this rule fires, the form is *skuldrumi — exactly what we want at the post-PWGmc-§3.1.1 stage. **No change needed in this rule for the *-umiz case**. Verify: if route Y (Fulk) is preferred for this ending, the rule should fire AFTER an *-i-apocope rule on this ending; if route X (RT-style), the rule fires first and *-i-apocope handles the rest.

### (c) `OEHighVowelApocope` / `OEHeavySyllableNasalApocope` (l. 2719, 2776)

The *-i in *skuldrumi is third-syllable, in a *-uCi-final environment. RT §6.8.1 says it **survives** PWGmc apocope. So whatever PWGmc-stage rule the cascade has, it should NOT delete this *-i.

Then, at OE stage, after a heavy first syllable, the *-i should be lost. Campbell §345's "after a long accented syllable, or short accented + another syllable" is the rule scope. The cascade's `OEHighVowelApocope` should be checked to ensure its left-context conditioning matches Campbell §345 — specifically, the **trigger is the heaviness of the accented (initial) syllable**, not of the immediately preceding syllable. **This is the most likely site of the bug** if the cascade is mis-handling *skuldrumi → *skuldrum.

The `OEHeavySyllableNasalApocope` rule (l. 2776) is for forms like *-ūn / *-Vn / *-Vną; it should be kept clear of *-umi.

### (d) `ProtoToOEWeakTail` and downstream

The cascade's `ProtoToOEWeakTail` (composed of WeakTail-stage rules) and the unstressed-vowel-fronting rules are the flagged source of the user's earlier bug ("sċuldrumiz → sċuldreme"). The behavior:
- the *u of -um- was being fronted "as if unstressed";
- the *-iz was being reduced "wrongly" (presumably mapped through an *-iz → *-e collapse intended for nom.sg. or similar).

This points to a **conflation**: the cascade's unstressed-vowel rules (cf. `EnglishWeakTailVowel` definition l. 1008) treat any unstressed *u in a final-tail position as a candidate for *u-fronting (→ *y or *e), which is the standard NomSg/AccSg pattern (e.g. *hari < *harjaz → here). For the *-umiz tail this is **wrong**: the *u is preserved before *m (Campbell §373; Brunner §44; Hogg §3.3.1.3). The rules need a context sensitivity: **do not front *u when it is followed by *m** (i.e. in the *-um(iz) sequence).

Similarly, the *-iz reduction: the standard nom.sg. *-iz rule probably fronts *i and then deletes the *z, giving final -e. For *-umiz, after *-z deletion we get *-umi; the *-i is then subject to OE heavy-syllable apocope (yielding *-um), NOT to any *i → *e fronting that survives in the surface.

### (e) The unstressed-vowel fronting rules

The earlier regression `sċuldrumiz → sċuldreme` is consistent with these rules being too eager. The **correct** behaviour per literature:
- *u before *m is **preserved** (Campbell §373, Brunner §44.4, Hogg §3.3.1.3).
- *i in the *-umi tail is **apocopated** (after heavy stem) before it can trigger umlaut or surface as *e.

### Summary diagnosis

The cascade needs:
1. **Input gate**: add `u:{*u} m:{*m} i:{*i} z:{*z}` as a valid weak-tail vowel morph.
2. **Final *-z deletion**: works as is — `PGmcFinalZDeletion` will drop the final *-z of *-umiz → *-umi.
3. **High-vowel apocope on *-i**: need a rule (or extension of an existing rule) that deletes the *-i of *-Vmi after a heavy initial syllable, **without** triggering umlaut. Per Fulk, this could equivalently be modelled as an early (pre-i-umlaut) *-i loss specific to the *-umiz/*-umi morpheme.
4. **Unstressed-u fronting rule**: needs a *m-blocker context. It must NOT front *u in the sequence *u m (anywhere in a tail).
5. **i-umlaut rule**: must apply *after* the *-i of *-umi has been removed, to avoid producing *sċyldrum.

## Q8. Tricky paradigm-cell interactions

### Existing DatPl admission

The cascade currently admits `u:{*u} m:{*m}` directly (l. 412), as the post-apocope shorthand. Other DatPl forms in TSV-fed paradigms presumably ride on this. Adding `*-umiz` as an alternative tail does not require removing the shorthand, but eventually the shorthand should be deprecated for clean etymology.

### Potential collisions with other endings

- **NomPl masc. *-ōz** (PGmc): in the cascade, if *-ōz is treated separately (NWGmc *-ō- final → -as) it should not collide with *-umiz. But if the cascade's gate over-permits *-Vz tails, a misanalysis is conceivable. The `pgrmWeakTailVowel` pattern at l. 296ff. lists `ō:{*ō}` and `o:{*o}` separately from *-iz, so it should be safe.
- **NomSg/GenSg *-iz** (i-stem nom.sg. like *gastiz; r-stem gen.sg. like *fadurz): both have the *-iz tail. If a cascade rule keys off *-iz suffixes and doesn't check the preceding context (-um- vs. light root), it could mishandle one or the other. The fix is to make the *-i-apocope rule look at the **whole word's metrics** (heavy initial syllable + post-stem *-umi), not just the local *-iz cluster.
- **AccPl masc. *-anz / *-unz / *-inz**: per RT l. 3231–3243, these had compensatory lengthening (*-ą̄/-ī̄/-ū̄). If the cascade treats final *-z deletion uniformly (as the current `PGmcFinalZDeletion` does), it must be paired with a vowel-lengthening rule for the AccPl case. The *-umiz tail does NOT participate in the lengthening (because the *-iz is unstressed and PWGmc-period, not the post-*-nz cluster).

### Other contexts where *-um- appears

The string `-um-` is **morphologically heavy** in PGmc/NWGmc, recurring in:
- a-stem dat.pl. *-amaz → *-umaz (RT §2.1.2 l. 1788)
- ō-stem dat.pl. *-ōmiz → *-ōmiz (no *a-raising; OE -um from *ōm by §6.8.1 shortening)
- 1pl. pres.indic. *-amaz / *-amiz → *-umaz / *-umiz (RT §2.1.2 l. 1797: "ON berum, OHG berumēs 'we carry' < PNWGmc *berumaz < PGmc *beramaz")
- strong adj. masc./neut. dat.sg. *-aimaz → *-amē → -um (RT §2.1.2 l. 1794–1797; but with more morphological reshaping)
- inst.pl. = dat.pl. throughout (syncretised)
- a-stem n-stem dat.pl. *-an-miz → *-ammiz → *-amiz/-umiz (Bammesberger §7.2.3.3)

So an FST pattern for *-um(iz)* should be expected to cover **all** of these, since they all converge on OE -um. The cascade developer should consider whether to:
- generalise the rule to "*-um(iz) → *-um in all contexts" (clean), or
- subdivide by morphology (verb ending vs. nominal ending), with the same phonological output.

The 1pl. pres.indic. case is structurally identical to the DatPl: heavy or light root + *-um(iz). E.g. *beramaz → *berumaz → OE berum (poetic) / berað (post-syncretism with 3pl). The *m-protection of *u is identical.

## Synthesis & actionable summary for the FST developer

**Reconstruction.** The PGmc a-stem masc./neut. DatPl is *-amaz, the InstrPl is *-amiz; both gave NWGmc *-umaz and *-umiz by *a → *u / _m. Most handbooks (Campbell §331, Brunner §44, Fulk §5.5, Hogg §2.5.1, Bammesberger §7.2.3.3) write the protoform of the OE -um as *-omiz / *-umiz / *-um(m)iz, even though strictly the dat.pl. proper was *-omos/*-amaz; the dat./instr. merger is universal and very early. **The user's choice of *-umiz as the cascade input is etymologically standard** and matches Fulk, Bammesberger, and (post-NWGmc-raising) RT.

**Sound-change chronology to encode.** Following Fulk §5.1 (route Y, supported by Stentoften runic -umʀ):
1. PGmc/early-NWGmc: *a → *u / _m  (yields *-umiz / *-umaz from *-amiz / *-amaz). [RT §2.1.2 l. 1788]
2. Early loss of *-i in *-umiz (specific to this ending; perhaps PGmc itself per Fulk §5.1). Result: *-umz. [Stentoften -umʀ mid-7th c.]
3. PWGmc *-z loss in unstressed position: *-umz → *-um. [RT §3.1.1 l. 3263]
4. PWGmc *-a loss: independent, irrelevant for *-um(iz) per se but eats *-uma → *-um for the dat.pl. proper. [RT §3.1.2]
5. OE i-umlaut (~700 AD): does not affect *-um because the *-i is already gone. [Hogg §3.3.4; explicit at RT vol.2 l. 4019]
6. *u of *-um- is **never** lowered to *o (Campbell §373; Brunner §44; Hogg §3.3.1.3) — the *m blocks unstressed-u lowering.
7. *u of *-um- is **never** fronted in OE — same protective conditioning.

Equivalently, on RT route X: *-z loss first (*-umiz → *-umi), then *-i apocope after a heavy first syllable (*-umi → *-um). Both routes converge.

**Cascade fixes (concrete diagnostics, not patches).**

| Site | Current behaviour | Required behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| `pgrmWeakTailVowel` (l. 296) | admits `u m` shorthand only | also admit `u m i z` as a tail |
| `PGmcFinalZDeletion` (l. 1134) | unconditional `{*z} → 0 / _#` | OK as is for this ending |
| OE/PWGmc *-i-apocope rules | apparently fire on *-Vi anywhere | must (a) preserve *-umi at PWGmc stage (per RT §6.8.1), (b) drop *-i in *-Vmi after heavy initial syllable at OE stage. Alternatively, encode Fulk's early apocope of *-i specifically in the *-umiz morpheme |
| Unstressed-u fronting rules (in `ProtoToOEWeakTail` / English layer) | front *u in tail position regardless of context | must NOT front *u when followed by *m (Campbell §373) — add right context `\| _ m` exclusion |
| i-umlaut rule | applies if *-i present at trigger time | should never see *-i in *-umiz forms because *-i is dropped earlier — guarantee ordering |

**Why the user's earlier probe gave `sċuldreme`:** Two compounding errors:
- The unstressed-u fronting rule fired on the *u of *-um-, treating it like the *u of *-uz (NomSg) or other reducible tails.
- The *-iz reduction rule fired in a way appropriate for nom.sg. *-iz (→ -e after umlaut), not for the polysyllabic *-umi tail (which should silently lose its *-i without leaving any *e trace).

Both are fixed by giving the *-um(iz) tail morphological identity distinct from monosyllabic *-iz / *-uz, and by adding the *m-blocker on unstressed-u fronting.

**Sanity-check forms** the cascade should derive correctly once fixed:

| Input | Expected OE | Test |
|---|---|---|
| *dagamaz / *dagumiz | dagum | a-stem light root |
| *skuldrumiz | sċuldrum | a-stem heavy root |
| *stainamaz / *stainumiz | stānum | a-stem heavy root with *ai |
| *gebōmiz | giefum | ō-stem (parallel) |
| *beramaz / *berumiz | berum | 1pl.pres. (verb) |
| *guman-miz / *gumumiz | gumum | n-stem (parallel) |

The last row is the exception that proves the rule: *twaimi has the *-i preserved long enough to trigger umlaut, because (i) it's a monosyllabic-ish pronominal form, (ii) it's the only context where the ending wasn't levelled away by the dat./inst. syncretism. For all common nominal a-stems, the *-i is gone before umlaut — by route Y (Fulk) phonologically, or by route X (RT) plus paradigmatic levelling.
