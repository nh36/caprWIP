# Dossier: OE *rēc* 'smoke' — the velar-/k/ requirement (2026 follow-up)

**Scope.** This dossier is a **focused supplement** to DEV_NOTES §17.22 and §17.22.13.
It addresses gaps left open in those sections, in particular the **Modern-English-velar
constraint** raised by the user and the **Anglian-smoothing pathway** that §17.22.13
treated only briefly (as "Option E", and rejected too quickly).

§17.22.13's Option B (rewrite proto as `*rōkiz`) gives FST output **rēċ** (palatal),
which is **falsified by ModE *reek* /riːk/**. That recommendation should now be
withdrawn. The lautgesetzlich solution lies elsewhere.

For full background (cognates, paradigm, attestation survey, options A–E), see
DEV_NOTES §17.22.1–13. Cross-references below use the form `§17.22.13.x`.

## §1  Modern reflex evidence: ModE *reek* is /riːk/, velar throughout

### §1.1  Surface facts

| Stage      | Form                          | Velar/Palatal? | Source                                   |

| OE         | **rēc** (NomSg masc.)         | velar [k]      | Clark Hall p. 255 s.v. *rēc m. smoke*; BT p. 110676 s.v. *réc*; Bosworth-Toller spells with c, never ċ; pan-dialectal |

### §1.2  The diagnostic value of velar [k]

OE *c* is graphically ambiguous (palatal [tʃ] or velar [k]); modern dictionaries
mark with a dot only conventionally. The **conclusive** evidence that OE *rēc* had
velar [k], not palatal [tʃ], is the ME/ModE outcome:

- A palatal /tʃ/ outcome would have given ME **\*reche/reech** /reːtʃ/ → ModE
  **\*reech**, parallel to OE *spǣc/sprǣc* > ME *speche/speech* (palatal); OE
  *bēċ* 'beech' > ME *beche* > ModE *beech*; OE *brēċ* (NomSg fem. cons. stem
  'breech-cloth') > ME *breche* > ModE *breeches*.
- The actual ME reflex *rek* and ModE *reek* (homophone of *wreak*) **rule out
  any [tʃ] stage in the OE→ModE history**.

### §1.3  Parallel cases: where OE [tʃ]/[k] split visibly

Campbell §437 explicitly lists the surviving OE velar/palatal doublets that
yield ModE doublets:

> "many forms like NE *like, dike*, Scots *breeks*, beside *such, ditch,
> breeches* are explained from OE doublets like *ġelīċ-ġelīcum*, *dīċ-dīcas*,
> *brēċ-brēc*."  — Campbell §437 (1959, p. 177)

This is precisely the diagnostic we need. The **noun rēc patterns with the velar
member of these doublets** (*dike*, *breeks*) — i.e. it generalises the velar
allomorph from paradigm cells where a back vowel followed *k. The lexeme is
**not orthographically irregular** in OE; the grapheme *c* simply realises [k]
because the phonology that established the velar member of the doublet operated
in this lexeme too.

## §2  Handbook silence vs. parallel cases

### §2.1  Direct handbook treatment of *rēc*

Searches in Campbell, Hogg vol. 1, Luick, Brunner (vision OCR), Ringe & Taylor
vol. 2, Fulk 2018, Kroonen EDPG, Holthausen (we have only Kluge–Seebold
indirectly), Bosworth-Toller, Clark Hall, MED-via-Wiktionary returned **no
explicit philological flag** of *rēc* as irregular in either:

- (a) the i-umlaut chapter (§§Campbell 200; Hogg 5.78–5.93; R/T 6.6.3), nor
- (b) the palatalisation chapter (§§Campbell 426–440; R/T 6.4.1; Hogg 7.30–
  7.50; Luick §685–690).

This silence was already noted in §17.22.13.2.7. **No new explicit handbook
treatment was found.** Not in Luick (the Luick search located the lemma only
indirectly through *smic* 'smoke', §194.1, which is the i-stem **\*smukiz** byform
treated separately, and through unrelated *Rauch* citations in Kluge).

### §2.2  The implicit handbook treatment

Although no author flags *rēc* by name, two converging mechanisms in the standard
literature **fully cover the case** without exception-marking:

**(i) Campbell §§437–438; Luick §690 — paradigmatic levelling of velar/palatal
allomorphs in i-stem and root-stem nouns.**

> "It is more difficult to explain similar alternative forms in nouns, e.g.
> dial. *brig, rig, steek, eg, seg, weg, birk*, ME *benk*, for *bridge, ridge,
> stitch, edge, sedge, wedge, birch, bench*. Luick (Hist. Gramm., §690) would
> explain such forms by assuming that **assibilation did not take place before
> a back vowel**, and hence would derive them from inflected *brycgum, ecgas,
> sticas, bircan*, &c."  — Campbell §438 (p. 177)

The mechanism is exactly the same as for *brēc/breeks* in §437: paradigm cells
with following back vowel (NomPl *rēcas*, GenPl *rēca*, DatPl *rēcum*) preserve
[k]; the velar is then generalised to the NomSg, replacing whatever the
phonologically expected NomSg form was (whether *\*rīeċ* or *\*rēċ*).

**(ii) Campbell §§222–225 — Anglian smoothing of ēa/ē̆o → æ/ē̆ before velars
{k, g, h, x}.**

Campbell §225 lists the relevant lexemes: *bēacen* > *bēcen*; *-bēag* > *-bēg*;
*-lēac* > *-lēc*; *ēagan* > *ēgan*. The crucial chronological remark:

> "smoothing began earlier and was complete before velar umlaut" — Toon, in
> Hogg (ed.) *Cambridge History* vol. 1 ch. 6 (= our `oxford_chapter.txt`,
> lines 20935–6).

If smoothing precedes both i-umlaut and palatalisation in the relevant chronology
(or, more precisely, if its output **bleeds the front-vowel context** that would
have triggered them), then `*raukaz`/`*raukiz` → *rēac* / *rēaki* → smoothing →
*rēc* / *rēki* preserves velar [k] because the trigger is removed before
palatalisation can fire on the noun (Hogg 7.32 similarly notes that "smoothing"
removes the diphthongal off-glide *before* palatalisation feeds on the surface
front vowel, in Anglian). See §3.ε below.

### §2.3  Parallel OE lexemes: distinguishing the two patterns

| OE                | OE (Anglian smoothed) | Modern reflex   | Pattern                          |
|-------------------|-----------------------|-----------------|----------------------------------|
| *bēacen* (WS)     | *bēcen* (Anglian)     | *beacon* /iː-/  | Smoothing local; WS won out      |
| *-lēac*           | *-lēc*                | *leek* /iːk/    | Smoothing + velar; Anglian form generalised at the lexeme; ModE velar |
| *bēaġ* / *bēg*    | *bēg*                 | (lost)          | Smoothing local                  |
| *brēċ* (NomSg fem.) ~ *brēc* (NomPl) | —     | *breech / breeks* (Sc.) | Paradigm doublet (Campbell §437) |
| *dīċ* ~ *dīcas*   | —                     | *ditch / dike*  | Paradigm doublet (Campbell §437) |
| **rēc**           | rēc (universal)       | *reek* /iːk/    | **Same surface as smoothed *-lēc*; ModE velar**, like *leek* |

The closest parallel is **OE *-lēac* → *-lēc* → ModE *leek*** (velar [k], front
long ē): exactly the velar+ē monophthong we need, and it is well-attested as a
smoothing outcome of `*laukaz`. The TSV row 2151 problem is the *rēc* analogue.
Also note: cf. row 859 / 706 / 858 of the TSV (Du. *rook*, Ger. *Rauch*, Eng.
*reek*) all line up with *raukaz/raukiz* without controversy; the OE row alone
diverges, exactly as expected if the OE-specific pathway runs through smoothing.

## §3  Hypothesis evaluation under the velar-/k/ requirement

The five hypotheses (α–ε) are graded against three criteria:

- **(i) velar [k]** in OE rēc (the new hard constraint, from ModE);
- **(ii) monophthongal ē** (long-standing constraint);
- **(iii) lautgesetzlich derivability** under the existing FST cascade or with a
  minimally invasive, well-attested addition.

The hypothesis labels α–ε are the user's; the H1–H7 of §17.22.13.3 map onto
these as noted.

### §3.α  Analogical levelling from oblique cases (≈ §17.22.13.3 H6)

**Mechanism.** PGmc i-stem masc. paradigm: NomSg *raukiz, GenSg *raukez/-as,
DatSg *raukai → -ē, AccSg *raukį, NomPl *raukijiz → -e, GenPl *raukōn → -a,
DatPl *raukimiz → -um. Cells with **front** suffix (NomSg, AccSg, GenSg, DatSg,
NomPl) → palatal; cells with **back** suffix (GenPl, DatPl, plus all derivatives
in `-els`, `-ung`, etc.) → velar. Levelling generalises [k] to NomSg.

This is **exactly the brēc–brēc / dīċ–dīcas mechanism Campbell §437–438 invokes**
and Luick §690 attempts to elevate to a sound-law (§438 reports Luick's
hypothesis but is sceptical, attributing the velar member to Norse interference
in some lexemes; for words like *rēc* with no plausible Norse loan history,
Luick's analogical mechanism remains the standard explanation).

| Criterion              | Verdict                                                  |
|------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| (i) velar [k]          | ✓ Direct prediction (velar from oblique-stem cells)      |
| (ii) monophthongal ē   | ✗ Does **not** explain ē; predicts NomSg *\*rīeċ*/*\*rēaċ* analogically replaced only in stem-final consonant, not in the vowel |
| (iii) lautgesetzlich   | Partial — analogical, not a sound law                    |

**Verdict.** Necessary but not sufficient. Combine with §3.ε (smoothing) for
full coverage of the vocalism.

### §3.β  Palatalisation feeds only on *primary* front vowels

**Claim.** Hogg vol. 1 §7.32–7.36 dates first-fronting palatalisation **before**
i-umlaut, so a *k followed by a vowel that became front *only* via i-umlaut
should remain velar.

**Test.** This predicts velar [k] in *all* OE \*au + i + k/g lexemes. But §17.22.13.5.1
and §17.22.13.5.2 confirm:

- *baukijaną → **bīeċan** 'beckon' (palatal — Campbell §200(5))
- *kaugijaną → **ċīeġan** 'call' (palatal)
- *fláugiz → FST **flīeġ** (palatal)
- *láukiz → FST **līeċ** (palatal)

The palatal outcome in these verbs is universal and uncontroversial. So
palatalisation **does** feed on i-umlauted ē/ie. β is **falsified** as a general
rule. (Hogg's ordering allows *some* late-applying palatalisations; what is
operative for our case is not the chronology but the smoothing-bleeding effect
of §3.ε.)

| Criterion          | Verdict |
|--------------------|---------|
| (i) velar [k]      | Would be ✓ but predicts wrong velar in baukijaną etc. |
| (ii) ē             | Silent  |
| (iii) lautgesetzlich | ✗ predicts wrong outcome elsewhere |

**Verdict.** **Rejected** — overgenerates velar /k/ in *bīeċan*, *ċīeġan*.

### §3.γ  Stem-class shift to a-stem in WGmc

Already discussed as §17.22.13.3 H5. ON *reykr* (with i-umlaut ey) is decisive
*against* a fully PGmc a-stem reconstruction; but a **WGmc-internal** reanalysis
to a-stem is plausible (and supported by OHG *rouh* without umlaut). Under that
view, OE input would be `*raukaz`, FST output `rēac`. Still needs §3.ε
(smoothing) to deliver the monophthong.

| Criterion      | Verdict                                            |
|----------------|----------------------------------------------------|
| (i) velar      | ✓ (no i-trigger ⇒ no palatalisation)               |
| (ii) ē         | ✗ alone (gives diphthong rēac); ✓ if + §3.ε        |
| (iii) lgesetz. | ✓ if WGmc a-stem reanalysis is accepted (cf. OHG)  |

**Verdict.** Strong candidate **when paired with §3.ε**.

### §3.δ  ō-grade nominal *rōkiz (= §17.22.13.3 H1, §17.22.13.8 Option B)

`*rōkiz` → FST **rēċ** (palatal). Falsified by ModE velar /k/ in *reek* (§1
above). The §17.22.13 recommendation that adopted this option must now be
**withdrawn**; it solved (ii) at the cost of (i).

**Verdict.** **Rejected.** Produces palatal ċ, contradicting ModE.

### §3.ε  Anglian smoothing of ēa → ē / _C[+velar] (a-stem variant)

**Mechanism.** Campbell §§222–225, R/T vol. 2 §6.6.4–6.6.5. Anglian (= Mercian
+ Northumbrian) monophthongises **ēa → ē** before /k, g, h/. Examples in §225:

- *-bēacn* → *-bēcen* (BCorpus, VPs, Ru¹: *bēcun, bēcn*)
- *-lēac* → *-lēc*  (most attestations smoothed by Corpus Glossary)
- *-bēag* → *-bēg*
- *ēagan* → *ēgan*

The output of smoothing is a **monophthongal long ē before a velar**, and
crucially the smoothing **bleeds palatalisation** of the following *k/g, because
(a) the trigger is now an unambiguous secondary front vowel, but (b) more
importantly the FST's palatalisation Rule 2 (`{*k} -> {*ʧ} || _ [{*i} | {*ī}]`)
requires *i*/*ī* in the *next* syllable, which is the i-stem ending — gone
already by syncope/apocope by the time smoothing operates on the Tonsilbe.

**Smoothing → FST behaviour.** The current FST does not implement Anglian
smoothing: `*raukaz` produces **rēac** (diphthong, velar), confirmed by direct
probe (§17.22.13.6.3 and reconfirmed today: `echo raukaz | flookup -i
old_english.bin → rēac`). The **minimally invasive, well-attested** addition:

```
# Anglian smoothing — Campbell §§222–225, R/T §6.6.4
# ēa → ē before /k, g, h, x/  (and ēo → ē in same context, not used here)
# Generalised pan-dialectally for high-frequency lexemes (Campbell §225 lists -bēcn, -bēc, -lēc, ēgan)
{*ēa} -> {*ē} || _ [{*k} | {*g} | {*x} | {*h}] (EnglishStarConsonant | .#.)
```

Inserted **before** OEVelarPalatalization (germanic.txt:2625). Order matters:
applying smoothing first removes the diphthong off-glide; then palatalisation
checks its right context. With the i-stem trigger already gone via apocope of
NomSg `*-iz` (Heavy-Syllable Apocope rule, germanic.txt:~2697) and no *i/ī*
following *k, palatalisation Rule 2 does **not** fire ⇒ velar [k] retained.

| Criterion          | Verdict                                                       |
|--------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------|
| (i) velar [k]      | ✓                                                             |
| (ii) ē             | ✓                                                             |
| (iii) lautgesetz.  | ✓ — smoothing is a real, named, well-cited Anglian sound law  |

The pan-dialectal generalisation of *rēc* (≈ §17.22.13.2.6 attestation in WS,
Anglian, and Kentish) is parallel to *bēcen*, which §225 reports as the Mercian
(VPs, Ru¹, Cp) outcome that overwhelms the WS-archaic *bēacen* in compounds.
For very high-frequency everyday lexemes, the smoothed Anglian form is the one
that propagates pan-dialectally — a long-recognised pattern (Hogg 1992 §5.94;
Toon 1983).

**Verdict.** **Strongest candidate** — fully lautgesetzlich, no exception
markers, supported by the bēcen/lēc parallel and the OHG *rouh* a-stem
convergence. Requires (a) shift of TSV PROTOFORM to `*raukaz` (a-stem) **or**
to `*raukiz` with a documented WGmc levelling note, **plus** (b) a smoothing
rule in the FST.

## §4  Recommendation

### §4.1  Revising the §17.22.13 conclusion

§17.22.13.9 recommended: change PROTOFORM `*ráukiz → *rōkiz`, no FST changes.
**That recommendation is wrong** under the (i) velar-[k] constraint, because
`*rōkiz` produces palatal **rēċ**, not velar **rēc**, and ModE *reek* /riːk/
proves the OE was velar (§1.2). It also has the philological cost of
contradicting ON *reykr* (§17.22.13.4.1).

### §4.2  Two viable paths, in priority order

**Priority 1 — Smoothing path (recommended).**

- **TSV row 2151:** PROTOFORM = `*raukaz` (a-stem). Justification: matches OHG
  *rouh* (no i-umlaut, §17.22.13.4.4); explains pan-WGmc monophthongs as the
  joint product of a-stem (no palatalisation trigger, no i-umlaut on root) +
  smoothing (ēa → ē before velar). ON *reykr* is then a Norse-internal
  retention/innovation of the i-stem (NomSg `-iz`-marked a-stem ⇄ i-stem
  conversion is a documented WGmc/PGmc pattern; cf. *fish* < *fiskaz* a-stem,
  but ON *fiskr* unmarked).
- **FST cascade change:** add Anglian smoothing rule before
  OEVelarPalatalization (germanic.txt:2625):

  ```
  define OEAnglianSmoothing [
      {*ēa} -> {*ē} || _ [{*k}|{*g}|{*x}|{*h}] [EnglishStarConsonant | .#.],
      # ēo→ē in the same environment (for completeness; not exercised by row 2151)
      {*ēo} -> {*ē} || _ [{*k}|{*g}|{*x}|{*h}] [EnglishStarConsonant | .#.]
  ];
  ```

  Insert into the cascade **between** OEDiphthongLeveling /
  OEAngloFrisianBrightening and OEVelarPalatalization. Cite Campbell §§222–225
  and Toon in *Cambridge History* vol. 1 ch. 6.

- **Resulting derivation:** `*raukaz → *rauk- → *rēak (a-fronting+breaking) →
  *rēak (no i-umlaut) → rēac → smoothing → rēc`. Final-consonant `*k` is in
  word-final position with *no* following *i/ī* and *no* preceding palatal
  context, so it remains [k]. **Velar rēc, monophthong, lautgesetzlich.**

- **Sibling rows preserved:** rows 857 (Du. rook), 858 (Eng. reek), 860 (Ger.
  Rauch) all derive cleanly from `*raukaz` (or from `*raukiz` for the Norse
  branch, which we don't model here). Row 707 (English *reek* < *raukiz* in the
  current TSV) should be considered alongside row 2151 (currently `*raukiz`)
  for consistency: either both → *raukaz*, or note the cross-branch ablaut
  alternation.

- **Verification probe (after change):**
  ```
  echo raukaz | flookup -i /usr/app/old_english.bin
  # Expected (after rule add): rēc
  ```

**Priority 2 — Levelling path (analogical, philologically defensible but
invokes Schliemann's Razor).**

- **TSV row 2151:** PROTOFORM = `*raukiz` unchanged. Add COMMENT field:
  "NomSg *rīeċ expected; attested *rēc* generalises the velar allomorph from
  oblique cells with back-vowel suffix (GenPl *rēca, DatPl *rēcum, deriv.
  *rēcels*); cf. paradigmatic doublets *brēċ–brēc → ME breech–breeks, *dīċ–
  dīcas → ME ditch–dike (Campbell §437; Luick HG §690). The vowel ē is the
  Anglian-smoothed form generalised pan-dialectally (Campbell §225; cf.
  *bēcen, -lēc*)."

- **FST changes:** none required if we accept this lexeme as analogical;
  alternatively, if we want to derive the velar via paradigm coexistence,
  add the smoothing rule of Priority 1 *and* a paradigm-specific override.
  This is more invasive than Priority 1 and offers no additional explanatory
  power.

### §4.3  Why Priority 1 wins

- **Lautgesetzlich span maximised:** `*raukaz` → `rēc` runs without analogy,
  with one well-attested rule addition.
- **Comparative coherence:** OHG *rouh* (no umlaut) is naturally explained by
  a-stem; ON *reykr* requires only that the Norse branch retained or
  remodelled to the i-stem (a separate, documented PGmc/WGmc pattern of
  i-stem ⇄ a-stem oscillation in monosyllabic neuters/masculines).
- **No new "exception":** smoothing is already a standard part of every
  description of OE phonology since Sievers 1893; the FST simply lacks it
  today.

## §5  Open questions

1. **OS *rēk*, OFris *rēk*, stem class.** Bremmer 2009 (Old Frisian) and Lasch
   1914 (MLG → OS) would clarify whether the OS/OFris cognates are i-stem or
   a-stem. The dossier in §17.22.13.4 left this ambiguous. If they are a-stems,
   that strengthens Priority 1.
2. **Did smoothing operate on long *ēa before *k pan-dialectally for this
   specific lexeme, or did the Anglian form propagate?** Campbell §225 and
   Toon 1978a treat smoothing as Anglian-specific; the WS attestation of *rēc*
   (CP, Or, Ælfric) thus needs a sociolinguistic explanation — high-frequency
   everyday vocabulary often takes the Anglian form (cf. *bēcen* in some WS
   manuscripts). DOEC search would confirm whether any WS manuscript ever
   spells *\*rēac*. §17.22.13.10 already flagged DOEC access as a desideratum.
3. **The verb *rēcan* 'to fumigate'.** Clark Hall p. 255 lists pret. *rēhte*,
   diagnostic of a velar-stem weak-verb formation. This is an independent
   confirmation of velar [k] in the noun (denominal verbs in -jan generally
   inherit the noun's stem-final consonant). The inflectional details of
   *rēcan* would benefit from a parallel mini-dossier; see also Campbell §753.5.
4. **Any 8th-century gloss attestation of *rēac/*rīec*?** A search of Épinal-
   Erfurt-Corpus (`aneightcenturyl00librgoog.txt`) returned no gloss for
   *fumus* with these forms; the earliest secure attestation appears to be VPs
   (Vespasian Psalter, Anglian, ca. 825) per BT line 110676 (citing Ps. Vos.
   67,3). VPs is **Anglian**, where *rēc* is the regular smoothed form — this
   is consistent with Priority 1.
5. **PIE base.** Wiktionary cites *\*rowgi-* (full grade *\*rowg-*); Pokorny
   reconstructs *\*reug-* "to belch, smoke" with *\*roug-/\*reug-* alternation.
   An *o*-grade nominal *\*roug-i-* > PGmc *\*raukiz* is uncontroversial; the
   *\*rauk-az* a-stem we want for OE is then a WGmc (morphological) renewal,
   not a phonological alternative.
