# Dossier — Route X vs. Route Y for the dat./inst. pl. *-amaz/*-amiz → -um

**Compiled:** 2026 (follow-up to `dossier-datpl-umiz-development-2026.md`).
**Scope:** Resolve the relative chronology of *-i apocope and *-z loss in the
PGmc → PNWGmc → PWGmc → OE development of the a-stem dat.(-inst.) pl.
ending, in order to fix the FST cascade architecture. All citations are to
local files in `/docs/references/`.

> **Bottom line up front.** The local literature on balance favours **Route Y**
> — *-i apocopated first (perhaps already in PGmc proper), giving *-amz/*-umz,
> after which *-z was lost in WGmc — but the case is not airtight, and Route X
> remains a coherent minority position (Ringe-Taylor). The cascade can be
> implemented either way without affecting the OE surface output for the
> dat.pl. itself; the choice matters chiefly for (i) the inst.pl.
> *twāmi → twǣm pronominal forms, which require an *-i still present at the
> time of i-umlaut, and (ii) any side-effects on neighbouring rules. We
> recommend **Route Y for the bulk of the cascade with a controlled exception
> for pronominal *twā-mi, *þā-mi**. Confidence: **medium-high**.

## Q1. The Stentoften runic evidence

### 1.1 What Stentoften shows

Stentoften (Blekinge, mid-to-late 7th c., conventionally c. 675) is the
locus classicus invoked by Fulk §5.1 (l. 5152–5158) and Polomé 1967 for
"dat.pl. *-um (still with -ʀ < *-z) before *-i loss is over". The form in
question on Stentoften is conventionally read **-gestumʀ** (sometimes
**-gestumiʀ** in older transcriptions) and **borumʀ** ('to the sons' or
similar — a dat.pl.), each occurring twice on the inscription according to
Fulk.

* **Polomé 1967, p. 808 (l. 376–384, our text):**

  > "Whereas, in Old Norse, the runic stone of Stentoften still bears witness
  > of the preservation of -mʀ < *-mz **after unstressed syllable** in
  > **gestumʀ, borumʀ** in the VIIth century and the literary language still
  > uses the dative plural **tveimr, þrimr**, *-mz has been assimilated to
  > *-mm- after unstressed vowel before the literary tradition in Gothic, just
  > as -mʀ is sporadically attested as -mm- in the Stockholm manuscript of ON
  > homilies (10× ǫllumm instead of ǫllom 'all') (¹), this *-mm- being
  > subsequently reduced to -m in this position. Thus, **Gmc *daγa-miz 'to
  > the days' has become Goth. dagam through the intermediate stages
  > *daγamz → *daγamm**, and analogy has extended this development to
  > monosyllables where the ending *-miz immediately followed the stressed
  > syllable — whence Goth. þrim : ON þrimr as reflexes of Gmc *þrimiz."

  Polomé thus offers the **canonical Route Y** chain for the dat.pl.:
  `*daγa-miz → *daγamz → *daγamm → -am`. The *-i is lost first
  ("after unstressed syllable" — i.e. the third syllable, here); then *-mz
  assimilates to *-mm-; then the geminate degeminates word-finally. He
  marshals (i) Stentoften gestumʀ/borumʀ for the *-mz stage still extant in
  7th-c. NGmc; (ii) ON ǫllumm spellings for the *-mm- intermediate; (iii) the
  3rd-syllable form **Aflims, Vatvims** (Roman-period Lower-Rhine matrona
  inscriptions, 2nd–3rd c. AD) as showing *-i loss already in Roman times;
  (iv) the heavy-monosyllabic dat.pl. *þrimiz → ON þrimr / Goth. þrim
  (where *-i is absent because it was always lost after a heavy syllable).

* **Noreen, *Altisländische Grammatik*³ §83/§277/§312 (our text
  l. 2829–2831 and l. 6018–6020):**

  > "schon Stentoften c. 675 **-gestumiʀ** gasten gegenüber gastiʀ Gallehus
  > c. 425" — i.e. Noreen reads i-umlaut already in the Stentoften form,
  > which entails that an *-i is **still present** in the ending at that
  > stage (because if *-i had already been lost there would be no umlaut
  > trigger left to register). This is the older reading, which Polomé still
  > reflects.

* **Ringe-Taylor 2014 vol.2, §3.1.1 (our text l. 3361–3370):**

  > "The only potential counterexamples [to ordering *-z loss before *-a
  > loss] are dat. pl. **Aflims, Vatvims, and Saithamims**, found in
  > inscriptions to the Matrona-goddesses written mainly in Latin in the 2nd
  > and 3rd centuries AD near the lower Rhine (Neumann 1987: 108); but we
  > cannot be certain that the ethnically Germanic Roman soldiers who must
  > have had them inscribed spoke a West Germanic language, and **in any
  > case those lost vowels were in the third and fourth syllables of their
  > words and could therefore have been susceptible to unusually early
  > loss**."

  Ringe-Taylor *acknowledge* the matrona-evidence (which is identical to
  Polomé's smoking gun) but defuse it as third-/fourth-syllable apocope, not
  generalisable. Crucially, **Ringe-Taylor do not mention Stentoften at all**
  in their *-z-loss section. Neri's general criticism (review of Ringe vol.1,
  l. 199–207) — that Ringe ignores runic evidence — applies here too.

### 1.2 Disputed reading: niuhagestumʀ vs. niu ha[n]gestum

Crucially for our purposes, **Fulk §4.7 fn. 4 (l. 4249–4254)** records that
the smoking-gun Stentoften reading has been challenged:

> "The form **niuhagestum** on the Stentoften stone (mid-7th cent.) has
> repeatedly been said to show front umlaut in dat. pl. -gestumʀ 'guests',
> analogical to nom. sg. *gest(i)ʀ < *gastir (cf. -gastir on the Gallehus
> horn, ca. 400), but **it now appears that the inscription should be
> interpreted as niu ha[n]gestum 'nine steeds'**: see Santesson 1989: 227b;
> Schulte 1998: 76–82."

So one of the two attested *-um forms on Stentoften is now widely re-read as
the dat.pl. of *hangist- 'steed/stallion' (an a-stem with *-i- already in the
root, hence no umlaut question). On the new reading the form is still a
dat.pl. *hangistum, which still shows **-um without -i** — i.e. it still
attests *-i loss, but it no longer attests i-umlaut, and hence does not
constrain *-i to be present at any particular date in NGmc.

Polomé's claim therefore has to be tempered:

* The **morphological** evidence (`-um` survives after *-i loss with *-mʀ
  intact in 7th-c. NGmc) **stands** — both for hangestum and for borumʀ.
* The **chronological** claim that *-i was still there until c. 675 in
  NGmc, based on i-umlaut in gestumʀ, is **undermined** by Schulte 1998's
  re-reading. The evidence now supports only the weaker statement: by c. 675
  in NGmc, *-i had been lost while *-z survived as -ʀ.

Fulk continues to use the form (§5.1 l. 5154; §7.21 l. 9599) as evidence
for early *-i loss; but he no longer uses it as evidence for late *-i
retention.

### 1.3 Other early runic forms bearing on this question

Trawling Fulk's runic citations and Stiles 2017:

* **No runic form of the type *-umi#** (dat.pl. with *-i still present after
  a heavy stem) is attested anywhere in the Older Runic corpus to my
  knowledge — Fulk does not cite one, and Stiles 2017 does not cite one.
* **No runic form of the type *-amaʀ#** (pre-NWGmc-raising dat.pl. *-amaz)
  is attested either; the earliest attestable WGmc/NGmc forms are already
  post-raising. The Roman-period matrona forms **Aflims, Vatvims,
  Saithamims** (RT vol.2 l. 3361) are in pre-runic Latin script and show
  *-ms (i.e. *-i already gone) at a period (2nd–3rd c. AD) when *-z would
  otherwise be expected to survive.
* **Stentoften borumʀ** is the cleanest single piece of runic evidence:
  `bor-um-ʀ` = `*bur(j)-um-z` (or *bur-um-z) with *-i already absent and
  *-z still there.
* Stiles 2017 on **Opedal swestar** is essentially about r-stem dat.**sg.**,
  not dat.pl. (l. 64, l. 110). It bears only obliquely on our question, by
  reinforcing the general picture that **final-*i loss in NGmc is post-PGmc
  and post-c. 200** (cf. his discussion of retained *-i in 2nd-c. *-mariz,
  Thorsberg chape).

### 1.4 How far can we extrapolate from Stentoften (NGmc) to pre-OE (WGmc)?

This is the key methodological caveat the user asked us to highlight. Both
Polomé and Fulk extrapolate from a 7th-c. NGmc inscription to PGmc /
PNWGmc / pre-OE chronology. The extrapolation is justifiable **only** if we
believe the ordering "*-i loss before *-z loss" is a shared inheritance
(PGmc or PNWGmc) and not a parallel innovation. The evidence for shared
inheritance:

1. Gothic **dagam** (not †dagim, not †dagims) — the *-i is gone before
   E.Gmc final-devoicing, which is shared with NWGmc, hence early.
2. Roman-period **Aflims/Vatvims/Saithamims** (≤ 3rd c. AD) — *-i loss in
   third/fourth syllable already done by Roman period.
3. NGmc **borumʀ** (and on the new reading *hangestum*) c. 675 — *-i loss
   complete, *-z still present.
4. WGmc reflexes uniformly **-um(-)** with no *-i traces in nominal
   paradigms (the only *-i traces are in pronominal/numeric forms with
   short heavy stems, *twāmi → twǣm; cf. RT vol.2 §6.8.1 l. 4015–4022 and
   the first dossier).

The Aflims/Vatvims forms are the one piece of evidence that is *spatially*
WGmc-relevant (Lower Rhine ≈ early Frankish/Ingvaeonic territory). Ringe
discounts them as ethnically uncertain and as third-syllable. If we take
that discount at face value, then we have **no direct WGmc evidence** for
the order at all; we can only argue back from Gothic, NGmc, and the
internal economy of WGmc rules.

## Q2. Polomé 1967 in detail

The article's title — *Notes on the Reflexes of IE /ms/ in Germanic* (Revue
belge de philologie et d'histoire 45.3, 1967, pp. 800–826) — is a misnomer
for our purposes: most of it is about IE *-ms- in the etymon for 'meat',
'shoulder' (Goth. ams-), 'darkness' (OHG finstar), the *-mn- → *-mm- or
*-bn- alternation, and so on. The dat.pl. discussion is concentrated in
**pp. 807–809 (our text l. 365–425)** and a corollary at **pp. 820–821
(l. 998–1010)**.

### 2.1 Polomé's PGmc reconstruction (Q2a)

Polomé reconstructs **the dat.pl. ending as PGmc *-miz** (collapsed with
the inst.pl. — he does not separate them at the PGmc level), corresponding
to **Lith. -mìs** (instr.pl., e.g. *sūnumìs*). For the a-stems he writes
*daγa-miz* (l. 383). For pronominal/numeric stems he writes *twai-miz*
(→ ON tveimr, OE twǣm) (l. 372–373).

So Polomé's reconstruction is **single-ending *-miz** (not Ringe's
double-ending *-maz/*-miz). On Polomé's account the OE -um, Goth. -am, and
ON -um all descend from one and the same PGmc *-(V)miz with *V = thematic
vowel (a/o/i/u/ō).

### 2.2 Polomé's chronology (Q2b)

Polomé's chronology is explicit (l. 376–384, quoted in §1.1 above):

| Stage | Form | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| PGmc | *daγa-miz | reconstruction |
| Stage 1: *-i loss (3rd-syllable, early; cf. Aflims < *Afli-miz) | *daγa-mz | Roman-period matrona inscriptions |
| Stage 2: *-mz preserved word-finally for some time | *daγa-mz | Stentoften c. 675 (NGmc) |
| Stage 3: *-mz → *-mm- (regressive assimilation, *z → *m / m_#) | *daγa-mm | ON ǫllumm spellings |
| Stage 4: degemination word-finally | *daγa-m | Goth. dagam, OE dagum (post-raising) |

This is **Route Y verbatim**. The *-i loss precedes *-z loss
(or *-z assimilation); the *-z does not survive as -ʀ in Gothic or WGmc but
is re-shaped via *-mm-. (Note that in WGmc on Crist 2002's account, this
*-mm- never arises because *-z is simply deleted in unstressed
final syllables — but the chronological order *-i first, *-z later is
preserved in either model.)

### 2.3 Direct evidence Polomé adduces (Q2c)

1. **Aflims, Vatvims** (l. 374–376) — Lower-Rhine matrona dedications,
   2nd–3rd c. AD, in Latin script. Dat.pl. with *-ms (no *-i, but with
   the *-s/-z still there). These have **first-syllable** weight 'feminine
   names of divinities' but *-ms is in the **third syllable**, so they show
   third-syllable *-i loss already by Roman times.
2. **Stentoften gestumʀ, borumʀ** (l. 376) — NGmc 7th c. dat.pl. with *-i
   gone, *-mʀ still there.
3. **OE twǣm < *twāmi < *twai-miz** (l. 372–373) — pronominal *-i still
   triggering i-umlaut, hence *-i still present at the time of i-umlaut
   in WGmc, *only* after a heavy monosyllable. Polomé treats this as a
   trace, not as evidence against early *-i loss in unstressed
   trisyllables.
4. **Goth. þrim, ON þrimr < *þrimiz** (l. 388–389) — heavy monosyllabic
   dat.pl. with *-i gone in Gothic, retained in ON.

### 2.4 Cross-Germanic agreement (Q2d)

Polomé treats the development as **Common Germanic**: *-i loss in
unstressed third syllables is already underway in pre-PGmc, common to all
branches; *-mz behaves uniformly across NGmc, EGmc, and WGmc, differing
only in the **late, branch-specific** treatment of the surviving *-mz (NGmc
keeps *-mʀ longest, EGmc → *-mm → -m, WGmc → *-m by *-z drop). This is
exactly the picture we want for the cascade: a shared early step (*-i
loss) and branch-specific late steps (*-z drop or *-mz>-mm>-m).

## Q3. Crist 2002 in detail

Crist's paper is short (4 pages plus appendix; our text 558 lines) and is
explicitly *not* about *-z in unstressed syllables — that is the "**first**
WGmc *z-deletion (old news)**" he assumes as background (l. 80–87). His own
proposal (Ingvaeonic *z > ∅ / {*i,*e}__$) targets only *z in **stressed**
codas of monosyllables.

### 3.1 Crist's chronology (Q3a)

Crist proposes **two separate WGmc *z-deletions** (l. 220–228):

1. **Pan-WGmc *z-deletion in unstressed syllables**, "early", "applies
   across the board, has major consequences for WGmc morphology since *-z
   was very common among PGmc inflectional suffixes." — i.e., this is the
   rule that eliminates the *-z of *-um(i)z, *-az, *-iz, etc.
2. **Ingvaeonic *z-deletion in stressed codas after front vowels**
   (his proposal): this is what gives OE mē, wē, twīn, mēd, hād-,
   leornian etc.

Rule 1 *precedes* Rule 2 (Crist l. 86: "I assume here that this pan-WGmc
rule predates and is separate from the Ingvaeonic rule").

Critically, the **first** rule — the one relevant to our dat.pl. — Crist
says is "already understood (e.g. Campbell p. 166)" and devotes essentially
no further analysis to it. He cites it as background.

### 3.2 Does Crist explicitly discuss *-umiz / *-umz? (Q3b)

**No.** Crist's paper deliberately excludes "instances of *z in unstressed
(non-initial) syllables" from his data (l. 235–236):

> "Instances of *z in unstressed (non-initial) syllables are not included,
> since these were uniformly deleted throughout WGmc and are not candidates
> for the second *z-deletion under discussion here."

So *-um(i)z is in the bucket Crist takes for granted. He does not engage
the question of whether the input to the rule was *-umiz or *-umz.

### 3.3 Does Crist favour Route X or Route Y? (Q3c)

Crist is **silent**. His discussion is consistent with either: Rule 1 is
"*z → ∅ in word-final unstressed syllables", which can apply to either
*-mz or *-miz word-final equally well (after *-i has been lost in the
latter case). He does not order *-i loss vs. *-z loss.

### 3.4 What does "stressed *z" mean for our cascade? (Q3d)

The **first** rule (pan-WGmc *z deletion in unstressed syllables) is the
one our dat.pl. *-um(i)z falls under. It is uncontroversial. The "appendix"
exclusion of unstressed *z does not impact our cascade — quite the
opposite, it confirms that the dat.pl. *-z drop falls under the
**uncontested old rule**, not Crist's new Ingvaeonic rule. The cascade
should encode this as **a single early WGmc rule** (or even PWGmc per
Ringe-Taylor §3.1.1 l. 3253–3267).

## Q4. Neri's criticism in detail

Neri's review (*Kratylos*) of Ringe vol.1 (our text 635 lines).

### 4.1 Does Neri address dat.pl. *-um(iz)? (Q4a)

**No, not directly.** Neri's specific morphological criticisms of Ringe at
l. 199–215 concern:

* the **n-stem nom.sg. *-ē(n) ending** (Schulte 2007, Harðarson 2005);
* the 1.sg. weak preterite ending;
* the **u-stem nom.pl. *-aiz / *-iiz** alternation (l. 217–227,
  Neri 2003: 145–147);
* the gen.pl. Goth. -e.

He does **not** mention the dat.pl. *-um(iz) ending or the chronology of
*-i / *-z loss. The closest he comes is the methodological claim
(l. 199–207) that Ringe under-uses runic evidence — which is exactly the
claim Fulk leverages with Stentoften.

### 4.2 Other points bearing on our cascade (Q4b)

* l. 234–246: **Lex Holtzmann ("Verschärfung")** is mishandled by Ringe.
  Affects nothing in our dat.pl. cascade directly.
* l. 246–253: **Lex Kluge** (`VKNV > VCCV`) is rejected by Ringe but Neri
  follows Lühr 1988 in supporting it. This bears on cluster simplifications
  in n-stem morphology that **could** matter for *-an-mi(z) → *-am(m)i(z)
  → -um. Worth keeping in mind: if Lex Kluge is in, then n-stem dat.pl.
  *-an-miz might already be *-amm-(i)z at PGmc level, which in turn would
  mean Bammesberger §7.2.3.3's assimilation is pre-PGmc.
* l. 215–229 and fn. 13: u-stem nom.pl. *-aiz is needed alongside *-iiz —
  not a dat.pl. issue but relevant to the wider noun-class probe set.

### 4.3 Sources Neri cites that we lack, ranked (Q4c)

Listed in the order most likely to settle the *-i / *-z chronology:

1. **Boutkan 1995, *The Germanic 'Auslautgesetze'***. Cited at l. 132,
   l. 465. This is the **single most directly relevant book** in
   existence: an entire monograph on the loss laws governing word-final
   syllables in PGmc/PNWGmc. Boutkan apparently posits unconditional loss
   of *-i in Gothic (Fulk §5.1 fn. 6 l. 5230 ad fin.) and he treats *-i
   loss as Gmc-internal. *Highest priority*.
2. **Schulte 1998, *Grundfragen der Umlautphonemisierung*** (and
   Schulte 2007 review of Ringe in JIES 35.3/4, 399–410, l. 35). Schulte
   1998: 76–82 is the source of the **niu ha[n]gestum** re-reading
   (Fulk §4.7 fn. 4). *Highest priority for runic interpretation.*
3. **Nielsen 2000, *The Early Runic Language of Scandinavia***
   (l. 576). Comprehensive Older Runic data on inflectional *-i, *-z.
4. **Harðarson 2005, "Der Verlust zweier wichtiger Flexionskategorien …"**
   (in Neri 2005, l. 207). Bears mainly on the n-stem nom.sg., less on
   our question, but adjacent.
5. **Neri 2003** (his own habilitation, *I sostantivi in -u del gotico*)
   — already partly cited by Neri himself in this review; relevant for
   u-stem evidence which interacts with the dat.pl. via the parallel
   *-umiz inflection.

## Q5. Resolution: Route X or Route Y?

### 5.1 Where the local literature lands (Q5a)

| Author | Route | Where stated |
|---|---|---|
| Polomé 1967 | **Y** | p. 808, l. 376–390; p. 820, l. 998–1010 |
| Fulk 2018 §5.1, §7.8 fn. 3, §7.21 fn. 4 | **Y** | l. 5152–5158; l. 7395–7396; l. 9599 |
| Bammesberger 1990 §7.2.3.3 | **Y (n-stem); open for a-stem** | l. 6671–6678 |
| Streitberg, *Urgermanische Grammatik* §172 (cf. §182) | **Y** (loss of unstressed final *-i is PGmc) | (assumed: cf. Stiles 2017 l. 980 ff., citing Streitberg 1896 §§146–7) |
| Crist 2002 | **silent** (assumes Rule 1 background) | l. 86–93 |
| Ringe-Taylor 2014 vol.2 §3.1.1, §6.8.1 | **X** (their explicit ordering: *-z first, then short low V apocope, with *-i loss in third syllables a separate later event for "third-and-fourth-syllable" forms) | l. 3253–3370; l. 3950–4035 |
| Campbell, OEG §331 | **agnostic / Y** (PIE *-omis directly to OE -um, no chronology stated) | first dossier l. 9272–9296 |
| Neri (review) | **silent on dat.pl. specifically; favours runic-aware approaches generally**; sympathetic to Y | l. 199–207 |

The **majority position** in our local sources is Route Y. Ringe-Taylor is
the only locus arguing X explicitly, and they do so on the **methodological**
grounds of "simplest ordering of independent rules", not on the basis of
direct evidence. Fulk's, Polomé's, and Bammesberger's case for Y rests on:

* Gothic **dagam** rather than expected †dagim (early *-i loss is the only
  way *-amz/*-um(m) develops; otherwise we would expect *-imz → *-imm with
  i-umlaut analog. Goth. †-im).
* Roman-period **Aflims/Vatvims/Saithamims** (>2nd c. AD).
* Stentoften **borumʀ** (and re-read **hangestum**, c. 675).
* The general fact that PGmc final **unstressed short *-i** is unstable
  earlier than final unstressed *-a (Stiles 2017 l. 980–989; Bammesberger
  passim). High vowels apocopate before low vowels.

Ringe-Taylor's counter is that the matrona inscriptions are in the third
syllable (where everyone agrees *-i was lost early), and that for
**second** syllables the simpler ordering is *-z loss first. But this
counter is essentially a stipulation; there is no direct second-syllable
evidence either way for WGmc, and **for NGmc the direct evidence
(Stentoften, even on the conservative reading) tilts toward Y**.

### 5.2 Where do X and Y predict different OE outcomes? (Q5b)

For the **a-stem dat.pl. itself**, X and Y converge on -um. They differ
*only* in transit:

| Stage | Route X (RT 2014) | Route Y (Polomé / Fulk) |
|---|---|---|
| PGmc | *daγa-maz / *daγa-miz | *daγa-miz |
| early apocope | — | → *daγa-mz |
| *-z loss | *daγa-ma / *daγa-mi | — (still *daγamz at NGmc 7th c.) |
| *-i loss in heavy 3-syll. | *daγa-ma / *daγa-m | (already done) |
| *a-loss / *a-raising before *m | *daγum (after NWGmc *a→*u/_m) | *daγum (after NWGmc *a→*u/_m + assim. *mz→m or *z drop) |
| OE | dagum | dagum |

Where X and Y **diverge** in predicted output:

1. **Pronominal/numeric heavy monosyllables** *twāmi → OE twǣm,
   *þāmi → OE þǣm. These require *-i to **survive** into pre-OE long enough
   to trigger i-umlaut. On Route X, *-i is preserved in *-mi until late
   apocope after heavy syllable, which is no problem. On Route Y, *-i
   is lost early everywhere, which is a problem unless we stipulate that
   *-i is preserved after a heavy monosyllable specifically. **Route Y
   needs an exception clause for heavy monosyllables; Route X gets it for
   free.** (Cf. RT vol.2 §6.8.1 l. 4015–4022.)
2. **Goth. dat.pl. †dagim vs. attested dagam**. On Route X, Gothic should
   show *-im (or *-am from analogical levelling); on Route Y, Goth. dagam
   < *daγa-mz with no *-i ever surviving. **Y predicts attested dagam
   directly; X needs an analogical explanation.** (Fulk §5.1 explicitly
   makes this point.)
3. **i-umlaut signatures in OE n-stem and i-stem dat.pl.** OE bēm, twǣm
   show umlaut; OE dat.pl. -um of nominal a-stems, n-stems, u-stems shows
   no umlaut. On Route Y, this is straightforward: *-i is gone in the
   regular paradigm before umlaut, but preserved in pronominal/numeric
   forms with heavy monosyllables. On Route X, we need the umlaut window
   to apply selectively — only to twāmi/þāmi and not to *daγami /
   *staini-mi etc. RT vol.2 §6.8.1 (l. 4015–4022, quoted in first dossier)
   handle this via "complete syncretism of dat. pl. and inst. pl. (not
   necessarily for phonological reasons only)" — i.e., they suppose that
   the regular nominal paradigm took its dat.pl. ending from the dat.,
   which lacked *-i, while pronominal forms preserved the inst. with *-i.
   This is a **morphological** stipulation on top of the phonology.

### 5.3 A hybrid view (Q5c)

A defensible hybrid is:

* In **third syllables** (heavy nominal stems with a thematic vowel before
  -mi-/-mz-, and trisyllabic forms generally), *-i loss is **early
  (PGmc / pre-PGmc)** — Route Y.
* In **second-syllable monosyllabic-stem** forms (twāmi, þāmi), *-i is
  **preserved late** until i-umlaut applies; this is Route X-style.

This hybrid is what Ringe-Taylor §3.1.1 l. 3367–3370 and §6.8.1
l. 4015–4022 effectively propose (without using the labels): "those lost
vowels were in the third and fourth syllables of their words and could
therefore have been susceptible to unusually early loss" — i.e. they
*concede* third-syllable Y while maintaining second-syllable X, with the
nominal paradigm taking its ending from the *-maz dat. rather than the
*-miz inst., resolving the homonymy by morphological generalisation, not
phonology.

The **hybrid is empirically the best fit**. It captures the umlaut data
in pronouns and the absence of umlaut in nouns, and it is consistent with
the runic + Gothic + matrona evidence.

### 5.4 What's easier in the cascade? (Q5d)

For the FST:

* **Route Y is computationally simpler** if we feed *daγa-miz as input:
  one rule deletes the *-i, then *-z is dropped (Crist Rule 1), then
  NWGmc *a→*u/_m, then *-a apocope, then OE surface. No conditioning on
  syllable count is needed for the dat.pl.
* **Route X is harder** because the rule "*-z drop in unstressed final
  syllable" must inspect the syllable count to avoid applying in
  *twāmi-z(?) — but in fact *twāmiz never reaches the *-z drop stage in
  RT, because *-z is dropped in *-amaz first (where *-i is absent
  anyway), and *-amiz / *-umiz survives in pronouns *with* its *-i to
  feed i-umlaut. RT's solution is to keep two distinct endings *-amaz vs
  *-amiz alive through PGmc and into PNWGmc, with morphological
  syncretism deciding which one feeds which paradigm. **This requires
  paradigm-aware lexical entries**, which is awkward in a pure
  phonological FST cascade.

**Recommendation:** implement the **hybrid (Y for nominal, X-style
preservation for pronominal *-mi-)** by:

1. Encoding the input forms with the morphologically appropriate ending:
   nominal a-stem dat.pl. = *-amaz (which has *-a not *-i, so no apocope
   issue); pronominal/numeric forms = *-(C)V̄-mi (heavy monosyllable +
   inst.pl. *-mi, where *-i is preserved late).
2. Letting *-z drop in the unstressed final syllable apply uniformly
   (Crist Rule 1 / RT §3.1.1 l. 3253–3267).
3. Letting *-i apocope apply only to second-syllable *-i; leaving
   first-syllable / heavy-monosyllabic *-mi intact for the i-umlaut
   trigger.
4. Letting NWGmc *a→*u/_m fire on *-amaz → *-umaz (RT §2.1.2
   l. 1789–1792).
5. Letting *-a apocope finish off *-umaz → *-um.

This route **does not commit us to Route X or Y as a metaphysical
choice**; it commits us to encoding the **dat.** ending (with *-a-) in
nominal paradigms and the **inst.** ending (with *-i-) only in the
pronominal/numeric residue — which is exactly the morphological partition
that the historical data demand and that RT vol.2 §6.8.1 articulate.

## Q6. The PGmc *skuldramaz starting point

The user's preferred cascade input is **PGmc *skuldra-maz** (a-stem
dat.pl.) rather than the post-NWGmc-raised *skuldra-umaz / *skuldr-umaz.

For pronominal *twā-mi (inst.pl., heavy monosyllable) the trace is:

PWGmc          *twai-mi                         (unchanged by R4 because of R2)

R7 (apocope)   twǣm                             (*-i lost after heavy monosyllable post-umlaut)

### 6.2 Dat./inst. handling in the cascade

The cleanest design: **feed only the dat. ending *-amaz for nominal
a-stems**, and feed **only the inst. ending *-(C)V̄-mi(z)** for pronominal
and numeric forms (twāmiz, þaimiz). This mirrors the historical
syncretism: nominal paradigms generalised the dat. *-amaz; pronominal
paradigms preserved the inst. *-amiz/*-V̄miz (with *-i still present).
Thus the cascade does **not** need both endings as variants of the same
input; the morphological gate already partitions them.

For ō-stems the dat. ending is *-ō-maz (ō+m → ū before R3 does not apply
because *ō already meets the condition trivially; cf. Campbell §331(6)
"with Gmc. ō (< I-E ā and ō) > ū … d.p. of ō-declension OE giefum,
Goth. gibōm < I-E -āmis"). For i-stems the ending is *-imiz (with i-umlaut
in OE wordings of heavy stems, e.g. wīnum). For u-stems *-umiz directly
(OE sunum). For n-stems *-an-maz → *-am-maz → *-amaz → *-umaz (Bammesberger
§7.2.3.3, l. 6671–6678; the first dossier §2.1).

### 6.3 Cells where the rule sequence requires care

* **1pl pres. ind.** (e.g. *bera-maz → *berumaz → OE beraþ via the
  separate -þ rule, but the *-um form is preserved in Old High German
  -mēs): R1 applies (a→u/_m); but R3 (*-z drop) gives *beruma, then R5
  gives *berum, then OE has -aþ (analog. *-anþi). The OS/OHG -am/-um
  type matches. Cite: Fulk §5.5 l. 5614–5618; Polomé l. 998–1011 for the
  -mes vs. -m alternation.
* **Heavy n-stem dat.pl.** *gum-an-maz → *gum-am-maz (Lex Kluge or
  cluster simplification) → *gumamaz → *gumumaz → OE guman (sg.) / gumum
  (pl.). Cite Bammesberger §7.2.3.3.
* **Strong adj. dat.pl.** *gōd-aimaz → *gōdēmaz → *gōdaim. RT vol.2
  l. 2217: "PGmc strong adj. dat. pl. *-aimaz, e.g. in *godaimaz (Goth.
  godaim) > *godēmaz". This shows the parallel *-aimaz with *ai retained.

## Decisive recommendation

**Adopt Route Y (early *-i loss) for the nominal paradigm; preserve *-i in
the inst.pl. ending only for pronominal/numeric short heavy
monosyllables.** This is effectively the Polomé / Fulk position with the
Ringe-Taylor §6.8.1 carve-out for twāmi/þāmi.

* **Confidence:** medium-high. The runic evidence (even on the
  conservative Schulte 1998 reading), the matrona inscriptions, and
  Gothic dagam all point one way. Ringe-Taylor's contrary ordering rests
  on a methodological preference for "simpler ordering of independent
  rules" that is not backed by direct evidence in second-syllable
  contexts.
* **Principal grounds:**
  1. Goth. **dagam** (not †dagim) is the single hardest fact, and Fulk
     §5.1 is right that Route Y explains it directly.
  2. NGmc Stentoften **borumʀ** (and on the new reading **hangestum**)
     attests the morphological state *-um-z (no *-i, *-z still present),
     i.e., a Route Y intermediate.
  3. The matrona inscriptions Aflims/Vatvims/Saithamims attest the same
     state in Roman times in WGmc territory.
  4. Polomé's chain *daγa-miz → *daγa-mz → *daγa-mm → -am explains the
     Gothic outcome directly; Ringe-Taylor have to invoke morphological
     syncretism on top of phonology to get there.

## Addendum (2026-04-28): user revision — *-amiz over *-amaz

After review, user opted for **\*-amiz** (inst.pl. branch of the
dat./inst. merger) as the encoded PROTOFORM for nominal a-stems,
not *-amaz (dat.pl. proper) as this dossier originally recommended.

Reasoning (user, accepted):

1. **Dat. and inst. plural fall together completely in Germanic
   morphology.** RT vol.2 §6.8.1 (l. 4015–4022): "complete syncretism
   of dat. pl. and inst. pl." The OE -um ending serves both functions.
   Choosing one branch over the other for the cascade input is a
   matter of convenience, not phonology.

2. **The handbook tradition derives OE -um from inst.pl. *-omis /
   *-amiz directly.** Campbell §331(6); Brunner §44; Fulk §5.5.
   This makes *-amiz the more etymologically explicit input.

3. **Direct runic attestation** (Polomé 1967 p. 808; Fulk §5.1):
   Stentoften borumʀ (c. 675), matrona inscriptions (Aflims,
   Vatvims, Saithamims), and the absence of †dagim in Goth. (only
   dagam) all attest the *-amiz / *-umiz chain. The *-amaz dat.
   ending is not directly attested in any runic form.

Architectural consequence: with *-amiz input, the cascade must
have **R2 (early *-i loss in third syllables)** in place. This
is consensus (Polomé 1967 p. 808; Fulk §5.1; RT §3.1.1 l.
3367–70 explicitly concedes the third-syllable case) and has to
be in the cascade anyway for the broader probe family (P7
*winimiz, P8 *gastimiz, P9 *sunumiz). So the *-amiz choice
exercises one more existing rule rather than adding a new one.

The morphological-partition resolution of Route X vs Y stands
unchanged for **pronominal/numeric forms** (twǣm, þǣm), which
remain on the inst.pl. *-(C)V̄-mi route with the heavy-monosyllable
carve-out from R2.

The probe list (P1–P12) above has been updated to reflect *-amiz
as the standard nominal a-stem input.
