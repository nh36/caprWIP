# Dossier: PGmc → OE history of 'to spare' (`*spar-`)

**Scope.** Inform the TSV PROTOFORM choice for row 2205
(`PROTOFORM=*spárēną`, `COUNTERPART=sparian`, `gloss='spare'`). The current
PROTOFORM is a class-III reconstruction; the OE target is a class-II form.
This dossier evaluates whether to (A) switch the PROTOFORM to a class-II
"transponent" `*spárōjaną`, (B/C) retarget to one of the Northumbrian
Durham-Ritual relics (`spæria`, `spær`, `spærede`), (D) keep two rows, or
(E) something else. Recommendation at end.

---

## 1. The PGmc reconstruction of `*spar-`

### 1.1 What the etymological dictionaries reconstruct

**Kroonen (EDPG p. 465**, s.v. `*spara-` adj.):

> "ON sparr adj. 'id.', OE spær adj. 'id.', E spare, MDu. spaer adj. 'id.',
> OHG spar adj. 'sparse' > *sphₕ₂-ro- (IE) — Etymologically identical to Skt.
> sphirá- adj. 'fat', Lat. pro-sperus adj. 'favorable, lucky'. … Also cf.
> ON spara, OE sparian, OFri. spara, OS sparon, Du. sparen, OHG sparēn,
> sparōn, G sparen ww. **'to spare, save' < `*sparēn-`**."

Kroonen lemmatises the verb explicitly as **class III `*sparēn-`**, with
no Gothic cognate, and notes the OHG doublet `sparēn ~ sparōn`.

**Orel (HGE p. 363**, s.v. `*sparēnan` wk.vb.):

> "ON spara 'to spare', OE sparian id., OFris sparia 'to receive, to
> protect', OS sparòn 'to spare', OHG sparèn id. Derived from *sparaz."

Orel lists **only `*sparēnan`** (no `*sparōnan` doublet at the PGmc level —
the user's prompt overstated this). The verb is denominal from the
adjective `*sparaz` (ON sparr, OE spær).

**Kluge–Seebold (²⁵p. 859**, s.v. *sparen*):

> "g. `*sparā-` Vsw. 'sparen', auch in anord. spara, ae. sparian, afr. sparia.
> Die alte Bedeutung ist 'bewahren, sicher behalten'… Heidermanns (1993),
> 532f."

K-S supply the same set of cognates and flag Heidermanns 1993 as the locus
classicus for the morphological details.

**Ringe / Taylor vol. 2** (the most explicit treatment for our purposes).
Two parallel passages:

- **p. 162** (NWGmc innovations, §3.3.2):
  > "PNWGmc `*sparai- ~ *sparja-` (`*spara-`?) 'spare' (ON spara) >—
  > PWGmc `*sparē-` (OHG sparēn ~ sparōn) — `*sparē- ~ *sparēja-` >
  > OE (North., Rit.) **spæria** 'to spare', pres. iptv. sg. **spær**
  > (with various levellings and remodellings, though the front vowel in
  > the root points to a class III present); otherwise class II, cf. OE
  > class II (WS) sparian, (southwestern Merc.) spearian."

- **p. 191** (OE sound-changes, A-restoration):
  > "PNWGmc `*sparja-` (`*spara-`?) `~ *sparai-` 'spare' (ON spara) >
  > PWGmc `*sparē-` (OHG sparen) >— **`*spærōjan` > OE sparian** (OF
  > sparia 'to keep safe')."

R/T's chain is therefore:

```
PNWGmc  *sparja- ~ *sparai-  (≈ Kroonen's *sparēn-)
   │
   ↓  (class-III generalisation in WGmc)
PWGmc   *sparē-               ─── Anglian relics: *sparē- ~ *sparēja-
   │                                ↳ Rit. spæria, spær, spærede
   ↓  (class III → class II refashioning, NWGmc innovation)
pre-OE  *spærōjan             ─── OE (WS) sparian
```

### 1.2 Cognate class assignments

| Language | Form | Class | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gothic | (unattested) | — | Verb not preserved; cf. only adj. †(`*sparʷa-`) and noun `sparwa` 'sparrow' (different etymon). |
| ON | `spara` | III (in ON system: `*-ai-` stem) | Inherited. |
| OHG | `sparēn` ~ `sparōn` | III ~ II | Doublet. The III form is the conservative member; -ōn is the refashioning shared with OS. |
| OS | `sparon` | II | WGmc innovation (class III → II). |
| OFris | `spara` | II | WGmc innovation. |
| OE | `sparian` (WS), `spæria/spær/spærede` (Rit.) | II + III relics | See §2. |

**Verdict.** The PGmc verb is securely reconstructable as class III
`*sparēn-` from ON+OHG (and the consistency of Heidermanns' analysis with
Kroonen and Orel). The class-II form `*sparōn-` / `*sparōjaną` is **not**
PGmc: it is a WGmc / NWGmc innovation, perhaps already incipient as an
OHG-internal doublet. Reconstructing `*spárōjaną` for PGmc is therefore a
back-projection from the post-WGmc evidence.

### 1.3 IE etymology

Both Kroonen and K-S derive the adj. `*spara-` from PIE `*sph₂-ro-`
(Skt. *sphirá-* 'fat', Lat. *prosperus*). The verb is denominal from the
adjective; the IE etymology bears on the adjective, not on the
weak-verb class assignment, which is a Germanic-internal matter. No
case for original class II from the IE side.

## 2. OE weak class III and the Durham Ritual relics

### 2.1 OE class III as a category

OE class III is a **residual** class with only four full members in WS:
`habban, libban, secgan, hycgan` (Campbell §762 paradigm, p. 339;
Brunner §415 ff.). All other PGmc class-III verbs were reanalysed into
class II during WGmc and pre-OE, leaving only morphological "traces"
(Campbell's three diagnostics, §763): paradigmatic alternation between
forms with vs. without (a) i-umlaut, (b) consonant gemination, plus
(c) idiosyncratic 2/3 sg. endings.

Campbell §764 p. 340 gives a long list of "verbs which … may reasonably
be regarded as having been originally Class III verbs in OE", including
`bifian, bismerian, fetian, hlinian, sorgian, sparian, þolian, wunian,
…`. For most, only Anglian (esp. Northumbrian) traces survive; the WS
norm is class II.

### 2.2 Campbell §764 p. 340 on `sparian` (verbatim)

> "**sparian** spare: this verb does not show any of the characteristics
> listed above, but **Rit. inf. spæria, imper. spær, past -spærede**
> beside VP forms with back mutation (pres. indic. 3 sg. spearad, &c.)
> suggest Prim. OE forms both with and without back vowels in the
> syllables after the root syllable, hence conjugation according to
> Class II or Class III."

Campbell's **explicit reading** of the Rit. paradigm is therefore that
it is a **mixture** of class-II and class-III cells, not a clean
descendant of a single PGmc class-III paradigm. R/T (p. 162) say the
same: "with various levellings and remodellings".

### 2.3 Cell-by-cell morphological analysis of the Rit. forms

| Form | Cell | What its shape implies | PGmc input cell |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`spæria`** (inf.) | infinitive | Anglian -ia = class II `-ian` ending; but root vowel `æ` shows i-umlaut. So this is a **class-II ending grafted onto a class-III stem with `*-ēja-` umlaut**. R/T: "*sparēja-* > spær(ija)". | `*sparējaną`-type (with `*-j-`) |
| **`spær`** (imper. sg.) | bare imperative | Class-III imperative had bare stem in `*-ē` (cf. Goth. *habái*). PGmc `*sparē` → pre-OE `*spæri` → OE `spær` after weak-tail apocope, with i-umlauted root. **Cell-internally consistent with class III.** | `*sparē` |
| **`spærede`** (past) | wk. past 1/3 sg. | -ede ending = class-II Anglian past (Campbell §757 p. 333: "sg. -ede, occurs in all dialects"); but `æ` in root again requires i-umlaut. So a **class-II past inflection on a stem that has been front-vocalised by analogy** with the present cells in `*-ēja-`. | mixed: class-II `*sparōdē` ending + analogical `æ` from the present |

So of the three Rit. cells, only **`spær`** is a clean unbroken descent
from a single PGmc class-III cell. **`spæria`** and **`spærede`**
require analogical mixing (class-II inflection grafted onto a
class-III-i-umlauted stem).

R/T p. 162 summarise: the front vowel "points to a class III present"
(via the `*-ēja-` alternant), but the paradigm shape is the result of
"various levellings and remodellings" — i.e. the Rit. paradigm is
itself a hybrid, not a regular reflex.

## 3. Lautgesetzlich derivation in the CAPR pipeline

### 3.1 FST probes (`docker compose exec backend bash -lc 'echo … | flookup -i /usr/app/old_english.bin'`)

| PGmc input | FST output | Attested? |
|---|---|---|
| `spárēną` (class-III inf.) | **`sparen`** | ✗ no OE form `sparen` for 'spare' |
| `spárōjaną` (class-II inf.) | **`sparian`** | ✓ WS attested |
| `spárē` (bare iptv. / class-III) | **`spære`** | ≈ Rit. `spær` (FST keeps a final -e) |
| `spárēsi` (2 sg. pres. III) | **`spæres`** | (Anglian-style) |
| `spárō` (bare iptv. / class-II) | **`sparu`** | (1 sg. pres. type) |
| `spárōjun` (3 pl. class II) | **`sparion`** | (cf. WS sparia(þ)) |
| `spáraz` (adj. nom. sg.) | **`spær`** | ✓ OE adj. `spær` |
| `spárjaną` (j-class I) | **`sperġan`** | (with j-gemination, not 'spare') |
| `spárai` (`*-ai` cls-III) | **`spære`** | (parallel to `spárē`) |
| `spárēdē`, `spárōdē`, `spáridē` (past-tense forms) | **`+?` (rejected)** | — pipeline lacks a recognised input slot for the bare past stem of class III/II |
| `spárējiþi`, `spárejun` | **`+?` (rejected)** | — pipeline does not accept a `*-ēja-` alternant for class III |

Salient consequences:

1. **`spárēną → sparen`**. The pipeline derives a regular class-III
   infinitive output `sparen`, which corresponds to **no attested OE
   form** for this lexeme. The FST has no `*-ēja-` alternant for
   class-III stems, so it cannot produce `spæria`.
2. **`spárōjaną → sparian`** is the only PGmc input that yields an
   attested OE infinitive cleanly.
3. **`spárē → spære`** is *almost* `spær`; the residual `-e` would be
   apocopated by the OE rule that removes weak-tail `-e` in monosyllabic
   imperatives (cf. Campbell §766 sg. `sæge`, but `hafa` after
   class-II refashioning). That apocope is not currently realised in
   the FST for this exact input, so `spær` is **not produced**, only
   approximated.
4. **No past-tense input is accepted by the pipeline.** The FST is
   keyed on a small set of citation-form morpheme shapes (infinitive
   `*-ną`, nominative `*-az`, etc.). `*spárēdē` and friends are
   rejected, so `spærede` cannot be derived as a cell-target at all.

## 4. Lautgesetzlich-span comparison and recommendation

### 4.1 Comparison table

| # | Candidate PGmc input | Indep. reconstructable? | Predicted regular OE output (our pipeline) | Attested OE form? | Lautgesetzlich span PGmc → OE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | `*spárōjaną` | **No** for PGmc; yes for PWGmc (R/T's `*spærōjan`) | `sparian` | ✓ WS `sparian` | **PWGmc → OE clean.** From PGmc, requires ONE analogical step (class III → II refashioning) but that step is **before** the FST input, so the FST's derivation chain is uninterrupted. |
| B | `*spárēną` → target Rit. iptv. **`spær`** | **Yes** | `spære` (≈ `spær`, modulo final-`-e` apocope not realised) | ✓ Rit. `spær` | Almost clean: a single missing apocope rule for monosyllabic class-III imperatives. | C₁ | `*spárēną` → target Rit. inf. **`spæria`** | **Yes** for the class-III stem; the `*-ēja-` alternant is PWGmc-level | `sparen` | ✗ does not match `spæria` | **Broken span.** The FST has no `*-ēja-` alternant for class III, so it cannot model the i-umlauted `æ` of the Rit. infinitive. R/T explicitly call this OE form "remodelled". |

| E | Add a `*-ēja-` alternant to FST class III + monosyllabic-iptv apocope; then `*spárēną` → `spæria/spær` directly | **Yes** | `spæria, spær` | ✓ ✓ | Engineering effort beyond a single TSV row. |

### 4.2 Reasoning

The project's stated ethos prefers the **longest unbroken lautgesetzlich
span from a genuinely reconstructable PGmc form to an actually attested
OE form**. Two opposing pressures:

- **Span length.** `*spárēną → spær` (Option B) is the longest single
  unbroken cell-derivation: the PGmc class-III imperative is preserved
  morpheme-for-morpheme into the Rit. cell. Modulo a one-rule apocope
  fix in the FST, the chain is fully Lautgesetz.
- **Scope/representativeness.** A WS infinitive `sparian` is the
  canonical OE citation form (it is what `tokens` and `IPA` columns
  encode in row 2205). Option B targets a Northumbrian imperative that
  is sparsely attested (Durham Ritual only) and morphologically a
  different cell from what the rest of the row encodes.

Option C₁ (target Rit. inf. `spæria`) **superficially looks like the
best of both worlds** — same infinitive cell as WS, plus a
freestanding class-III PGmc input. But it does **not** in fact give a
clean Lautgesetz span, because:

1. The FST has no `*-ēja-` alternant for class III, so `*spárēną`
   regularly yields `sparen`, not `spæria`.
2. The Rit. `spæria` itself is a **hybrid cell** (class-III stem +
   class-II `-ia` ending), as both Campbell §764 and R/T p. 162 spell
   out. So even a perfect FST could only reach it via an analogical
   step inside OE.

Option C₂ (target Rit. past) is worse on both grounds (FST rejects
input; OE form is also hybrid).

Option A (TSV → `*spárōjaną → sparian`) places the analogical break
**before** the PROTOFORM, so within the FST everything is regular. The
cost is that `*spárōjaną` is a PWGmc-level reconstruction labelled as
PGmc — but the same is implicitly true of every TSV PROTOFORM that
encodes a WGmc-level innovation (cf. all class-III → II refashioned
verbs already in the lexicon: `wacian, þolian, wunian, sorgian,
hlinian, bifian, sparian` — the same set). It is the project's
established convention.

Option B as a **second** row alongside Option A (i.e. **Option D**)
captures both witnesses and is the most "honest" representation of the
lexeme's split history. It costs one extra row and a one-rule FST
patch (final-`-e` apocope in monosyllabic class-III imperatives) to
produce `spær` exactly.

### 4.3 Final recommendation

**Primary: Option A.** Switch the row-2205 PROTOFORM from `*spárēną` to
**`*spárōjaną`**, and update the NOTE to reflect the WGmc origin:

> **NOTE:** WS sparian is the OE class-II refashioning of an inherited
> PGmc class-III verb `*sparēn-` (ON spara, OHG sparēn; Kroonen p. 465,
> Orel p. 363). The class-II remodelling is a NWGmc innovation
> (`*spærōjan`, R/T vol. 2 p. 191). Anglian relics of the original
> class-III paradigm — Rit. `spæria, spær, spærede` (Campbell §764
> p. 340) — survive but reflect a hybrid paradigm with various
> levellings (R/T p. 162); none of them is the regular Lautgesetz
> output of any single PGmc cell in the current FST.

Reasoning: among single-row options, A is the only one whose chain is
fully Lautgesetzlich within the FST. B is "longer-span" only for one
non-canonical cell (Rit. imper.) and requires an FST patch. C₁/C₂ are
broken by the OE-internal hybridity that Campbell and R/T flag.

**Secondary, if the project wants to witness the class-III heritage:
Option D.** Add a sister row:

| ID | TOKENS | PROTOFORM | COUNTERPART | NOTE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2205 | s p a r i a n | `*spárōjaną` | sparian | WGmc class-II refashioning (R/T p. 191). |
| 2205b | s p ǣ r | `*spárē` | spær | PGmc class-III imperative; Rit. relic (Campbell §764). Requires a one-rule fix in the FST for monosyllabic-imperative final-`-e` apocope to derive `spær` exactly (currently yields `spære`). |

Option D best fits the stated ethos *if* a small FST patch is
acceptable: it actually realises the longest unbroken Lautgesetz span
(PGmc cell → OE attested cell, no analogical step) **for one cell**,
while preserving Option A for the canonical WS infinitive.

**Not recommended: Options B, C₁, C₂, E.** B alone abandons the WS
infinitive that the row's `tokens`/`IPA` columns encode. C₁/C₂ are
factually broken by Campbell's and R/T's analysis of the Rit.
paradigm as a hybrid. E is a disproportionate engineering cost for one
verb.
