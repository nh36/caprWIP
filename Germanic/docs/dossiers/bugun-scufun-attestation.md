# Attestation of bugun / sċufun in Old English

## Question

Appendix D.2 of `widuwe-u-preservation.md` established, on the
authority of Campbell §735(e), Brunner §364.2 Anm. 4, Bülbring §364,
and Luick §326, that the **paradigmatic** strong-verb pret. pl. ending
in the oldest OE texts is `-un` (Épinal exclusively; Corpus, Vespasian
Psalter (VP), and Rushworth-1 (R¹) dominantly), and that `-on` is a
late West-Saxon analogical levelling. That is a statement about the
*ending* in the abstract. The present dossier asks the narrower
**lexeme-specific** question: is the 3 pl. pret. of *bēogan / búgan*
'to bow' actually attested anywhere in the OE corpus as `bugun`, and
is the 3 pl. pret. of *sċūfan* 'to shove' actually attested as
`sċufun` (or `scufun`, `sceofun`, etc.)? Or do these two specific
verbs only happen to surface with `-on` even though the wider `-un`
pattern is well attested for other Class-II strong verbs?

## bēogan / búgan — pret. pl.

### Local handbook evidence

**Bosworth-Toller** (`docs/references/bosworth_toller_anglo_saxon_dictionary.vision.txt`)
— the entry s.v. `búgan` and the supplement entry `bugan` (line ≈18013
ff.) cite the following pret. pl. tokens, all of them with `-on`:

> "burgware þe þā burg ǣr budon, **Chr. 919**; P. 100, 12.  þæt
> mennisc and eorlas georne him to **bugon**, **Chr. 959**; P. 114,
> 24."  (s.v. `búgan` II)

> "Hi **bugon** and flugon they turned and fled, **Chr. 999**; P. 131,
> 22."

> "Ealle men him to **abugon** and him āðas sworon, **Chr. 1086**;
> P. 143, 14."

> "**þā bugon** gehwylce ūs wǣron wīðtogone, **Gr. D. 256**, 21."
> (Gregory's Dialogues, late WS / 11c.)

> "be him to **gebugon**, **Chr. 901**; P. 92, 7."

> "**Wylisce men** … hi fram þām cynge **gebugon**, **Chr.** [s.a.
> 1063]" (Toller s.v. `gebūgan`)

> "**þā nigon werod bugon** tō heora Scyppende, **Hml. Th.** [Ælfric]"

> "Tō manega of þām folce … tō þām hǣðengilde **bugon**." (Toller,
> supplement)

> "Hi **onbugon** tō þām wynsuman ġeoke wuldres cyninges, **Hml. S.**
> 29, 178." (Ælfric, *Lives of Saints*)

> "Þām wǣpenleāsum menn **onbugon** þā Norþ-mann." (Toller, supplement
> s.v. `onbūgan`)

> "Him **onbugon** þā Francan tō þām cyninge." (Toller s.v.
> `onbūgan`)

> "Wylisce men syððan hi fram þām cynge **gebugon**." (Chr.)

> "**genihtsumlīce gebugon** (uberes fructus ager attulit, Lk. 12,
> 16), Wlfst." (Wulfstan)

> "**Hī onbugon** þā fyrlenan norðdǣlas, Hml. Th. i. 106, 21."

> "**þæt wē bugon** fram yfele and dōn gōda, Coll. M. 33, 9."
> (Ælfric's Colloquy)

Toller's *Supplement* entry `bugan. Add:` (line 122487) likewise gives
`þā ābūgendan declinantes, Ps. L. 124, 5` (a participle from the
Lambeth Psalter — a late WS gloss, not VP/R¹, and not a finite pret.
pl.).

**Verbatim**: every pret. pl. citation in Bosworth-Toller for
*búgan*, *ābūgan*, *onbūgan*, *gebūgan*, *forbūgan*, *bebūgan*,
*tōbūgan*, etc., spells the ending `-on`. **No `bugun` is cited.**

**Clark Hall** (`docs/references/clark_hall_concise_anglo_saxon_dictionary.vision.txt`,
line 4567): "**būgan** to turn back, flee. clǽne b. habban …";
principal parts given as `būgan, beag, bugon, bogen` (paradigm
header). No locus citations of any `bugun` form.

**Sweet's Anglo-Saxon Primer**
(`docs/references/sweet_anglo_saxon_primer.txt`, line 1891):

> "bugan (bow)   bȳhþ   beag   **bugon**   bogen"

i.e. the pret. pl. is given canonically as `bugon`.

**Bright's Anglo-Saxon Reader**
(`docs/references/bright_anglo_saxon_reader.vision.txt`, line 16965):
"būgan, beag **bugon** bogen". The text-portion of Bright (the *Battle
of Maldon*, line 185) reads: "**Hi bugon** þā fram beaduwe."

**Campbell §735(e)** (`campbell_old_english_grammar.txt`, line
20229ff.) and **Brunner §364.2 Anm. 4**
(`brunner_1965_altenglische_grammatik.vision.txt`, line 14580ff.)
make general statements about the `-un` ending in Ép./Cp./VP/R¹ but
**neither cites `bugun` as a specific token**.

**Bülbring §364** (line 6033, in the §302 area) cites a Northumbrian
form of `sċūfan`'s pret. pl. (`scyufon` Ri. 'shoved') but **not** any
form of `búgan` pret. pl. with `-un`. **Luick §326** likewise does
not adduce *búgan* pret. pl. as an exemplar.

### Direct corpus attestations

**None located.** Every pret. pl. token of `būgan` / `ābūgan` /
`onbūgan` / `gebūgan` etc. that I could find in the standard
lexicographic record (Bosworth-Toller, Toller's Supplement, Clark
Hall) is spelled `-on`. The earliest text attesting any pret. pl. of
this verb in BT is the *Anglo-Saxon Chronicle* annal s.a. 901
(`gebugon`) and 919 (`budon` = inhabited; pret. pl. of `būan`, not
*būgan*; the verbs are conflated in the BT entry but the `bug-` forms
are 10–11c. Chronicle and Ælfric).

The early Anglian/Mercian texts that *would* show `-un` if they
preserved this verb in 3 pl. pret. — the Épinal, Erfurt, and Corpus
Glossaries; the Vespasian Psalter; the Rushworth-1 gloss to Matthew —
**do not happen to contain a finite 3 pl. pret. of `búgan`**. (The
Latin Psalter's verbs of bowing are mostly singular: `inclinavit`,
`incurvavit`; where plural occurs, e.g. *incurvaverunt animam meam*
Ps 56:7, the VP gloss uses the causative `gebēgdun` ~ `gebēgdon`,
i.e. weak `bīegan` 'to bend (something)', not the strong intransitive
`būgan`.) The `-un` Anglian preterite-plurals well-attested for other
verbs (e.g. VP `forleortun`, `fornōmun`, `āwoestun`, `gnornadun`,
`edwitadun`, `cynisse gestihtadun` — see BT under the relevant
entries) therefore have no `bug-` analogue extant.

### Verdict

**`bugun` is not attested in the OE corpus** in any source I can
verify. The verb's pret. pl. is robustly attested as `bugon` (and
prefixed `abugon`, `onbugon`, `gebugon`, `forbugon`, `bebugon`,
`tōbugon`) but only in the *Anglo-Saxon Chronicle* (10th–11th c.
annals), Ælfric (late WS, c. 990–1010), Wulfstan (early 11c.),
Gregory's Dialogues (late WS), the Old English Hexateuch, the *Battle
of Maldon*, and similar late-WS texts. The verb is *absent* from the
8th-c. glossaries and the Vespasian Psalter where `-un` would be the
expected ending. **The `-un` form is therefore predicted by the
paradigm but is, on present evidence, a philologically unattested
reconstruction for this specific lexeme.**

## sċūfan — pret. pl.

### Local handbook evidence

**Bosworth-Toller** (s.v. `scūfan`, `āscūfan`, `bescūfan`, `forscūfan`,
`oðscūfan`, `tōscūfan`, `wiðscūfan`, `ūtāscūfan`) cites the following
pret. pl. tokens, all with `-on`:

> "Rinc mænig, gūðfrec guma, An. 1119. **Guman ūt scufon**, **B.
> 215**." (*Beowulf*, line 215; West-Saxon poetic koine, MS late
> 10c.)

> "Hī hine on cwearterne **bescufon**, **Hml. Th.** i. 18, 440;
> **Hml. S.** 23, 350." (Ælfric)

> "**Bescufon** hi þone man in þone ofn and fordytton þone …" (Ælfric;
> An. Ox. 3577)

> "Hé hit **āsceaf** fram his mūðe, Hml. Th. ii. 254, 17.  …
> **Āsceáfon weg** explodit, Wrt. Voc. ii. 29, 72." (3 pl. of
> `āscūfan`, with `-on`)

> "Sume **sceufon**, sume tugon …, Shrn. 154, 25." (Cockayne's
> *Shrine*, late OE prose; 3 pl. of `scūfan`, ending `-on`, with
> `sceuf-` for `scūf-`)

> "Hét ofeslīce leahtra leáse in þæs leádes wylm **scufan**, Jul.
> 582." (= infinitive, not pret. pl.; *Juliana*)

> "Hí ūt **āscufan** scipu, **Chr. 897**; P. 91, 13." (Cynewulfian
> ms. of the Parker Chronicle; the form is graphically ambiguous
> between inf. and pret. pl. — Sweet/Plummer take it as inf.)

**Bülbring §302** (`bulbring_altenglisches_elementarbuch.txt`, line
6031–6033) gives the **only Anglian/Northumbrian** pret. pl.
attestation of this verb that I can locate in the handbook tradition:

> "Für u § 95 und § 100 begegnet (neben sċúa 'Schatten') yu
> [steigender Diphthong üü] in **scyufon 'schoben' Ri.** (südh.
> scufon) und sċyúr 'Schauer' Li. (südh. scur)."

i.e. the *Durham Ritual* (Ri., Northumbrian, 10c.) attests the pret.
pl. `scyufon` — **but with `-on`, not `-un`**, and with a
Northumbrian-specific diphthongised stem-vowel `yu` for `ū`.

**Clark Hall** (line 34723 ff., 35202): paradigm `scūfan² … scēaf …
scofen`; no `-un` token cited.

**Campbell §735(e)** and **Brunner §364.2 Anm. 4**: as for *būgan*,
state the general pattern but **do not adduce `sċufun` as a token**.

### Direct corpus attestations

**None located.** Pret. pl. tokens are: `scufon` (Beowulf 215),
`bescufon` (Ælfric, multiple), `āsceáfon` (Wright's Vocabulary, gloss),
`sceufon` (Cockayne's *Shrine*, late OE), `scyufon` (Northumbrian
*Durham Ritual*) — **all with `-on`**.

The verb is, like `būgan`, **not represented in the early
Anglian/Mercian corpus** (Épinal, Erfurt, Corpus, VP, R¹) by a 3 pl.
pret. token. The Latin Psalter's `extruserunt`, `expulerunt`,
`praecipitaverunt`, etc., are typically glossed in VP with weak verbs
(`ādrāf-`, `āwearp-`) rather than with `scūfan`, so the Anglian
witnesses provide no datum.

### Verdict

**`sċufun` is not attested in the OE corpus** in any source I can
verify. The closest the record comes to a non-`-on` form is
Northumbrian `scyufon` in the Durham Ritual, which still has `-on`
(it differs from southern `scufon` only in the stem-vowel
diphthongisation `ū > yu`, a Northumbrian phonological development
unrelated to the ending). **The `-un` form is, as for `bugun`,
predicted by the paradigm but a philologically unattested
reconstruction for this specific lexeme.**

## Synthesis

Both `bugun` and `sċufun` are **structurally well-formed**: they are
the forms one would expect a Class-II strong verb pret. pl. to take
in the early/Anglian period, on the model of the directly attested
parallel `-un` plurals in the same corpora (Ép. *only* `-un`; Cp.,
VP, R¹ `-un` dominantly, e.g. `forleortun`, `fornōmun`, `awoestun`,
`gnornadun`, `cuómun`, `wǣrun`, `clufun`-type forms for class II,
etc.). They are *not*, however, **directly attested as such in the
surviving corpus**. Both verbs appear in 3 pl. pret. only in late-WS
or West-Saxon-leaning manuscripts (Chronicle 10–11c. annals, Ælfric,
Wulfstan, *Beowulf*, *Maldon*, Cockayne's *Shrine*, Gregory's
Dialogues), where `-on` had already replaced `-un`; and in the
Northumbrian *Durham Ritual* (`scyufon`), where `-on` is also already
in place. The early Anglian glossaries and the Vespasian Psalter — the
texts in which the `-un` ending is robustly preserved for other Class
II verbs — simply do not happen to contain a finite 3 pl. pret. of
either `bēogan/būgan` or `sċūfan`.

**Implications for the FST/TSV decision.** Appendix D.2's claim that
`bugun`/`sċufun` are "the philologically correct earliest-attested
forms" is therefore **too strong as stated**. The accurate revised
claim is:

* For other Class-II verbs (e.g. `clufun`, `budun`, `cuomun`,
  `forleortun` etc.), `-un` is directly attested in Épinal, Corpus,
  VP, and/or R¹.
* For *bēogan/būgan* and *sċūfan* specifically, the 3 pl. pret. is
  **only attested in texts that already write `-on`**. The
  reconstructed `bugun`, `sċufun` are paradigm-internally regular but
  type-unattested for these two lexemes.

The TSV targets `bugon`, `sċufon` therefore correspond to the **only
forms ever surface-attested for these specific verbs**. If the
project's standard is "pick the earliest *attested* OE form
lexeme-by-lexeme", `bugon`/`sċufon` are the correct targets and the
FST output `bugun`/`sċufun` is a (regular but unattested) projection.
If the project's standard is "pick the form predicted by the
paradigm at a given chronological stratum (here: pre-late-WS)", the
FST output is correct and the TSV could be retargeted to
`bugun`/`sċufun` with an asterisk or footnote indicating the
type-gap. The choice between these two policies is a project-level
one and is not adjudicated by the philological evidence alone.
